**PROVED CONDITIONAL ON `CubeBlockPath` and `CubeBgHyp` (explicit R2 estimate).** Let $x$ be the top block of a parent datum site over $y_0\in Y$, $D_\square$ its `cubeData`, $\phi_\square$ its real unit cube background, $b=Q\phi_\square(x)$, and $\delta=\delta_k$. `CubeBlockPath` says that every point of `G_\square.blk k x` lies in $\Omega_1(G_\square)$ and every two such points have an axis path inside $\square^\sim\cap\Omega_1$ of at most $d(L^k-1)$ edges. $$\bigl||\Psi(x)|^2-1\bigr|<(2d+3)\delta<(2d+4)\delta=: \eta_Y.\tag{R2Y}$$

## 2. MASS: physical mass, two scale ranges, and the retained shell

**PROVED (purpose of R3).** `Background/Data.lean:InPsiTilde.r3` is $\mu L^{2\ell(x)}|1-(\Psi/|\Psi|)\cdot h|<r^2$. In `Background/Approximate.lean:phi0_mass_real`, `real_mass_bound` consumes R2, R3 and R4 to prove $\sqrt\mu\,\ell|\phi_0-h|\le C r$; `Background/Mass116.lean:bg_mass_bound` transfers it to the solution and `Def5.lean:theorem71` packages the resulting `BgBounds`. `Background/CompositionDomain.lean:lemma62_core` uses the parent R3 directly on the inherited data sites and its mass-field estimate on newly composed sites, including collars. The contraction's mass forcing is controlled through this approximate/base bound (`Contraction.lean:Prop51Hyp.setup`, `Approximate.lean:lemma53`); `BgHyp.massLe`, $\mu L^{2k}\le1$, alone does not supply it.

**PROVED (source and mass identity).** P1 (3.18) requires the angular mass term to be $O(\delta^2)$. P4 §5 constructs complete small-field backgrounds with the physical $\nu_k$; its Theorem 1 allows small positive initial mass, while P4 §6 (6.1)–(6.2) obtains a directional bound near the last step. P5 (1.9) and its paragraph on p. 490 explicitly switch the *large-field* variational backgrounds to mass zero when $\beta_k\nu_k\le1$ (fixed $k_0$), retaining the mass in the main action; it says this omission was unnecessary in the small-field analysis. In scheme A, `SmallField/Def6v2Regime.lean:sfBgMassAt,physMass_mul_sq` set the parent small-field solver mass $\mu=\nu_kL^{-2k}$, so `ParentDomain.lean` must be instantiated with this $\mu$ and its `hmass` is $2\nu_k<\eta^2$. `Def7.lean:FieldClauses.mu_eq` instead sets the `chiY` cube-solver mass to `bgMass k0 …`, zero for $k\le k_0$ and physical above. These masses must not be identified below the switch.

**PROVED CONDITIONAL ON `EarlyMassRatio` (the early inequality).** `SmallField/Def6v2Regime.lean:RunningHyp.beta_mul_nu_le` gives $\beta_k\nu_k\le E=e^{2\epsilon}$ for $k\le k_0$ in `KZeroGenuine`; `LargeField/Analytic/Constants.lean:deltaRun_eq` and `Def7.lean:Coh.delta_cs` give $\delta_k^2=A_0^2\lambda_k^{2p_0}/(a\beta_k)$, $\lambda_k=\log(a\beta_k)$. Therefore
$$\frac{2\nu_k}{\delta_k^2}\le\frac{2aE}{A_0^2\lambda_k^{2p_0}}.\tag{M1}$$
The exact required ledger bridge is `EarlyMassRatio`: $2aE<A_0^2\lambda_k^{2p_0}$ at every used $k\le k_0$ (it follows from $p_0\ge1$ and a sufficiently large initial $\lambda_0$; the cited definitions/flow lemma do not state this as a theorem). With $\eta_Y=(2d+4)\delta_k$ from (R2Y), $\eta=\max(3dL\delta_{k-1},\eta_Y)$, (M1) gives $2\nu_k<\delta_k^2<\eta^2$. Thus R3 is paid by small physical mass even at $k_0$; no direction test is needed below $k_0$. If the `kZero` convention is nongenuine, $k_0=0$ and there is no positive early scale.

**REFUTED (a uniform mass-only assertion above $k_0$).** `BgHyp.massLe` gives only $\nu_k\le1$ and `RunningHyp.lt_beta_mul_nu` gives a *lower* bound on $\beta_k\nu_k$ above $k_0$; neither yields $2\nu_k<O(\delta_k^2)$. In fact $\nu_k/\delta_k^2=a\beta_k\nu_k/(A_0^2\lambda_k^{2p_0})$ grows along the running flow after the switch. `Q13-MARGIN.md` proves $\nu_k/\varepsilon_k^2\to0$ for the early range; that is not the needed late comparison with $\delta_k^2$.

**PROVED CONDITIONAL ON `CubeFieldAverage` and R2Y (late Y sites only).** Above $k_0$, `chiK` uses physical mass and `RegIneq.FieldClose` supplies $|\phi_\square(z)-h|<\delta_k/\sqrt{\nu_k}$ on the tested cube. `CubeFieldAverage` says its whole averaging block lies in that tested set; convexity then gives $|Q\phi_\square-h|\le\delta_k/\sqrt{\nu_k}$, so `BlockClose` gives $\sqrt{\nu_k}|\Psi-h|<(1+\sqrt{\nu_k})\delta_k\le2\delta_k$ using $\nu_k\le1$. For $u=\Psi/|\Psi|$ (nonzero by R2Y and $\eta_Y<1$), $|u-h|\le2|\Psi-h|$ and $1-u\cdot h=|u-h|^2/2$. Hence $\nu_k(1-u\cdot h)<8\delta_k^2<\eta_Y^2\le\eta^2$ for $d\ge3$, $\delta_k>0$. This is the directional test already in P5 (1.19) and landed `Reg`; it is not a new factor for Y.

**OPEN (late retained sites; exact failing statement and repair).** A parent datum site over a retained component but outside every Y-cube stencil is covered by `hAct` only through `ChiDomAt`; `Characteristic/OperatorDomain.lean:PsiTildeMulti,ChiDomProp` test pair distances and sphere defects, with **no** $h$ direction. Thus the late bound $\nu_kL^{2\ell(s)}(1-u_s\cdot h)<\eta^2$ for *all* `dataSites(genW Y)` does not follow from `hAct`, `hR1Y`, R2Y, or `chiY`. The exact extra input for a late extension is `RetainedDirectionAtParent`: every such site satisfies $\sqrt{\nu_k}L^{\ell(s)-k}|u_s-h|<\sqrt2\,\eta$ (equivalently R3, with a strict margin). One route is a sharp directional factor on the retained stencil, at tolerance $c\delta_k/\sqrt{\nu_k}$ for the tested background (or directly $\nu_kL^{2\ell(s)}(1-u_s\cdot h)<c^2\delta_k^2$ for data), plus a site-transport theorem and the cost of its complementary sector. P5 (1.24)–(1.25) and the landed `ChiDomProp` do not already supply it. `Def7.lean:D9Coeff.k_le` and `Def7v2Class.lean:IsRep2.coeff` restrict the current nonflat representation to $k\le k_0$; the late repair is needed only if this parent-domain theorem is used beyond that range (e.g. an `IsRepFlat` history with retained sites).
