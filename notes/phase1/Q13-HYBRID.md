# Q13: hybrid cutoffs, complete actions, and the remaining interface

## Decision

The proposed A0′ is a useful **four-object convention**: the physical action has mass $m_j=\nu_jL^{-2j}>0$; the field used to evaluate that action is the complete solution $\phi_j^m$; the sharp LF partition uses the massless solution $\phi_j^0$ for $j\le k_0$; and the auxiliary $R$ reference is the massless $\psi_k^0,\phi_k^0$. These objects cannot all be represented by the present single `OpConsts.mu`: it is passed to `chiK`, `chiPrimeNew`, `InUnionDomainR`, and `mainActionLoc` (`LargeField/Operators/Quiet.lean`, lines 153–193). A0′ requires separate action, cutoff, and reference masses, and an exact density construction using them.

This hybrid preserves the invariant **sharp sets** needed in LF-RECONSTRUCTION (S5)(i) and III.9.4–III.9.6. The real overlap with complete backgrounds is small in size but is **not automatic from a sharp `<δ_j` cutoff**: a fixed margin needs a narrower real predicate or a separately proved data-domain margin. The physical action and inherited SF terms can be carried in the complete convention without a free/complete comparison at the SF-to-LF boundary, if the LF density is *defined* to retain those exact factors. The resulting new proof obligation is the physical factorization and the complete-action $R$ window estimate. Neither is established by the response bound alone. I therefore recommend **A0′ as the next construction to try**, with the author-approved A retained until these two assertions have proofs. It reuses the landed massless $R$ reference and avoids A0's replacement of `PsiZero*`; it does not yet close Q13.

## 1. What P5–P7 already do, and what A0′ changes

P5 pp. 489–490, (1.8)–(1.9), keeps the external-field term in the physical main action while setting the background mass to zero below the crossing. P6 pp. 10–11, (2.22)–(2.25), describes the crossing; P7 pp. 506–512, (2.34)–(2.60), removes a ripe component using a massless reference and a physical main action with its $\nu$ term. LF-RECONSTRUCTION (A-III.2) says the background and cutoffs at $j\le k_0$ are massless; its III.6.3 (2.58′) explicitly retains $\nu_k\langle\delta\phi,\phi_k^0-h\rangle$, and III.6.4 plus (Λ25)(c) bounds it. Its III.8.1 reference density contains the physical mass action evaluated at the massless reference. Thus **the massless cutoff, massless $R$ reference, and physical mass term are already the P5–P7 LF convention**.

A0′ differs from that convention at exactly the action background: it substitutes $\phi^m$ for P5–P7's $\phi^0$ in the main action of each new large-field region and in its inherited action conversion. It also keeps the SF effective terms in their complete-background convention, rather than P5's old massless composition. This is not “P7 verbatim.” P7's massless $R$ reference is allowed as an auxiliary expansion point and reference density, but if the phrase “every background entering an expansion is complete” is taken literally, the proposed massless $R$ reference contradicts it. The mathematically consistent A0′ convention makes this one explicit exception.

The complete SF class is represented by `SmallField/Def6v2Regime.lean:Regime.sfBgMass`; its terms, Xi domains, renormalization jets, and fluctuation expansion read that mass. A cutoff chosen for an LF history is a **real indicator multiplying the density**, not the definition of a complex Xi domain. `Def6v2Bridge.lean` and `Def6v2Renorm*.lean` do not change their background mass when an LF indicator is multiplied in. The SF step still has to be proved on the real configurations selected by the massless cutoff; the overlap lemma below is the required bridge from that support to the complete solver's real domain. The existing abstract SF bounds do not infer such a support inclusion or an exact conditional integral from the indicator's definition. Analyticity remains in the SF terms and in $g$; a sharp indicator is kept on the real contour and is never analytically continued.

## 2. The overlap lemma: a proved implication with named domain inputs

Use $n_j:=\nu_j$ for the dimensionless mass in this section, to avoid confusing it with the fine mass $m_j=n_jL^{-2j}$. Let $E=e^{2\epsilon}$, $\beta_jn_j\le E$ for $j\le k_0$, and
$$\delta_j=\frac{A_0\lambda_j^{p_0}}{\sqrt{a\beta_j}},\qquad
W_j:=K_mB_hn_j,\qquad K_m=\sqrt N K_w,\quad B_h=2\quad(h\in S^{N-1}).\tag{2.1}$$
For a generating set of top level $j$, assume one **same datum**, including the full Dirichlet collar, and the real existence/`BgBounds`, `GreenSetting`, `Dec2.OpSetting`, multiplier, and smallness premises of `MASS-RESPONSE.md` (1.3). These are precisely the hypotheses of the landed `LargeField/R/MassResponseHyp.lean:massChange_sameData_response_unit`; the theorem then gives, with the local spacing $\ell(x)$,
$$|\phi^m-\phi^0|,\quad \ell|\partial(\phi^m-\phi^0)|,\quad
\ell^2|\Delta^D(\phi^m-\phi^0)|,\quad
\ell^2|\alpha^m-\alpha^0|\le W_j.\tag{2.2}$$
The source is distributed, so (2.2) has a constant layer profile; it supplies no extra decay toward lower layers.

**Quantitative regularity implication — PROVED CONDITIONAL ON (2.2).** If the massless `BlockClose` and `GradClose` tests hold with tolerance $t$, the corresponding complete tests hold with tolerance $t+2W_j$. The block average changes by at most $W_j$. Each scaled first difference, Dirichlet Laplacian, and multiplier changes by at most $W_j$ under (2.2); the factor $2$ allows either orientation of a boundary edge and the passage from $\Delta^D$ to the extended field on the collar. At sites in the complete variational region $|\phi^m-h|\le2$ and $h\cdot h-1=0$. Therefore the complete `FieldClose` test holds at tolerance $t+2W_j$ provided
$$t+2W_j>2\sqrt{n_j}.\tag{2.3}$$
In particular, massless `RegIneq(δ_j)` implies complete `RegIneq(δ_j+2W_j)` under (2.3). For `RegMixedIneq`, apply the same argument separately to its $3\delta_j/4$ and $5\delta_j/4$ tests, with the mass parameter that actually appears in each test. This is a pointwise implication for strict inequalities; it does not create a fixed margin inside `RegIneq(δ_j)`.

Here are explicit final-scale sufficient conditions. Since $n_j\le E/\beta_j$,
$$\frac{2W_j}{\delta_j}\le\frac{2K_mB_hE\sqrt a}{A_0\lambda_j^{p_0}\sqrt{\beta_j}},\qquad
\frac{2\sqrt{n_j}}{\delta_j}\le\frac{2\sqrt{aE}}{A_0\lambda_j^{p_0}}.\tag{2.4}$$
Fix $0<\sigma<1/4$ before row 16. Choose $\lambda_*,\beta$ so that for every $j\le k_0$ the first ratio in (2.4) is at most $\sigma/2$ and the second is less than $1-\sigma$. Then a massless test at the **inner** tolerance $(1-\sigma)\delta_j$ implies the complete test at $\delta_j$ with at least $\sigma\delta_j/2$ numerical slack; the single-scale field test follows from (2.3). For a mixed $3\delta_j/4$ test, use the stronger $2W_j\le(3\sigma/8)\delta_j$ and $2\sqrt{n_j}<(3/4)(1-\sigma)\delta_j$. These conditions are eventual because $\lambda_j\ge\lambda_*$ and $\beta_j\ge\beta_{min}$; they are compatible with the LFR order of choices. If the literal massless cutoff uses $\delta_j$ rather than the inner tolerance, the conclusion is only complete regularity at $\delta_j+2W_j$.

**Complete real-domain implication — PROVED CONDITIONAL ON `cutoffDataRegularity`.** The preceding inequalities concern `RegIneq`, not the `InUnionDomainR` conjunct of `Reg`. Let `cutoffDataRegularity` assert that the real datum selected by the massless predicate has P2 R1–R2 left sides at most $\eta_j/2$, where $\eta_j=C_{\rm dat}\delta_j\le c_1/2$ and $C_{\rm dat}$ is fixed before row 16. For real unit $h$, P2 R3 is bounded by $2n_j$ (the normalized real data direction and $h$ are unit), and R4 is zero. If
$$\lambda_*^{2p_0}>\frac{8aE}{C_{\rm dat}^2A_0^2},\tag{2.5}$$
then $2n_j<\eta_j^2/4$. The **same datum** is consequently in `InDomainR(m_j,η_j)` and hence `InUnionDomainR(c₁,m_j)`, with a half-radius margin for R1–R2, a three-quarter-squared-radius margin for R3, and at least $c_1/2$ in the upper radius constraint. BG Theorem 7.1 gives the complete solution and its bounds, after which `massChange_sameData_response_unit` yields (2.2). The logical order is domain inclusion, existence and `BgBounds`, then mass response; invoking (2.2) to establish its own complete-solution existence would be circular. `cutoffDataRegularity` includes the necessary strict R1–R2 witness and has not been verified for every actual LF predicate and mixed collar.

**Failure of an unconditional fixed-margin claim — PROVED.** A sharp condition $u<\delta_j$ permits $u=\delta_j-1/n$ for arbitrarily large $n$. Therefore it has no positive margin independent of the configuration. A perturbation bounded by $W_j>0$ may cross $\delta_j$. Likewise `InUnionDomainR` only asserts existence of some radius $\eta\le c_1$; its witness may approach $c_1$. Neither fixed `RegIneq(δ_j)` margin nor a prescribed P2 domain witness follows from that Boolean predicate alone. Thus the overlap lemma requested for the *literal* $\delta_j$ cutoff is false if “fixed margin” means a fixed slack at the same $\delta_j$. Equations (2.4)–(2.5) show the repair: keep the literal support but use a wider complete tolerance and an independently proved domain witness, or introduce an inner massless cutoff $(1-\sigma)\delta_j$ and a shell branch.

## 3. Complement branches and the SF-to-LF boundary

With one massless-predicate cutoff $\chi^0_j(C)$ used on both sides, the partition is exactly
$$1=\prod_{C\in\pi_j}\bigl(\chi_j^0(C)+1-\chi_j^0(C)\bigr).\tag{3.1}$$
Its sets are invariant under rotation of spin variables with $h$ fixed because $\phi^0(\Psi)$ has no $h$ parameter. There is **no symmetric-difference comparison** at the SF-to-LF boundary. If an inner cutoff is used to obtain fixed margin, the strip $\chi_j^0(\delta_j)-\chi_j^0((1-\sigma)\delta_j)$ is itself a real, invariant, nonnegative branch; on its support some inner test fails. It must be assigned an (S2) gain and cannot simply be discarded.

For a massless-predicate failure, P6 (3.6)–(3.8) and LF-RECONSTRUCTION II.7.8, IV.1.4, LF-1 provide the corresponding gain **for the P5 action evaluated at its massless background**, subject to their hypotheses. If the cutoff is shrunk by $(1-\sigma)$, its quadratic witness gain is $(1-\sigma)^2$ times the original gain before entropy costs. The complete background changes the real action. On the *same field*, its added mass potential obeys
$$0\le\tfrac12\beta_jn_j\sum_{x\in C}|\phi_x-h|^2\le2E\,\#C.\tag{3.2}$$
This is Q13-DECISION (5.1); it does **not** compare $A_{n_j}(\phi^m)$ with $A_{n_j}(\phi^0)$. On a region and collar satisfying the actual incidence and data assumptions of MASS-RESPONSE §2, the latter comparison is instead
$$\beta_j|A_{n_j}(\phi^m)-A_{n_j}(\phi^0)|
\le q_j|X|_j,qquad q_j:=C_D\beta_jn_j^2+C_W\beta_jn_j\delta_{j-1}.\tag{3.3}$$
**PROVED CONDITIONAL ON** `massChange_sameData_response_unit`, `newRegion_spacing_ge`, `crossingBond_count`, and the complete wall-gradient premise: (3.3) is MASS-RESPONSE (2.5) at that scale. Since $\beta_jn_j\le E$, $q_j\le C_DE^2/\beta_j+C_WE\delta_{j-1}$. If the old failure gain is $G_j$ per marked cube, the new gain is at least $G_j-q_j$ for the same counted region. In particular, choose row-16 parameters so that $q_j\le G_j/2$; for $G_j\asymp A_3\lambda_j^{2p_0}$ this is eventual. A volume factor from (3.2) may instead be paid by $c_2$ only after its site count is bounded per new cube. The nonquiet mixed and domain-failure branches need the exact witness-to-action implication on the complete background; they are **OPEN** beyond the named comparison inputs. A wall or an inherited region with no new-cube assignment is not covered by (3.3).

$$\delta\phi=\phi^{m}_{k_1,k}-\phi^0_k

## 5. A0's invariant envelope: what is proved and what is not

The following one-spin computation refutes direct fixed-$h$ invariance of A0's complete cutoff **even when $m\ll\delta$**. On $S^1$ let $h=e_1$ and minimize $\frac12|s-\Psi|^2+\frac m2|s-h|^2$. The solution is $s=(\Psi+mh)/|\Psi+mh|$. Take $\Psi=(1+t)e_1$ and cutoff $|\Psi-s|<\delta$: its left side is $t$. Rotate the spin datum to $\Psi'=(1+t)e_2$ while keeping $h=e_1$. The new left side is
$$F_m(t)^2=\left((1+t)-\frac{1+t}{\sqrt{(1+t)^2+m^2}}\right)^2
+\frac{m^2}{(1+t)^2+m^2}.\tag{5.1}$$
For every $m>0$ and $t\ge0$, $F_m(t)^{1/2}>t$: the first term's square root component exceeds $t$ because $\sqrt{(1+t)^2+m^2}>1+t$. Choose $0<t<\delta$ sufficiently close to $\delta$ that $F_m(t)^{1/2}>\delta$. The complete cutoff is one before rotation and zero after it. The same holds for arbitrarily small $m/\delta$; the bad set is a thin shell near the sharp boundary. Hence unmodified A0 (S5)(i) fails as a mathematical statement, not merely as a missing proof.

There **is** a quantitative invariant sandwich for geometric tests, conditional on (2.2) and domain overlap. With $r=2W_j$, define $A_-:=\mathrm{RegIneq}^0(\delta_j-r)$, $A_m:=\mathrm{RegIneq}^m(\delta_j)$, and $A_+:=\mathrm{RegIneq}^0(\delta_j+r)$, using corresponding shifted mixed thresholds. The triangle inequality proves
$$A_-\subset A_m\subset A_+.\tag{5.2}$$
Both $A_\pm$ are invariant at fixed $h$, and $A_+$ contains the complete-background geometric small-field set. But `Reg` also has `InUnionDomainR`, so (5.2) for the **full characteristic** requires a common real-domain witness with fixed margin, precisely the input isolated in §2. Moreover $\mathbf1_{A_m}=\mathbf1_{A_+}-\mathbf1_{A_+\setminus A_m}$ expresses the outer envelope with a **negative** sharp branch; it is not the nonnegative kernel decomposition used in (S2). The positive split is
$$\mathbf1_{A_m}=\mathbf1_{A_-}+\mathbf1_{A_m\setminus A_-}.\tag{5.3}$$
The second branch violates an inner massless test, so it can earn a gain *conditional on* the LF-1 witness-to-action estimate and (3.3). It must be kept out of the $R$-rotated core or given its own invariant-support construction. Therefore an outer envelope and (5.1)'s thin-shell observation do not by themselves prove `a0_rotation_support`; the full invariant-core-plus-shell $R$ theorem is **OPEN**. The one-spin calculation also shows why a pointwise multiplicative comparison of complete and massless sharp indicators fails on the shell.

P7's massless $R$ reference, its $\nu$ linear term in (2.58), and the (Λ25)(c) estimate are consistent with that reading.
We are testing a hybrid that keeps those massless cutoffs and the $R$ reference but evaluates the physical action and inherited SF terms at complete backgrounds.
The free cutoff is rotation invariant with $h$ fixed; the complete action background changes by $O(\nu_j)$ on a common real domain.
A literal sharp cutoff supplies no fixed margin at its threshold; an inner cutoff or a separate domain witness is needed.
The inherited SF terms can avoid a free/complete interface only if an exact LF factorization carries them unchanged.
P7's $R$ window then needs an added mass-response estimate, although its existing $\nu$ linear term is retained.
Until these estimates are proved, we keep the author-approved two-convention scheme A as the formal reconstruction.
