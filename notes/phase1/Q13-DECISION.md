# Q13 decision: complete backgrounds in the large-field operators

## Summary

I recommend **A0 as the design for a new reconstruction**: use the physical positive background mass at every scale in both the small-field class and every large-field operator. Then there is no SF-to-LF change of solver convention, including in factors inherited from $j\le k_0$. The a priori $k_0$ still separates the part of the history on which $R$ and window bounds are used from the frozen part. This is a change to P5–P7, whose printed analysis uses field-free backgrounds below $k_0$; it is not a theorem obtained by relabeling their constants.

A0 replaces the difficult regional interface of variant A by a **positive-mass $R$ theorem**. That theorem must redo P7's reference minimizer, window estimates, denominator, and analytic rotation argument. In particular, the exact zero-tolerance conclusion of P7 Lemma 2.1 is false with positive mass, and its invariant-indicator hypothesis (S5) is no longer automatic. Below I prove the first changed cutoff assertion with constants and give a counterexample to the unmodified reference lemma. The positive-mass $R$ theorem, the complete-background small-field fluctuation bounds on the actual class domain, and their Lean instantiation remain open. Thus Q13 is not closed by this choice. The physical root, with fixed real unit $h$ and complex observable $g$, is not contradicted by the transverse-source counterexamples.

## 2. The first changed assertion, with constants

Write $\delta_j=A_0\lambda_j^{p_0}/\sqrt{a\beta_j}$, as in LF-RECONSTRUCTION III.6.4 and (Λ25), and suppose $\beta_j\nu_j\le E:=e^{2\epsilon}$ for $j\le k_0$, $\beta_j>0$, $a,A_0>0$, and $\lambda_j\ge\lambda_*$. Choose the row-16 threshold after all earlier constants so that

$$A_0^2\lambda_*^{2p_0}>\frac{64}{9}aE.\tag{2.1}$$

**Real field-test lemma (proved).** For every real sphere-valued background $\phi$ and real unit $h$, the `FieldClose` conjunct of `Reg` with tolerance $\delta_j$ and dimensionless mass $\nu_j$ is true. The same holds for the mixed `RegMixedIneq` field test with tolerance $3\delta_j/4$ whenever its dimensionless mass is $\nu_j$; with $\nu_{j-1}$ and tolerance $3\delta_{j-1}/4$, apply the condition at $j-1$. At the base, the site indicator $\mathbf1[|\phi_x-h|<\delta_0/\sqrt\nu]$ is identically one in regime $k_0\ge1$ under the analogous base inequality $A_0^2\lambda_0^{2p_0}>4a\beta_0\nu$.

**Proof.** $|\phi-h|\le|\phi|+|h|=2$ and $h\cdot h-1=0$. By $\beta_j\nu_j\le E$ and (2.1),
$$\frac{3\delta_j}{4\sqrt{\nu_j}}\ge \frac{3A_0\lambda_*^{p_0}}{4\sqrt{aE}}>2.$$
Thus both strict `FieldClose` inequalities hold, including the single-scale one with the larger tolerance. If $\nu_j=0$, `FieldClose` is true by its implication definition. For a hard spin at $j=0$ the same triangle inequality gives the base claim. All inequalities are pointwise, so their products equal one on **every** real configuration, with no cutoff cost and no exceptional shell. $\square$

The real domain `InUnionDomainR` also contains mass-weighted source inequalities (R3)–(R4), not merely `FieldClose`. They are automatic for a *fixed real unit* $h$ and real normalized data direction when $2\nu_j<\delta_{\rm dom}^2$, but this conclusion needs a witness $\delta_{\rm dom}\le c_1$ whose R1–R2 inequalities have a strict margin. For example, if R1–R2 hold with $\delta_{\rm dom}/2$, $\delta_{\rm dom}\le c_1$, and $2\nu_j<\delta_{\rm dom}^2$, then R3 follows from $|1-u\cdot h|\le2$ for unit $u$, and R4 is zero. Since $\nu_j\le E/\beta_j$, the numerical condition follows by taking $\beta_j>2E/\delta_{\rm dom}^2$. This is a sufficient **overlap lemma**, not equality of all unrestricted existential-domain indicators: points arbitrarily close to the boundary of `InUnionDomainR` have no guaranteed half-margin.

The field-test lemma does **not** make the complete-background geometric indicators independent of $h$. `BlockClose`, `GradClose`, and the domain use $\phi^{\nu,h}(\Psi)$, which depends on $h$. This distinction controls the $R$ verdict below.

$$1=\chi^+_C+(1-\chi^+_C),\qquad

This is the exact history partition, including all failures of the complete-background geometric tests. For the explicit site failure, if $\frac12\beta_j\nu_j|\phi-h|^2$ is in the main action and $|\phi-h|\ge\delta_j/\sqrt{\nu_j}$, then half of that term gives $\beta_j\delta_j^2/4=A_0^2\lambda_j^{2p_0}/(4a)$ of gain. ## 4. The P7 reference: an exact failure and the replacement

P7 Lemma 2.1, LF-RECONSTRUCTION Lemma III.3.3, minimizes the **massless** problem (2.34) and places $\psi_k^0$ in a tube of radius $K_6\delta$ about the boundary direction. At $\delta=0$, this asserts equality with that direction. Its unchanged positive-mass analogue is false.

Take one sphere spin $s\in S^1$, boundary direction $e=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$, and real unit $h=e_1$ with $0<\theta<\pi/2$. Set
$$A_m(s)=\tfrac12|s-e|^2+\tfrac m2|s-h|^2,qquad 0<m<1/2.$$
The unique minimizers are $s_0=e$ and
$$s_m=\frac{e+mh}{\sqrt{1+2m\cos\theta+m^2}}\ne e.\tag{4.1}$$
Indeed the action equals the constant $1+m-(e+mh)\cdot s$; Cauchy–Schwarz gives (4.1). The tangent component of the forcing at $e$ is $m\sin\theta>0$, so the shift is nonzero however small $m$ is. The zero-tolerance $K_6\delta$ conclusion fails. A direct estimate from $|e+mh|\ge1-m$ gives
$$|s_m-e|\le\frac{2m}{1-m}\le4m.\tag{4.2}$$
The corresponding repaired multispin statement must use a tube $K_6\delta+C_m\nu_k$ (or absorb $C_m\nu_k$ into a larger $K_6'\delta$ after proving $\nu_k\ll\delta_k$), and a minimizer $\psi_k^{m,h}$ analytic jointly in the old spin data and a specified **local** complex germ of $h$. It is jointly $O(N)$-equivariant, $(\psi,h)\mapsto(R\psi,Rh)$; the stronger $\psi$-alone equivariance of the massless lemma is false by (4.1). The Dirichlet Hessian margin in III.3.3 is $\gamma_0c_{D_0}/2$, $c_{D_0}=4/(D_0+1)^2$. A sufficient perturbative hypothesis is an operator bound $\|H_m-H_0\|\le C_H\nu_k\le\gamma_0c_{D_0}/4$; then the new Hessian is at least $\gamma_0c_{D_0}/4$. This is a **reduction**, since the constrained multiplier contribution to $C_H$ and the analytic contraction have not been proved here.

There is a second, distinct obstruction. LF-RECONSTRUCTION Definition II.5.7 (S5)(i) requires the Borel indicator set of a core kernel to be invariant when the spin variables rotate with **$h$ fixed**. III.9.4–III.9.5 use precisely that equality to rotate the real integration contour while leaving each sharp indicator at real arguments. Under A0 the geometric predicates evaluate $\phi^{m,h}(\Psi)$, so joint covariance gives only $P^+(R\Psi,Rh)=P^+(\Psi,h)$, not $P^+(R\Psi,h)=P^+(\Psi,h)$. The field-test lemma removes the *explicit* $h$ test but does not remove this dependence. Consequently the unmodified (S5) and III.9.5 proof do **not** apply. A new $R$ theorem must either prove an invariant common-support envelope with its complement paid, or integrate a changed indicator under rotation with a quantitative boundary-shell estimate. This is part of the named `A0PositiveMassR` input; it cannot be charged by $e^{C\beta\nu|C|}$ pointwise, since unequal indicators have infinite ratio on their symmetric difference.

## 5. Parts II–IV: statement-by-statement change ledger

$$0\le\frac12\beta_j\nu_j\sum_{x\in C}|\phi_x-h|^2\le2E\,n_j(C),\qquad
e^{-2E n_j(C)}\le e^{-\beta_jA_m}/e^{-\beta_jA_0}\le1,\tag{5.1}$$

If the action uses weighted bonds or scale-unit volumes, $n_j(C)$ means the corresponding weighted site count; the bound follows term by term from $|\phi-h|^2\le4$.
