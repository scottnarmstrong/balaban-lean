# Lemma F: local large-field operators and the background-field tail

**Choice (b).** The literal background in P6 (3.2) is a solution of a variational problem on the generating set $\mathbb B_{q}(\partial\Omega_q)$, not on a component of $Z_q$. Its influence has exponential decay but no finite support. The exact factorization required by P5 (1.22) is a factorization of the **localized representation** below. The density itself has interactions between components, carried by $\mathcal B$.

This lemma uses the analytic decoupling and localization statements I-4a, I-4b and I-5 of `LOCALIZATION.md` at $\lambda=\infty$, with the one-cube variation of I-5 described in its §4.6(V3). The latter is obtained by the same interpolation proof: for a one-cube anchor the initial tree-length term is zero, so the extra $X_1\subset\Omega_{k+1}$ condition of I-5 is not needed. The input includes uniform estimates on the complex domains of P5 (1.36)–(1.37), not just on real configurations. See the status paragraph at the end for the present proof status of these inputs.

## Statement for Part II.5

**Lemma F (localization and factorization).** Let the parameters and admissible history be as in v4 §§3.1–3.3 and §5. At scale $q$, put $X_q=Z_{q-1}^{c}\cap Z_q$. For a component $Z$ of $Z_q$, put $X_Z=X_q\cap Z$, $U_Z=Z^\approx$ and $V_Z=U_Z^\sim$. The main-action contribution in the literal one-step operator P6 (3.2) is

$$\mathcal A_q(X_Z;\psi,\widetilde\psi)=\beta_q A_q^*(X_Z,\mathbb B_q;\psi,\phi_q(\mathbb B_q(\partial\Omega_q);\widetilde\psi)).\tag{F.1}$$

There is a canonical local action $\mathcal A_q^{\mathrm{loc}}(X_Z)$, defined below from the I-4 decoupled family, and an **exact** identity

$$\mathcal A_q(X_Z)=\mathcal A_q^{\mathrm{loc}}(X_Z)+\sum_{Y\in\mathcal D_q(\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega_q^c):\,Y\cap Z\ne\varnothing,\,Y\cap Z_q^c\ne\varnothing}\Delta_q(Y;Z).\tag{F.2}$$

The local action depends on the field and history only through $V_Z$. Every $\Delta_q(Y;Z)$ is analytic on the H7 domains of P5 (1.36)–(1.37), depends only on the variables and history on $Y$, and has the H7 bound

$$|\Delta_q(Y;Z)|\le\varepsilon_F e^{-\kappa d_q(Y\,\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega_q^c)},\qquad \varepsilon_F\le1,\tag{F.3}$$

after increasing the final lower threshold $\lambda_*$, with the constants through row 11 of v4 §5 fixed. More precisely, if $\kappa_{\rm loc}>\kappa$ is the output decay exponent of the one-cube localization expansion, then for constants $C_F,p_F,c_F>0$ depending only on the constants in rows 0–11,

$$\varepsilon_F\le C_F\sup_{q\ge1}\left[\beta_q(1+\widehat\lambda_q)^{p_F}\exp\{-c_F(\kappa_{\rm loc}-\kappa)([R_{q-1}]-s_*)\}\right].\tag{F.4}$$

Here $s_*$ is the fixed interaction-cube collar of Lemma G, and the supremum is over scales in the history. Since $R_{q-1}=R_0\widehat\lambda_{q-1}^{2}$ and $\log\beta_q=O(\widehat\lambda_q)$, this tends to zero as $\widehat\lambda_0\to\infty$, uniformly in the torus and the history. Formula (F.3) is therefore available with the fixed choice $\varepsilon_F=1$ made **before** $B_0,H_0,c_1,c_2$.

Define the one-step operator $\mathbb T^{(q-1),\mathrm{loc}}$ by P5 (2.47) and P6 (3.2), with the same reference measures, Gaussian, characteristic functions and vacuum factors, and with $\exp(-\mathcal A_q^{\mathrm{loc}}(X_Z))$ in place of the main-action factor (F.1). In the recursion P6 (3.3), include in the operator only those terms of P6's $\mathcal B^{(q-1)}$ whose connected localization domain is contained in the current component. Terms crossing its exterior remain in the incoming H7 exponent with their existing bound; add $-\sum_{Z,Y}\Delta_q(Y;Z)$ to that exponent. Then the resulting $\mathbb T_q^{\mathrm{loc}}(Z,\mathfrak a)$ is a kernel on $V_Z$ and, whenever $V_{Z'}\cap V_{Z''}=\varnothing$,

$$\mathbb T_q^{\mathrm{loc}}(Z'\cup Z'',\mathfrak a)=\mathbb T_q^{\mathrm{loc}}(Z',\mathfrak a|_{Z'})\,\mathbb T_q^{\mathrm{loc}}(Z'',\mathfrak a|_{Z''});\tag{F.5}$$

the factors commute. In particular this holds for distinct v4 $\omega_q$-components, because v4 §3.1 proves that their $U^{\sim4}$ are disjoint. This is P5 (1.22), journal p. 493, for the localized representation. The full density is unchanged by (F.2).

The same support and factorization assertion applies to both retained kinds of operators. K1 is $\mathbb T_q^{\mathrm{loc}}(Z,\mathfrak a)$ multiplied by the successive small-field indicators and the first complementary indicator of P7 §2, including (2.18), (2.21) and (2.61). K2 is the componentwise operator (3.7) of P7, formed from $\mathbb T_{k_1}^{\mathrm{loc}}$, multiplied by the selected term of (3.20) and by $\exp[-\beta_q A_q^*(Z;\psi_q,\phi_q(\mathbb B_q(Z^\approx);\widetilde\psi_q))]$. The latter background is the actual minimizer on the **restricted** generating set $\mathbb B_q(Z^\approx)$; this restriction is part of P7's construction. The coset average used to make (3.7) analytic is taken separately for each component. The removed branch of $R^{(q)}$ uses the same componentwise (3.7); the terms generated by its polymer expansion that connect components stay in H7, as in P7 (3.23), (3.26)–(3.28). No equality of full densities with a product of component densities is asserted.

### Definition of the local action

Fix a scale-$q$ cube $b\subset X_Z$. Split $A_q^*$ into its finite site, bond and block summands $a_{q,b}$. A summand touching several scale-$q$ cubes is divided equally among them, so this anchoring preserves the Euclidean symmetries of P5 H5. Use the generating set $\mathbb B_q(\partial\Omega_q)$ of P6 (2.15) and the I-4b analytic background family $(\phi_q(s),\alpha_q(s))$, with $s=\mathbf1$ equal to the P2/BACKGROUND I-1 minimizer. Use the **full** family of I-4 parameter cubes on this generating set: the versions with parameters only outside an inner region are its restrictions obtained by fixing the other parameters at $1$. This full-family extension follows from the proofs of LOCALIZATION DG1–DG5 and I-4a Steps 1–3, 5: the Neumann-series and fixed-point bounds use only the bounded overlap of interaction cubes and do not use $X_0,X_1$; those sets first enter the weighted comparison of Step 4. Thus one family supplies one coherent field on $X_Z$. Parameter cubes and interaction cubes are those of LOCALIZATION I-4, including its layer-interface cover. Write

$$\Phi_q(s,x)=\frac{\phi_q(s,x)}{\sqrt{\phi_q(s,x)\cdot\phi_q(s,x)}}\quad\text{on the stencil of }a_{q,b},\qquad F_{q,b}(s)=\beta_q a_{q,b}(\psi,\Phi_q(s)).\tag{F.6}$$

The square root is the analytic branch near $1$. I-4b(ii) makes it well defined throughout the parameter polydisc when the small-field datum is sufficiently small. On real data $|\Phi_q(s,x)|=1$; at $s=\mathbf1$ it is exactly the original minimizer. This normalization is necessary: the decoupled field at $s\ne\mathbf1$ need not obey the unit constraint (LOCALIZATION I-4b(ii)).

For each component $Z$, let $\mathfrak P_Z$ consist of parameter cubes all of whose associated interaction cubes and the local data stencil lie inside $V_Z$. Let $s^Z_P=1$ on $\mathfrak P_Z$ and $s^Z_P=0$ elsewhere. Define

$$\phi_{q,Z}^{\mathrm{loc}}(x)=\Phi_q(s^Z,x),\qquad \mathcal A_q^{\mathrm{loc}}(X_Z)=\sum_{b\subset X_Z}F_{q,b}(s^Z).\tag{F.7}$$

Only the values on stencils meeting $X_Z$ are needed. I-4b(iv) and the locality of the approximate field in BACKGROUND Lemma 5.3 imply that these values depend only on the data and generating-set geometry in $V_Z$. The gap from $X_Z$ to the parameter cubes suppressed in $s^Z$ is at least $[R_{q-1}]-s_*$ in scale-$q$ cube units. To use the weighted comparison I-4b(iii), freeze the parameters in $\mathfrak P_Z$ at $1$ and vary only those outside it; the set $\mathcal W$ of I-4b(iii) then lies outside this gap. Applied on each anchored stencil, it gives

$$\|\phi_{q,Z}^{\mathrm{loc}}-\phi_q(\mathbb B_q(\partial\Omega_q);\widetilde\psi)\|_{C^2_{\rm scaled}(X_Z)}\le C_F\delta_q\exp[-c_F M([R_{q-1}]-s_*)].\tag{F.8}$$

The scaled norm comprises the field and the first two differences used in the action. P5 (2.18)–(2.20), journal p. 505, gives the same short-range construction and an explicit $e^{-R_k}$ comparison for its fluctuation shift; (F.8) uses I-4b for the present background. The local field need not solve the variational equation. On $X_Z$ its Euler–Lagrange residual is bounded by the right side of (F.8), because the original field solves that equation and the local coefficients agree on the stencil. This residual is included in the cost accounting below.

### Proof

**The raw factor is not exactly local.** Linearize BACKGROUND (EL) at constant unit data in a transverse spin direction. The response to a perturbation of block-spin data supported away from $b$ contains $H_\perp^{-1}Q^*a\,\delta\Psi$. The connected elliptic inverse has decaying, generally nonzero distant matrix elements; the variational problem has no finite-range rule. Its value on the stencil of $b$ therefore changes under some distant perturbations. The restricted action in (F.1) changes for generic $\psi$ on $b$ (the block term already contains $|\psi-Q\phi|^2$). P5 explicitly identifies this nonlocal dependence for the analogous global minimizer (2.17) and says that it destroys locality, journal p. 505; it replaces that minimizer by the short-range field (2.18)–(2.20). Thus the sentence following P6 (3.2), journal p. 16, cannot be read as a proof of literal finite support for the displayed unmodified background. P6 (2.15)–(2.17), journal pp. 6–7, instead demonstrates the needed method of moving background-change remainders into boundary terms.

**Localization of the difference.** Apply the multivariable fundamental theorem of calculus to $F_{q,b}(s)$ in the I-4 parameter cubes, as in the interpolation formula of LOCALIZATION Lemma AE/P3 (4.41)–(4.45). It gives the exact finite sum

$$F_{q,b}(\mathbf1)=\sum_{\mathcal C\ \mathrm{connected}}F_{q,b,\mathcal C},\qquad F_{q,b}(s^Z)=\sum_{\mathcal C:\,Y(b,\mathcal C)\subset V_Z}F_{q,b,\mathcal C}.\tag{F.9}$$

Here $Y(b,\mathcal C)$ is the connected support obtained by adjoining **all** interaction cubes associated with each parameter in $\mathcal C$ to the anchor, then applying the deterministic cover of P3 (4.44)–(4.45). With this footprint convention, $\mathcal C\subset\mathfrak P_Z$ is equivalent to $Y(b,\mathcal C)\subset V_Z$; the second identity follows by evaluating the same interpolation formula at $s^Z$. Exact locality of I-4b(iv) kills disconnected families. This is a finite algebraic identity on the finite torus; there is no approximation in (F.9).

On the complex H7 domain, I-4b(ii) and (F.6) give $|F_{q,b}(s)|\le C_A\beta_q(1+\widehat\lambda_q)^{p_F}$ uniformly for $|s_P|\le e^{\kappa_1}$. Indeed the fields and their scaled first differences are uniformly bounded there; P5 (1.24)–(1.25) bounds the remaining variables on the operator domain, and the canonical scale relations used in P6 (3.5)–(3.6), journal p. 17, bound the sum of site, bond and block terms anchored in one scale-$q$ cube by a fixed power of $1+\widehat\lambda_q$ times $\beta_q$. The Cauchy estimate, the interaction-cube count, and the resummation of I-5 (LOCALIZATION Lemma AE and GM3–GM4) give the one-cube bound

$$\sum_{\mathcal C:\,Y(b,\mathcal C)=Y}|F_{q,b,\mathcal C}|\le C_A\beta_q(1+\widehat\lambda_q)^{p_F}e^{-\kappa_{\rm loc}d_q(Y)}.\tag{F.10}$$

The constants are uniform in the number of parameter cubes and in the torus. The one-cube anchor has zero initial tree length, so this use does not invoke the unresolved nonzero-anchor variation V1 of LOCALIZATION §4.5. Every term in the difference of the two sums in (F.9) reaches outside $V_Z$, while its anchor lies in $Z$; its tree length is at least $c_F([R_{q-1}]-s_*)$ and its connected support crosses $Z_q^c$. Complete its support to a member of $\mathcal D_q(\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega_q^c)$ as in P5 (1.33)–(1.34), journal p. 496, and group terms with the same completed support. The extra factor counting anchors in that support is absorbed by the difference $\kappa_{\rm loc}-\kappa$. This gives (F.2)–(F.4). The sign in the boundary exponent is minus the sign of $\Delta$: $e^{-\mathcal A_q}=e^{-\mathcal A_q^{\rm loc}}e^{-\sum\Delta_q}$. P6 (2.31)–(2.33), journal pp. 14–15, and P7 (3.22)–(3.23), journal pp. 525–526, perform the same boundary assignment for other changes of background; those formulas are precedent, while (F.9)–(F.10) supply the specific missing argument for (3.2).

**Recursion and $R$.** Induct on $q$. P5 (2.47), journal p. 512, is a product of a restricted transformation, ultralocal Gaussian factors and cube-local characteristic functions. P6 (3.2) adds those factors and the local action (F.7). The old operators in P6 (3.3) factor by induction. Every connected term of $\mathcal B^{(q-1)}$ retained *inside* the operator lies in a unique current component; terms meeting its exterior are H7 terms. Thus every integration variable and every field argument of the new operator belongs to $V_Z$, and disjoint $V_Z$ give (F.5). This is the support premise required by P5 (1.22), not a consequence of geometric separation alone.

Apply P7's definition (1.6), journal p. 496, to the re-expressed density. Its numerator is unchanged **term by term** by (F.2), and its normalizing density (1.5) already uses the restricted generating set $\mathbb B_q(Z^\approx)$; thus this is the same $R$ transformation on the full density. In P7 §2 the K1 branch multiplies a single component operator by characteristic functions whose supports are in the lineage of that component (P7 (2.1), journal p. 498; the supports are recorded on journal p. 516). Its variable shifts are componentwise, so K1 preserves (F.5). In P7 §3 the transformation (3.7) acts on one component, integrates only its old local variables, and uses the background on $\mathbb B_q(Z^\approx)$; the coset averaging is explicitly componentwise (journal pp. 518–521). The indicator of (3.20) and the displayed main action in the K2 definition are also componentwise (journal p. 525). Hence K2 preserves (F.5). For the removed branch, P7 (3.22)–(3.28), journal pp. 525–528, sends connected terms crossing component boundaries to H7 and leaves componentwise local operators.

For the estimates, the local action differs on each integration domain from the stationary one by $O(\beta_q(1+\widehat\lambda_q)^{p_F}e^{-c_FM R_{q-1}})$ per cube, by (F.8) and the local action formula. Equivalently, its Euler–Lagrange residual has that bound. P6 Prop. 3.1, journal pp. 16–17, still gives its action lower bound because $\phi_{q,Z}^{\rm loc}$ is unit valued. In P7's componentwise numerator and normalizing integral, a uniform error $E$ in the exponent multiplies each positive real integral by a factor between $e^{-E}$ and $e^E$; the logarithm of the normalizer changes by at most $E$. The complex analytic estimates follow from the same Cauchy bounds when $E$ is small on their domain. On a ripe component the accumulated $E$ is smaller than $1$ after increasing $\lambda_*$, since its size and the number of window steps are polynomial in $\widehat\lambda_q$ (v4 §3.5). Enlarge $C_R$ by $1$ in the K1/K2 refund estimate. This proves the asserted support through both branches and $R$. $\square$

## Constants and the use in Lemma L

Previously existing H7 terms retain their previous bound when they cross a current component. Take **row 12** of v4 §5 as $B_0\ge B^\sharp_{\rm old}+1$.
