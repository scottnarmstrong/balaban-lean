# P6 (2.31) and the window action

**Scope and status.** This is a specification for the action conversion in
LF-RECONSTRUCTION §III.2, Lemma III.2.2. The finite-sum identity below is proved
here. Its analytic estimate is proved **conditional on** the named P2 Proposition
3.1 variational estimate, P6 (2.25) and its wall estimate, and the scale estimate
P7 (2.11). The final background replacement is **conditional on** the window
decay input (W), stated below. The landed Lean declarations do not yet prove the
regional identity, (W), or the uniform P7 (2.11) instance. Therefore this note
does not claim an unconditional formal proof of (2.15′).

## 1. Objects, domains, and the exact one-step statement

Work in the finite torus at the $L^{-1}\eta$ scale of P6 §2. Let $G$ be the
generating set $\mathbb B_{r}(\partial\Omega_r)$ and let $D$ be a finite union
of its scale $r-1$ and scale $r$ cubes; the application is
$D=Z_{r-1}^{c}\cap Z_r$. Put $D_1=D\cap\Omega_1(G)$, retaining the
layer index in every coefficient $a(x)$ and block average $Q_x$; equivalently,
the block pairing is the sum of its two layer restrictions. The set $D^*$ is the
weighted set of bonds used by the restricted action; its crossing bonds form
${\rm st}(D)$, with the orientations and half-bond weights of P6 (2.22)–(2.23).
Write $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_D$ for the complex **bilinear** pairing; no
complex conjugation is taken. For hard spins $\lambda=\infty$, define

$$A_D(a,\nu;\psi,\phi)=\tfrac12\langle\psi-Q\phi,
 a(\psi-Q\phi)\rangle_{D_1}
 +\tfrac12\|\partial\phi\|_{D^*}^{2}
 +\tfrac\nu2\|\phi-h\|_D^{2}.\tag{1}$$

All coefficients and scalar products in (1) are those of the same generating
set; in particular $Q$ is **not** replaced by a homogeneous block average.
For finite $\lambda$, add the constraint term of P6 to (1) and the
$\lambda^{-1}$ terms displayed in P6 (2.23); they vanish in the present
$\lambda=\infty$ specialization.

Let $\beta_u=\beta+b$, $\beta_u\nu_u=\beta\nu+\nu e$, and assume the block
coefficient identity $\beta_u a_u=\beta a$ in these units. Put
$\theta=\psi+v$, $\phi_u=\phi+w$, $\alpha_u=\alpha+t$. Both backgrounds
satisfy the respective Euler–Lagrange equations on the *whole* first domain
of $G$, and their hard-spin constraints hold there. For $r-1\le k_0$, both
Euler–Lagrange equations use mass $0$, even though (1) contains the physical
mass $\nu$ or $\nu_u$. For $r-1>k_0$, they use the masses $\nu$ and $\nu_u$.
The pair $(w,t)$ is the small solution of the corresponding P6 (2.24)
variational system. More explicitly, with $s=(1+b/\beta)^{-1}$, its first
equation on $\Omega_1(G)$ has the form

$$\big(Q^*saQ-\Delta^D_{\Omega_1}+\alpha+t
+\mathbf1_{r-1>k_0}\nu_u\big)w
=Q^*sa v-t\phi+f_0.\tag{0}$$

For $r-1\le k_0$, $f_0$ has no mass term and contains the additional cut
source
$\chi_{\Omega_1(G)}\Delta\chi_{\Omega_1(G)^c\cap\Omega_r}Q_r^*v$;
its remaining coefficient-change term is
$-(b/\beta)s(-\Delta\phi+\alpha\phi)$. For $r-1>k_0$, use the
complete-mass source of P4 (4.4) as cited in P6 p. 11; P6 explicitly says
that its mass contribution has coefficient $\nu\beta^{-1}e$, in contrast
to the coefficient $\nu$ in the exceptional $r-1=k_0$ case of P6 (2.24).
Add $\nu_u$ to the operator as in P6 p. 14. The second equation is the hard-spin condition
$2\phi\cdot w+w\cdot w=0$ on $\Omega_1(G)$ (the
$\lambda^{-1}t$ term in P6 (2.24) is zero). This branch distinction is
part of the statement, not a later convention change.

For a bond $b$ crossing $\partial D$, set
$\bar w_b=(w_{b,+}+w_{b,-})/2$, and use the outward oriented derivative of
$\phi$. Define

$$S_D(w,\phi)=\langle\bar w,\partial\phi\rangle_{{\rm st}(D)}.\tag{2}$$

The following formulas define the *regional* counterterm. The subscript on a
quadratic form always restricts its sites or bonds before summation.

$$\begin{aligned}
P_D={}&\langle v,a(\psi-Q\phi)\rangle_{D_1}
 +\frac b{2\beta}\|\partial\phi\|_{D^*}^2
 +\frac{\nu e}{2\beta}\|\phi-h\|_D^2,\\
Q_D={}&\tfrac12\langle v-Qw,a(v-Qw)\rangle_{D_1}
 +\frac12(1+b/\beta)
       \big(\|\partial w\|_{D^*}^2+\langle w,\alpha w\rangle_D\big)
 +\tfrac\nu2(1+e/\beta)\|w\|_D^2,\\
R_D={}&\frac b\beta\langle w,-\Delta\phi+\alpha\phi\rangle_D
       +\frac{\nu e}{\beta}\langle w,\phi-h\rangle_D
       +\mathbf1_{r-1\le k_0}\nu\langle w,\phi-h\rangle_D,\\
C_D={}&P_D+Q_D+R_D.
\end{aligned}\tag{3}$$

Here $\alpha$ is extended by zero off $\Omega_1(G)$ in the terms restricted
to $D$, and $-\Delta\phi$ is the regional bulk divergence in the finite Green
formula below. Terms supported outside $\Omega_1$ use the source's boundary
convention for the extended background; the cut source in P6 (2.24) is needed
to make the Euler–Lagrange cancellation on those sites. Formula (3) is P6
(2.22)–(2.23), specialized to $\lambda=\infty$ and to (2.31); its last
summand is exactly the additional $\nu\langle w,\phi-h\rangle_D$ prescribed
after P6 (2.31). The $\langle w,\alpha w\rangle_D$ term has coefficient
$1+b/\beta$, not $1$.

**Regional conversion (P6 (2.31)).** Under these hypotheses, for every
admissible complex datum,

$$\boxed{\quad
\beta_u A_D(a_u,\nu_u;\theta,\phi_u)
=\beta A_D(a,\nu;\psi,\phi)+\beta C_D+\beta_u S_D(w,\phi).
\quad}\tag{4}$$

This is an identity of complex numbers. It holds on the real window domain
and on an open complex neighbourhood $\mathcal U_r$ on which both backgrounds
and the variational solution are defined. The neighbourhood must contain the
P5 (1.32) domain localized to $W_{r-1}\cap U_r$; all quantitative bounds below
are required there and on a slightly larger open neighbourhood when Cauchy
estimates are invoked. The use of open neighbourhoods avoids identifying a
closed small-field set with a domain of holomorphy.

**Proof of (4).** Expand the three squares in (1) after substituting
$(\psi+v,\phi+w)$ and the three coefficient identities. With
$u:=\psi-Q\phi$, the raw difference, before using either field equation, is

$$\begin{aligned}
\beta_u A_{D,u}-\beta A_D={}&
\beta\langle v-Qw,a(\psi-Q\phi)\rangle_{D_1}
+\tfrac\beta2\langle v-Qw,a(v-Qw)\rangle_{D_1}\\
&+\beta\langle\partial w,\partial\phi\rangle_{D^*}
+\tfrac b2\|\partial\phi\|_{D^*}^{2}
+b\langle\partial w,\partial\phi\rangle_{D^*}
+\tfrac{\beta+b}{2}\|\partial w\|_{D^*}^{2}\\
&+\beta\nu\langle w,\phi-h\rangle_D
+\tfrac{\beta\nu}{2}\|w\|_D^2
+\tfrac{\nu e}{2}\|\phi-h\|_D^2
+\nu e\langle w,\phi-h\rangle_D
+\tfrac{\nu e}{2}\|w\|_D^2.
\end{aligned}\tag{4a}$$

The terms independent of $w$ give $P_D$. The terms quadratic in $(v,w)$
give the block, gradient and mass terms in $Q_D$; the terms proportional to
$\nu e$ give the second term of $R_D$. On each oriented bond, the finite
Green identity with the half-bond
weights is

$$\langle\partial w,\partial\phi\rangle_{D^*}
=\langle w,-\Delta\phi\rangle_D+S_D(w,\phi).\tag{5}$$

It follows by expanding the two endpoint contributions of every internal
bond; they cancel in opposite orientations. On a crossing bond the remaining
two endpoint coefficients combine to $(w_++w_-)/2$, giving (2). Thus (5)
requires no limiting or infinite-volume argument.

Use (5) once with coefficient $\beta$ and once with coefficient $b$.
The base Euler–Lagrange equation cancels the $-Qw$ block term, the bulk
gradient term and, in the complete-mass branch, the $\nu$ mass term. What
remains from this cancellation is $-\beta\langle w,\alpha\phi\rangle_D$.
For hard spins, the pointwise difference of the two constraints gives
$2\phi\cdot w+w\cdot w=0$, hence
$-\langle w,\alpha\phi\rangle_D
=\frac12\langle w,\alpha w\rangle_D$. To put the $b$-part of this
quadratic term into $Q_D$, add
$\frac b{2\beta}\langle w,\alpha w\rangle_D$ and
$\frac b\beta\langle w,\alpha\phi\rangle_D$; their sum is zero by the same
constraint. These are precisely the $b$ contributions to $Q_D$ and $R_D$.
In the massless-background branch, the physical base mass linear term did
not enter the Euler–Lagrange cancellation and gives the final summand of
$R_D$. Both uses of (5) yield $(\beta+b)S_D=\beta_u S_D$.
All remaining terms are (3), which proves (4). The regional cancellation
uses the cut-source equation on the part outside $\Omega_1$; omitting that
source would leave an uncancelled linear term. $\square$

## 2. Analytic and geometric estimate for a single conversion

The following is the exact analytic interface needed to turn (4) into the
window bound. It must be supplied for the actual mixed-scale generating set
and for **both** mass conventions.

**(V) Variational solution.** On $\mathcal U_r$, the P6 (2.24) solution
$(w,t)$ is unique in the small class, analytic in datum and localization
parameters, and satisfies the P2 Proposition 3.1, (3.47) bounds. In the
$r-1\le k_0$ branch the source includes the cut term stated above and the
massless Euler–Lagrange convention. These are stronger specifications than
the currently landed whole-lattice `IsRenSol`.

**(L) Cube and wall bounds.** Decompose $C_D$ into the P6 (2.31) cubes:
$\square_{r-1}(y)$ for $y\in D\cap\Omega_r^c$ and $\square_r(z)$ for
$z\in D\cap\Omega_r$. On the complex neighbourhood, each cube term obeys
P6 (2.25), $|C_D(\square)|\le 3C_4B_6K_5/\beta$ in its normalization.
The wall decomposition of $S_D$ uses the $(d-1)$-dimensional $M$-cube walls
described immediately after P6 (2.25), and has the stated P6 wall bound.
Every wall is charged a bounded number of times to a cube meeting $D$.
Consequently the sum is bounded by a fixed constant times
$|D\cap\Omega_r^c|_\eta+|D\cap\Omega_r|$; the area estimate is an
alternative. The assertion also gives dependence only on the datum in
$W_{r-1}^{\sim}\cap U_r^{\sim}$. No polymer localization of these two terms
is required by P6.

The proof of the last consequence is a finite triangle inequality: sum the
cube bounds, sum the wall bounds, and use the bounded wall-to-cube incidence.
The factor $\beta_u$ multiplying (2) must be included before applying the
wall bound. The constants in (V) and (L) must be uniform over all admissible
window indices and independent of $B_0,H_0,c_1,c_2$; this independence is
an input to, not a consequence of, the algebraic identity (4).

For use in P7, one needs the stronger scale-normalized conclusion, exactly
the first inequality in P7 (2.11): if $R_{j,n}$ denotes the cost of converting
the $j+1$ action one step toward the coefficients of scale $n$, then

$$|R_{j,n}|\le K_{\rm conv}
\frac{\beta_{j+1}\varepsilon_{j+1}^{,2}}
     {\beta_n\varepsilon_n^{,2}},|X_j|_{j+1}
\le K_{\rm conv}L^{-2\gamma(n-j-1)}|X_j|_{j+1},
\qquad j+2\le n\le k.\tag{6}$$

Here $X_j=Z_j^c\cap Z_{j+1}$ and $|X_j|_{j+1}$ is the mixed cube count in
P7 (2.11), with the same unit convention on both sides. The first inequality
uses (V), (L), the running-coefficient bounds of P4 §5 as invoked in P6, and
the change of scale; it is **not** an immediate consequence of (2.25) alone.
The second is the running-radius estimate quoted in P7 (2.11). A Lean worker
should not replace (6) by an unscaled $O(|X_j|)$ statement before proving the
uniformity in $n$. If $n=j+1$ there is no conversion and $R_{j,n}=0$.

## 3. Window theorem and proof

Let $k_1<k$, $X_j=Z_j^c\cap Z_{j+1}$ for $k_1\le j<k$, and
$X_{\rm win}=\bigsqcup_{j=k_1}^{k-1}X_j=Z\setminus Z_{k_1}$.
Assume $L>1$ and $\gamma>0$, as required for the geometric sum.
Let $\mathcal A_{j+1}(X_j;\psi)$ denote the original step action, including
its original local background $\phi^{\rm loc}_{j+1}$, with the normalization
used in LF-RECONSTRUCTION §III.2. The converted action at scale $n$ uses the
coefficients of P7 (2.10), evaluated on the **same spin variables**. Under the
free flow, these are $a_m^{(k)}=a_m$,
$\beta_m^{(k)}=\beta_k L^{-(k-m)(d-2)}$, and
$\nu_m^{(k)}=\nu_kL^{-2(k-m)}$; this equality is a required coefficient
identity, not a new RG conclusion. Let $\mathbb B_{k_1,k}$ and
$\phi_{k_1,k}$ be exactly Definition III.2.1 of the reconstruction.

The remaining analytic input is:

**(W) Background replacement.** For each $j$, P7 (2.14) and the
`theorem73_real`/`theorem73_complex` composition identities express the
converted local background as $\phi_{k_1,k}+\delta\phi_j$. The data change
vanishes on the $[R_j]$ collar of each lineage component. The P3
(2.55)–(2.56), P6 (2.13)–(2.14) weighted response estimate then gives a
holomorphic replacement remainder $B_j$ on the window complex domain, with

$$|B_j|\le K_{\rm bg}
e^{-\gamma_w\{M([\hat R_{j-1}]-1)-2M_1\}}
|X_j|_{j+1}\le K_{\rm bg}|X_j|_{j+1}.\tag{7}$$

The last inequality assumes the displayed separation is nonnegative. The
localization of the data change is Lemma II.2.5(c) of the reconstruction;
the relevant LOC I-4b/I-5 hypotheses and their instantiation remain part of
(W). A whole-lattice composition theorem by itself does not give (7).

**Window conversion theorem, conditional on (V), (L), (6), and (W).** On the
window configurations with $\mathcal X_{\rm win}=1$, and analytically on
the P5 (1.32) complex domain with $X=W\cap U_k$,

$$\beta_k A_k^*(X_{\rm win},\mathbb B_k;
\psi,\phi_{k_1,k})
=\mathcal A_{\rm win}(\psi)+C_{k_1,k}(\psi),\qquad
\mathcal A_{\rm win}:=\sum_{j=k_1}^{k-1}\mathcal A_{j+1}(X_j;\psi),\tag{8}$$

$$|C_{k_1,k}(\psi)|\le C_{15}\sum_{j=k_1}^{k-1}|X_j|_{j+1},
\qquad C_{15}:=\frac{K_{\rm conv}}{1-L^{-2\gamma}}+K_{\rm bg}.\tag{9}$$

$C_{k_1,k}$ is holomorphic on an open neighbourhood of that domain and
depends only on $\psi|_{W^{\sim}\cap U_k^{\sim}}$. If the constants in
(6)–(7) are chosen from the fixed row 0–11 estimates independently of
$B_0,H_0,c_1,c_2$, then so is $C_{15}$.

**Proof.** Fix $j$. Apply (4) successively with the same $X_j$ and the
coefficient sets for scales $j+2,\ldots,k$, without changing the spin
variables. With $T_{j,n}$ the action after conversion through scale $n$,
the orientation of (4) gives
$T_{j,j+1}=T_{j,k}+\sum_{n=j+2}^{k}R_{j,n}$.
The number of conversions is exactly $k-j-1$. By (6) and a finite geometric
sum,

$$\left|\sum_{n=j+2}^{k}R_{j,n}\right|
\le K_{\rm conv}|X_j|_{j+1}
\sum_{m=1}^{k-j-1}L^{-2\gamma m}
\le\frac{K_{\rm conv}}{1-L^{-2\gamma}}|X_j|_{j+1}.\tag{10}$$

The bound deliberately allows the larger sum beginning at $m=0$; it also
covers the empty sum. By (W), $T_{j,k}$ differs from the restriction of the
scale-$k$ action with $\phi_{k_1,k}$ by $B_j$, with (7). The regions $X_j$
are disjoint and the regional block, bond and mass sums are additive under
the half-bond convention, including their interface allocation. Summing the
identities over $j$ gives (8) with
$C_{k_1,k}:=-\sum_j(\sum_nR_{j,n}+B_j)$.
The sign is fixed by (8), and does not affect (9). Finite sums and
compositions of holomorphic functions are holomorphic. P6's regional
dependence statement and the support clause in (W) give the asserted
restriction of the datum. Summing (10) and (7) proves (9). The only
parameters in the displayed choice of $C_{15}$ are $K_{\rm conv}$,
$K_{\rm bg}$, $L$, and $\gamma$; the claimed independence follows exactly
when their uniform input bounds have that independence. $\square$

P7's further bound in printed (2.15) by a fixed power of $\log\beta$ is
not asserted: a window with $\nu$ pieces has no such size estimate. Equation
(9) is the per-volume statement used by the refund in reconstruction §III.8.

## 4. Lean dependency order and existing declarations

1. **Regional carriers and finite algebra.** Define `Region` as a finite
   union of generating-set cubes, its restricted block-site set, half-weighted
   bond set, `star`, regional `blockPair`, `bondPair`, `massPair`, and
   `actionCRegion`. Prove disjoint-union additivity with the boundary
   allocation. The whole-lattice bilinear definitions `blockPair`,
   `bondPair`, `massPair`, `actionC_eq_pairs` in
   `Balaban/Fluctuation/RenormIdentity.lean` are models, not regional
   definitions.
2. **Regional Green formula.** Prove (5) by a finite bond sum, with the
   actual P6 half-bond convention; record its `st(D)` support and additivity.
   This lemma must precede the Taylor identity.
3. **Variational branches.** Define the two P6 (2.24) systems on
   $\mathbb B_r(\partial\Omega_r)$, including the cut source for
   $r-1\le k_0$ and the $\nu_u$ operator for $r-1>k_0$. Establish (V) from
   P2 Proposition 3.1 with the P6 bounds. `IsRenSol` in
   `Balaban/Fluctuation/RenormExpLemma35.lean` supplies a whole-lattice
   analytic solution interface; it does not include this regional cut source.
4. **Localized Taylor remainder.** Define `CRegion` by (3) and prove (4)
   using the coefficient identities, regional Green formula, Euler–Lagrange
   equation and hard-spin constraint. The existing
   `renormC1`, `renormC2`, `renormC3`, `lemma35_i_complete`, and
   `lemma35_i_nuFree` in
   `Balaban/Fluctuation/RenormIdentity.lean` confirm the two whole-lattice
   branches but have no $D$ or surface term. The regional theorem must reduce
   to them when $D$ is the full torus and ${\rm st}(D)=\varnothing$.
5. **Complex regional bounds.** Prove (L) with the cube bounds P6 (2.25),
   wall estimate after (2.25), and uniform incidence counting, then prove
   (6) with the actual running coefficients as in P7 (2.11). Keep the open
   complex neighbourhood in the statement.
6. **Background replacement.** Instantiate the composition identities
   `theorem73_real`, `theorem73_complex` in
   `Balaban/Background/Theorem73.lean`; provide the local-data collar
   agreement and weighted response estimate (W). The landed
   `i4b_local` in `Balaban/Localization/DecoupledBackgroundLocal.lean` and
   `i5_background` in `Balaban/Localization/I5.lean` are relevant
   conditional localization tools; their hypotheses must be discharged for
   the window sets. They do not state (W) directly.
7. **Window finite sum.** Prove the coefficient recursion of P7 (2.10),
   telescope the one-step conversions, sum the geometric bound, replace
   backgrounds, and use regional additivity to obtain (8)–(9). Record
   `C_{15}` as a constant fixed before $B_0,H_0,c_1,c_2$.
