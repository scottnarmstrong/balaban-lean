# The large-field part: architecture (phase 1, document D-LF, first part)

Version 1, 2026-10-11. Written for the orchestrator, Scott Armstrong and Roland Bauerschmidt.
Status: draft for independent refute-first review. Nothing here is approved.

This document fixes the architecture of the large-field analysis from which the reconstruction
document (roadmap §4.5) and the Lean interfaces Def-7 and LF-1, …, LF-8 will be written. It
contains no Lean.

**Sources and conventions.** Keys as in `review/SOURCES.md`; page numbers are journal pages (arXiv
pages for D1–D3). Tags: **[checked]** = re-read in the PDF for this document (text layer or page
image); **[review]** = taken from `review/papers/*.md` or `review/QC_LOG.md` and not re-read here.
Status words: *proved* (a complete proof exists in a source), *cited* (a source states it and
refers elsewhere), *sketched* (a source argues it in outline), *reconstructed* (no source proves
it; this document or the reconstruction document supplies it). Doubts are marked UNCERTAIN.

Rulings in force (`review/ROLAND_ANSWERS.md`, batch 2, 2026-10-11): large fields are the crux and
are committed; "anything that works is okay" for the large-field mechanism, including the
bookkeeping of P5–P7 and the operation $R$ itself; the repair of P6 Thm 4 (freeze the threshold at
$p_2(\beta_{k_0})$) is agreed; the step counter must be reconstructed; limits along tori suffice;
hard spins only ($\lambda=\infty$).

**Summary of the recommendation.** Keep Balaban's analytic machinery (the step $S^{(k)}T^{(k)}$ of
P5 §2–P6 §3 with large-field regions left unintegrated, the operation $R^{(k)}$ of P7 §§2–3, the
last step of P7 §4) and replace its bookkeeping: histories become data generated by explicit rules;
the functions $\kappa_k$, $C$ and the counter $K$ with hypothesis H6 are replaced by a single
*budget* $G_k(Z)$ and an explicit *budget invariant* with a potential function; the trigger for
$R$ becomes geometric (*ripeness*: no new large fields for $n_q$ scales, small for the last $n_2$
scales); after $k_0$ the threshold is frozen at $p_2(\beta_{k_0})$. §4 proves the budget invariant
(the reconstructed step counter LF-2) from explicitly listed geometric and analytic axioms. One
change to P7 is required: $R$ must accept components that were not small during the whole quiet
period but only during its last $n_2=O(\log\log\beta)$ scales. The reason (§2.1, §4.9) is that
P6 Prop 3.2 (ii) appears to fail for components formed by merging, and with Balaban's literal
requirement no counting argument can close (UNCERTAIN, argued in §4.9).

## 1. What the large-field part must deliver

### 1.1 The target

Root-F (`Balaban/Frozen/Roots/LogPartitionMultiscale.lean`) asserts: there is $\delta>0$ such that
for all $\kappa,c_9>0$ there are integers $L\ge2$, $M\ge1$ and $\beta_0$ with the following
property for all $\beta\ge\beta_0$, $\nu\in(0,1]$ and unit vectors $h\in\mathbb R^N$. There is
$k_f\in\mathbb N$ with $L^{k_f}\sqrt\nu\le L$, $\tfrac12\le L^{k_f+1}\sqrt\nu$, and there are functions
$T_\ell(j,x,X;g)\in\mathbb C^N$, defined for every torus $\mathbb T_\ell=(\mathbb Z/\ell\mathbb Z)^d$ with
$ML^{k_f+1}\mid\ell$, such that

1. $T_\ell(j,x,X;\cdot)=0$ unless $1\le j\le k_f+1$, $x\in X$ and $X$ is a scale-$j$ polymer (a
   nearest-neighbour connected union of blocks of side $ML^j$);
2. $T_\ell(j,x,X;g)$ depends only on $g|_X$, and is analytic on $\{\sum_y\sum_i|g_i(y)|<1\}$;
3. on $\{\sum_y\sum_i|g_i(y)|\le\frac12\}$: $|T_\ell(j,x,X;g)|\le c_9L^{-j(\frac{d-2}2+\delta)}\exp(-\kappa|X|/(ML^j)^d)$;
4. on $\{\sum\sum|g_i(y)|<1\}$:
   $\log\frac{Z(g)}{Z(0)}=\langle g,h\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{k_f+1}\sum_x\sum_X\langle g(x),T_\ell(j,x,X;g)\rangle$;
5. the terms of two admissible tori agree on images of finite $A\subset\mathbb Z^d$ in the box
   $4|y_\mu|<\min(\ell,\ell')$.

Here $Z(g)=\int\exp(-\beta H(\varphi)+\langle g,\varphi\rangle)\prod_x d\sigma(\varphi_x)$ with
$H=\frac12\|\partial\varphi\|^2+\frac\nu2\|\varphi-h\|^2$ and $\varphi_x\in S^{N-1}$.

### 1.2 How Root-F decomposes

Write $\rho_0(\varphi;g)=\exp(-\beta H(\varphi)+\langle g,\varphi\rangle)$ and let $k_f$ be the first
$k$ with $\nu_k>\frac78L^{-2}$ (P4 §6, P7 p. 529 [checked]); $k_0$ is the last $k$ with
$\nu_k\beta_k\le1$ (P5 p. 490 [checked]). Root-F follows from four ingredients.

* **(F1) The flow (LF-7).** Densities $\rho_k$, $0\le k\le k_f$, with $\rho_{k+1}=R^{(k+1)}S^{(k)}T^{(k)}\rho_k$
  ($R^{(k+1)}=\mathrm{id}$ for $k\ge k_0$), each in the inductive class of §3.6, and
  $\int\rho_k\,d\psi_k=c_k\,Z(g)$ with $c_k>0$ independent of $g$. The normalizations are
  $\int T\rho=\int\rho$ (P1 (1.11)) and $\int R^{(k)}\rho=\int\rho$ (P7 (1.7) [checked]).
* **(F2) The last step (LF-8).** For $\rho_{k_f}$ in the class,
  $\int\rho_{k_f}d\psi_{k_f}=\exp\big[-E|\mathbb T|+\mathcal F_{k_f+1}(h,g)+\mathcal R^{(k_f+1)}(h,g)+\mathcal P^{(k_f+1)}(g)\big]$
  with $\mathcal F_{k_f+1}=\langle g,h\rangle+\sum_{j\le k_f}\langle g,\mathcal M^{(j)}(h,g)\rangle+\sum_{n=0}^m\langle g,\mathcal M_n^{(k_f+1)}(h,g)\rangle$
  (P7 (4.15)–(4.17) [checked]) and $\mathcal P^{(k_f+1)}(g)=\sum_{Y\in\mathcal D_{k_f+1}}\mathcal P(Y;g)$,
  $|\mathcal P(Y;g)|\le\beta_{k_f}^{-2}e^{-2\kappa d_{k_f+1}(Y)}$ (P9 (4.4)–(4.6) [checked]).
* **(F3) Conversion.** $\log Z(g)/Z(0)$ is the difference of the exponents at $g$ and at $0$. Every
  term is a sum of localized analytic functions $f(X;g)$; write $f(X;g)-f(X;0)=\sum_{x\in X}\langle g(x),\int_0^1\partial_{g(x)}f(X;tg)\,dt\rangle$
  (the device of P7 (4.15)). A one-variable Cauchy estimate on the disc of radius $\frac12$ gives
  $|\partial_{g_i(x)}f(X;tg)|\le2\sup|f|$ on $\sum\sum|g_i|\le\frac12$. Tree length converts to block
  count by $d_j(X)\ge|X|_\#/C_d-1$ (surface audit C.4), so the flow is run with decay rate
  $\kappa_{\rm flow}:=C_d(\kappa+1)$ and amplitude $c_9e^{-\kappa_{\rm flow}}$ (P4 Thm 1 allows any small
  $c_9$). The large-field terms $\mathcal P(Y;g)$ fit item 3 because
  $\beta_{k_f}^{-2}\le\beta^{-2}L^{-2k_f(d-2)}(1+o(1))\le c_9L^{-(k_f+1)(\frac{d-2}2+\delta)}$ for
  $\beta\ge\beta_0(c_9)$.
* **(F4) Torus independence.** Every localized term is a function of local data and is defined on
  finite subsets of $\mathbb Z^d$ before being pushed to the torus (roadmap §3.5).

Two regimes are not covered by P5–P7 as written. **$k_f=0$** ($\nu>\frac78L^{-2}$): the last step is
taken on $\rho_0$ directly, which is not of the form H1 (surface record, m4). **$k_0\le0$**
($\nu\beta\ge1$): there is no $R$ at all and the frozen regime starts at $k=0$. Both are folded into
LF-6 and LF-8 below.

### 1.3 The deliverable, stated once

**LF-DEL.** Fix $d\ge3$, $N\ge2$. There is $\delta>0$ (depending on $d$; $\delta=\gamma-\alpha_1$) such
that for all $\kappa,c_9>0$ there are $L,M=L^m,\beta_0$ and the constants of §5 such that for
$\beta\ge\beta_0$, $\nu\in(0,1]$, unit $h$, the following hold on every torus with $ML^{k_f+1}\mid\ell$.

(a) (F1) holds: the densities exist, lie in the classes $\mathcal R_k$ ($k\le k_0$) and
$\mathcal R^\flat_k$ ($k_0<k\le k_f$) of §3.6, and $\int\rho_k=c_kZ(g)$.

(b) (F2) holds with all localized terms analytic in $g$ on $\|g\|_{\ell^1}<1$, depending only on $g$
on their domains, torus independent for domains that do not wind, and with the bounds of P4
(H.7) for $\mathcal M^{(j)}$, of P7 (4.17) for $\mathcal M_n^{(k_f+1)}$, and of P9 (4.6) for $\mathcal P$.

Root-F follows from LF-DEL by (F3)–(F4). The large-field contributions enter Root-F in two
places only: through the terms $\mathcal R''^{(j)}_m$ that $R^{(j)}$ adds to the effective action
at scales $j\le k_0$ (they reach $\mathcal E^{(j+m)},\mathcal M^{(j+m)}$ after $m$ steps, P5 Prop 3.3
and p. 518 [review]), and through $\mathcal P^{(k_f+1)}$.

## 2. Architectures compared

### 2.1 (A) Balaban P5–P7, reconstructed literally

*Objects.* Large-field regions $Z_k$, nested domains $\Omega_j\supset\Omega''_j\supset Z_j^c$ (P5
(1.20)), new large-field cubes $P,Q,R,S$ per step (P5 (2.5)–(2.14), (2.43); P6 (2.1)), multi-indices
$\mathbb A_k$ (P6 (3.1)), operators $\mathbb T_{k+1}=\mathbb T^{(k)}\mathbb T_ke^{\mathcal B^{(k)}}$ (P6
(3.2)–(3.3) [checked]), exponents $\kappa_k$ (P6 (3.9)–(3.10) [checked]), combinatorial factors $C$
(P6 (3.11) [checked]), predicted exponents $\kappa_{k,n}$ (P6 (3.19) [checked]), the counter $K$
(index where $\kappa_{k,n}-\ell p_2(\beta_{k+n})$ changes sign, P6 pp. 20–21 [checked]), $R^{(k)}$
(P7 (1.6) [checked]).

*Inductive hypothesis.* P5 H1–H7 (pp. 488–497 [checked]); H6: $K(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)>0$ for $k\le k_0$.

*Hardest step.* $R^{(k)}$ (P7 §§2–3): cancellation of main actions, Lemma 2.5, the coset average
of Lemma 3.1, the polymer expansion with non-small boundary terms. Next hardest: P6 Prop 3.2.

*What the sources prove.* P6 Prop 3.2 is cited to E10 pp. 383–387, which proves (1.80) for a
geometric counter under a slowly running coupling (QC_LOG, E10 row, p. 384 verified). P6 Thm 4's
last inequality fails as printed (§4.8, re-verified on the page image of P6 p. 22). Two further
problems found here:

1. **P6 Prop 3.2 (ii) appears false for merged components** (§4.9). Two components that merge
   when both have $K=1$ produce a component whose $K$ is about $\frac{\ln2}{p_2(d-2)\log L}\lambda$
   ($\lambda=\log\beta a$), while (ii) asserts a history in which the component's domains have had
   diameter below the critical size for more than $\log\beta_{k_1}a\approx\lambda$ scales; before the
   merge the two pieces were further apart than the critical size. UNCERTAIN: depends on reading
   "the domains $Z_j\cap Z$ have diameters smaller than the critical sizes" for disconnected
   $Z_j\cap Z$; I see no other reading under which P7 §2 could use the statement.
2. **The growing threshold makes merges expensive.** Any repair that keeps P7's requirement (small
   throughout the quiet period) must restart a quiet period of $n_q\approx\lambda/(\alpha\log L)$ scales
   after a merge, and over such a period the threshold $2p_2(\beta_k)$ grows by the factor
   $\approx(1+\frac{d-2}\alpha)^{p_2}\ge3^{p_2}$; the reserve freed by a merge is one threshold. So
   the budget cannot pay (§4.9).

*Written length.* Sources P5 37 pp., P6 §§2–3 22 pp., P7 42 pp. Reconstruction on top: 60–80 pp
(definitions of histories and admissibility; Prop 3.2 in a corrected form, which per item 2 does
not exist without modifying $R$; base case; Lemma 2.5; Lemma 3.1; constants).

*Verdict.* Not viable literally (items 1–2); viable after the modifications of (A′).

### 2.2 (B) Dimock D2–D3: carry all large-field regions to the end

*Objects.* Nested regions $\Lambda_0\supset\Omega_1\supset\Lambda_1\supset\cdots$ with buffers
$r_k=(-\log\lambda_k)^r$; per step three families $P,Q,R$; the large-field integrals are left
explicit as Gaussian measures and weights $K_{k,\Pi}\mathcal C_{k,\Pi}$ (D2 Thm 3.1, (201)–(220)).
No counter, no admissibility, no $R$: "This difficult step is not necessary for this model" (D2
p. 31 [checked, text layer]). All large-field configurations are summed in one pass after the
last step (D3 §3).

*Inductive hypothesis.* The representation D2 (201) with properties 1–9; no bound on the
large-field part during the flow.

*Hardest step.* The final sum (D3 Lemmas 14–15). Lemma 14: $\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda_k^c)\le O(1)(Mr_k)^3\sum_{i\le k}|\mathcal C_i|$
(D3 (195)). Lemma 15 bounds the accumulated losses (D3 (205) [checked]):
$$\sum_{j=0}^{N+1}(-\log\lambda_j)|(\Lambda_j^c)^{(j)}|\le O(1)\sum_iM^3|\mathcal C_i|\sum_{j\ge i}(-\log\lambda_j)r_j^3\le O(1)\sum_iM^3(-\log\lambda_i)^{3r+2}|\mathcal C_i|,$$
using "$N-i\le(N-i)\log L-\log\lambda=-\log\lambda_i$": **the number of steps remaining after scale
$i$ is at most the logarithm of the coupling at scale $i$.** The creation factor
$\exp(-c(-\log\lambda_i)^{2p_0})$ then beats the losses since $3r+2<2p_0$ (D3 (206)–(207)).

*Why it fails for O(N).* In $\phi^4_3$ the coupling grows along the flow ($\lambda_j=L^{j-N}\lambda$),
so early scales have the strongest smallness and the fewest remaining steps relative to it. In the
O(N) flow the coupling weakens ($\beta_j\approx\beta L^{j(d-2)}$). The analogue of D3 (205) reads
$$\sum_{j\ge i}^{k_f}\lambda_j\,r_j^d\ \ge\ (k_f-i)\,R_0^d\lambda_i^{2d+1},\qquad\lambda_j=\log\beta_ja,$$
and $k_f-i\approx\frac12\log_L\nu^{-1}-i$ is unbounded as $\nu\downarrow0$ for fixed $i$ and $\beta$, while
the creation factor $\exp(-A_3\lambda_i^{2p_0})$ is fixed. Two facts make this unavoidable: a quiet
large-field region converges to the critical size $\asymp MR_k$ and then costs
$\asymp\lambda_k^{2d+1}$ per step for ever (the buffers are re-added each step, P6 (3.14)–(3.16)
[checked]); and the smallness required at the end is a power of $\beta_{k_f}^{-1}$ (P9 (4.6); (F3)),
which also tends to $0$ as $\nu\downarrow0$. The same estimate fails if the per-site loss is $O(1)$
instead of $O(\lambda)$.

*What must be added.* A mechanism that retires a large-field region once it has been quiet for a
while: Balaban's $R$. With $R$ added, (B) becomes (A′) below, with Dimock as the template for the
representation (D2 (201)), the base case (D2 §3.4), the cluster expansion with holes (D2 Thm F.1)
and the final sum (D3 Lemmas 14–15) in the regime after $k_0$, where at most $\log\beta_{k_0}$
steps remain (P5 p. 500 [checked]).

*Length.* D2 §3 (52 pp.) and D3 (30 pp.) as templates; the O(N) adaptation without $R$ is not
possible, so no estimate.

### 2.3 (C1) E10's collapsed representation (1.104)

E10 pp. 391–392 [review] rewrites each surviving large-field operator as
$(\mathbf T'_k(Y)1)\cdot[(\mathbf T'_k(Y)1)^{-1}\mathbf T'_k(Y)\,\cdot\,]$ and expands the normalized
expectation in a polymer gas in which every polymer either contains a surviving region or is
disjoint from it. Afterwards a large-field region carries only the scalar weight
$\mathbf T'_k(Y)1$, a function of the current field; the integral-operator structure disappears.

*Gain.* Depth-one operators; no multi-indices in the class.
*Cost.* A polymer expansion at every step with non-small boundary terms; $R$ needs the explicit
composition of the quiet-period transformations (P7 (2.6)–(2.9), (2.53)–(2.58), Lemma 2.5), which
the collapse destroys, so $R$ would have to be re-derived for collapsed weights. No source does
this for O(N); Balaban does not use (1.104) in P7.
*Verdict.* Not recommended. It may be useful locally (e.g. to state the last step), not as the
inductive form.

### 2.4 (C2) Regulator-based approaches

Large-field regulators in the style of Bauerschmidt–Brydges–Slade replace characteristic
functions by weights in norms. No source in the library treats the low-temperature O(N) model
this way; adopting it would be new research. Not considered further.

### 2.5 (A′) Balaban's analysis with reconstructed bookkeeping (recommended)

*Objects.* As (A) for everything analytic: regions, one-step operators, $\mathbb T_k$, boundary terms,
$R^{(k)}$. Changed: (i) histories $\mathbb A_k$ are data generated by explicit rules (§3.2), with
admissibility = "generated by the rules", not a restriction on the sum; (ii) $\kappa_k$, $C$ and
$H_k|Z|$ are combined into a budget $G_k(Z)$ with a one-line recursion (§3.4); (iii) each component
carries bookkeeping data: a *spine* (a connected union of segments controlling its size), a
*gain clock* (last scale at which new large-field restrictions appeared in its history) and a
*smallness clock* (§3.5); (iv) $R$ is triggered by *ripeness* (quiet for $n_q$ scales, small for
the last $n_2$ scales), not by $K=0$; (v) the counter and H6 are replaced by the budget
invariant $G_k(Z)\ge\tau_{t^*(Z)}+V_k(Z)$ (§3.6, (H6′)), whose proof is LF-2 (§4); (vi) after $k_0$
the threshold is frozen at $p_2(\beta_{k_0})$; (vii) combinatorial factors may be taken in product
form (§3.4).

*Inductive hypothesis.* Def-7 (§3.6): P5 H1–H5, H7 unchanged in substance; H6 replaced by (H6′).

*Hardest step.* Still $R$ (LF-4), now in a slightly generalized form (components may contain pieces
that merged during the quiet period). LF-2 becomes elementary (§4: about 6 pages of inequalities).

*What the sources prove and what is reconstructed.* The step, the fluctuation integrals and the
renormalization on $Z_{k+1}^c$ are P5–P6 (sketched there, in full in the small-field case in
P3–P4); $R$ is P7 (sketched, with the generalization of §3.7 LF-4 to check); LF-1, LF-2, LF-6,
the $k_f=0$ case and the constant chain are reconstructed. List in §6.

*Length.* Reconstruction document 45–60 pp.: definitions and the region calculus (Lemma G of
§4.1) 10 pp.; LF-2 8 pp.; LF-1 4 pp.; LF-6 incl. the sphere chart 10 pp.; the changes to P7
(ripeness, multi-piece histories, product $C$-factors, Lemma 2.5, Lemma 3.1) 15 pp.; last step and
$k_f=0$ 5 pp.; constants 3 pp.

### 2.6 Recommendation

(A′). Reasons:

1. It is the only option for which a closed counting argument exists (§4); (A) literal has an
   apparently false counting proposition and an unpayable merge cost (§4.9), (B) cannot control the regime $k<k_0$,
   (C1) requires re-deriving $R$ without a source.
2. It changes only bookkeeping and one structural condition of $R$; every analytic estimate
   is still taken from P5–P7, so the source dependency graph keeps pointing at Balaban's pages.
3. The budget invariant is linear in the budget and needs no case distinction on "first/second
   kind", so the Lean interface for LF-2 is a statement about numbers, finite sets of cubes and
   segments, independent of the model (P6 p. 21: "completely model-independent").
4. Admissibility becomes a theorem about all generated histories rather than a restriction on
   the sum, which removes the circularity noted in roadmap §4.3 ("(1.30) is both an admissibility
   condition and a consequence").
5. Roland's ruling 7 permits all of this.

## 3. The recommended architecture (A′) in full

Definitions are in dependency order. Objects that are unchanged from P5–P7 are cited, not
repeated. Everything is per torus $\mathbb T=(\mathbb Z/\ell\mathbb Z)^d$ with $ML^{k_f+1}\mid\ell$; all
local objects are defined on finite subsets of $\mathbb Z^d$ and pushed to the torus (roadmap §3.5).

### 3.1 Lattices, scales, cubes, constants

* **Scale-$k$ lattice.** $T^{(k)}:=L^k\mathbb Z^d/\ell\mathbb Z^d$, written in scale-$k$ units as
  $(\mathbb Z/\ell L^{-k}\mathbb Z)^d$ (P5 p. 488, $T_1^{(k)}$). Block of $y\in T^{(k)}$:
  $B_k(y)=y+\{0,\dots,L^k-1\}^d\subset\mathbb T$.
* **Cubes.** $\pi_k$ = partition of $\mathbb T$ into cubes of side $ML^k$ aligned with $ML^k\mathbb Z^d$
  (these are the blocks of Def-2 `IsPolymer` with $s=ML^k$; side $M$ in scale-$k$ units). A
  *domain at scale $k$* is a union of $\pi_k$-cubes; $|X|_\#$ = number of cubes; $|X|_k$ = volume
  in scale-$k$ unit cubes $=M^d|X|_\#$. $\mathcal D_k$ = nearest-neighbour connected domains; $d_k(X)$
  = tree length in units of $M$ (P1 (2.27)); $\mathcal D_k(\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega^c)$ and
  $d_k(X\,\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega^c)$ as P5 (1.33)–(1.34).
* **Distances and neighbourhoods.** $\mathrm{dist}_k$ = sup-norm distance in units of $\pi_k$-cubes;
  for $X\subset\mathbb R^d$ (in these units) and real $r\ge0$, $N_r(X)$ = union of $\pi_k$-cubes at
  distance $\le r$ from $X$. Coarsening $X^\uparrow$ = union of $\pi_{k+1}$-cubes meeting $X$; in
  coordinates, the scale change is $x\mapsto x/L$.
* **Hull.** For a domain $X$ of diameter $\le\mathrm{crit}_k$, $\mathrm{Hull}(X)$ = smallest box (union
  of $\pi_k$-cubes) containing $X$ (P6 p. 22 [checked]: components below critical size are replaced
  by the smallest rectangular parallelepiped).
* **Running constants.** $\beta_k,a_k,\nu_k$ from the RG equations (P4 Thm 2 with P5 (1.40):
  $c_j=0$, $d_j=b_j$); $\lambda_k:=\log(\beta_ka)$. By P4 (H.6), $\theta_-\le\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_k\le\theta_+$
  with $\theta_-=\frac12(d-2)\log L$, $\theta_+=2(d-2)\log L$ for $c_8$ small. $R_k:=R_0\lambda_k^2$
  (P5 (1.2)), $r_k:=\lceil R_k\rceil$, $\rho_k:=c_0r_k$, $\mathrm{crit}_k:=2\rho_k+7$ (in $\pi_k$-cubes).

### 3.5 Bookkeeping data and ripeness

Each component $Z$ of $Z_k$ carries, as deterministic functions of the history:

* a **spine** $\Gamma(Z)\subset\mathbb R^d$ (scale-$k$ cube units): a point, or a compact connected
  union of finitely many segments of Euclidean length $\ell(Z)>1$, with
  $$Z\subset N_{\rho_k+3}(\Gamma(Z));\tag{3.1}$$
* a **gain clock** $b(Z)$: the last scale at which new large-field cubes ($\mathcal N_j$ or
  $\mathcal N^R$) appeared in the history of $Z$ (including the histories of all components merged
  into it);
* a **smallness clock** $s(Z)$: if $\Gamma(Z)$ is a point, the scale since which it has been a point;
  otherwise the projected epoch $s(Z):=k+\lceil\log_L\ell(Z)\rceil$.

*Update rules* (proved consistent with (3.1) in §4.3): (U1) a component obtained by a pure shadow
step (one old component, no new cubes) gets $\Gamma'=\Gamma/L$, replaced by one of its points if
$\ell(\Gamma)/L\le1$; $b'=b$; (U2) an *event* (several old components, or new cubes) gets the union of
the contracted spines, the centres of the new cubes and connecting segments of length $\le D_{k+1}:=\sqrt d(2\rho_{k+1}+8)$
along a spanning tree of the contact graph; $b'=k+1$ if there are new cubes, else $b'=\max b_i$;
(U3) when the hull rule boxes a component, its spine becomes the centre of the box and $s$ the
current scale; (U4) a kept $R$-component gets $b'=k$.

* **Ripeness time** $t^*(Z):=\max\{b+n_q(b),\ s+n_2(s)\}$. $Z$ is **ripe** at scale $k$ if $t^*(Z)=k$.

A ripe component has had no new large-field cubes in the last $n_q(b)$ scales (P7 (2.31)), and
has been a box of diameter $\le\mathrm{crit}$ for the last $n_2(s)$ scales; P7 needs exactly these
facts (LF-4 (M1)–(M2)).

## 4. The step counter (LF-2)

This section proves LF-2 from explicitly listed axioms. The axioms are statements about the
region calculus and the one-step bounds of P5–P7; their verification from the sources is Lemma G
(§3.7) and Lemma B (§3.4), and the $R$-rule (Rg) is part of LF-4. Everything else here is
elementary. "For $\lambda_0\ge\lambda_*$" means: for $\lambda_0=\log(\beta a)$ larger than a constant
depending only on the constants fixed before $\beta$ in §5.

### 4.1 Setting and axioms

Fixed: $d\ge3$, $L\ge2$, $M$, $\alpha\in(0,\frac12]$, a nondecreasing sequence $\lambda_k$ with
$\theta_-\le\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_k\le\theta_+$ (§3.1), $r_k=\lceil R_0\lambda_k^2\rceil$, $\rho_k=c_0r_k$,
$\mathrm{crit}_k=2\rho_k+7$, $n_q,n_2$ as in §3.1. Components are taken after $R^{(k)}$ unless said
otherwise. The *seeds* of step $k\to k+1$ are the sets $Z^\uparrow$ ($Z$ a component of $Z_k$) and the
cubes $q\in\mathcal N_{k+1}$.

* **(G1) Attribution.** Before the hull rule, $Z_{k+1}=\bigcup_\sigma B_\sigma$ over the seeds, where each
  $B_\sigma$ is a connected domain containing $\sigma$, $B_{Z^\uparrow}\subset N_{s_*r_k}(Z^\uparrow)$ and
  $B_q\subset N_{s'r_k}(q)$; in particular $Z^\uparrow\subset Z_{k+1}$.
* **(G2) Hull rule.** $Z_{k+1}$ is obtained by repeatedly replacing a component of diameter
  $\le\mathrm{crit}_{k+1}$ by its hull, until no further change.
* **(B) Budget recursion.** Lemma B (§3.4), with gain $g_k:=A_3\lambda_k^{2p_0}$ per new cube.
* **(Rg) $R$-rule** ($k\le k_0$). Every ripe component is either removed, or kept with its domain
  unchanged, its gain clock set to $k$, and $G$ increased by at least $g'_k-L_R$, where
  $g'_k:=A'_3\lambda_k^{2p_0-4}$ (the weakest new factor of P7: (2.33) [checked], also (2.61)–(2.62)),
  and $L_R\le C_R\lambda_k^{2d+2}$ (P7 (3.9)–(3.10) [checked]). Non-ripe components and their budgets
  are not changed by $R^{(k)}$.
* **(K) Constants.** $p_2\ge2d+3$; $2p_0-4>p_2$; $c_0\ge\max\{(s_*+1)L/(L-1),\ s'+1\}$.

### 4.2 Volume lemma and cost coefficients

**Lemma V.** If $\Gamma\subset\mathbb R^d$ is a point or a compact connected union of finitely many
segments of total Euclidean length $\ell$, and $\rho\ge1$, then
$|N_{\rho+3}(\Gamma)|_\#\le C_V\rho^{d-1}(\ell+\rho)$ with $C_V=2\cdot12^d$.

*Proof.* Take a maximal family $p_1,\dots,p_n\in\Gamma$ with pairwise sup-distances $>\rho$; then
$\Gamma\subset\bigcup_iB_\infty(p_i,\rho)$. If $n\ge2$, the balls $B_\infty(p_i,\rho/2)$ are disjoint, and for
each $i$ a path in $\Gamma$ from $p_i$ to some $p_j$ has an initial piece inside $B_\infty(p_i,\rho/2)$ of
Euclidean length $\ge\rho/2$; so $n\rho/2\le\ell$. Hence $n\le2\ell/\rho+1$. Each $N_{2\rho+3}(p_i)$ meets at
most $(4\rho+8)^d\le(12\rho)^d$ cubes, and $N_{\rho+3}(\Gamma)\subset\bigcup_iN_{2\rho+3}(p_i)$. $\square$

Define
$$v_j:=c_vC_V\lambda_j\rho_j^{d-1},\qquad w_j:=v_j\rho_j,\qquad\sigma_k:=\sum_{j>k}v_jL^{-(j-k)},\qquad\tau_t:=2A_2\lambda_t^{p_2}=2p_2(\beta_t).$$
So a component $Z\subset N_{\rho_j+3}(\Gamma)$ at scale $j$ costs at most $c_v\lambda_j|Z|_\#\le v_j\ell(\Gamma)+w_j$.

**Growth facts** (for $\lambda_0\ge\lambda_*$):
(F1) $\lambda_j\le\lambda_k+(j-k)\theta_+$ for $j\ge k$; $r,\rho,v,w,\tau$, $n_q$, $n_2$, $j\mapsto j+n_q(j)$ and
$j\mapsto j+n_2(j)$ are nondecreasing.
(F2) $\sigma_k=L^{-1}(v_{k+1}+\sigma_{k+1})$, and $v_{k+n}\le2^{d-1}n^{2d-1}v_{k+1}$ for $n\ge1$, so
$\sigma_k\le C_\sigma v_{k+1}$ with $C_\sigma=2^{d-1}\sum_{n\ge1}n^{2d-1}L^{-n}$.
(F3) $\tau_t-\tau_{t-1}\le2A_2p_2\theta_+\lambda_t^{p_2-1}$.
(F4) $n_q(j)\le\lambda_j/(\alpha\log L)+1$, $n_2(j)\le3\log_L\lambda_j+C_2$.
(F5) $w_j\le C_w\lambda_j^{2d+1}$ and $E_k:=(1+2C_\sigma 2^{3d})v_kD_k\le C_E\lambda_k^{2d+1}$, where
$D_k:=\sqrt d(2\rho_k+8)$ is the connector length of (U2).

### 4.3 Consistency of the bookkeeping

Invariant (3.1) is $Z\subset N_{\rho_k+3}(\Gamma(Z))$. A component that is a point-spine component has
diameter $\le2\rho_k+7=\mathrm{crit}_k$, so the hull rule makes it a box, and a box of side $\le\mathrm{crit}_k$
lies in $N_{\rho_k+3}$ of its centre.

(U1) If $Z\subset N_{\rho_k+3}(\Gamma)$ then $Z^\uparrow\subset N_{(\rho_k+3)/L+1}(\Gamma/L)$ and, by (G1),
$B_{Z^\uparrow}\subset N_{s_*r_k+(\rho_k+3)/L+1}(\Gamma/L)\subset N_{\rho_{k+1}+2}(\Gamma/L)$, because
$c_0(1-L^{-1})\ge s_*+1$ and $r_k\le r_{k+1}$. If $\ell(\Gamma/L)\le1$ and $x\in\Gamma/L$,
$N_{\rho+2}(\Gamma/L)\subset N_{\rho+3}(x)$.
(U2) $B_q\subset N_{s'r_k+1}(c(q))\subset N_{\rho_{k+1}+3}(c(q))$. The contact graph on the seeds of a new
component (edge when $B_\sigma\cup B_{\sigma'}$ is connected) is connected because the component is
the union of the connected sets $B_\sigma$; along a spanning tree, adjacent $B$'s give spine points
at Euclidean distance $\le D_{k+1}$. So $\Gamma'$ is connected, (3.1) holds, and
$$\ell(\Gamma')\le\sum_i\ell_i/L+(m+\nu'-1)D_{k+1}\tag{4.1}$$
for $m$ old parts with spine lengths $\ell_i$ and $\nu'$ new cubes.
(U3) Hulling: the box lies in $N_{\rho+3}$ of its centre, which becomes the spine; if the component had
a non-point spine, its projected epoch was $\ge k+2>k+1$, so $s$ decreases and $t^*$ does not
increase. A hull that meets another component merges them; this is an event of type (U2) with
no new cubes.
(U4) Kept $R$-components: domain and spine unchanged, $b=k$.

**Lemma T (ripeness time after an event).** For a component $Z'$ created by an event at step
$k\to k+1$, with $\Lambda':=\log_L\max(\ell(\Gamma'),1)$:
$t^*(Z')\le k+3+n_q(k+1)+\Lambda'+n_2(k+2+\Lambda')$ and $\lambda_{t^*(Z')}\le c_*\lambda_{k+1}+2\theta_+\Lambda'$, with
$c_*:=1+2\theta_+/(\alpha\log L)$.

*Proof.* $b'\le k+1$ gives $b'+n_q(b')\le k+1+n_q(k+1)$ by (F1). The projected epoch is
$s'\le k+1+\lceil\Lambda'\rceil$, so $s'+n_2(s')\le k+2+\Lambda'+n_2(k+2+\Lambda')$. Insert (F1), (F4), and
absorb $3\theta_+\log_L(\lambda_{k+1}+\theta_+(1+\Lambda'))+C$ into $\theta_+\lambda_{k+1}/(\alpha\log L)+\theta_+\Lambda'$ for
$\lambda_{k+1}\ge\lambda_*$. $\square$

### 4.4 The invariant

$$\text{(H6′)}\qquad t^*(Z)>k\quad\text{and}\quad G_k(Z)\ \ge\ \tau_{t^*(Z)}+V_k(Z),\qquad V_k(Z)=2\sigma_k\ell(Z)+\sum_{j=k+1}^{t^*(Z)}w_j .$$
(H6′)$^{\rm pre}$: the same with $t^*(Z)\ge k$. The two summands of $V$ are the predicted costs of
the future steps (spine part: the shrinking phase, cf. E10 (1.81) [review]; critical part: the
quiet phase), and $\tau_{t^*}$ is the threshold that $R$ needs at the ripeness time. The factor $2$
on the spine part creates a reserve proportional to $\ell$ used in Cases 1b and 2b.

**Theorem (LF-2).** Assume (G1), (G2), (B), (Rg), (K) and $\lambda_0\ge\lambda_*$.
(i) If every component of $Z_k$ satisfies (H6′), every component of $Z_{k+1}$ (before $R^{(k+1)}$)
satisfies (H6′)$^{\rm pre}$.
(ii) For $k\le k_0$: if every component of $Z_k$ satisfies (H6′)$^{\rm pre}$, then after $R^{(k)}$ every
component satisfies (H6′).
(iii) Components created by the base rule satisfy (H6′) at $k=1$.
(iv) (H6′) at $k_0$ implies (H6♭) at $k_0$; (H6♭) at $k$ implies (H6♭) at $k+1$ for $k_0\le k<k_f$.

### 4.5 Proof of (i)

Every component $Z'$ of $Z_{k+1}$ arises either from a pure shadow step or from an event.

**Pure shadow** (one old part $Z$, no new cube; possibly hulled). By (B),
$G_{k+1}(Z')\ge G_k(Z)-c_v\lambda_{k+1}|Z'|_\#\ge G_k(Z)-v_{k+1}\ell/L-w_{k+1}$, by (U1) (or (U3) if hulled) and Lemma V. By
(H6′) and (F2),
$$G_k(Z)\ge\tau_{t^*}+\tfrac2L(v_{k+1}+\sigma_{k+1})\ell+w_{k+1}+\sum_{j=k+2}^{t^*}w_j\quad(t^*\ge k+1).$$
Hence $G_{k+1}(Z')\ge\tau_{t^*}+2\sigma_{k+1}\ell/L+\sum_{j=k+2}^{t^*}w_j+v_{k+1}\ell/L\ge\tau_{t^*(Z')}+V_{k+1}(Z')$,
since $t^*(Z')\le t^*(Z)$ and $\ell(Z')\le\ell/L$. $t^*(Z')\ge k+1$ holds. This is the exact telescoping
that replaces "$K(Z')\ge K(Z)-1$" (P6 Prop 3.2 (iii)).

**Event** ($m$ old parts $Z_1,\dots,Z_m$, $\nu'$ new cubes, $(m,\nu')\neq(1,0)$). By (B), Lemma V and
(4.1),
$$G_{k+1}(Z')\ge\sum_iG_k(Z_i)+g_k\nu'-v_{k+1}\Big(\sum_i\ell_i/L+(m+\nu'-1)D_{k+1}\Big)-w_{k+1}.$$
Insert (H6′) for the $Z_i$, $2\sigma_k\ell_i=\frac2L(v_{k+1}+\sigma_{k+1})\ell_i$, and the required
$V_{k+1}(Z')\le2\sigma_{k+1}(\sum\ell_i/L+(m+\nu'-1)D_{k+1})+\sum_{j=k+2}^{t^{*\prime}}w_j$. It suffices that
$$\sum_i\tau_{t^*_i}+\sum_i\sum_{j=k+1}^{t^*_i}w_j+g_k\nu'+v_{k+1}\sum_i\frac{\ell_i}L\ \ge\ \tau_{t^{*\prime}}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{t^{*\prime}}w_j+(m+\nu'-1)E_{k+1}.\tag{4.2}$$
Write $\lambda=\lambda_{k+1}$, note $\lambda_k\ge\lambda/2$ and $\sum_i\tau_{t^*_i}\ge m\tau_{k+1}$.

*Case 1: $\nu'\ge1$ (new large-field cubes).*
(1a) $\Lambda'\le\lambda$. By Lemma T, $\lambda_{t^{*\prime}}\le c'\lambda$ with $c'=c_*+2\theta_+$, and
$t^{*\prime}-k\le C\lambda$. The right side of (4.2) is at most
$2A_2c'^{p_2}\lambda^{p_2}+C\lambda\,C_wc'^{2d+1}\lambda^{2d+1}+(m+\nu'-1)C_E\lambda^{2d+1}$. The left side is at least
$m\cdot2A_2\lambda^{p_2}+A_32^{-2p_0}\nu'\lambda^{2p_0}$. Since $2p_0>p_2\ge2d+3$, $\nu'\ge1$ and
$2A_2\lambda^{p_2}\ge C_E\lambda^{2d+1}$, (4.2) holds for $\lambda\ge\lambda_*$.
(1b) $\Lambda'>\lambda$. Then $\lambda_{t^{*\prime}}\le c'\Lambda'$, $t^{*\prime}-k\le C\Lambda'$, and the right side is at most
$C\Lambda'^{p_2}+(m+\nu'-1)C_E\lambda^{2d+1}$. By (4.1), either $\sum_i\ell_i/L\ge\frac12L^{\Lambda'}$, and then
$v_{k+1}\sum\ell_i/L\ge\frac12\lambda L^{\Lambda'}\ge C\Lambda'^{p_2}$ for $\Lambda'>\lambda\ge\lambda_*$; or
$(m+\nu'-1)D_{k+1}\ge\frac12L^{\Lambda'}$, and then $C\Lambda'^{p_2}\le(m+\nu'-1)\cdot2D_{k+1}C\Lambda'^{p_2}L^{-\Lambda'}\le m+\nu'-1$,
which is covered by $m\tau_{k+1}+\nu'g_k$. The terms $(m+\nu'-1)C_E\lambda^{2d+1}$ are covered as in (1a).

*Case 2: $\nu'=0$, $m\ge2$ (pure merge; gain clock $b'=\max_ib_i$, no reset of the quiet period).*
Let $i_0$ maximize $t^*_i$. Then $b'+n_q(b')\le t^*_{i_0}$.
If $t^{*\prime}\le t^*_{i_0}$, the right side of (4.2) minus the $i_0$ terms on the left is at most
$(m-1)E_{k+1}\le(m-1)\tau_{k+1}$. Done.
Otherwise $t^{*\prime}=s'+n_2(s')$. Let $\ell_{\max}=\max(\max_i\ell_i,1)$. The part carrying the longest
spine has $t^*\ge s\ge k+\log_L\ell_{\max}$ (its projected epoch; trivial if all spines are points), so
$t^*_{i_0}\ge k+\log_L\ell_{\max}$, while $\Lambda'\le\log_L\ell_{\max}+\log_L(m(1+D_{k+1}))$ by (4.1). Lemma T gives
$$\Delta:=t^{*\prime}-t^*_{i_0}\le\bar\Delta:=3+\log_Lm+\log_L(1+D_{k+1})+n_2(k+2+\Lambda'),$$
and by (F3), (F5), it suffices that
$$(m-1)\tau_{k+1}+v_{k+1}\ell_{\max}/L\ \ge\ \bar\Delta\big(2A_2p_2\theta_+\lambda_{t^{*\prime}}^{p_2-1}+C_w\lambda_{t^{*\prime}}^{2d+1}\big)+(m-1)C_E\lambda^{2d+1}.\tag{4.3}$$
(2a) $\Lambda'\le\lambda$: then $\lambda_{t^{*\prime}}\le c'\lambda$ and $\bar\Delta\le\log_Lm+C\log\lambda\le(m-1)(1+C\log\lambda)$ (as
$\ln m\le m-1$); the right side of (4.3) is $\le(m-1)C(1+\log\lambda)\lambda^{\max(p_2-1,2d+1)}\le(m-1)\tau_{k+1}$ for
$\lambda\ge\lambda_*$, because $p_2\ge2d+3$.
(2b) $\Lambda'>\lambda$: either $\log_L\ell_{\max}\ge\Lambda'/2$, and the spine reserve
$v_{k+1}\ell_{\max}/L\ge\lambda L^{\Lambda'/2-1}$ dominates $C(\log_Lm+\Lambda')\Lambda'^{p_2-1}$ when $m-1\le L^{\Lambda'/4}$, while for
$m-1>L^{\Lambda'/4}$ the part $C\log m\,\Lambda'^{p_2-1}\le C(\log m)^{p_2}$ is $\le(m-1)$; or
$\log_L(m(1+D_{k+1}))\ge\Lambda'/2$, so $m\ge L^{\lambda/4}$ and the right side is $\le C(\log m)^{p_2}+(m-1)C_E\lambda^{2d+1}\le(m-1)\tau_{k+1}$.

This proves (i). $\square$

**Remark (what replaced E10's tree induction).** E10 (1.84)–(1.88) handles merges by an induction
over a spanning tree of the merged domains, paying the extra steps after a merge with a reserve
$2p_0(g_{j})$ that does not grow along the flow. Here additivity of $G$ (Lemma B), the union bound
for spines (4.1) and the quantitative shift $\Delta$ of the ripeness time replace that induction; the
shift is $O(\log m+\log\lambda)$ only because a merge restarts the *smallness* clock ($n_2$ scales), not
the quiet clock ($n_q$ scales). With a quiet-clock restart the argument fails (§4.9).

### 4.6 Proof of (ii) and (iii)

(ii) Non-ripe components are unchanged by $R^{(k)}$ and satisfy (H6′) because $t^*>k$. A ripe
component ($t^*=k$, point spine) is removed, or kept with $G_{\rm new}\ge G_k-L_R+g'_k\ge\tau_k-C_R\lambda_k^{2d+2}+A'_3\lambda_k^{2p_0-4}$
and new ripeness time $t^{*\prime}=k+n_q(k)$ (its smallness clock satisfies $s+n_2(s)\le k$). Since
$\lambda_{k+n_q(k)}\le c_*\lambda_k$, it suffices that
$$A'_3\lambda_k^{2p_0-4}\ \ge\ C_R\lambda_k^{2d+2}+2A_2c_*^{p_2}\lambda_k^{p_2}+\Big(\frac{\lambda_k}{\alpha\log L}+1\Big)C_wc_*^{2d+1}\lambda_k^{2d+1},$$
true for $\lambda_k\ge\lambda_*$ because $2p_0-4>p_2\ge2d+3$. After $R^{(k)}$ no component is ripe.

(iii) A component created by the base rule is an event with $m=0$ at step $0\to1$, with gain
$g_0=A_3\lambda_0^{2p_0}$ per cube (LF-6 (ii)); Case 1 of §4.5 with $m=0$ applies verbatim. $\square$

### 4.7 Proof of (iv): transfer at $k_0$ and the frozen regime

*Two facts from P5 p. 500 [checked].* $\nu_{k_0}>\frac78L^{-d}\beta_{k_0}^{-1}$ and $\nu_{k_f}\le1$ give
$L^{2(k_f-k_0)}<(\frac87)^2L^d\beta_{k_0}$, hence $2(k_f-k_0)\log L<d\log L+\log\frac32+\lambda_{k_0}$, so
$k_f-k_0<\lambda_{k_0}$ (since $\log L>\frac12$, $\beta$ large). By P4 (H.6) the increments $\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_j$
equal $(d-2)\log L$ up to errors whose sum over all $j$ is $O(c_8)$, so
$\lambda_{k_f}\le\lambda_{k_0}+(k_f-k_0)(d-2)\log L+O(c_8)\le\frac d2\lambda_{k_0}+c_d$. (In the displays below
$\frac d2(1+\epsilon)$ may be read as $\frac d2$.)

*Transfer.* At $k_0$, (H6′) gives $G\ge\tau_{t^*}+2\sigma_{k_0}\ell+\sum_{j=k_0+1}^{t^*}w_j$ with $t^*>k_0$. (H6♭)
needs $G\ge p_2(\beta_{k_0})+2\sigma_{k_0}\ell+\sum_{j=k_0+1}^{k_f}w_j$. It suffices that
$\tau_{k_0+1}\ge p_2(\beta_{k_0})+(k_f-k_0)w_{k_f}$, i.e.
$A_2\lambda_{k_0}^{p_2}\ge\lambda_{k_0}C_w(\frac d2(1+\epsilon)\lambda_{k_0}+c_d)^{2d+1}$: true for $\lambda_{k_0}\ge\lambda_*$ since $p_2>2d+2$.

*Frozen steps* ($k_0\le k<k_f$, no $R$). Pure shadow: the computation of §4.5 with $\tau_{t^*}$
replaced by the constant $p_2(\beta_{k_0})$ and $t^*$ by $k_f$. Event: (4.2) becomes
$$(m-1)_+p_2(\beta_{k_0})+g_k\nu'+[m=0]\,\big(-p_2(\beta_{k_0})-\textstyle\sum_{j=k+1}^{k_f}w_j\big)\ \ge\ (m+\nu'-1)E_{k+1}$$
(the spine terms are handled as in §4.5). With $\lambda_{k+1}\le\lambda_{k_f}\le d\lambda_{k_0}$, $E_{k+1}\le C_E(d\lambda_{k_0})^{2d+1}$,
and $g_k\ge A_3\lambda_{k_0}^{2p_0}$, this holds for $\lambda_{k_0}\ge\lambda_*$. There is no ripeness time in
(H6♭), so no analogue of Cases 1b, 2b is needed. $\square$

*Consequences.* For $k\le k_0$: $G_k\ge\tau_{t^*}\ge\tau_{k+1}>2p_2(\beta_k)$, which is P5 (1.30) with $\ell=2$; at
ripeness $G_k\ge2p_2(\beta_k)$, the input of P7 (3.8); for $k_0\le k\le k_f$: $G_k\ge p_2(\beta_{k_0})$, which is
P5 (1.31) with $\ell=1$ and the frozen threshold.

### 4.8 P6 Thm 4: the printed inequality fails; the repair works

*The printed chain* (P6 p. 22, page image [checked]):
$$\kappa_{k,n}>\kappa_{k,K}-\mathrm{const}\sum_{p=K+1}^n\log\beta_{k+p}(12MR_{k+p})^d>2p_2(\beta_{k+K})-\mathrm{const}(12MR_0)^d\sum_{p=K+1}^n(\log\beta_{k+p}a)^{2d+1}$$
$$>2p_2(\beta_{k+K})-\mathrm{const}'(\log\beta_{k+K}a)^{2d+2}>p_2(\beta_{k+n}),\qquad\text{"if }n\le K+\log\beta_{k+K}a,\ p_2\ge2d+2,\ A_2\text{ large".}$$
With $p_2(\beta)=A_2(\log\beta)^{p_2}$ (P5 p. 495 [checked]) and $\lambda:=\log\beta_{k+K}a$, take $n-K=\lfloor\lambda\rfloor$.
By P4 (H.6), $\log\beta_{k+n}\ge\lambda+(\lambda-1)(d-2)\log L-Cc_8$. The last inequality requires
$p_2(\beta_{k+n})<2p_2(\beta_{k+K})$, i.e. $\big(\log\beta_{k+n}/\log\beta_{k+K}\big)^{p_2}<2$, but the ratio tends to
$(1+(d-2)\log L)^{p_2}\ge(1+\ln2)^8\approx67.5$ as $\lambda\to\infty$, for every $A_2$ (both sides are
proportional to $A_2$, and the $\mathrm{const}'$ term only makes it worse). The inequality holds only for
$n-K\lesssim(2^{1/p_2}-1)\lambda/((d-2)\log L)$. **Confirmed: the last inequality fails as printed**
(agrees with QC_LOG Phase 4 and ER-091).

*The repair in P6's own terms.* Suppose at $k_0$ the component has $K\ge1$ (so $\kappa_{k_0,n}>2p_2(\beta_{k_0+n})$
for $n\le K$) and, from step $K$ on, is below critical size. For $K<n\le k_f-k_0$:
$$\kappa_{k_0,n}>2p_2(\beta_{k_0+K})-c_{12}(n-K)\lambda_{k_f}^{2d+1}\ge2p_2(\beta_{k_0})-c_{12}\lambda_{k_0}\big(\tfrac d2(1+\epsilon)\lambda_{k_0}+c_d\big)^{2d+1}\ge p_2(\beta_{k_0}),$$
with $c_{12}=\mathrm{const}(12MR_0)^d$, using $n-K<k_f-k_0<\lambda_{k_0}$ and the bound on $\lambda_{k_f}$ of §4.7; the last
step holds if $A_2\lambda_{k_0}^{p_2}\ge c_{12}(\frac d2+1)^{2d+1}\lambda_{k_0}^{2d+2}$, i.e. for $p_2>2d+2$ and $\beta$ large, or
$p_2=2d+2$ and $A_2\ge c_{12}(\frac d2+1)^{2d+1}$. For $n\le K$, $\kappa_{k_0,n}>2p_2(\beta_{k_0+n})\ge p_2(\beta_{k_0})$. So
the $\ell=1$ bound holds for all remaining steps with the threshold $p_2(\beta_{k_0})$. The phase in
which the component is still above critical size needs P6 Prop 3.2 (i) in some form; in (A′) both
phases are covered by §4.7.

*Sufficiency for the last step.* P7 (4.12)–(4.13) [checked] need $\varepsilon^{-2}e^{-p_2}\le\frac12\beta_{k_f}^{-m-1}$
with $\varepsilon^{-2}=3^dK_0e^{\frac32dL\kappa}$. With the frozen threshold:
$p_2(\beta_{k_0})=A_2\lambda_{k_0}^{p_2}\ge A_2\big(\tfrac2d(1+\epsilon)^{-1}(\lambda_{k_f}-c_d)\big)^{p_2}\ge(m+2)\lambda_{k_f}+\log(2\varepsilon^{-2})$
for $\beta\ge\beta_0$, since $p_2>1$. So the repair suffices for LF-8 and Root-F. This confirms the
proposal of roadmap §4.3 (agreed by Roland, ruling 8).

### 4.9 Why the literal P6 Prop 3.2 (ii) fails, and why $R$ must be modified

*Counterexample to (ii)* (UNCERTAIN only as to the reading of "diameter" for disconnected sets).
Let $Z^{(1)},Z^{(2)}$ be two components that are quiet, below critical size, and have
$K(Z^{(i)})=1$ at scale $k-1$, i.e. $\kappa_{k-1,1}(Z^{(i)})>2p_2(\beta_k)$, $\kappa_{k-1,2}(Z^{(i)})\le2p_2(\beta_{k+1})$.
Such configurations occur in the sum: the creation scales and positions of large-field cubes are
free, and the merge time of two quiet components is fixed by their distance (it shrinks by $L$ per
step). Suppose their shadows touch at step $k-1\to k$. By (3.10) the merged component has
$\kappa_k(Z')\ge\kappa_{k-1,1}(Z^{(1)})+\kappa_{k-1,1}(Z^{(2)})-O(\lambda^{2d+1})>4p_2(\beta_k)-O(\lambda^{2d+1})$, and its counter
satisfies $K(Z')\approx\max\{n:p_2(\beta_{k+n})<2p_2(\beta_k)\}\approx\frac{\ln2}{p_2(d-2)\log L}\lambda_k<\lambda_k-3$. When $K(Z')$ reaches $0$
at scale $k'=k+K(Z')$, (ii) asserts $k_1$ with $k'-k_1>\log\beta_{k_1}a$ such that $Z_j\cap Z'$ has diameter
below the critical size for $k_1\le j\le k'$; this forces $k_1<k-3$ (if $k_1\ge k-3$ then
$k'-k_1\le K(Z')+3<\lambda_k-3\theta_+\le\log\beta_{k_1}a$ for $\lambda_k$ large). But each step adds around each
piece a layer of width $\ge R_j$ cubes of $\pi_{j+1}$ (P5 (2.3): distance $>LMR_j$ in scale-$j$ units) and
contracts distances by $L$, so $\mathrm{dist}_j\ge L(\mathrm{dist}_{j+1}+2R_j-1)$; the pieces being separate at
$k-1$, $\mathrm{dist}_{k-3}\ge2L^2R_{k-3}$ cubes, more than the critical size $12[R_{k-3}]$ cubes of P6 (3.16)
[checked] for $L\ge3$. So no such $k_1$ exists.

*Why a full restart cannot be paid.* If $R$ requires smallness throughout the quiet period (P7
as written), the merged component above must wait a new quiet period of $n_q\approx\lambda/(\alpha\log L)$
scales, at the end of which the threshold is
$\tau_{k+n_q}\approx(1+\frac{(d-2)}\alpha)^{p_2}\tau_k\ge3^{p_2}\tau_k$, plus the quiet-period cost
$\asymp\lambda^{2d+2}$, while the budget available is $\approx2\tau_k$ (two nearly exhausted parts). For
thresholds $\tau_t=C\lambda_t^{q}$ the requirement $\tau_{k+n_q}+Q\le2\tau_k$ forces $q<\log2/\log(1+\frac{d-2}\alpha)<1$,
incompatible with $\tau\ge C_R\lambda^{2d+2}$ (needed by $R$, P7 (3.10)). This is the same mechanism as
the Thm 4 defect: thresholds polynomial in $\log\beta_k$ grow by a fixed factor over $\asymp\log\beta_k$
scales.

*The way out used in (A′).* $R$ needs smallness only at the end of the quiet period ($n_2$ scales:
P7 (2.2)–(2.5) at $k_2,k_3$; the chain (2.27) at scale $k$), so a merge restarts only the smallness
clock; the threshold then grows by the factor $(1+O(\log\lambda/\lambda))^{p_2}$, which the freed
threshold pays (Case 2 of §4.5). This is modification (M2) of LF-4.

*Fallback if (M2) fails.* Keep P7's requirement, give each component a reserve frozen at its last
gain scale (as E10 does with $2p_0(g_{j(Z)})$), and bound the lifetime of a lineage after its last
gain by a doubling argument: a lineage with $N$ gain atoms has at most $\log_2N$ quiet-clock restarts
on any root-to-leaf chain of its merge tree, so it is alive at most $(\log_2N+1)(n_q+C)$ scales after
its last gain. I have not closed this argument for lineages with astronomically many old atoms;
it is not part of the recommendation.
