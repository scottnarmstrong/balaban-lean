## Findings

### P3-01 — MAJOR, survived refutation: III.7.1 has the wrong support for removing indicators

**Location.** III.7.1, frozen-v1 lines 3477–3482, starts “**On the support of the window kernel $K_{\rm win}$** and of $\tilde\chi_k\chi_S\chi^0\chi''$, every characteristic function of $\mathcal X_{\rm win}$ ... equals $1$,” then concludes $K_{\rm win}N=K^{\rm red}_{\rm win}N$, where $N=\tilde\chi_k\chi_S\chi^0\chi''$. Definition III.1.3, lines 2980–2989, already includes $\mathcal X_{\rm win}$ in $K_{\rm win}$.

**Argument and counterexample.** The premise only proves $\chi_{\rm old}=1$ where $H\chi_{\rm old}N\ne0$; this is automatic. The conclusion requires $\chi_{\rm old}=1$ where $HN\ne0$, with $H$ the window kernel after deleting the selected old indicators. In a one-point model, let $H=N=1$ and $\chi_{\rm old}=0$. The stated premise is vacuous, while $H\chi_{\rm old}N=0\ne1=HN$. The manuscript's Case A/B calculations may be intended to prove the stronger implication, but the statement and its stated support do not, and the proof invokes “the support of the window kernel” at the outset. P7 Lemma 2.5, pp. 512–516, is a claim that the **new** small-field restrictions imply the old ones; it cannot justify the weakened formulation. The equality is used in Definition III.8.5, the K2 support claim in III.8.8, and LF-4R.

**Repair.** Restate and prove $N\,K^{\rm red}_{\rm win,0}>0\Rightarrow\chi_{\rm old}=1$ for each deleted factor, with all *retained* factors stated explicitly. In Cases A/B, identify every use of a deleted factor and replace it by a retained condition or a separate estimate. Then the equality follows pointwise. The abstract counterexample does not refute that stronger mathematical assertion.

### P3-02 — MAJOR, survived refutation: the complex core kernel is undefined at the point used

**Location.** (S5)(i)–(ii), frozen-v1 lines 2305–2312, writes $K=\mathbf1_Ae^{-a}$ with $A$ a **Borel set of real values** and only $a$ analytically extended. III.9.5, lines 3696–3703, asserts an integral of $|K_{k_1}(C)(\tilde R x)e^{b_C(\tilde R x)}|$ for $\tilde R\in O(N,\mathbb C)$; III.9.6 uses it to dominate a holomorphic chart integral.

**Argument.** For real $x$ and a nonreal complex orthogonal $\tilde R$, $\tilde R x\in\mathbb C^N$ in general, whereas $\mathbf1_A$ was defined on real configurations. A simple shell indicator $A=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:||x|-1|<\delta\}$ makes $\mathbf1_A(\tilde R x)$ meaningless. Extending the inequality by the same modulus formula gives a discontinuous indicator, not a holomorphic integrand. The charitable reading is to keep $\mathbf1_A(x)$ fixed after the real change of variables and analytically continue only $a(\tilde R x)$ and $b_C(\tilde R x)$. That is compatible with P7 p. 521, which says the rotation moves output-field dependence into exponentials, but it is **not the expression asserted in III.9.5** and requires a precise formula for every indicator in the complete core/window integrand. The real group covariance and the small $h$ shift in (S5) plausibly bound that corrected expression; they do not define $K(\tilde R x)$.

**Repair.** Define the complexified rotated kernel as $\mathbf1_A(x)\exp[-a(\tilde R x;h)+b_C(\tilde R x;h,g)]$, after showing each indicator becomes a fixed function of the real integration variables. Prove the uniform majorant for this expression, including products of inherited kept kernels. Do not assert an analytic continuation of the indicator itself.

### P3-03 — MAJOR, survived refutation: (3.10″) retains a nonanalytic front function

**Location.** III.9.6(b), frozen-v1 lines 3705–3745, defines $K^{(3.7)}_{\rm in}$ by deleting the three front functions, but the displayed analytic bound (3.10″) still integrates $K^{(3.7)}_{\rm in}\,\chi_{Z''}$. Definition III.5.1(5), lines 3310–3320, defines $\chi_{Z''}$ as a product of threshold indicators depending on $\psi_k$. III.10.5, lines 3818–3838, cites (3.10″) as the analytic activity bound.

**Argument and counterexample.** Even a single factor $u\mapsto\mathbf1[|u|<1]$ has no holomorphic extension agreeing with its real values across the threshold. The same obstruction applies to $\chi_{Z''}(\psi_k)$; it is not one of the indicators made fixed by rotating *internal* core variables. P7 Lemma 3.1, p. 525, explicitly concerns the kernel of (3.7) **without the characteristic functions in front**; P7 (3.21), p. 525, uses these front functions only in the real-field density identity. Thus the cited source supports the version **without** $\chi_{Z''}$ in the analytic activity. Treating $\chi_{Z''}$ as $1$ on the real removed branch cannot make the complex expression analytic.

**Repair.** State the supremum bound for the front-free operator and use that analytic operator as the heavy activity in GAS. Keep $\chi_{Z''}$ in the real density identity (3.21) only. Check the front-free bound after deleting an indicator, including the real positivity and residual budget.

### P3-04 — MINOR, survived refutation: the $Z_0$ volume factor in the refund is too small

**Location.** Definition III.8.6 and III.8.7 proof, frozen-v1 lines 3571–3573 and 3590–3595. They set $C_{\rm vol}=\log(v_N\max\{3,C_6\}^NA_0^Na^{-N/2})$ and claim the $Z_0$-ball with its $\beta_k^{N/2}$ prefactor costs at most $e^{Np_0\log\lambda_k+C_{\rm vol}}$ per block.

**Counterexample.** For one $Z_0$ block, the ball has radius $C_6\beta_k^{-1/2}A_0\lambda_k^{p_0}$. Its Lebesgue volume times $\beta_k^{N/2}$ is exactly $v_NC_6^NA_0^N\lambda_k^{Np_0}$. If $C_6\ge3$, the claimed upper bound is this value times $a^{-N/2}<1$, because $a\in(1,3/2)$. The factor $a^{-N/2}$ belongs to the **other** estimate for a Gaussian normalization with $\delta_k=(\beta_ka)^{-1/2}p_0$. The physical integral may be smaller because of other factors, but the printed volume comparison is false.

**Repair.** Increase $C_{\rm vol}$ to $\log(v_N\max\{3,C_6\}^NA_0^N)$ (or keep separate constants), then update $C_R$ in (III.8.4). This costs only a fixed multiple of $D_0^d$ and does not defeat the polynomial refund.

### P3-05 — MAJOR, UNREFUTED-TENTATIVE: the operator-valued GAS/PE passage is still an unproved adaptation

**Location.** III.10.5, frozen-v1 lines 3818–3842, says GAS of `POLYMER.md` §2.6 is applied “pointwise in the external variables, with $|\tau|$ read as the operator norm of (3.10″), since every coupling ... is a function of the variables integrated by the weights.” III.O.1 and III.L item P10 expressly say the operator-valued combination is not landed.

**Argument and refutation attempt.** The scalar GAS hypothesis is a bound on scalar activities $|\tau(C,u)|$ before multiplying the Mayer factors $e^{B(X,u)}-1$. Here a removed component has internal variables, and a coupling can read variables from *two* components. A norm bound on each isolated integral does not by itself give a product bound after inserting a coupling. A uniform pointwise bound on the coupling, together with an explicit Fubini/conditional-integral argument, might supply the missing inequality; no counterexample to that corrected route was found. The text does not specify such an operator-valued GAS statement, its norm, or the locality/holomorphy proof after the internal integrations. This is a substantial extra proof step, not an existing theorem of the cited scalar `POLYMER.md` GAS or the landed scalar `gas_eq`/`pe_hole` declarations.

**Repair.** State an operator-valued GAS lemma with disjoint integration spaces, external parameter domains, uniform kernel majorants, and coupled Mayer factors; prove its activity estimate and local analytic continuation before invoking PE-with-hole. Apply it to the **front-free** analytic activity from a repaired III.9.6. Until then III.10.5 and III.10.8 are conditional at this point.

## Gaussian and first-variation stress test

For a one-step block, $z_k^Nt_{k-1}(z_k\psi_k,Q\psi_{k-1})$ is a probability density with mean $z_k^{-1}Q\psi_{k-1}$ and variance $(z_k^2c_{k-1})^{-1}I$. Iterating after $\widehat\psi_j=(\prod_{i=k_1+1}^jz_i)\psi_j$ gives precisely the mean $\sum_xw_x\varpi_{n_x}\psi^G(x)$ and variance $\sum_{j,y}\varpi_j^2w_y^2/c_j$ in (III.1.5). Thus the $z_i$ shift is real, and P7 (2.9) applies literally only to the ideal coefficients. The top-scale weighting in the estimate of $|\eta_z|$ uses the exact last-layer term $W_{k-1}=(\beta_ka)^{-1}$; no reversal of the coefficient ratio was found.

In III.6.3, set $D_z=\mathcal Q_z\delta\psi$, $r_z=\psi_k-Q_k\phi^0_k$, $\varrho_z=(a_k/a_z)r_z$, and $s_z=(\mathcal Q_z^\natural-\mathcal Q_z)\tilde\psi$. Expanding $\frac12\beta_ka_z^\natural|\varrho_z-D_z-s_z|^2$, the ideal cross term is exactly $-\beta_ka_kr_z\cdot D_z$; P7 (2.54) supplies $+\beta_ka_kr_z\cdot D_z$. The remaining $-\beta_k\eta_za_kr_z\cdot D_z$ and $+\beta_ka_z^\natural D_z\cdot s_z$ are **linear** and are not claimed to vanish. Young's inequality assigns them $\frac18+\frac14=\frac38$ of $\sum_z\beta_ka_z^\natural|D_z|^2$ and leaves constant terms with the necessary $\beta_k$. The real-domain estimates use $\beta_k\delta_k^2=A_0^2\lambda_k^{2p_0}/a$, $\Theta^\natural=\beta_k\varepsilon_k\sum\bar\varsigma_z$, and $\Sigma^\natural=\sqrt{\beta_k}\max\bar\varsigma_z$; no missing $\beta_k$ was found. The complex bound is polynomial because $\beta_k\eta_k\varepsilon_k^2=O(1)$ and $\Theta^\natural=O(\hat\lambda_k^{2d+1})$. These checks depend on SF Theorem 5.3 and the geometry of III.1.7–III.1.7′. They do not validate the later analytic interfaces flagged above.
