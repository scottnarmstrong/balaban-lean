## Q-A. Paving and Euclidean symmetry

**Source evidence.** P1 p. 116, (H.4), explicitly gives covariance of the *density* $\mathscr E^{(j)}(y;\psi,\nu_jh)$ under translations, coordinate reflections, and permutations of the **whole level-$j$ lattice** $T^{(j)}$. Its (2.23) is the point group fixing $y$, and (2.24) gives equality of constant-field values at all centres. P1 p. 117, (2.26), constructs $\mathcal D_j$ from a compatible partition by $M$-cubes and covers $\pi'_j$ made of $L^d$ such cubes; the $\pi'_j$ cubes may overlap, but their bases still lie on the fixed $M$-grid. P1 p. 118, paragraph following (H.5), says the localized terms have Euclidean covariance with the transformation applied to $X$ too, and says this property is not used there. P4 p. 677, (H.3), repeats full level-$j$ covariance of the **density**. P4 pp. 677–678, (H.4), gives covariance of individual terms only for transformations of the lattice of **large cubes** $T^{(j+m)}$, where $M=L^m$. P4 p. 706, immediately before Proposition 4.1, says the newly constructed functions satisfy (H.3); Proposition 4.1 then supplies (H.4) for the new terms. These are separate source claims.

**Ruling.** With the level-$j$ sites indexed as $q\in\mathbb Z^d$ and paving cells $\{Ma,\ldots,Ma+M-1\}$, the fixed $M$-paving is preserved by translations in $M\mathbb Z^d$, coordinate permutations, and affine reflections $q_\mu\mapsto M-1-q_\mu$ (and their composites). It is not preserved by $q_\mu\mapsto-q_\mu$ or by a one-block translation. For example, the cell $\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$ reflects through the site $q=0$ to $\{-(M-1),\ldots,0\}$. There is a coordinate nuance in P1 p. 117: Balaban embeds the original sites at half-integers and takes paving vertices in $M\mathbb Z^d$, so reflection through the *paving vertex* zero does preserve that paving. The reflection fixing an **arbitrary site** $y$ required by P1 (2.23) does not. In integer site indices, the paving-preserving vertex reflection is precisely the affine reflection above. Allowing overlapping $LM$-cubes does not remove the base-grid obstruction. The landed `IsSF.eucE`/`eucM` incorrectly quantify over `latSymGroup` from `Def6Terms.lean`, whose `transJ` moves by one $j$-block and whose `flipJ` reflects about a $j$-block centre. Conversely, P4 does **not** weaken (H.3) to paving symmetries: the RG construction must supply full covariance of the summed density separately.

**Amended Def-6 (H.3) and (H.4), at every $j\le k$.** Let $G_j=\mathbb Z^d\rtimes B_d$ act on the $j$-block lattice, $B_d$ being signed coordinate permutations, and let $H_j\le G_j$ preserve the fixed $M$-paving. For all $r\in G_j$ and all data in the common analytic domain,
$$\mathscr E^{(j)}(r y;r\psi,rh)=\mathscr E^{(j)}(y;\psi,h).\tag{H.3-E}$$
The same full group acts on $\mathscr M^{(j)}(x)$ in the (H.7) analogue. Termwise, for $r\in H_j$ only,
$$\mathscr E^{(j)}(r y,rX;r\psi,rh)=\mathscr E^{(j)}(y,X;\psi,h),\qquad rX\in\mathcal D_j.\tag{H.4-E}$$
Here the external field is constant in the paper; `actField r h` is the natural representation of it in `Def6.lean`. Internal $O(N)$ invariance remains termwise as printed in P1 p. 118 and P4 p. 677. The two equations must not be conflated.

**Lean-level change.** In `Def6Terms.lean`, replace `latSymGroup` as used by `IsSF.eucE`/`eucM` with a `pavingSymGroup` generated on the fine torus by translations of $ML^j$, reflections $x_\mu\mapsto ML^j-1-x_\mu$, and coordinate swaps. Keep a distinct `fullJSymGroup` with translations of $L^j$, reflections $x_\mu\mapsto L^j-1-x_\mu$, and swaps. In `Def6.lean`, add `IsSF.eucEloc` (and `eucMloc`) over `fullJSymGroup` for `Eloc` (respectively `Mloc`); restrict `eucE`/`eucM` to `pavingSymGroup`, with explicit `IsPolyDom` preservation and equivariance of the generating-set domain. P4 (H.3) is a claim about `Eloc`, **not** about `Edens` alone. In `RenormDefs.lean` and `RenormTermwiseDefs.lean`, do not take `Premises.sym_mem`, `Premises.sym_inv`, `PremisesTerm.sym_mem`, `PremisesTerm.sym_inv` on the original `liftTF`: `Def6BridgeGeom.IsSFFamily.premisesCore` is the correct bridge endpoint for the unsymmetrized family. The public I-6 source premises should instead carry (H.3-E) for the density and (H.4-E) for its representation, or accept the symmetrized family described next. The existing `RenormIso.moment_tensor` may consume the strong `sym_mem`/`sym_inv` fields **only for that derived family**.

**Proof and repair of the centred I-6 symmetry.** This is a finite Reynolds average, and makes the present per-centre proof available without asserting false symmetry of the original polymers. Extend each term by zero to all finite $X$ and let $Q=G_j/H_j$ be the finite set of paving offsets and point-group cosets (on a torus, use its finite group action). For a representative $r_q$ put
$$\overline{\mathscr E}(y,X;\psi,h)=|Q|^{-1}\sum_{q\in Q}\mathscr E(r_q^{-1}y,r_q^{-1}X;r_q^{-1}\psi,r_q^{-1}h),$$
where a summand is zero unless $r_q^{-1}X\in\mathcal D_j$. By (H.3-E), summing over $X$ gives $\mathscr E(y;\psi,h)$ for **each** $y$, not merely after summing over $y$. Changing coset representatives uses (H.4-E); reindexing cosets proves full $G_j$ covariance of $\overline{\mathscr E}$ and closure of its enlarged polymer set under the point group about $y$. The new supports are translates or reflections of the old supports. Locality, analyticity, and the exponential bound follow term by term; the weighted polymer-count constant increases by at most the fixed index $|Q|$, so remains uniform in torus size and scale. One must transport the generating set with the offset, and then prove the corresponding `XiDom` equivariance; silently reusing the old fixed-grid `gs` is insufficient. This is the repair required if the RG construction presents only a fixed-paving localization. P1's direct route, (3.109)–(3.126), instead works with density covariance and proves a global decomposition; it never needs false point-group covariance of the original $X$ family.

## Q-B. The second moment in (H.5)(4)

**Source evidence and ruling.** P1 p. 119, (H.7), differentiates the **full effective action** $\mathscr E^{(j)}$ at $x$ and $0$ before taking the torus limit. P4 p. 678, (1.3) and (H.5), defines the thermodynamic kernel $\mathscr G^{(j,\infty)}(x-x')$ from the Hessian of the full action and sets $\sum_x\operatorname{tr}[\mathscr G^{(j,\infty)}(x)(e_2\otimes e_2)]|x|^2=0$. P1 pp. 148–150, (3.110)–(3.115) and (3.125), first sums the derivatives over centres and then identifies the relative-pair second moment. Thus `Def6.momentSum` is wrong as written: it differentiates `Eloc ... 0`, although its formula needs `Edens`.

**Exact amendment.** In `Def6.lean`, change only the function inside `momentSum` from `Eloc m₀ Tm j 0 ψ h₀` to `Edens m₀ Tm j ψ h₀`, with the same block directions, reference field, and minimal torus representative. The condition is the limit as torus circumference tends to infinity; it is not an exact finite-torus zero. Equivalently, after lifting the non-winding terms and using full translation covariance, define $B_0(u,v)$ as the transverse Hessian of $\mathscr E(0)$ and $K(z)=\sum_y\partial_{\psi_2(z)}\partial_{\psi_2(0)}\mathscr E(y)$. Absolute weighted convergence allows the change of variables
$$K(z)=\sum_y B_0(z-y,-y),\qquad
\sum_zK(z)|z|^2=\sum_{u,v}B_0(u,v)|u-v|^2.\tag{1}$$
This proves that the existing relative-pair `Premises.moment` and `SumConds.moment` are the right **infinite-volume** algebraic form after the density symmetry and limit bridges; the defect is the Def-6 finite-torus definition, not that pair formula. Full point-group covariance of $\mathscr E(0)$ makes $S_{\mu\nu}=\sum_{u,v}B_0(u,v)(u-v)_\mu(u-v)_\nu$ diagonal by reflections and equal on the diagonal by coordinate swaps. Equation (1) and (H.5)(4) give $\operatorname{tr}S=0$, hence $S=0$. This is the isotropy used in `SMALLFIELD.md` §2.3 Step 5; it is a conclusion from density (H.3) and (H.5), not an independent renormalization condition at every centre. Translation covariance transfers it to every $y$.

**The complete amended (H.5).** On the infinite-volume family at each scale $j$, with $e=e_1$ and $b=\nu_jh$, write $V_j(\psi,h)=\mathscr E_j(y;\text{constant }\psi,h)$; (H.3-E) makes it independent of $y$. The four scalar equations are
$$V_j(e,e)=0,\qquad \partial_hV_j(e,e)\cdot e=0,\qquad
\sum_z\partial_{\psi_1(z)}\mathscr E_j(y;e,e)=0\ (\text{every }y),\qquad
\sum_z\partial_{\psi_2(z)}\partial_{\psi_2(0)}\mathscr E_j(e,e)\,|z|^2=0.\tag{H.5}$$
The last derivative is of the total action and is understood through its thermodynamic kernel, P4 (1.3); any fixed transverse component may replace $2$ by internal $O(N)$ invariance (P4 p. 680). The second equation is $\nu_j\partial_bV_j\cdot e=0$, so division by $\nu_j$ in `RenormCond.dh` is valid only for $\nu_j>0$. The third equation at one centre is equivalent to every centre by (H.3-E). P1 p. 118, (H.6), phrases the first three on the finite torus, while P4 pp. 677–678, (1.2) and (H.5), explicitly introduces the thermodynamic potential. The infinite-volume version is the common statement needed by I-6 and the version proved by the limit construction below.

## Q-C. Winding polymers and exact I-6 sum conditions

**Source evidence.** P4 p. 677, (H.4), says a term does not depend on the torus when $X$ does not wind around it. P4 p. 678, (1.2)–(1.3), explicitly takes thermodynamic limits for the effective potential and Hessian; (H.5)(4) is written in terms of that limit. P1 p. 119, (H.7), also has the torus limit. The printed source therefore does not require the lifted non-winding family to satisfy finite-torus exact sum conditions. P4 pp. 707–708, (5.1)–(5.5), uses the effective potential for the first conditions; p. 711, (5.14), uses the thermodynamic transverse second moment for the last.

**Choice: an infinite-volume family, obtained by stabilization.** For fixed finite $X\subset\mathbb Z^d$ and centre $y$, P4's torus-independence makes $\mathscr E_N(y,X)$ eventually constant as the torus side $R_N$ grows; define $\mathscr E_\infty(y,X)$ by this eventual value. If the implementation has only uniform analytic bounds rather than the stated torus-independence, a diagonal Montel subsequence on each finite coordinate set gives an alternative, but compatibility and equality of the limiting density must then be proved. Use the source's stronger stabilization. `T∞.polys` consists of the admissible finite non-winding lifts; `T∞.term` is the eventual term, and it inherits locality, analyticity, internal rotation invariance, and the weighted exponential bound. There is no fixed-torus exact `SumConds` assertion.

Here is the tail proof needed for the bridge. A winding polymer, or one whose fixed lift is too large for the fundamental domain, has covering length at least $cR_N/M$ in level-$j$ units. If the number of centred polymers of size in $[t,t+1)$ is at most $C e^{\kappa_0t}$ and $\kappa>\kappa_0$, then for each derivative order $q\le2$ and each moment weight of polynomial degree $p\le2$, Cauchy estimates on one common small analytic neighbourhood give
$$\sum_{X:\,d_j(X)\ge cR_N/M}(1+d_j(X))^p\|D^q\mathscr E_N(y,X)\|
 \le C_{p,q}E_0 r^{-q}e^{-c'(\kappa-\kappa_0)R_N/M}.\tag{2}$$
The same estimate bounds the discrepancy from large non-winding polymers. On each fixed finite set the summands stabilize, while (2) controls all omitted summands uniformly. Dominated convergence therefore commutes the torus limit with the sums and derivatives through order two, including the $|x|^2$ weight. The finite-volume complete sums, if the class has their exact vanishing, converge to exact infinite-volume `SumConds`; if only P4's thermodynamic (H.5) is assumed, they are exact by that assumption directly. Either way the winding error vanishes and no finite-volume false equality is inserted.

**Lean-level change.** Replace `Def6.RenormCond`'s torus-indexed `val`, `dh`, `dpsi`, and `moment` by their precise thermodynamic forms on an `InfiniteTerms`/`T∞` object, or retain stronger finite-volume first-three fields as optional data while making the class-facing (H.5) the infinite-volume version. In particular the landed `IsSFFamily.renorm` must imply `SumConds T∞` via the stabilization and tail lemmas; it does not imply `SumConds (liftTF ... (Tm N₁))` for a fixed `N₁`. `Def6BridgeLift.liftTF`, `Def6BridgeGeom.premisesCore`, and `PremisesTerm.toPremises` must be redirected to the infinite family (then to its symmetry average for the strong `RenormIso` route). This uses the existing `NonWind` predicate and P4 (H.4), not a new assumption that winding terms vanish.

## Q-D. The $h'$ inequalities and the I-6 analytic radius

**Exact print.** P3 p. 73, (4.25), gives the real background domain; p. 74, (4.26), gives the complex perturbation inequalities. With $s_n=L^n\xi$ and $\sigma_y=\sigma(\mathbb B_j;y)$, its last two relevant inequalities are
$$|h'(y)|<\nu^{-1}\sigma_y^2s_n^{-2}\varepsilon,
\qquad |\phi(x)\cdot h'(y)|<\nu^{-1}\sigma_y^2s_n^{-2}\varepsilon^2,
\quad x\in\Delta_n(y),\ n\le j.\tag{3}$$
The preceding same-line inequalities bound $|\phi'(x)|$ and its first and second differences. P3 p. 74, (4.28), defines the localized complex $\Xi^c$ space using (4.26). P4 p. 677, (H.4), **refers** to this P3 space for term analyticity and bounds; it does not reprint (3). Thus `XiCBounds.hAbs` ($\sigma^2\varepsilon$) and `XiCBounds.hDot` ($\sigma^2\varepsilon^2$) in `Xi.lean` have the printed powers. The quadratic longitudinal condition is not a scan error and must remain.

**Radius ruling.** At the constant reference background $\phi=e_1$, $\sigma=1$, and $b=\nu_jh$, (3) permits a general $b$ perturbation of size $O(\varepsilon_j)$ but its component along $e_1$ only of size $O(\varepsilon_j^2)$. Hence an **isotropic** `Tube i₀ ν ρ X` around `(e₁,ν_je₁)` can lie in `XiDom` in the massive branch only for $\rho\lesssim\varepsilon_j^2$; `Def6BridgeDomain.xiDom_of_close` and `Def6BridgeLift.liftRadius` reflect exactly this. When the background mass is zero, the $h'$ restrictions vanish and this particular obstruction disappears. The landed `RenormTermwiseIrrel.termwise_irrelevant` bound is $CE_0e^{-(\kappa-1)d_j(X)}\sigma^{3-\alpha_1}(\varepsilon_k/\rho)^2$ and `termwise_irrelevant_scaled` needs $\varepsilon_k/\rho\lesssim s_{jk}^{(d-2)/2-\gamma}$. The source scale estimate is $\varepsilon_k/\varepsilon_j\lesssim s_{jk}^{(d-2)/2-\gamma}$. Substituting $\rho\asymp\varepsilon_j^2$ costs $\varepsilon_j^{-2}$ in the squared factor, a scale-dependent loss. The present isotropic Tube proof therefore does **not** establish I-6 uniformly for massive fields.

**Exact I-6 interface amendment.** In `RenormDefs.Premises` and `RenormTermwiseDefs.PremisesTerm`, remove the scalar `ρ` as the sole analytic radius and replace `analytic`, `bdd_tube`, and `rot_inv` on `Tube i₀ ν ρ X` by `analyticΩ`, `bddΩ`, and `rot_invΩ` on the packed localized `XiDom` for each $X$ (the current `PremisesTerm.ofDomain` already exhibits the relevant domain parameterization). Keep `bdd_dom` for the evaluation domain. Pass the scale radius $\varepsilon_j$ separately to I-6's analytic estimates. Its spin-slice and invariant-coordinate inclusion lemmas must be proved from `XiC` and the class generating-set geometry, rather than inserted as new class fields. The replacement theorem `termwise_irrelevant` should conclude a bound with $(\varepsilon_k/\varepsilon_j)^2$ under those proved inclusions; `termwise_irrelevant_scaled` should use the source ratio $\varepsilon_k/\varepsilon_j$. This is a change to the analytic proof, not to `XiCBounds.hDot`.

**Repair and proof route.** Keep the correct `XiC` clause and use `XiDom` in the I-6 analytic fields as specified above. Derive from it the anisotropic probes required by the proof: spin interpolation radius $c\varepsilon_j$ at fixed $h$; longitudinal $b$ radius $c\varepsilon_j^2$; and the invariant-coordinate polydisc of P1 pp. 127–129, (3.29)–(3.31), (3.37)–(3.45). The latter controls $U-1$, $V-1$, and $W^2$, with radii printed in (3.31), and is the domain used in P1's proof; it is not an isotropic ball in $(\psi,b)$. P1 pp. 123–124, (3.14)–(3.22), states the necessary domain chain, citing a background inclusion established in the following paper; P3 p. 74, (4.28)–(4.29), gives the localized domain and a generating-set inclusion. The bridge must prove the resulting local probe inclusion for `gs j X` explicitly. Cauchy differentiation in the varying **spin** coordinates then costs $\varepsilon_j^{-q}$, $q=1,2,3$. Derivatives involving $b$ must instead be estimated in the thin invariant coordinates: P1 (3.30)–(3.36) handles the potential, and pp. 146–147, (3.104)–(3.108), handles the mixed derivatives in the localized remainder. The Ward identity P1 p. 151, (3.122)–(3.125), disposes of the transverse marginal coefficient. P1 Proposition 3.10, pp. 152–153, and Corollary 3.11, p. 153, (3.126)–(3.131), then give the desired $\sigma^{3-\alpha_1}(\varepsilon_k/\varepsilon_j)^2$ estimate and the uniform global bound. In Lean this requires replacing the isotropic Cauchy lemmas in `RenormSmall`, `RenormIso`, and `RenormTermwiseIrrel` by spin-slice and invariant-coordinate lemmas; changing only `liftRadius` cannot work. Proving those domain inclusions and Cauchy estimates is an outstanding formal proof task, even though the printed source supplies the mathematical argument.

For completeness, the corrected per-centre argument can be run on the averaged family of Q-A: termwise $O(N)$ invariance gives the Ward identity, full point-group covariance makes its radial first-derivative kernel even and its relative-pair tensor isotropic, and (1) plus (H.5)(4) makes that tensor zero. The radial first-order pieces cancel on summing $y$ by interchanging $x$ and $y$. The P1 analytic estimates bound the remaining term by $CE_0\sigma^{3-\alpha_1}(\varepsilon_k/\varepsilon_j)^2$ per centre. With $s=L^{-(k-j)}$, $\varepsilon_k/\varepsilon_j\le Cs^{(d-2)/2-\gamma}$, and $\alpha_3=1-2\gamma-\alpha_1>0$, this is at most $CE_0s^{d+\alpha_3}$. Since $|\Lambda_j|=s^{-d}|T_1^{(k)}|$, the old-scale sum is bounded by $CE_0|T_1^{(k)}|\sum_{n\ge n_1}L^{-n\alpha_3}$; the last $n_1$ scales use the crude localization bound. This is the uniform conclusion (3.131), and shows explicitly why both the symmetry repair and the $\varepsilon_j$ spin radius are needed.

## Q-E. Connectivity of components of the hole

**Source evidence and ruling.** P1 p. 117, (2.26), defines $\mathcal D_j$ as connected unions of localization cubes. P5 p. 496, (1.33), says exactly that every **connected component** of $\Omega^c$ is either contained in $X$ or disjoint from it; it does not define a separate $*$-adjacency there. P6 p. 44, proof of Proposition 3.1 [P5], continues to use $\mathcal D_{k+1}(\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega^c)$ and defines connectedness of two such domains through intersection of their parts in $\Omega_{k+1}$; it does not replace the components in (1.33) by diagonal adjacency. The consistent discrete interpretation for the P5/P6 domains is nearest-neighbour (face) connectivity of the site/cube graph. This is an interpretation of the unqualified word “component” in (1.33), supported by the connected-domain convention and by the support argument in `FLUCTUATION.md` §1.2 (G4); the papers do not state a separate graph formula for it.

There is also a direct mathematical test. In two dimensions take two sites of $\Omega^c$ touching diagonally but not by a nearest-neighbour edge, with one adjacent to $S\subset\Omega$ and the other not. A $*$-component joins both; then $\widehat Y(S)$ contains a disconnected appendage, so `dOmega S = ∞` in `SizeModDefs.lean`, while a connected $Y$ meeting $\Omega$ in $S$ has finite size. This refutes (G4) for that mix of $*$-hole components and nearest-neighbour polymers. With nearest-neighbour components, every component added to $\widehat Y(S)$ has an actual edge to $S$ and $\widehat Y(S)$ is connected whenever $S$ is connected.

**Lean-level change.** Keep `Fluctuation.SizeModDefs.OmegaComp` as nearest-neighbour reachability. In `LargeField/Geometry/DomainsMod.lean`, define `nnComp Ωᶜ a` using `NNAdj`, and use it in `DmodOmega` instead of `starComp`. Do **not** delete `starComp`: other large-field boundary and escape lemmas use it as a distinct auxiliary notion. Reprove the affected `DmodOmega` closure, support, and comparison lemmas, then a bridge showing that the large-field and fluctuation predicates agree under the common cube/site identification. Any theorem genuinely needing $*$-components must say so under a separate name; it cannot be cited as P5 (1.33). The diagonal example is the refutation check for this ruling.

## Q-F. Margins of the generating-set construction

**Source evidence.** P3 p. 69, (4.7), constructs $X_0$ so that $\Omega_1(X)\subset X_0$. The same page, (4.8), chooses $X_1$ with the $r_0$-neighbourhood of $X_0$ contained in $X_1$ and $X_1\subset\Omega_k$. These are steps in constructing the localization expansion from an ambient cover. P4 p. 677, (H.4), asks only for a suitable generating set $\mathbb B_j(X)$, its local complex domain, locality, invariance, and bound; it does not put an $X_0,X_1$ margin axiom into the small-field class.

**Ruling and Lean-level statement.** `Def6.Terms.gs` and `IsSF.gs_spec` should continue to record the generating set used by the class analytic domain, without adding `X₀` or `X₁` fields. Put the margin data and proof at the theorem constructing the next localization expansion (P3 (4.7)–(4.8)), with `X₁` at the next cover level and the explicit `j+1≤K` index condition already present in `Localization/TreeLengthCov.lean` `CovSetup`. The proof obligation there is $\Omega_1(X)\subset X_0$, $N_{r_0}(X_0)\subset X_1\subset\Omega_K$, and compatibility of $\mathbb B_j(X)$ with that construction. It is not a new Def-6 hypothesis, and no `RenormDefs` or `RenormTermwiseDefs` premise changes for Q-F.

## Dependency order for a Lean repair

First correct `Def6.momentSum` and the two symmetry groups; introduce density (H.3) and the infinite-volume stabilized family. Next prove the finite offset averaging and transfer (H.5) to its centred family. Then replace I-6's isotropic Tube argument by the P1/P3 analytic-domain route. Finally align the two modular-domain predicates and keep the P3 margins in localization. Until those proofs land, `IsSFFamily.premisesCore` is a partial bridge, and the existing `PremisesTerm.toPremises` must not be applied to a finite-torus `liftTF` using unproved symmetry or exact sum conditions.
