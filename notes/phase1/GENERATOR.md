# Generators for the small-field terms and their large-field inheritance

12 October 2026. Status words refer to mathematical deductions or to the cited landed Lean declarations, not to a completed RG induction. All Balaban sources and Lean files mentioned below were read without modification.

## Decision

**PROVED:** the landed `IsSF2` class admits a whole-level generator `levelGS j` for every polymer, but this does not give the local data support needed by the proposed history transfer. **PROVED:** Balaban's P3 output construction assigns a generator to the *new polymer* and establishes analyticity on that generator's domain by replacing the generator inside the pre-resummation formula. P5's large-field description then localizes inherited terms and defines boundary-term domains by the local region and its layers. Thus the global-generator class is a possible weaker formal interface for the small-field rate, while the local-generator class is the source-faithful interface for the large-field step.

**REFUTED as a logical shortcut:** sharp locality of a function on the global admissible domain, even with a uniform bound there, does not imply holomorphy on a larger local admissible domain. The zero-extension example only rules out that particular extension; it does not prove that no admissible global extension exists for each local datum. **OPEN for the actual `XiC` domains:** whether a quantitative extension/section theorem plus equality of the global and local physical formulas could make a mixed construction work. That theorem would carry essentially the local chart and domain work, so option (M) currently removes no justified obligation.

## 1. What the papers put on each set

**PROVED, P1 scan:** P1 p. 113, (H.2), defines the inherited $\mathcal E^{(j)}(y;\psi_j,\nu_jh)$ on the whole scale-$j$ lattice. P1 p. 117, (H.5), says the localized $\mathcal E^{(j)}(y,X)$ extends analytically on $\Xi_j^c(1,\varepsilon_j)$ and its extension depends on $(\psi_j+\psi'_j,h+h')$ restricted to $X$. This is a **whole-lattice analytic domain with a local dependence clause**; (H.5) alone does not name the later P3 generator $\mathbb B_j(X)$. P1 p. 116, (H.4), states the symmetries on the corresponding complex domain. Thus it would overstate P1 to say that its defining sentence already specifies the minimal generator of each term.

**PROVED, P3 scans:** P3 p. 68, (4.4), begins the minimal-layer construction using $M_1$ cubes. P3 p. 78, after (4.45), defines $\mathbb B_{k+1}(Y)$ with top layer $\Omega'_{k+1}=Y\cap\Omega_{k+1}$; for $n\le k$, $\Omega'_n$ is the thickened next layer joined to $Y\cap\Omega_n\cap\Omega_{n+1}^{c}$, with the original layer intersections retained. **PROVED, P5 scans:** P5 p. 489, (1.4), takes the background generator $\mathbb B_k(W_k)$, $W_k=(Z_k^c)^{\approx2}$, while (1.8) restricts the inherited $\mathcal E^{(j)}(y,X)$ sum to $y\in Z_k^c$, $X\subset(Z_k^c)^\approx$. It is also used in P5's large-field induction: (1.8), (1.17) and (1.36) restrict inherited and boundary terms to regional support; (1.24)–(1.25), p. 494, control only specified lineage/boundary cubes and fluctuation sites, not the whole torus; and P5 p. 508, (2.31)–(2.32), explicitly separates the part of $\Omega_1(W_k)$ outside the integration region as boundary terms. **OPEN:** a theorem that those particular characteristic factors supply strict complex-domain slack at every datum site and collar bond of every inherited term; `Q13-TRANSFER.md` identifies the missing cover and reference estimates.

## 3. The re-localization lemma (M)

**REFUTED as a general implication.** Let a global domain be $U=\{|z|<1\}$, a local domain $V=\{|z|<2\}$, and let $f(z)=1/(z-3/2)$. Then $f$ is holomorphic and bounded by $2$ on $U$ and depends only on its single local coordinate, but it has no holomorphic continuation to $V$. Adding unused remote coordinates makes its locality as sharp as desired. Therefore no theorem using only global holomorphy, a global bound, and `PhysicalSharpLoc` can conclude local-domain analyticity. To apply this obstruction literally to `XiC`, one would have to prove that the image of global admissible restrictions omits an open part of the local `XiC` domain; this has **not** been proved here. The example refutes the *proposed inference*, not the existence of some special physical extension.

**PROVED:** `BgData G` is zero off `dataSites G` (`Background/Data.lean:118`), while `XiC` additionally quantifies over real and complex solutions and their bounds (`SmallField/Xi.lean:126–170`). Extending a local datum by zero across a larger generator can fail the global R2 unit-length test on the new sites (`Background/Data.lean:265`) and changes the variational problem. This shows that zero extension is no section of the domain map. **OPEN:** an alternative extension with uniform strict slack, analytic local sections, and equality of solutions on the live reads. P3 pp. 78–79 avoids assuming such a section by recalculating each term with $\mathbb B(Y)$, then applying Lemma 4.2 and Cauchy formulas. Its comparison is on the common physical data, whereas the local analytic estimate is proved directly on the local domain.

**PROVED CONDITIONAL ON a quantitative domain section and formula comparison:** if every local datum has a neighbourhood in $\Xi^c(B_j(X))$ with a holomorphic section into the global domain, the section preserves the live $X$ data, and the physical term agrees with a function of those data on overlaps, composition gives a local holomorphic term. A uniform bound also transfers if the global section stays in the bounded global tube. This is a precise possible M theorem; `LocalDomain-Y`, `IndexMatch-Y`, `CrossGreen-Y` and replacement (R) are still **OPEN** and are the named route to its hypotheses.
