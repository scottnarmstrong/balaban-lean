## 1. Localization (GM2)

**Verdict.** The added `GM2Setup.contains` assertion is false for P3's $X_1$. P3 (4.2), p. 68, defines $X_1$ as the *union of all* coarse cover cubes $Q\in\pi'_{k+1}$ that contain a fine cover cube $q\in\pi'_j$ with $q\subset X$. It does not say that every other $Q\in\pi'_{k+1}$ subsequently contained in that union has this property. The latter is exactly the quantifier needed by the sentence in P3's proof of (4.48), p. 79, and by `TreeLength.lean`, `GM2Setup.contains`/`meets_Y`. P1 (2.26)–(2.27), p. 117, and P3 (1.2)–(1.3), p. 34, require a tree to meet **every** cover cube contained in the domain. The covers overlap: $\pi_n$ is the partition into cubes of side $M\ell_n$, while $\pi'_n$ consists of cubes of side $LM\ell_n$ translated on the $M\ell_n$ lattice. Here $\ell_n$ is the scale-$n$ lattice spacing. In particular, the cover is not a selected disjoint decomposition. P3 p. 68 explicitly distinguishes the RG size $M$ in (4.2) from the smaller localization size $M_1$ used in (4.4).

**Counterexample to the added assertion.** Take $L=3$, $j=k$, $\Omega_{k+1}$ equal to the whole torus, and a torus large enough to avoid wraparound. Measure coordinates in units of $M\ell_{k+1}$. A fine $\pi'_k$ cube has side $1$ and its lower corner lies in $(\frac13\mathbb Z)^d$; a coarse $\pi'_{k+1}$ cube has side $3$ and its lower corner lies in $\mathbb Z^d$. Let $Q=(0,3)^d$, $Q_- =(-1,2)\times(0,3)^{d-1}$, and $Q_+=(1,4)\times(0,3)^{d-1}$. Form a connected U-shaped $X$ from fine cubes with lower corners

$$(-2/3,t,1,\ldots,1),\quad(8/3,t,1,\ldots,1)\quad(t=0,1/3,\ldots,8/3),$$
$$ (t,8/3,1,\ldots,1)\quad(t=-2/3,-1/3,\ldots,8/3).$$

Adjacent cubes overlap, so $X\in\mathscr D_k$. Each of $Q_-$ and $Q_+$ contains a fine cube of $X$ (take $t=1$ on the corresponding vertical arm), hence both occur in (4.2). Their union contains $Q$, so $Q\subset X_1$. Yet $X\cap Q$ lies in the three strips $x_1<1/3$, $x_1>8/3$, or $x_2>8/3$; no axis-aligned cube of side $1$ contained in $Q$ fits in these strips. Thus $Q$ contains no fine $\pi'_k$ cube lying in $X$. The same construction works for every $d\ge3$ by the displayed fixed extra coordinates. It refutes the asserted cover property, not by itself the numerical inequality (GM2), which might admit another proof.

**Corrected statement for the original $\widetilde d$.** Let $\mathcal Q_0$ be the qualifying coarse cubes used in (4.2), and put
$$B(X_1)=\#\{Q\in\pi'_{k+1}:Q\subset X_1,\ Q\notin\mathcal Q_0\}.$$
With $G_0=(2L+1)^d$, the tree construction of P3 pp. 79–80 proves the following safe version of (GM2):
$$\widetilde d_{k+1}(Y[\mathcal C])\le L^{-1}d_j(X)+dG_0|\mathcal C|+2dL\,B(X_1).\tag{GM2-corrected}$$
Indeed, choose a tree $\Gamma_0\subset X$ within $\varepsilon$ of $d_j(X)$. It meets every $Q\in\mathcal Q_0$ because it meets the fine cube $q\subset Q\cap X$. P3's construction attaches the cubes generated by $\mathcal C$ at scaled cost at most $dG_0|\mathcal C|$. For each remaining coarse $Q\subset X_1$, choose a point in $Q$ lying in some $Q_0\in\mathcal Q_0$ (the latter cover $X_1$), and join it inside the convex cube $Q_0\subset X_1$ to $\Gamma_0\cap Q_0$. The scaled cost is at most $dL$, hence certainly $2dL$. This hits $Q$ and stays in $Y[\mathcal C]$. Prune cycles and let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The extra term cannot be charged to $|\mathcal C|$ when $\mathcal C=\varnothing$.

**Minimal definition repair preserving the advertised bound.** For a domain $Y$, replace $\widetilde d_n(Y)$ by $\widetilde d_n^{\mathrm{cov}}(Y)$: take the infimum of tree length jointly over the tree and over a *chosen subfamily* of the cubes of each $\pi'_r$ whose union is $Y\cap\Lambda_r$, and require the tree to meet every cube of that chosen family. Keep $d_j(X)$ as in P1 (2.27). In P3's proof choose $\mathcal Q_0$ for $X_1$ and the cover cubes used to form the sets $D(P)$ in P3 (4.44)–(4.45), pp. 78–79. These families cover $Y[\mathcal C]$; $\Gamma_0$ meets $\mathcal Q_0$, and the attached center trees meet the added cubes. The length calculation on pp. 79–80 then gives exactly
$$\widetilde d_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cov}}(Y[\mathcal C])\le L^{-1}d_j(X)+d(2L+1)^d|\mathcal C|.$$
This changes the size functional in P3 (1.3) (and the analogous P1 (2.27) convention if used uniformly); it is not a proof of (GM2) for Balaban's printed $\widetilde d$. **Confidence:** high for the counterexample and quantifier mismatch; moderate for the numerical correction, which retains P3's cited center-tree length calculation at layer interfaces.

## 2. Small field (R-$\nu$)

**Verdict.** The full §3 system is not unchanged. For the $\nu$-free backgrounds of P5 (1.9), p. 490, the $b$-form of (E2) has the form written in `SMALLFIELD.md`, and $a_{22}=1$ remains exact, but its $f_u,c_2$ are newly computed with the $\nu$-term treated as a perturbation. The transversal equation (E3), its Green operator, and its other matrix entries change. The $a_{21}=0$ entry is also correct for both conventions at $\lambda=\infty$: P4 (5.21), p. 714, prints $a_{21}=\varepsilon_{k+1}a/(\lambda+a+\nu_{k+1})$, which tends to zero.

Here is the constant-field calculation. Write $m=\nu_{k+1}$ for the mass coefficient, $h=t e_1$, $\psi=u e_1$, $\beta=\beta_{k+1}$, and $\zeta,b,c,e$ for P4's renormalization constants. In the $\nu$-free variational problem at $\lambda=\infty$, the local solution near $u=t=1$ is
$$\phi_0(u e_1,t e_1)=e_1,\qquad \alpha_0=a(u-1),$$
because the constraint is $|\phi|=1$ and the Euler equation contains no $m$ or $h$. P5 p. 490 says explicitly that $m$ remains in the action as a perturbation; P5 (2.23)–(2.24), pp. 506–507, displays its extra linear and quadratic fluctuation terms. Re-evaluating the first-order counterterm of P4 (4.14), p. 703, with this $\phi_0$ gives, at $(u,t)=(1,1)$,
$$\partial_u c_1=(a+\beta^{-1}c)(\zeta+\zeta^2),\qquad
\partial_t c_1=-m\beta^{-1}e.\tag{1}$$
The $\zeta$ contribution to $\partial_t c_1$ vanishes because $\partial_t\phi_0=0$; the explicit mass counterterm supplies the $e$ derivative. P4's full-background (5.4), p. 708, has a $\zeta$ contribution proportional to $1/(\lambda+a+m)$, which also vanishes in the $\lambda=\infty$ limit. Consequently the first two normalization conditions are
$$\partial_u f_u(1,1,0)-\beta(a+\beta^{-1}c)(\zeta+\zeta^2)+\beta\partial_u c_2(1,1,0)=0,\tag{E1-free}$$
$$\partial_{\mathbf b}f_u\cdot e_1+e+\beta\partial_{\mathbf b}c_2\cdot e_1=0,\qquad \mathbf b=mh.\tag{E2-free}$$
The derivatives in (E2-free) are evaluated at $\psi=h=e_1$, with $\mathbf b=m e_1$. For $m>0$ this is P4's $t$ equation divided by $m$; its analytic $\mathbf b$ form is the appropriate one at $m=0$. With $\xi_2=e/\beta$, division by $\beta$ gives the coefficient $a_{22}=1$, and no $\zeta$ coefficient: $a_{21}=0$. These two explicit matrix entries agree with P4's $\lambda=\infty$ limit. In (E1-free) the displayed algebraic form also survives that limit, but $f_u,c_2$ are different functions.

For completeness, the change to the third equation can be seen without guessing its coefficients. Take P4's free-flow supplementary choice $c=ab$, so the unrenormalized and renormalized block coefficient is the same $a$; put $z=1+\zeta$ and
$$G_z=(-\Delta+aQ^*Q+a(z-1))^{-1},\quad K_z=I-aQG_zQ^*,\quad B_z=a^2z^2QG_z^2Q^*.$$
At the constant field $z e_1$, the $\nu$-free minimizer has transverse first variation $azG_zQ^*\theta$. Expanding the sphere constraint gives longitudinal second variation $-\tfrac12|azG_zQ^*\theta|^2e_1$. Hence the quadratic transverse part of the minimized $\nu$-free action is $\tfrac12 a z^2\langle\theta,K_z\theta\rangle$, while the explicit mass term $m(1-h\cdot\phi_0)$ contributes $\tfrac12m\langle\theta,B_z\theta\rangle$ at $h=e_1$. Using $\beta_u=\beta+b$ and $\beta_um_u=\beta m+me$, the quadratic main-action counterterm is therefore
$$\frac12\left\langle\theta,\left[(\beta+b)a z^2K_z+(\beta m+me)B_z-\beta aK_1-\beta mB_1\right]\theta\right\rangle.\tag{2}$$
Its linear part in the renormalization constants is
$$\frac12\langle\theta,\{\beta\zeta[2aK_1+a^3QG_1^2Q^*+2mB_1-2ma^3QG_1^3Q^*]+baK_1+meB_1\}\theta\rangle.\tag{3}$$
Apply the same transversal second-moment functional used in P4 (5.11)–(5.14), pp. 710–711, to (3), then add the second moment of the newly computed fluctuation term and of $C_2$, to obtain the replacement for (E3). Formula (3) shows both changes: $G_1=(-\Delta+aQ^*Q)^{-1}$ rather than P4's $(-\Delta+aQ^*Q+m)^{-1}$, and an explicit $m\zeta$ term. For example, retaining P4's printed $\tau=1-L^{-2}\eta^2$ notation, the explicit $e$ coefficient in the divided triangular system is $a_{32}^{\rm free}=\frac ma(-1+\frac a{24}\tau)$: it is the $m$ prefactor from the mass counterterm times the zero-propagator-mass limit of P4 (5.21). P4's full-background $a_{32}$ has denominator $(a+m)^3$ and the functions $c_i(m)$. In the same free-flow choice, the explicit $b$ coefficient is $a_{33}^{\rm free}=1/2$, the $m=0$ value in P4 (5.21). Thus the $m$-dependent entries in `SMALLFIELD.md` (5.21) cannot be copied to this convention. At $m=0$, (3) reduces to P4's $m\downarrow0$ transversal formula, as a check. At the P5 switch from incomplete to complete configurations (the $k_0\to k_0+1$ step, p. 490), the two conventions must be compared in the renormalization step; it is not covered by a calculation assuming both backgrounds are $\nu$-free. **Confidence:** high for (1), (E2-free), $a_{21}=0$, and $a_{22}=1$; moderate for the compact transversal formula (2)–(3), whose full localized $f_u,C_2$ bounds are outside this local check.
