**What the paper does.** Four-dimensional pure lattice gauge theory (compact semisimple $G\subset U(N)$, Wilson action) is renormalized by iterated block-averaging transformations $\mathbf T_k$ restricted to small fields. Assuming the previous effective action has a certain inductive form (background-field main term with running coupling $g_k$, plus a sum over RG steps $j\le k$ of localized "$\mathbf E^{(j)}$" terms, analytic on complex gauge configurations, gauge and Euclidean invariant, exponentially decaying in the tree length of the localization domain), the paper shows how one more step regenerates this form, except for the *localization* of the newest term $\mathbf E^{(k+1)}$ (its representation as a sum over domains with bound (1.18)), which is deferred to the sequel (E7 = CMP 116, "II. Cluster expansions"; stated p. 298: "In the next paper we will construct such localizations and prove the bounds... used to construct a cluster expansion of the integral in (2.13), and to finish the proof of the inductive assumptions"). What is carried out here is: the gauge-fixed formalism and change of variables (§2), a first localization/analytic extension of the difference $\mathbf E_k(U_k(\exp iB'V^{(k)}))-\mathbf E_k(U_k(V^{(k)}))$ via a partition of unity (§3), the extraction by Ward–Takahashi identities of the only non-irrelevant terms (the quadratic one, the vacuum polarization tensor $\Pi$) and the exact cancellation of these against the coupling renormalization $\beta_j(g_{j-1})A$ (§4), and the analysis of $\Pi$ by symmetry and Laurent expansion that defines $\beta_j$ as $-\partial_{p_1}\partial_{p_2}\hat\Pi_{12}(0)$ (§5). The perturbative asymptotics of the coupling constants (Theorem 2) is stated and deferred to a separate paper.

## 1. Setting and notation

### 1.1 Geometry (pp. 251–253)

- Lattice $\subset\mathbb R^d$; here $d=4$ (the paper says all formulas are written for $d=4$; $d=3$ is covered by the same proof, p. 259). Torus $T_\varepsilon=\{x\in\varepsilon\mathbb Z^d+\sum_\mu\frac12\varepsilon e_\mu:-L_\mu<x_\mu<L_\mu\}$ (0.1), with $L_\mu=L^{m}$-type side lengths, **$L$ odd integer $>11$**, $m$ positive integer; lattice spacing $\varepsilon=L^{-K}$. Tori $T^{(k)}_{L^k\varepsilon}$ for $k=1,2,\dots$ (spacing $L^k\varepsilon$), the sequence of coarser lattices; $T^{(k)}_1$ denotes the lattice after rescaling to unit spacing ("unit lattice"). Points $y\in T^{(k)}$ are centres of cubes $\Delta(y)$ of size $L^k\varepsilon$. Block of order $k$: $B^k(y)=\Delta(y)\cap T^{(n-k)}_{L^{-k}\delta}$ (0.3). Sets of lattice points, bonds $b=\langle b_-,b_+\rangle$, plaquettes are identified with their continuum cubes.
- **Localization cubes and domains (p. 257).** Fix $j\ge0$, $\xi=L^{-j}$, rescale so that $T_\eta$ is the lattice. Decompose space into closed cubes of size $M=L^m$ with centres at points of $T^{(j+m)}_M$; the family is $\pi_j$. $M\gg$ the scale $M_0$ (exponential decay of basic propagators) and $M_1$ (variational problem). For a cube $\square$, $\tilde\square^n$ is the concentric cube of size $(1+2n)M$. A *localization domain* $X$ is a union of cubes of $\pi_j$ forming a connected family (consecutive cubes share a $(d-1)$-dimensional wall). Class $\mathbf D_j$. Tree length $d_j(X)$: the length of the shortest tree graph contained in $X$ and meeting every cube of $X$, divided by $M$ (so lengths measured in the $\xi$-scale where cubes of $\pi_j$ are unit cubes). Equivalent definition via shortest trees formed by cube edges (p. 257).

### 1.2 Fields, averaging, gauge fixing (pp. 251–255)

- Gauge fields $U_b=U(\langle x,x'\rangle)\in G$ on bonds; complexification $G^c$, $\mathbf U=U'U$, $U=\exp iA$... precisely $\mathbf U=U'U$ with $U\in G$, $U'=\exp iA'$, $A'\in\mathfrak g^c$ (p. 252). Wilson action (0.2): $A^\varepsilon(U)=\sum_{p\in T_\varepsilon}\varepsilon^{d-4}[1-\mathrm{Re\,tr}\,U(\partial p)]$, tr normalized, $\mathrm{tr}\,1=1$. $\partial U(p)$ is the plaquette variable. (Also the principal chiral $\sigma$-model (0.32) $A(U)=\sum_{b}[1-\mathrm{Re\,tr}\,U(\partial b)]$, $U(\partial b)=(\partial U)(b)=U(b_-)U^{-1}(b_+)$, p. 260: all results hold for it, "technically much easier".)
- **Contours and averages.** For $x\in B(y)$ a family $\mathbf G(y,x)$ of shortest contours (all permutations of coordinate directions), $|n_\mu|\le (L-1)/2$ (p. 252). The averaging operation of [12] (CMP 98) is modified to be Euclidean symmetric. Group-valued averages $M(\{\mathbf U_j\})$ axiomatized by (0.5)–(0.9) (inverse, left/right translation covariance, permutation invariance, $\frac1i\log M(\{\exp iA_j\})=\frac1n\sum A_j+\dots$, stays in $G$); Federbush's definition (0.10): $\sum_j\frac1i\log\mathbf U_j\mathbf U^{-1}=0$ (p. 253). The results are "valid universally for all averages satisfying the above properties". Averaged contour variable $U(y,x)=M(\{U(\Gamma)\}_{\Gamma\in\mathbf G(y,x)})$ (0.11). Averaged configuration $\bar U(c)$ on bonds $c\in T^{(1)}$ (0.4), (0.12).
- **Renormalization transformation** (0.13): $(\mathbf T\varrho)(V)=\int dU\,t(V,U)\varrho(U)$, $t$ gauge-invariant kernel (concretely a $\delta(\bar U(c)V^{-1}(c))$ for $c\in T^{(1)}$ times an exponential gauge-fixing factor). Restriction to small fields: $|U(\partial p)-1|<\varepsilon_0$ ($p\in T$), $|\bar U-V|<\varepsilon_0$.
- **Gauge fixing** (pp. 254–255): instead of $\delta$-functions $\delta(U(y,x))$ (non-Euclidean-invariant) the exponentials $\exp[-\frac1{2\alpha}|U(y,x)-1|^2]=\exp[-\frac1\alpha(1-\mathrm{Re\,tr}\,U(y,x))]$ (0.14), with the Faddeev–Popov identity (0.15) and result (0.16). Block axial gauge ($u=1$ on $T^{(1)}$) is used only to remove the gauge orbit; Euclidean covariance is preserved by using the contour averages $U(y,x)$ instead of contour products.
- **Actions** (0.17), (0.19):
$$A_{k+1}(g_{k+1},V)=(\mathbf T_kA_k)(g_{k+1},V)=\log\mathbf N_k^{-1}\!\int\! dU\prod_{c\in T^{(k+1)}}\delta(\bar U(c)V^{-1}(c))\,\chi_k\exp\Big[-\tfrac1{g_k^2}\!\!\sum_{y\in T^{(k+1)}}\sum_{x\in B(y),x\ne y}\![1-\mathrm{Re\,tr}\,U(y,x)]+A_k(g_k,U)\Big],$$
$\mathbf N_k$ = same integral with $V=1$; the term $\frac{1}{g^2_k}\mathbf G$ in (2.1) is the gauge fixing $\mathbf G(V)=\sum_{y}\sum_{x\in B(y),x\neq y}\frac1{2}|U(y,x)-1|^2$-type sum (see (2.5)). $A_0$ is the Wilson action with $g_0$.
- **Coupling constants**: $\frac1{g_k^2}=\frac1{g_{k+1}^2}+\beta_{k+1}(g_k)$ (0.20), i.e. $\frac1{g_{k+1}^2}=\frac1{g_k^2}-\beta_{k+1}(g_k)$ (0.18 for $k=0$); a finite-difference form of the Callan–Symanzik equation $\frac d{ds}(1/g^2)=-\beta(g)$, $\frac{dg}{ds}=\frac12\beta(g)g^3$ (p. 255). $\beta_k$'s are defined in (1.22) below. $K=\log_L(1/\varepsilon)$ is the last index (unit lattice reached).

### 1.3 Background fields and the form of the effective action (pp. 256, 260–261)

- Variational problem: for $V$ on $T^{(k)}_1$, the functional $U\mapsto A^\eta(U)$ on $\{U:\bar U^k=M^k(U)=V\}$ (0.21), (1.1); it is invariant under gauge transformations $u$ with $u=1$ on $T^{(k)}$, and "in [15] (= CMP 102 277) it was proved that in a space of regular orbits there exists exactly one critical orbit, which is a set of minima". The configuration in this orbit, in the **axial gauge**, is $U_k(V)$ (background field). Space $\mathscr U_k(\varepsilon_0)$ of $U$ with $|U(\partial p)-1|=|(\partial U)(p)-1|<\varepsilon_0\eta^2$, $\eta=L^{-k}$, and $|J|<\varepsilon_0$, $J=D^{\ast}_U\eta^{-2}\pi\,\mathrm{Im}\,\partial U$ (1.2) (the second condition is a bound on the "second-order derivative" of the configuration).
- $A_k(g_k,V)=A_k(g_k,U_k(V))=-\frac1{g_k^2}A^\eta(U_k(V))+\mathbf E_k(U_k(V))$ (0.22), and
$$\mathbf E_k(U_k)=\sum_{j=1}^k\big[-\beta_j(g_{j-1})A^\eta(U_k)+\mathbf E^{(j)}(U_k)\big] \quad(0.23),$$
the sum over the $k$ renormalization steps: each step contributed one function $\mathbf E^{(j)}$ of its own background $U_j$, now evaluated at the coarser-scale background $U_k$. Explicitly (1.3), (1.6) (**inductive hypothesis**):
$$A_k(U_k)=-\tfrac1{g_k^2}A(U_k)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big\{-\beta_{j+1}(g_j)A(U_k)+[\log Z^{(j)}(U_k)-\log Z^{(j)}(1)]+[\mathbf E^{(j+1)}(g_j,U_k)-\mathbf E^{(j+1)}(g_j,1)]\Big\},$$
with the Gaussian normalization (1.4) $Z^{(j)}(U_k)=\int dB\,\delta(\tilde QB)\exp[-\frac12\langle B,\Delta^{(j)}(U_k)B\rangle]$ and quadratic form (1.5) $\langle B,\Delta^{(j)}B\rangle=\langle H_{1,j}B,\Delta_1(U_k)H_{1,j}B\rangle-2\langle H_{1,j}h\tilde C^{(2)}(\bar U_k^j,B),J\rangle+\mathbf G^{(2)}(B)$ ($H_{1,j},\Delta_1$ from CMP 99 389 [13]; $\tilde C^{(2)}$ second order part of $\tilde Q(\cdot)$). The explicit terms are said to extend to $G^c$-valued configurations by [13].
- Vacuum energy: only one renormalization counterterm needed (vacuum energy, p. 258); it is absorbed in the normalization $\mathbf N$, so $\mathbf E^{(j)}(1)=0$.

### 1.4 Spaces of complex configurations (pp. 261–263, (1.9)–(1.16))

$\mathbf E^{(j)}(g_{j-1},U_j)=\sum_{X\in\mathbf D_j}\mathbf E^{(j)}(X,g_{j-1},U_j)$ (1.7), each term depending only on $U_j|_X$. To have a weak regularity requirement the dependence on the second derivative is separated into a second variable $\mathbf J$: $\mathbf E^{(j)}(X,g_{j-1},U_j)=\mathbf E^{(j)}(X,g_{j-1},U_j,J_j)$ (1.9), $J_j=D^{\ast}_{U_j}\xi^{-2}\pi\,\mathrm{Im}\,\partial U_j$ (1.8). The pair $(\mathbf U,\mathbf J)$ carries the gauge action $(\mathbf U,\mathbf J)^u=(\mathbf U^u,R(u)\mathbf J)=(u_-\mathbf Uu_+^{-1},R(u_-)\mathbf J)$ (1.10).

**Definition (space $\mathscr U^c_j(X,\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\gamma_0)$, p. 262).** A union of orbits $[(\mathbf U,\mathbf J)]$ of $G^c$-gauge transformations of pairs satisfying
- (i) $\mathbf U=U'U$, $U\in G$, $|\partial\mathbf U-1|<\alpha_0\xi^2$ on $X$ (1.11), and for each $O(1)LM$-cube $\square\subset X$ there is a $G$-valued gauge transformation $u$ on $\square$ with $U^u=\exp i\xi A$, $|A|,|\nabla^\xi A|<O(1)LMB\alpha_0$ on $\square$ ($B$ large, fixed later) (1.12);
- (ii) $U'=\exp i\xi A'$, $A'\in\mathfrak g^c$, $|A'|,|\nabla^\xi_{U}A'|<\alpha_1$ on $X$ (1.13);
- (iii) $|\partial\mathbf U-1|<\alpha_0\xi^2$, $|\mathbf J|<\gamma_0$ on $X$ (1.14);
- (iv) viewing $X$ as $\Omega_0$ and building the maximal-domain sequence $\{\Omega_n\}$ (conditions (1.3)–(1.6) of [14]), the pair of axial-gauge configurations $(U_n(M'(\mathbf U)),J_n(M'(\mathbf U)))$, $n=1,\dots,j$ satisfies the same bounds on $\tilde X^{-2}$ (1.15), (1.16), where $\tilde X^{-2}$ is $X$ minus two layers of $\pi_j$-cubes.
Usually $\gamma_0=\alpha_0$ and it is dropped from the notation. Remark (p. 263): configurations built from small-field real configurations belong to these spaces (1.17).

## 2. Main results

All three theorems are stated in §0–1; none is completely proved in this paper (see §7).

**Theorem 1 (p. 259), informal.** *If the sequence of effective coupling constants is contained in an interval $]0,\gamma]$ with a sufficiently small positive $\gamma$, then the effective actions for small fields are given by the formulas (0.22)–(0.24) with terms satisfying (0.29).*
"Small fields" here means $|\partial U_k(V)-1|<\varepsilon_0\eta^2$ on $T_\eta$, or equivalently $|\partial V-1|<\varepsilon_0$ on $T^{(k)}_1$. Valid for arbitrary semisimple compact $G\subset U(N)$, $d=4$ (also $d=3$). No asymptotic freedom assumed. The bound (0.29): $|\mathbf V^{(j)}(X,U_k)|\le O(1)(L^j\eta)^{4+\alpha}e^{-\kappa d_j(X)}$ for some $\alpha>0$, where $-\beta_j(g_{j-1})A^\eta(U_k)+\mathbf E^{(j)}(U_k)=\sum_{X\in\mathbf D_j}\mathbf V^{(j)}(X,U_k)$ (0.28). Consequence (0.30): $|\mathbf E_k(U_k)|\le O(1)(1-L^{-\alpha})^{-1}M^{-4}|T^{(k)}_1|$, i.e. uniform in $\varepsilon$ (ultraviolet stability): the only dependence on $k$ is through the volume of the current unit lattice.

**Theorem 2 (p. 259), statement only, proof deferred.** *Let $d=4$, $G=SU(2)$, $\gamma>0$ sufficiently small. Then for sufficiently small $g>0$ there is a bare coupling constant $g_0=g_0(\varepsilon,g)$ such that the sequence of effective couplings $g_k$ lies in $]0,\gamma]$ and $g_K=g$. Moreover there are constants $0<\beta\le\beta'$ with*
$$\frac1{g^2}+\beta\log(L^k\varepsilon)^{-1}\le\frac1{g_k^2}\le\frac1{g^2}+\beta'\log(L^k\varepsilon)^{-1}\quad(0.31).$$
Proof "based on perturbative calculations, will be given in a separate paper". The restriction to $SU(2)$ is "superficial" (p. 260). For $d=3$ no RG equation analysis needed, $g_k^2=g^2L^k\varepsilon$.

**Theorem 3 (p. 264) — the precise version of Theorem 1.** *There exist positive constants $\kappa_0,M(\kappa),\gamma,\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon_1,\alpha_0,\alpha_1$ such that if $\kappa\ge\kappa_0$, $M\ge M(\kappa)$, $0<g_k\le\gamma$ for $k=0,1,\dots,K$, then the sequence of actions $A_k$ defined inductively by the small field renormalization transformations (0.17)–(0.20) satisfies all inductive assumptions described between (1.1)–(1.22). The constants $\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon_1,\alpha_0,\alpha_1$ depend on $M$ and satisfy numerous restrictions that will become clear in the proof. The constant $\gamma$ depends on all other constants.*

Order of choice: $\kappa\ge\kappa_0$ first, then $M\ge M(\kappa)$ ($M\ge$ scales $M_0$, $M_1$, p. 257), then $\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon_1,\alpha_0,\alpha_1$ (depending on $M$; with $B$, $B_3,\dots$ of (1.12), §3), finally $\gamma$ small depending on everything (p. 264). $L$ is a fixed odd integer $>11$ (p. 251), $E_0$ appears in (1.18) as a constant of the inductive hypothesis.

**Inductive hypotheses (labelled in the text only by equation numbers).** What is assumed about $A_k$:
- (H-a) $A_k$ has the form (1.3)/(1.6) with $\beta$-functions $\beta_j$ defined by (1.20)–(1.22) (p. 260–261, 264).
- (H-b) $\mathbf E^{(j)}$ has the localized representation (1.7), terms analytic in $(U,J)$ on $\mathscr U^c_j(X,\alpha_0,\alpha_1)$, $C^\infty$ (or analytic) in $g_{j-1}\in[0,\gamma]$ (p. 263), with $|\mathbf E^{(j)}(X,g_{j-1},\mathbf U,\mathbf J)|\le E_0\exp(-\kappa d_j(X))$ (1.18), for $M\ge M(\kappa)$, $\gamma$ small.
- (H-c) Gauge invariance (1.19) under all $G^c$ transformations of pairs; Euclidean lattice invariance of the full action (p. 263).
- (H-d) Normalization $\mathbf E^{(j)}(1)=0$ (p. 258).
- (H-e) $\beta_{j+1}(g_j)$ is a smooth function on $[0,\gamma]$, uniformly bounded with all derivatives (p. 264; investigated only in a "separate paper").

## 3. Proof architecture

### §0 (pp. 249–260): setup and the shape of the answer
Defines the geometry, averaging, gauge fixing, transformations, the finite-difference Callan–Symanzik equation, the representation (0.22)–(0.24), the crude volume bound (0.26) (before renormalization $|\sum\mathbf E^{(j)}(U_k)|\le E_0O(1)M^{-4}|T^{(j)}_1|\eta^{-4}$ summed over $j$ gives a bound proportional to $|T_\eta|\cdot\sum_j (L^{-j})^{...}$, i.e. a divergence typical of the vacuum energy; explicit chain on p. 257), the mechanism of renormalization (p. 258): terms with $X$ not contained in a cube $\tilde\square$ of $\pi_k$ are exponentially small, $|\mathbf E^{(j)}(X,U_k)|\le E_0\exp(-\kappa(L^j\eta)^{-1})\exp(-\frac12\kappa d_j(X))$ (0.27), which beats any power of $L^j\eta$; terms with $X\subset\tilde\square$ are treated by writing $U_k=\exp i\eta\mathbf H$ (gauge-equivalent, local coordinates of Sect. F of [15]), expanding in $\mathbf H$ to fifth order, bounding the 5th-order term by $E_0O((L^j\eta)^5)e^{-\kappa d_j(X)}$ and treating the lower orders by Ward–Takahashi identities, which show they sum to $\beta A^\eta(\exp i\eta\mathbf H)$ plus irrelevant remainders. The coefficient $\beta_j(g_{j-1})$ is defined to cancel the marginal part. The terms satisfying (0.29) are called "irrelevant" (the author warns this is borrowed vocabulary: there is no scaling operation here, p. 259).

### §1 (pp. 260–264): inductive assumption and the statement
See §§1.3–1.4 above and §2. The nontrivial design choices: (a) the second variable $\mathbf J$ so that $\mathbf E^{(j)}$ needs only first-order regularity of the configuration; (b) the orbit-space $\mathscr U^c_j$, (iv) added because later sections substitute the axial-gauge functions $U_n(M'(\mathbf U))$ for the configurations; (c) $\mathbf E^{(j)}$ is gauge invariant for complex gauge transformations, which follows from real invariance by analyticity (p. 263; also (4.8)). $\beta_{j+1}$ defined by the second derivative of $\mathbf E^{(j+1)}$ at $B=0$: with $\mathbf E^{(j+1)}(g_j,B)=\mathbf E^{(j+1)}(g_j,U_{j+1}(\exp iB))$,
$$\Pi^{ab}_{j+1,\mu\nu}(g_j,x,x')=\Big(\tfrac{\delta^2}{\delta B^a_\mu(x)\delta B^b_\nu(x')}\mathbf E^{(j+1)}\Big)(g_j,0)=\delta^{ab}\Pi_{j+1,\mu\nu}(g_j,x-x')\quad(1.20)\text{–}(1.21),$$
$$\beta_{j+1}(g_j)=-\Big(\tfrac{\partial^2}{\partial p_1\partial p_2}\hat\Pi_{j+1,12}\Big)(g_j,0)=-\Big(\tfrac{\partial^2}{\partial p_\mu\partial p_\nu}\hat\Pi_{j+1,\mu\nu}\Big)(g_j,0)=\sum_x\Pi_{j+1,\mu\nu}(g_j,x)x_\mu x_\nu\ (\mu\ne\nu)\quad(1.22).$$
(The thermodynamic limit $T^{(j+1)}\nearrow\mathbb Z^d$ exists by the localized representation.) The identity of the tensor with $\delta^{ab}$ uses gauge invariance: invariance under global $V\to R(v)V$ means $R(v)\otimes R(v)\Pi=\Pi$, which for $G$ semisimple forces proportionality to the identity on $\mathfrak g\otimes\mathfrak g$ (p. 264; the statement used is that the $G$-invariant bilinear form on a simple factor is the Killing form; for several simple factors with distinct couplings this would need care: UNCERTAIN, not discussed).

### §2 (pp. 265–269): the fluctuation integral
$(\mathbf T_kA_k)(W)=\log\mathbf N_k^{-1}\int dV\,\delta(\bar VW^{-1})\chi_k\exp[-\frac1{g_k^2}\mathbf G(V)-\frac1{g_k^2}A(U_k(V))+\mathbf E_k(U_k(V))]$ (2.1).
1. *Critical point.* Under regularity there is exactly one critical orbit of $V\mapsto \mathbf G(V)+A(U_k(V))$ on $\bar V=W$; choose in it the element with axial gauge condition $\mathbf G=0$; call it $V^{(k)}(W)$, related to the minimal configuration $U_{k+1}(W)$ by $V^{(k)}=\bar U^k_{k+1}=M^k(U_{k+1})$ (2.3). Integration variables $V=V'V^{(k)}$, $B'=\frac1i\log V'$. The gauge transformation from axial to Landau gauge $u_k$ (constructed in [14]) gives the regular Lie-algebra-valued configuration $\mathbf H_k(B')$, $U_k(V'V^{(k)})^{u_k^{-1}}=\exp i\eta\mathbf H_k(B')U_{k+1}$ (p. 265).
2. *Expansion of the action* (2.4)–(2.8): using [15] formulas (41), (174), (171): $A(U_k(V'V^{(k)}))=A(U_{k+1})+\langle H_1B',J_{k+1}\rangle+\frac12\langle H_1B',\Delta_1H_1B'\rangle+V(H_1B')$ (2.8), $V$ at least cubic. Quadratic gauge-fixing term $\mathbf G(B')=\sum\frac12|\tilde B'(y,x)|^2+\mathbf G_3(B')$ (2.5). Characteristic function $\chi_k=\prod_{b\in T^{(k+1)}\setminus\{b_0(c)\}}\chi(\{|B'(b)|<\varepsilon_1\})$ (2.9); alternative: $g_k/\gamma_k\,\varepsilon_1$ in place of $\varepsilon_1$ with $\gamma_k=C\log(L^k\varepsilon)^{-1}$ (UNCERTAIN reading of the printed expression), which keeps $\mathbf E^{(j)},\beta_j$ analytic in the couplings.
3. *Linearizing the averaging* (p. 267): operator $h$ (inverse of $L\tilde Q$ on the set of bonds $b_0(c)$; $L\tilde Qh=I$), analytic function $\tilde D(B')$ with $B'=B-h\tilde D(B)$ linearizing $\tilde Q(B')$: $L\tilde QB'+\tilde C(B')=L\tilde QB-\tilde D(B)$. Exact solution exists and is unique and analytic in $B$ (by the same arguments as in the earlier papers), $\tilde D^{(2)}=\tilde C^{(2)}$.
4. *Gaussian measure.* Scale $B=g_kB'$; the linear term vanishes ($\langle \delta A',J\rangle=0$ for $\tilde Q\delta A'=0$); the Gaussian measure $d\mu_{C^{(k)}}(B)$ has covariance $C^{(k)}=(C^*\Delta^{(k)}C)^{-1}$, $B'=CB$ (p. 268).
5. *Result* (2.12)–(2.14): $A_{k+1}(U_{k+1})=-\frac1{g_k^2}A(U_{k+1})+\mathbf E_k(U_{k+1})+[\log Z^{(k)}(U_{k+1})-\log Z^{(k)}(1)]+\log\mathbf N''_k{}^{-1}\int d\mu_{C^{(k)}}(B)\chi_k\exp[\dots]$, with exponent a sum of $\mathbf P^{(k)}(g_k,U_{k+1},B)$ (explicit, a polynomial of degree 2 in $B$ with $O(\varepsilon_1)$ coefficients, a small perturbation of the Gaussian) and the bracket $\{\mathbf E_k(U_k(\exp[g_kCB-h\tilde D(g_kCB)]V^{(k)}))-\mathbf E_k(U_k(V^{(k)}))\}$. Define $\mathbf E^{(k+1)}(g_k,U_{k+1})=\log\int d\mu_{C^{(k)}}\chi_k\exp[\mathbf P^{(k)}+\{\dots\}]$ (2.13); $\log\mathbf N''_k=\mathbf E^{(k+1)}(g_k,1)$ (2.14). Coupling renormalization $1/g_k^2=1/g_{k+1}^2+\beta_{k+1}(g_k)$ (2.15) closes the induction at the level of the form (1.3). All explicit terms satisfy the hypotheses; "possibly except the last term (2.13)" (p. 268).
6. *Invariance* (pp. 268–269): gauge invariance under $U_{k+1}\to U^u_{k+1}$, $B'\to R(u)B'$ (2.16); Euclidean covariance (2.17)–(2.18) with a twisted action on the fluctuation field for negatively oriented bonds, $(rV')(b)=R(V^{(k)}(rb))V'^{-1}(-(rb))$. The proof of Theorem 3 is then reduced to constructing (1.7) for the term (2.13) with bounds (1.18) ("the rest of the paper is almost completely devoted to these problems", p. 269).

### §3 (pp. 269–281): localization expansion, first stage, preliminary analytic extension
Genuine work begins. The term $\mathbf P^{(k)}$ is analytic in $\mathbf U,\mathbf J$ (3.1) (replace $J_{k+1}$ in propagators by $\mathbf J$ and $U_{k+1}$ elsewhere by $\mathbf U$). The nonlocal part $B'=g_kCB-h\tilde D(g_kCB)$ enters $\mathbf E^{(j)}(U_j(\exp iB'V^{(k)}))$ through $\mathbf H_k(B')$ (a nonlocal function, decaying exponentially in $B'$-dependence). Steps:
1. *Partition of unity* (p. 270): cover by $\square$ = unions of $2^d$ neighbouring $\pi_k$ cubes, $\sum_\square\zeta_\square=1$, $\zeta_\square(x)=\prod_\mu\zeta(M^{-1}(x_\mu-y_\mu))$, $\zeta\in C_0^\infty$, $\zeta=1$ on $|t|\le1/3$, $0$ for $|t|\ge2/3$, second derivatives $\le5$. Write difference as $\int_0^1dt\,\frac d{dt}\mathbf E^{(j)}(\dots)$ and decompose $\mathbf H_k(B')=\sum_\square\zeta_\square\mathbf H_k(B')$ (3.4); each cube gets its own interpolation parameter $t_\square$, differentiated at $t_\square=0$ (the Mayer/"localization parameter" idea, as in P3).
2. *Two kinds of domains* (3.5): $X\cap(\tilde\square^2)^c\ne\emptyset$ or $X\subset\tilde\square^2$. For $X$ meeting the complement: either $X$ is far from $\square$ (distance $\ge M(L^j\eta)^{-1}$ in $\xi$ scale; exponential decay of $\delta\mathbf H_j/\delta B$, rate $\delta_0$, from [15] Prop 9 (190), gives the bound (3.9)), or $X$ is big ($d_j(X)\ge(L^j\eta)^{-1}$) and (1.18) gives a factor $O((L^j\eta)^N)$ for any $N$. Both are irrelevant (p. 271–273). Needs the analytic extension (3.10)–(3.13) of $\mathbf E^{(j)}(X,\exp i\xi\mathbf A\,U_{k+1})$ to complex $\mathbf A$ with $|\mathbf A|,|P_1\mathbf A|,|\nabla^\xi\mathbf A|,|\Delta^\xi\mathbf A|<\alpha_2$ (3.14) and Cauchy integral in $t_\square$ (3.15)–(3.17). The Landau-gauge condition $RD^*\mathbf H_j=0$ is used to replace $D^*D$ by $\Delta_U^\xi-P_1+\dots$ so that bounds on a second-order *covariant Laplacian* are not needed (that is why $J$ appears; p. 272).
3. *Main case* $X\subset\tilde\square^2$ (pp. 273–281): transform to a standard form (3.18)–(3.28): choose the cube $\square_0=\tilde\square^3$ and the axial-gauge functions $U_j(\square_0,\cdot)$, $M'(U_{k+1})\to M'(U)$ with $U_{k+1}(\square_0,M'(U))$ extended by $\mathbf U$ outside $\square_0$ (so gauge transformations of the configuration are explicit analytic functions of $\mathbf U|_{\square_0}$); pass to Landau gauge $u_{k+1}$ with $\mathbf H_{k+1}(\square_0,\frac1i\log V)$ and $|u_{k+1}-1|<B_3O(1)M\alpha_0$ (3.27). The result is a function of a gauge-covariant variable $B=Q(\eta A)$, $A=\frac1{i\eta}\log\exp i\eta\mathbf A\,\exp iL^{-1}\eta\mathbf H_{k+1}$ (3.30), where all dependence on the nonlocal object is through the single Lie algebra-valued field $A$ on $\square_0$. Taylor expansion to order 5 in $B$ (3.34):
$$\mathbf E^{(j)}(X,U_j(\square_0,\exp iB))=\sum_{n=0}^4\tfrac1{n!}\Big\langle\tfrac{\delta^n}{\delta B^n}\mathbf E^{(j)}(X,U_j(\square_0,1)),\textstyle\bigotimes^nB\Big\rangle+\int_0^1d\tau\tfrac{(1-\tau)^4}{4!}\Big\langle\tfrac{\delta^5}{\delta B^5}\mathbf E^{(j)}(X,U_j(\square_0,\exp i\tau B)),\textstyle\bigotimes^5B\Big\rangle.$$
$|B|<O(1)L^j\eta$, so the fifth-order term should be $O(1)e^{-\kappa d_j(X)}(O(1)L^j\eta)^5$ (3.35): the heart of the technical work (pp. 277–280): **Lemma 4 (p. 280)**: for $\mathbf U\in\mathscr U^c_{k+1}(\square_0,(1+2\beta)\alpha_0,(1+2\beta)\alpha_1)$, $A$ defined on bonds of the construction of $U_j(\square_0,\cdot)$ with $|A|,|\nabla^\eta A|,\|A\|_{1,\beta}<\alpha_2$ and $B'$ with $|B'|<\alpha_3$, the pair $(U_j(\square_0,\exp i(\tau B+B')),J_j(\square_0,\exp i(\tau B+B')))|_X\in\mathscr U^c_j(X,\alpha_0,\alpha_1)$ for $\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_3$ sufficiently small. (This verifies conditions (i)–(iv) via the identities (3.37)–(3.52), using that gauge transformations $\bar u_j$ connecting the minimal configuration to the exponential are close to 1, and that second derivatives satisfy $|\partial^\xi\mathbf H_j(\square_0,\tau Q(L^{-1}\eta\mathbf H_{k+1}))-\partial^\xi H_{1,j}Q(\dots)|<\beta\alpha_0(L^{j-1}\eta)^2$; (3.45)–(3.47).) Consequently by Cauchy's estimate in $\sigma$ the fifth order term is bounded as (3.54): $\le E_0\alpha_3^{-5}|B|^5\exp(-\kappa d_j(X))$. The constant is much bigger than $E_0$; to recover $E_0$ one uses the differentiation in $t_\square$ at $t_\square=0$, which gives a factor $O(1)\alpha_2^{-1}\varepsilon_1$, small (p. 280).

### §4 (pp. 281–292): Ward–Takahashi identities
Lower-order terms of (3.34). The $m<n$ terms (derivatives with some factors $\zeta_\square B$ replaced by $(1-\zeta_\square)B$) are small (4.1)–(4.5) by exponential decay in dist$(X,\operatorname{supp}(1-\zeta_\square))$ (decay constants from the tree-graph structure of $\delta^{n(p)}\mathbf H_j/\delta B^{n(p)}$, the sum over trees of [15] Sect. G; (4.3)–(4.5)). Remaining, $m=n$ (4.6): $\sum_{n=1}^4\frac1{(n-1)!}\langle\frac{\delta^n}{\delta B^n}\mathbf E^{(j)}(X,U_j(\square_0,1)),\delta B,\bigotimes^{n-1}B\rangle$, $\delta B=\langle\frac\delta{\delta A}Q_j(\eta A),\eta\langle\frac\delta{\delta A}A,\zeta_\square\mathbf H_j\rangle\rangle$.
- **Ward identity** (4.9): for gauge-invariant analytic $\mathbf E(V)$ on a unit lattice, differentiating $\mathbf E(V^v)=\mathbf E(V)$ with $v=\exp i\lambda$ (4.8): $\big\langle\frac\delta{\delta B}\mathbf E(\exp iB),i[\lambda(b_-),B(b)]-g^{-1}(i\,\mathrm{ad}_{B(b)})(\partial\lambda)(b)\big\rangle=0$ where $g^{-1}(z)=(-z)/(e^{-z}-1)$ (printed as such; $=1+\frac12z+k_2z^2+\dots$). Higher: (4.10)–(4.12), at $B=0$: (4.13)–(4.15).
- **First consequence** (4.14): $\frac{\delta}{\delta B}\mathbf E(1)=0$ (uses $G$ semisimple: $[\lambda,\cdot]=0$ for all $\lambda\in\mathfrak g^c$ only for the element $0$). Then: $\langle\delta^2\mathbf E(1),B_1,\partial\lambda\rangle=0$, and two identities for $n=3,4$ (4.15). These let the third and fourth order terms be expressed through $\mathbf E^{(2)}$ only, modulo irrelevant terms (4.21)–(4.31); the second part of (4.21) has factor $(\partial B)(\Gamma_{x,x_3})$ with $|x_3-x|$ large, handled by exponential decay with a poorer rate $\delta_1=O(M^{-1})$ (4.22). The lattice Taylor formula (4.30) (p. 288) is used for $B_{\mu_3}(x_3)$ about $x$.
- Outcome (4.34): the sum (4.6) equals $\sum\mathbf E^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}(X,x,y)\mathrm{tr}\,\delta B_\mu(x)B_\nu(y)$ + explicit local cubic and quartic polynomials in $\delta B,B$ with coefficients $\sum_y\mathbf E^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}(X,x,y)(y_\kappa-x_\kappa)(y_\lambda-x_\lambda)$ + irrelevant terms. By $G$-invariance (4.32)–(4.33) the tensor $\mathbf E^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}(x,y)\in\mathfrak g\otimes\mathfrak g$ is $\propto\delta_{ab}$, so $\langle\mathbf E,A\,\mathrm{ad}\,B\rangle=E\,\mathrm{tr}AB$.
- **Extension of the sum over $X$** (4.35)–(4.37): replace $H_j(\square_0)$ by free-boundary $H_j$ (error $\propto B_0e^{-\delta_0M(L^j\eta)^{-1}}$), extend sums over $y$ to $\mathbb Z^4$ and over $X\in\mathbf D_j$ to the class $\mathbf D^0_j$ of domains in $\zeta\mathbb Z^4$, error bounded by $O(1)E_0e^{-(L^j\eta)^{-1}}e^{-\delta_1|x-y|}$ (4.36). Defines the **vacuum polarization tensor** $\Pi_{\mu\nu}(x,y)=\sum_{X\in\mathbf D^0_j}\mathbf E^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}(X,x,y)$ (4.37).
- **The cancellation** (4.38)–(4.45): the other term of the bracket in (2.12), $\beta_j(g_{j-1})(A(U_j(\exp iB'V^{(k)}))-A(U_j(V^{(k)})))$, is localized in the same way; its second-order part is, by (4.35), $\beta_j\langle\partial^\xi H_j,\partial^\xi H_j\rangle=\beta_j\Delta_j$ with $\Delta_j$ the explicit operator in [10] (1.66) (the Yang–Mills quadratic form), with momentum expansion $\Delta_{j,\mu\nu}(p')=\Delta_0(p')\delta_{\mu\nu}-\bar\partial_\mu(p')\partial_\nu(p')+\dots$ (4.41). This gives the terms (4.42), (4.44) (same shape as those from $\Pi$), and the first-order moment term vanishes (4.45). Hence the only terms of (4.38) not under control are exactly cancelled by (4.42), (4.44) *if* $\beta_j$ equals the coefficient of the transverse structure in $\Pi$ (next section). "Both groups cancel because (4.38) appears with the minus sign in the effective action (1.6)" (p. 292).

### §5 (pp. 292–298): the polarization tensor and the $\beta$-functions
Symmetries of $\Pi(b,b')=\lim\frac{\delta^2}{\delta B(b)\delta B(b')}\mathbf E^{(j)}(U_j(\exp iB))|_{B=0}$: Euclidean covariance (5.4), (5.6)–(5.8) for permutations $r$ and reflections $\epsilon$, translation invariance, symmetry $\Pi_{\mu\nu}(x)=\Pi_{\nu\mu}(-x)$, **transversality** from gauge invariance (4.15): $\sum_\mu\partial^*_\mu\Pi_{\mu\nu}=\sum_\nu\partial_\nu\Pi_{\mu\nu}=0$ (5.9). Exponential decay $|\Pi_{\mu\nu}(x-y)|\le O(1)E_0e^{-\delta_1|x-y|}$, $\delta_1=\frac12\min\{\delta_0,\kappa M^{-1}\}$ (5.10), so $\hat\Pi_{\mu\nu}(p)$ (5.11) extends analytically to $\zeta_\mu=p_\mu+iq_\mu$, $|q_\mu|<\delta_1$, with symmetries (5.12)–(5.15) ($\partial_\mu(\zeta)=e^{i\zeta_\mu}-1$). Goal: (5.16) $\hat\Pi_{\mu\nu}(p)=\beta(\delta_{\mu\nu}\Delta(p)-\bar\partial_\mu(p)\partial_\nu(p))+\text{(higher order in }\partial(p),\bar\partial(p))$ with analytic coefficients. Method: variables $z_\mu=e^{i\zeta_\mu}$, $f_{\mu\nu}(z)=\hat\Pi_{\mu\nu}(\frac1i\log z)$ analytic on the poly-ring (5.17), Laurent decomposition $f=g^++g^-(z^{-1})$ in each variable with normalization conditions fixed by the reflection law (5.19)–(5.25), giving $f_{\mu\nu}(z)=\sum_\epsilon\epsilon_\mu\epsilon_\nu z_\mu^{\dots}z_\nu^{\dots}g_{\mu\nu}(z^\epsilon)$ (5.26); Taylor expansion of $g_{\mu\nu}$ to order 3 at $(1,\dots,1)$ (5.30), reduction of coefficients by permutation symmetry (5.31)–(5.32), substitution (5.33), gauge invariance (5.21) gives two vanishing conditions (5.34): $f_{\mu\nu}(1)=0$ ($\mu\ne\nu$), $g_{\mu\mu}(1)=0$, and all $\beta_{\mu\nu}$ ($\mu\ne\nu$) equal to a single $\beta$ (5.36) ($2^{d-2}\beta_{\mu\nu}=\beta$). Result (5.37): $\hat\Pi_{\mu\nu}(p)=\beta(\delta_{\mu\nu}\Delta(p)-\bar\partial_\mu(p)\partial_\nu(p))+\hat\Pi'_{\mu\nu}(p)$, with $\hat\Pi'$ a third-order polynomial in $\partial,\bar\partial$ with coefficients analytic on a strip (5.38). Hence $\beta=-\frac{\partial^2}{\partial p_1\partial p_2}\hat\Pi_{12}(0)=-\sum_x\Pi_{12}(x)x_1x_2$ (5.39)–(5.42), i.e. the definition (1.22) is the coefficient of the transverse (Yang–Mills) structure. Then the sum (4.34) becomes $\beta\frac12\sum\mathrm{tr}(\partial\delta B)(\partial B)+\dots$ (5.43) with remainder bounds (5.44): third-order terms shifted so that derivatives fall onto the other field and are irrelevant by (4.14), (4.15), (4.17), (4.18). The first term cancels against (4.42) (with the same $\beta=\beta_j$), the second with (4.44), and the third vanishes because first and second derivatives of $\hat\Pi'$ vanish at $p=0$. Closing remarks (p. 298): the analysis could be done equally for $\mathbf E^{(3)},\mathbf E^{(4)}$ by Ward identities and Laurent expansions, an alternative to §4, but "it is connected with a lot of technicalities and calculations of an algebraic character and it seems that the method presented in the last section is more clear and simpler".

**Which steps are routine and which are the genuine difficulty.** Routine: §2 (Gaussian integral algebra, change of variables, verification of invariances), the Cauchy-estimate steps of §3. Genuine: the choice of spaces $\mathscr U^c_j$ so that a nonlocal gauge-covariant object $M'(\mathbf U)$ can be substituted and the Taylor expansion at 0 stays in the analytic domain (Lemma 4, (3.36)–(3.54)), and the bookkeeping of Ward identities (§4). The localization of the new term itself (construction of (1.7), bounds (1.18) for $\mathbf E^{(k+1)}$) is *not* done here.

## 7. Weak spots and open points

1. **Theorem 3 is not fully proved.** The paper constructs the *integrand* of $\mathbf E^{(k+1)}$ (2.13) and shows its dangerous low-order terms combine with $\beta A$; but the representation (1.7) with (1.18) for $\mathbf E^{(k+1)}$ (localization of the fluctuation integral, cluster expansion, analytic extension to $\mathscr U^c_{k+1}$) is deferred (pp. 269, 298; sequel = CMP 116). The analysis of $\mathbf E^{(j)}$-terms (§§3–5) shows only that the *given* inductive terms transform correctly under the nonlocal change $B'$.
2. **Theorem 2** (asymptotic freedom of the finite-difference RG with the stated bounds (0.31)): no proof, "separate paper" based on perturbative calculations.

## 8. Preliminary formalization notes

E6 itself is far from formalizable as stated for the O(N) project: its main theorem rests on [13]–[15], is only partially proved, and is gauge-specific. What is reusable:
- *Format* of the localization hypothesis (§1.1, (1.7), (1.18), $\mathbf D_j$, $d_j(X)$) as an abstract Lean structure: "a function of the configuration decomposed as a sum over connected cube unions $X$, each term depending only on the configuration restricted to $X$, bounded by $E_0e^{-\kappa d(X)}$, analytic on a complex neighbourhood". Formalizable early (finite combinatorics on cubes; tree length as an infimum over finite trees; Mathlib has `SimpleGraph` trees, no Steiner-tree length, so a hand-rolled definition is needed — guess).
- Cauchy estimates for analytic functions of several complex variables on polydiscs: Mathlib has one-variable Cauchy estimates; the several-variable version via iteration or `AnalyticOnNhd` slices is plausible but not ready (guess).
- The discrete Taylor formula (4.30) along coordinate-ordered lattice contours; elementary.

## 10. Relevance to the formalization

**Interfaces.** (i) The abstract definition "localization expansion" (§1.4–1.1 above; (1.7), (1.18)) — would be stated once in the O(N) formalization as `IsLocalized (E : Config → ℂ) (D : Set (Domain)) (E0 κ : ℝ)`; the paper that proves the combinatorics (sum over $X\ni y$ of $e^{-\kappa d(X)}$, volume bound) is not E6. (ii) The Taylor formula (4.30) as a lemma on $\mathbb Z^d$-functions along lattice contours.

**Reusable arguments.** (a) The *scheme* of using Ward identities from a symmetry to show that the sum of terms of order $\le4$ in the background expansion reduces to the quadratic form with coefficient determined by $\Pi$, and that this coefficient is precisely the one to be absorbed into the running coupling. In the O(N) series the same logic is P1 §3: $\mathcal V$ conditions + transverse second moment and Ward identity (3.121)–(3.125). Reading E6 §4–5 next to P1 §3.8–3.10 clarifies *why* only $\beta$ (coefficient of $\|\partial\psi\|^2$) is marginal. (b) The device of enlarging the analyticity domain by a second variable ($\mathbf J$ in E6; the $\psi'$ shift in the spaces $\Xi^c$ of P1) to avoid controlling second derivatives. (c) The interpolation trick per cube (derivative in $t_\square$ at 0 gives a small factor $O(\varepsilon_1)$ to recover the constant $E_0$, p. 280); this is the same trick that P3 develops for O(N) ("decoupling parameters"). (d) The exponential-gauge-fixing replacement of $\delta$-function constraints, which is the gauge counterpart of P4's treatment of the hard-spin constraint via the barrier $\frac\lambda8\||\phi|^2-1\|^2$ (both turn a constraint into a smooth weight to keep analyticity).

**Gauge-specific / irrelevant for O(N).** Averaging operations on group-valued bonds (0.4)–(0.12), contour averages and axiomatic averages, axial and Landau gauge, Faddeev–Popov (0.14)–(0.16), the operator $h$ and the linearization $\tilde Q$ of (2.10) (in O(N) the block average $Q\phi$ is linear), the space $\mathscr U^c_j$ (i)–(iv) with gauge orbits, $\mathbf J$, the transversality (5.9), §5 Laurent analysis, the $\Delta_j$ of [10], the (non)asymptotic-freedom Theorem 2. **What is *not* in E6 and would be needed:** the actual localization theorem (E7 for gauge, P3/P6 for O(N)); Green's function bounds ([10], [11], E3 for gauge; P2 cites E5 (2.57)); large fields (E8–E10).

**Summary for Roland's interest.** The inductive scheme (E6 §1) is the YM$_4$ small-field version of the exact scheme of P1 (H.1)–(H.9)/P4: same representation of the effective action as a "background action + sum over scales of localized, analytic, invariant terms bounded by $E_0e^{-\kappa d_j(X)}$", same crude-vs-renormalized volume estimate, same Ward-identity-driven removal of the unique marginal operator. Concepts that transfer verbatim to O(N): the localization class $\mathbf D_j$, tree length, the exponential bound (1.18), the form (0.22)–(0.24), the consequences (0.26)–(0.30), the lattice Taylor formula (4.30), the idea of the "$\mathbf J$" variable (avoid second derivatives). Gauge-specific: group-valued averages, gauge fixing, axial/Landau gauge, covariant Laplacian bounds, vacuum polarization transversality, asymptotic-freedom theorem. Differences that are not just gauge: in E6 the small-field region has fixed $\varepsilon_0$ and the fluctuation is controlled by $g_k\le\gamma$ (smallness of coupling), whereas in O(N) the fluctuation scale $\beta_k^{-1/2}\sim L^{-k(d-2)/2}$ decays by powers and the analyticity radii $\varepsilon_j$ decay only with the smaller exponent $\alpha=\frac{d-2}2-\gamma$, giving the "irrelevancy gap" $L^{j\gamma}$; E6 has no such gap since its irrelevance comes from the extra powers $(L^j\eta)^\alpha$ gained by gauge invariance and canonical dimension 4. Hence the O(N) series is not a verbatim transcription of E6: the dimension counting and the coupling flow are different, while the structural architecture is the same.
