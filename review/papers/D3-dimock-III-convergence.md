## 0. Identification

J. Dimock, "The renormalization group according to Balaban III. Convergence", Ann. Henri Poincare 15 (2014) 2133-2175; arXiv:1304.0705. Bundle key **D3**. 37 pages (text pp. 1-32, Theorem 2 at p. 33, Appendices A-C pp. 33-35, references pp. 36-37, 17 references).

Summary. The paper is the third part of an expository account of Balaban's method, illustrated on the UV problem for the scalar $\phi^4$ model on a toroidal lattice in $d=3$ (abstract, p. 1). The torus is $\mathbb T^{-N}_M=(L^{-N}\mathbb Z/L^M\mathbb Z)^3$, scaled up to the unit lattice $\mathbb T^0_{M+N}$. After $N$ block-averaging steps (parts I, II) the density has the representation (8)-(9) with small-field regions, large-field regions and boundary terms; D3 quotes this as Theorem 1. D3 then (a) performs the final integral over $\Phi_N$ using the fact that the rescaled mass is $\bar\mu_N=1$ (so the last Gaussian integration needs no averaging operator and has a normal massive propagator), (b) bounds the sum over all large-field region configurations $\Pi$ (small factors from characteristic functions, volume counting, covering Lemma 14), (c) cluster-expands and exponentiates. Result: Theorem 2, $Z_{M,N}=Z_{M,N}(0)\exp(\sum_X\mathcal H(X))$ with $|\mathcal H(X)|\le\mathcal O(1)\lambda^{\beta/2}e^{-\kappa_0 d_M(X)}$, and the stability bound (Corollary 1) uniform in $N$ and in the volume parameter $M$ (bulk dependence $\exp(\pm\lambda^\eta\mathrm{Vol})$). Dimock says explicitly (p. 2) that the stability bound is not new; the point is to illustrate the method.

## 1. Setting and notation

Model (p. 1): $\phi^4_3$ on $\mathbb T^{-N}_M$ with $\lambda_0^N=L^{-N}\lambda$, $\mu_0^N=L^{-2N}\mu$ (these are the lattice-spacing-scaled coupling constants; superscript $N$ usually omitted):
$$\rho^N_0(\Phi)=\exp(-S^N_0-V^N_0),\quad S_0^N=\tfrac12\|\partial\Phi\|^2+\tfrac12\bar\mu_0^N\|\Phi\|^2,\quad V_0^N=\varepsilon_0^N\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb T^0_{M+N})+\tfrac12\mu_0^N\|\Phi\|^2+\tfrac14\lambda_0^N\sum_x\Phi^4(x)\quad(3),(4).$$
$Z_{M,N}=\int\rho^N_0(\Phi)\,d\Phi$ (2). Here $\varepsilon_0^N,\mu_0^N$ are the counterterms (vacuum energy and mass counterterm); the claim of the paper is that suitable choices give uniform stability bounds. There is no wave-function counterterm (super-renormalizable).

RG step (5)-(7): $\tilde\rho_{k+1}(\Phi_{k+1})=\mathcal N^{-1}_{aL,\mathbb T^1_{M+N-k}}\int\exp(-\frac12aL|\Phi_{k+1}-Q\Phi_k|^2)\rho_k(\Phi_k)d\Phi_k$, then scaling $\rho_{k+1}(\Phi_{k+1})=\tilde\rho_{k+1}(\Phi_{k+1,L})L^{-|\mathbb T^1_{M+N-k}|/2}$. Block average $Q$ (plain average over $L^3$ points). $\mathsf Z_{M,N}=\int\rho_k^N\,d\Phi_k$ for any $k$.

Running couplings (Theorem 1): $\lambda_k=L^{-(N-k)}\lambda$, $\bar\mu_k=L^{-2(N-k)}\bar\mu$ (UV: the lattice is *refined*; going from step $0$ to $N$ the coupling grows to its physical value and the mass $\bar\mu_k$ grows to $\bar\mu$). $\alpha_k=\max\{\lambda_k^{1/4},\bar\mu_k^{1/2}\}$, $p_k=(-\log\lambda_k)^p$ (Theorem 1.2). Further $p_{0,k}=(-\log\lambda_k)^{p_0}$ (p. 22, used in Lemmas 11-12, 15), $r_k=(-\log\lambda_k)^r$ (footnote 3, p. 5; layer thickness $[r_k]$). Dynamical couplings $\varepsilon_k,\mu_k$ ("selected in part I", p. 3): in the proof one uses $|\varepsilon_{k-1}|\le\mathcal O(1)\lambda_j^\beta$, $|\mu_{k-1}|\le\mathcal O(1)\lambda_j^{1/2+\beta}$ (p. 25, before (170)), where here $\beta$ is an *exponent* ($\beta<\frac14-10\epsilon$, (20)), not an inverse temperature.

Regions (10)-(13): $\Pi=(\Lambda_0,\Omega_1,\Lambda_1,\dots,\Omega_k,\Lambda_k)$ a decreasing sequence of small-field regions in $\mathbb T^{-k}_{M+N-k}$, $\Omega_j,\Lambda_j$ unions of $L^{-(k-j)}M$-cubes; $\delta\Omega_j=\Omega_j-\Omega_{j+1}$. Basic variables $\Phi_{k,\Omega}=(\Phi_{1,\delta\Omega_1},\dots,\Phi_{k-1,\delta\Omega_{k-1}},\Phi_{k,\Omega_k})$; auxiliary fluctuation variables $W_{k,\Pi}=(W_{0,\Omega_1-\Lambda_1},\dots)$; convention $\Lambda_{-1},\Omega_0$ = full torus. Large-field regions $\Omega_j^c,\Lambda_j^c$ are generated by sets $P_j,Q_j,R_j$ of $LM$-cubes (p. 22, (151)): $\Omega^c_{j+1}=(\bar\Lambda_j)^{c,5*}\cup P^{5*}_{j+1}$, $\Lambda^c_{j+1}=(\Omega^c_{j+1})^{5*}\cup Q^{5*}_{j+1}\cup R^{5*}_{j+1}$. $X^*$ = enlargement by $[r_k]$ layers of $M$-cubes, $X^{\natural}$ = shrinkage by $[r_k]$ layers (footnote 3, p. 5). Polymers: connected unions $X$ of $M$-cubes, $d_M(X)$ with $Md_M(X)$ = length of shortest tree joining the cubes; $\mathcal D_N$ = such $X$; $\mathcal D_N(\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega^c)$ = connectedness modulo $\Omega^c$ (components of $\Omega^c$ count as connected, $d_M(X,\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega^c)$). $X\#\Lambda$ = $X$ meets both $\Lambda$ and $\Lambda^c$. $|X|_M$ = number of $M$-cubes.

Effective actions (Theorem 1, items 4-9): bare action $S_k^+=S^*_k+V_k$ with $S_k^*(\Lambda_k,\Phi_k,\phi)=\frac{a_k}2\|\Phi_k-Q_k\phi\|^2_{\Lambda_k}+\frac12\|\partial\phi\|^2_{*,\Lambda_k}+\frac12\bar\mu_k\|\phi\|^2_{\Lambda_k}$ (18), $V_k=\varepsilon_k\mathrm{Vol}+\frac12\mu_k\|\phi\|^2+\frac14\lambda_k\int\phi^4$; $\phi=\phi_{k,\Omega(\Lambda^*_k)}$ the (approximately localized) minimizer, see Appendix A. $E_k$ main correction (local expansion (19), bound (20): $|E_k(X)|\le\lambda_k^\beta e^{-\kappa d_M(X)}$ on a complex domain $\mathcal R_k(X,\epsilon)$ for $\beta<\frac14-10\epsilon$); $R_{k,\Pi}$ tiny remainder ((21),(22): $\le\lambda_k^{n_0}e^{-\kappa d_M(X)}$, $n_0\ge4$ fixed integer); $B_{k,\Pi}$ active boundary term ((23)-(25): $\mathcal D_k(\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega_k^c)$, $X\#\Lambda_k$, bound $B_0\lambda_k^\beta e^{-\kappa d_M(X,\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega_k^c)}$ with $B_0=B_0(L,M)$, and $|W_j|\le B_wp_jL^{\frac12(k-j)}$ on $\Omega_{j+1}-\Lambda_{j+1}$); $\tilde B_{k,\Pi}$ inactive boundary term (26): $|\tilde B|\le B_0|\Lambda^{(k)}_{k-1}-\Lambda^{(k)}_k|=B_0\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda_{k-1}-\Lambda_k)$, additive in components of $\Lambda_k^c$. Complex domains $\mathcal R_k,\mathcal P_k,\mathcal P_{k,\Omega}$ are defined in "section 3.2 of part II" (p. 4 Remark), not here.

## 2. Main results

**Theorem 1 (p. 3), quoted from part II, not proved here.** Let $0<\lambda<e^{-1}$ and $0<\bar\mu\le1$. Let $\lambda_k=L^{-(N-k)}\lambda$, $\bar\mu_k=L^{-2(N-k)}\bar\mu$. Let $L$ be sufficiently large, $M$ sufficiently large (depending on $L$), and $\lambda_k$ sufficiently small (depending on $L,M$). Let $\varepsilon_k,\mu_k$ be the dynamical coupling constants selected in part I. Then the representation
$$\rho_k(\Phi_k)=Z_k\sum_\Pi\int d\Phi_{k,\Omega^c}\,dW_{k,\Pi}\,K_{k,\Pi}\mathcal C_{k,\Pi}\,\chi_k(\Lambda_k)\exp\big(-S_k^+(\Lambda_k)+E_k(\Lambda_k)+R_{k,\Pi}(\Lambda_k)+B_{k,\Pi}(\Lambda_k)\big)\quad(8)$$
holds, with $d\Phi_{k,\Omega^c},dW_{k,\Pi},K_{k,\Pi},\mathcal C_{k,\Pi}$ as in (9) (Gaussian factors $\exp(-\frac12aL^{-(k-j)}|\Phi_{j+1}-Q\Phi_j|^2_{\Omega_{j+1}^c})$, fluctuation measures $\exp(-\frac12L^{-(k-j)}|W_j|^2)$, $K_{k,\Pi}=\prod_{j\le k}\exp(c_j|\Omega_j^{c,(j-1)}|-S^{+,u}_{j,L^{-(k-j)}}(\Lambda_{j-1}-\Lambda_j)+(\tilde B_{j,L^{-(k-j)}})_{\Pi_j}(\Lambda_{j-1},\Lambda_j))$, $\mathcal C_{k,\Pi}=\prod_j(\mathcal C_{j,L^{-(k-j)}})_{\Lambda_{j-1},\Omega_j,\Lambda_j}$), and:

**Theorem 2 (p. 33), main theorem of D3.** Let $\bar\mu=1$ and $\lambda$ sufficiently small. Then there is a choice of counterterms $\varepsilon_0^N,\mu_0^N$ such that
$$\mathsf Z_{M,N}=\mathsf Z_{M,N}(0)\exp\Big(\sum_X\mathcal H(X)\Big)\quad(221),\qquad |\mathcal H(X)|\le\mathcal O(1)\lambda^{\beta/2}e^{-\kappa_0d_M(X)}\quad(222),$$
sum over connected unions $X$ of $M$-cubes in $\mathbb T^{-N}_M$. Here $\mathsf Z_{M,N}(0)$ is the partition function with $\lambda_0=0$ and no counterterms ($V_0=0$), identified in (52) with $Z_N\det(\Delta_N^{-1/2})$. The constants $\mathcal O(1)$ are independent of all parameters (convention p. 4); "$C$" may depend on $L$. Quantifiers: "$\lambda$ sufficiently small" in D3 means depending on $L,M,\kappa,\kappa_0$, and the exponents $n_0,p,p_0,r$ (see Sect. 6). The bound is uniform in $N$ (UV cutoff) and in the volume parameter $\mathsf M$.

**Corollary 1 (stability, p. 33).** $\exp(-\lambda^\eta\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb T_{\mathsf M}))\le\mathsf Z_{M,N}/\mathsf Z_{M,N}(0)\le\exp(\lambda^\eta\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb T_{\mathsf M}))$ for some $\eta>0$ independent of $\mathsf M,N$; proof gives $\eta=\beta/2$ via $|\sum_X\mathcal H(X)|\le\mathcal O(1)\lambda^{\beta/2}|\mathbb T^{-N}_M|_M=\mathcal O(1)M^{-3}\lambda^{\beta/2}\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb T_M)$ (226).

Remark p. 33: correlation functions can be treated similarly, referring to [12] = P9 (Balaban-O'Carroll).

## 3. Proof architecture

**Sect. 1 (pp. 1-4): setup and Theorem 1.** Recalled from I, II. D3 treats Theorem 1 as a black box: the inductive hypothesis at scale $k$, for all $k\le N$. No statement about how the induction closes.

**Sect. 2 (pp. 5-18): the last step.** Take $\bar\mu=1$ (arranged by taking $\lambda$ small; for $\bar\mu\le1$ in Theorem 1, $\bar\mu=1$ is the extreme case), run the iteration to $k=N$ so the lattice is the original torus $\mathbb T^{-N}_{\mathsf M}$ (in the language of part I, $K=N$, $\Delta=0$). Then $\bar\mu_N=1$, $\lambda_N=\lambda$, and initial counterterms are fixed to make $\varepsilon_N=\mu_N=0$, i.e. the potential is moved from $S_N^+$ to $E_N^+$ (28). The mass $\bar\mu_N=1$ controls the final $\Phi_N$ integral. This is the analogue of P7 Sect. 4 / P4 Sect. 6 (last step with $\nu_{k_f}>\frac78L^{-2}$).

2.1 Minimizers (pp. 5-8). Split $\Omega_{N+1}\equiv\Lambda_N^{5\natural}$ (29): no new large-field region ($P_{N+1}=\emptyset$), and $\Omega_{N+1}$ is a union of $M$-cubes (not $LM$-cubes). The integral over $\Phi_{N,\Omega_{N+1}}$ is analyzed around the minimizer of a modified action: the field $\tilde\Phi_{N,\Omega(\Lambda_N^*)}$ (30)-(33), minimizer $\phi_{N,\delta\Omega'}$ of (31) with $\Omega'=(\Omega(\Lambda^*_N),\Omega_{N+1})$, given by (34)-(36) in terms of the Green's function $G_{N,\delta\Omega'}=[-\Delta+\bar\mu_N+Q^T_{N,\delta\Omega'}aQ_{N,\delta\Omega'}]^{-1}_{\Omega_1'}$ (35). Because the mass is now substantial, the kernel of $G$ decays exponentially and $\phi_{N,\delta\Omega'}$ is approximately localized in $\Omega^c_{N+1}$ ("tiny" inside $\Omega_{N+1}$). Translation $\Phi_{N,\Omega_{N+1}}=\Psi_{N,\Omega_{N+1}}(\delta\Omega')+Z$ (37); Lemma 1 (39): $S^*_N(\dots)=S^*_N(\Lambda_N-\Omega_{N+1},\dots)+\tilde S_N(\Omega_{N+1},\phi_{N,\delta\Omega'})+\frac12\langle Z,[\Delta_{\Omega(\Lambda^*_N)}]_{\Omega_{N+1}}Z\rangle+R^{(1)}_{\Pi,\Omega_{N+1}}$, proof via Lemma 2.4/2.5 of part II (not in D3). In practice a localized version of the translation (43) is used (random-walk weakening parameters $s_\square$), then undone at the cost of tiny terms $R^{(2)},R^{(3)}$. Result (44).

2.2 Fluctuation integral (pp. 7-9): $Z$ is Gaussian with covariance $\tilde C_{N,\Omega'}=[\Delta_{N,\Omega(\Lambda_N^*)}]^{-1}_{\Omega_{N+1}}$ (47). There is no $aL^{-2}Q^TQ$ term (unlike earlier steps) since there is no further averaging. Ultralocalization $Z=\tilde C^{1/2}W$, with $\tilde C^{1/2}$ represented by (48)-(50) (Appendix C of part II with $a=0$); localized version (51); determinant identification $\det\tilde C^{-1/2}\to\det\Delta_N^{-1/2}$ via the global case, costing $R^{(4)},\dots,R^{(7)}$ and a volume factor $\exp(\tilde c_N|\Omega^{c,(N)}_{N+1}|)$; (52)-(53). The new field $\tilde{\mathcal W}$ (54) enters $E^+_N,\chi_N$ (55).

2.3 Estimates (pp. 9-10). Lemma 2 (random-walk expansion of $G_{N,\delta\Omega'}$ on multiscale cubes, $M$ large, bounds (56) with decay $e^{-\frac12\gamma_0d_{\Omega'}(y,y')}$): "follows the proof of Theorem 2.2 of part II"; the new input is that in $\Omega_{N+1}$ the local inverse (57) becomes $[-\Delta+I]^{-1}_{\hat\square}$ (58) since $\bar\mu_N=1$, which satisfies the same bounds. Lemma 3 (resolvent $G_{N,\delta\Omega',r}$, $L^2$ bounds). Lemma 4 (bounds (60) for $\tilde C^{1/2},\tilde C^{-1/2}$ and their local versions).

2.4 New characteristic functions (pp. 10-11). Since $\phi_{N,\delta\Omega'}$ is already tiny in $\Omega_{N+1}$, no new field restriction is introduced ($Q_k=\emptyset$), only a fluctuation restriction: $1=\sum\zeta^w_{N+1}(R_{N+1})\chi^w_{N+1}(\Omega_{N+1}-R_{N+1})$ (61), $\chi^w$ enforces $|W_N|\le p_{0,N}$; new small-field region $\Lambda_{N+1}=\Omega^{5\natural}_{N+1}-R^{5*}_{N+1}$ (62). Lemma 5: $\tilde{\mathcal C}_{N+1,\Pi^+}$ is independent of $W_N$ on $\Lambda_{N+1}$ and $\chi_N(\Lambda^{**}_{N+1})=1$ on its support; proof uses $C p_{0,N}/p_N=C(-\log\lambda)^{p_0-p}\le\frac12$ for $\lambda$ small and $p_0<p$ (68).

2.5 Localization (pp. 11-14). Lemmas 6-8: localization of $\delta E_N^+$, $R_{N,\Pi}$, $B_{N,\Pi}$ in the shifted variables for complex $|\Phi_{N,\delta\Omega_N}|\le\lambda^{-\frac14-\delta}$, $|W_N|\le B_wp_N$, via decoupling (random-walk) expansions "as in lemmas 3.15-3.17 of part II" with modifications (M-cubes, no reblocking, simultaneous decoupling of $\phi$ and $\tilde{\mathcal W}$). Bounds (75),(76),(80),(89): exponents $(\kappa-\kappa_0-2)$, $(\kappa-2\kappa_0-3)$ etc. (each localization costs some of $\kappa$). Modified analyticity domain (85)-(87).

2.6-2.8 (pp. 15-18): Cluster expansion "as in section 3.14 of part II" (97)-(99), bounds with $\kappa-6\kappa_0-6$. Final localization: Lemmas 9 and 10 split the action into pieces in $\Omega^\natural_{N+1}$ and $\Lambda_N-\Omega^\natural_{N+1}$, using the exponential smallness $e^{-r_N/2}$ of $\phi_{N,\delta\Omega'}$ across $[r_N]$ layers (Cauchy integral (105), (115)) to produce strictly localized terms $R^{*,(i)}$, $i=1,\dots,5$; remark p. 17 notes the leading terms are bounded but not small, localized so as to preserve positivity. Summary (117)-(127): collected field-independent terms $E^*_N(\Lambda_{N+1})$ (119)-(120), boundary terms $B^*_N$ (121)-(124) (bound with $\kappa-7\kappa_0-7$), inactive $\tilde B$ (125); final formula (127): $\mathsf Z_{M,N}=\mathsf Z_{M,N}(0)\sum_{\Pi^+}\int d\bar\Phi_{N,\Omega^c}dW_{N+1,\Pi^+}\bar K_{N+1,\Pi^+}\tilde{\mathcal C}_{N+1,\Pi^+}\exp(E^*_N+R^\#_{N,\Pi^+}+B^*_{N,\Pi^+})$. This is, structurally, the entire proof that "the last step looks like a general step".

**Sect. 3 (pp. 19-32): large-field bounds and convergence of the polymer expansion.**

3.1 Rearrangement (pp. 19-20). Mayer expansions of $\exp(E^*_{N+1}+R^\#)=\prod_X(\mathcal I(X)+1)$ (128)-(129) and of the boundary terms $\exp(B)=\prod_Y(\mathcal J(Y)+1)$ (130)-(131) ($Y\in\mathcal D_N(\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda^c_{N+1})$, $Y\#\Lambda_{N+1}$). Classify the sum over $\Pi^+$ by $\Theta=\Lambda^c_{N+1}$ (a union of $M$-cubes with components $\Theta_\gamma$); then (132)-(133): $\mathsf Z_{M,N}=\mathsf Z_{M,N}(0)\sum_{\{\Theta_\gamma\}}\sum_{\{Y_\sigma\}}\sum_{\{X_\alpha\}}\sum_{\Pi^+:\Lambda^c_{N+1}=\Theta}\mathcal L_{\Pi^+}(\{X_\alpha\},\{Y_\sigma\})$ with $X_\alpha\subset\Theta^c$, $Y_\sigma\#\Theta$. Group into connected components $U_\ell$ (connected means sharing a cube; Figure 1, p. 20). Using additivity of $\tilde B$ over components of $\Lambda^c_k$ (135) and factorization (136)-(137): $\mathsf Z_{M,N}=\mathsf Z_{M,N}(0)\sum_{\{U_\ell\}}\prod_\ell\mathcal K(U_\ell)$ over disjoint connected $\{U_\ell\}$ (138), $\mathcal K(U)$ (139) invariant under $M$-lattice symmetries. Goal: bound $\mathcal K(U)$ to exponentiate.

3.2 First bounds (pp. 21-22). Bounds (140),(141) on $\mathcal I,\mathcal J$ with $\kappa'=\kappa-7\kappa_0-7$. Positivity of $V_N=\lambda\int\phi^4$ gives (144) $|\exp(-S^*_N-\tilde S_N-V_N)|\le1$. $\mathcal K(U)\le\sum\mathcal K'(\Theta)\prod_\alpha\mathcal O(1)\lambda^\beta e^{-\kappa'd_M(X_\alpha)}\prod_\sigma\lambda^\beta\mathcal O(1)B_0e^{-\kappa'd_M(Y_\sigma,\mathrm{mod}\Theta)}$ (145), with $\mathcal K'(\Theta)$ the field integral (146) over all large-field configurations with $\Lambda^c_{N+1}=\Theta$. Then all characteristic functions are dropped except large-field ones $\zeta^q_j(P_{j+1}),\zeta^0_{j+1}(Q_{j+1}),\zeta^w_j(R_{j+1})$ and the modified small-field functions $\tilde\chi_k(X,\Phi_k)=\prod\chi(|\Phi_k(x)|\le\lambda_k^{-\frac14-\delta})$ (152) needed for convergence of the remaining integrals. ## 7. Weak spots and open points

- The principal limitation relative to the task: D3 is not a full account of the induction. The induction over scales, the one-step small-field and large-field theorems, the flow/tuning of $(\varepsilon_k,\mu_k)$, the definition of the analyticity domains, and the K-function-type bookkeeping that controls "how long large-field smallness persists" (P5 H6 / P7) are all in part II or earlier. D3's Theorem 1 is a black box. A reader of D3 alone cannot say what $\Pi$, $\mathcal C_{k,\Pi}$, etc. mean precisely (the definitions of $\chi_j$, $\zeta$'s, $\mathcal C$ are in II).
- There is no global ordering of constants. Statements are "sufficiently large/small" with dependency only indicated: $L$, then $M(L)$, then $\lambda(L,M)$. The auxiliary exponents $p,p_0,r,n_0,\beta,\kappa,\kappa_0,\epsilon,\delta$ are introduced without a table; their constraints are scattered ($p_0<p$, $3r+2<2p_0$, $n_0\ge4$, $\beta<\frac14-10\epsilon$, $\kappa$ large). I could find no statement of the form "fix $p_0$..., then $\kappa$..." in D3. The reconstruction in Sect. 6 is mine.
- Several key lemmas (2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10) are "follows the proof of Lemma X of part II" with a list of modifications. These carry the genuine difficulty (random-walk expansion convergence for $G_{N,\delta\Omega'}$, localization/decoupling with analytic dependence). Not independently verifiable from D3.
- Typographical/consistency points: (i) $\beta$ is used both as an exponent here (the paper has no inverse temperature) and this clashes with the bundle's $\beta$; (ii) the statement of Lemma 15 proof (205): "$-\log\lambda_j\le-\log\lambda_i$" for $j\ge i$ holds since $\lambda_j$ increases with $j$; fine, but the exponent bookkeeping $3r+2$ vs $3r$ in (207) (final exponent $2p_0-3r$, while the first step produced $(-\log\lambda_i)^{3r+2}$ and then the second line absorbs the factor $p_{0}^2$): consistent; (iii) in Lemma 13 step (F) the bound "$C\lambda_j^{1/4}\lambda_j^{-1/4-\delta}p_j\le p_j$" hides a requirement $\delta\ge0$ small; (iv) in the Theorem 1 item 3 the subscript on $\alpha$ in (17); (v) "$(2\pi)^{|\mathbb T^0_{M+N-k}|/2}(\det C_k)^{1/2}$" in (14) vs (52): the identification $Z_N\det\Delta_N^{-1/2}=\mathsf Z_{M,N}(0)$ requires $C_N=\Delta_N^{-1}$ in the global case; stated but not proved; (vi) Lemma 14 and its proof indices (the cubes widths in (196)): I did not recheck the arithmetic; (vii) the appearance of "$\Lambda^{5\natural}_N$" notation in (29) with footnote 3 defining $\natural$ and $*$, readable but heavy.

7. Green's function suggestions (flag, not claim). The Green's function $G_{N,\delta\Omega'}=[-\Delta+\bar\mu_N+Q^TaQ]^{-1}$ restricted to a region, with $Q^TaQ$ absent on $\Omega_{N+1}$, is a positive operator of the form "discrete Laplacian + mass-type term bounded below by a block-average operator" (the multiscale averaging operators give a positive operator with a uniform lower bound by Lemma 20 type inequalities). The exponential decay (56) could probably be obtained by a Combes-Thomas argument (a bounded-range positive operator with uniform spectral gap $\ge c$ has exponentially decaying resolvent kernel), avoiding the random-walk expansion: when $\bar\mu_N=1$ the operator on $\Omega_{N+1}$ is $-\Delta+1$ (Combes-Thomas applies verbatim). For the intermediate scales the gap is not uniform in the naive sense (the mass $\bar\mu_k$ is small) but the combined operator $-\Delta+\bar\mu_k+Q^TaQ$ has a gap of order $a_k L^{-2}$ on the range of block-structure; a block Combes-Thomas/Neumann-series with block decomposition could be tried. This is a suggestion only, and D3 does not use it.
