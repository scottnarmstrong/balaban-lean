## 0. Identification

T. Balaban, "Regularity and decay of lattice Green's functions", Commun. Math. Phys. 89 (1983) 571–597 (received 14 Feb 1983; communicated by A. Jaffe; supported by NSF PHY82-03669; Harvard). Bundle key **E3**. 27 pages (571–597), all read as page images.

Summary. The paper proves, for a class of *covariant* lattice Laplacians with external (gauge) vector field $A$ and a block-projection term $aP_k(A)$, three things uniformly in the lattice spacing $\eta=L^{-k}$, in the domain $\Omega$ and in $A$: (i) a strictly positive lower bound $-\Delta^{\eta,N}_{A,\Omega}+aP_k(A)\ge\gamma_0$ (1.8); (ii) exponential decay of the Green's function and of its covariant first difference, together with a Hölder bound on the latter (Theorem, (1.9)–(1.10), p. 573) and on difference with respect to a change of domain ((1.11)–(1.12)); (iii) the same for the "unit-lattice" propagators $C^{(k)}_\Lambda(\Omega,A)$ of the effective Gaussian action after $k$ block-spin steps (Prop. 2.3 of [1], (1.15)–(1.20), p. 574), plus a lower bound for the quadratic form $\Delta^{(k)}(\Omega,A)$ (Prop. 3.1′ of [2], (1.22), p. 574). The method is a "generalized random-walk representation" (partition of unity into overlapping big cubes of side $2M$, local Neumann Green's functions on cubes, geometric series in a small error operator $R=O(M^{-1})$), with the local cube estimates obtained from the Gaussian renormalization-group equations and an explicit Fourier computation (§2, Lemma 2.4). Section 5 abstracts the method into a theorem about arbitrary unit-lattice operators (symmetric, positive, exponentially short-ranged) whose inverse then decays exponentially and depends exponentially locally on the domain (p. 594); it also gives "another proof of Prop. 2.3".

## 1. Setting and notation

**Lattices and blocks (p. 572).** $\eta\mathbb Z^d$, $\eta=L^{-k}$, $L>1$ a small integer (2 or 3), $k\ge1$ arbitrary; also a torus $T_\eta$ with periodic conditions. "Unit blocks" $B^k(y)=\{x\in\eta\mathbb Z^d:\ y_\mu\le x_\mu<y_\mu+1\}$, $y\in\mathbb Z^d$ ((1.1); each contains $L^{kd}$ points). "Big blocks": same with side $M$, centred/parametrised by $M\mathbb Z^d$; $M$ is a large integer fixed later ((2.22)). $\Omega\subset\eta\mathbb Z^d$ is a union of big blocks; $\Omega^{(k)}=\Omega\cap\mathbb Z^d$ is the unit-lattice set of block labels. $\Lambda\subset\Omega^{(k)}$ is a sum of big blocks. $\mathrm{dist}$ is the distance in the $\ell^1$ norm on the unit scale (so decay constants are in units of the unit block, not of $\eta$).

**Fields.** $\phi:\Omega\to\mathbb R^N$. A vector field $A$ is a real function on oriented nearest-neighbour bonds $b=\langle x,x+\eta e_\mu\rangle$ with $A_{-b}=-A_b$ ($A_{\langle x,x+\eta e_\mu\rangle}=A_\mu(x)$), taking values in antisymmetric $N\times N$ matrices; the parallel transporter is $U(A)=e^{q e\eta A}$, with $q$ an antisymmetric $N\times N$ matrix and $e$ a real parameter ((1.2); "$e$ sufficiently small" throughout). For a contour $\Gamma$ (sum of bonds), $A(\Gamma)=\sum_{b\subset\Gamma}A_b$ with orientation matching. (This is an $\mathrm{SO}(N)$/U(1)-type covariant structure; the model of the O(N) series corresponds to $e=0$ or $A=0$.)

**Covariant Laplacian (1.3), p. 572.** Neumann boundary conditions on $\partial\Omega$:
$$\langle\phi,(-\Delta^{\eta,N}_{A,\Omega})\phi\rangle=\sum_{b\subset\Omega}\eta^d\,|(D^\eta_A\phi)(b)|^2=\sum_{b\subset\Omega}\eta^{d}\eta^{-1}\,|(U(A_b)\phi(b_+)-\phi(b_-))|^2,$$
sum over bonds with both endpoints in $\Omega$; $D^\eta_{A,\mu}$ is the covariant difference. (The printed second form has $\eta^{d}|\eta^{-1}(U\phi-\phi)|^2$; reading is clear.)

**Covariant block averaging (1.4)–(1.5).**
$$(Q_k(A)\phi)(y)=\sum_{x\in B^k(y)}\eta^dU\big(A(\Gamma^{(k)}_{y,x})\big)\phi(x),\qquad y\in\mathbb Z^d,\qquad P_k(A)=Q^*_k(A)Q_k(A),$$
$\Gamma^{(k)}_{y,x}\subset B^k(y)$ an oriented contour from $y$ to $x$. $Q_kQ_k^*=I$ on the unit lattice (the functions $q_k(y,\cdot)$ of (3.7) have $L^2$ norm 1), so $P_k$ is an orthogonal projection onto "covariantly block-constant" functions (p. 576: $P_k$ is a projection onto a subspace of constant functions when $A=0$).

**Fundamental operator and Green's function (1.6).**
$$G_k(\Omega,A)=\big(-\Delta^{\eta,N}_{A,\Omega}+m^2+aP_k(A)\big)^{-1},\qquad m^2\ge0,\ a>0\ \text{close to }1;$$
also $m_k^2$, $a_k$ (constant $\propto a$) in later formulas, with $a_{k+1}=aa_k/(aL^{-2}+a_k)$ (before (3.5)). Compare with the scalar operator $-\Delta+Q^*aQ+\nu+\alpha$ of P2/P3: here $m^2\leftrightarrow\nu$ (a constant), $aP_k\leftrightarrow Q^*aQ$ at a single scale, and there is *no* variable potential $\alpha(x)$ (§9).

**Regularity of $A$ (1.7).** $|(\partial^\eta_\mu A)(x)|\le c\,e^{\beta-1}$ on $\Omega$ for $\mu=1..d$, some $\beta>0$, $c$ a universal constant (UNCERTAIN: the printed exponent reads "$e^{\beta-1}$" in the page image; I read it as $c\,e^{\beta-1}$, consistent with (2.23)). $\partial^\eta_\mu A=\eta^{-1}(A(x+\eta e_\mu)-A(x))$. A "regular" vector field in the later statements. Prop. 3.1′ uses the weaker (1.21): $|\partial^\eta_\mu A|\le O(1)p(e)$, $p(e)=a_0(1+|\log e^{-1}|^{p})$.

**Hölder norm (2.14), p. 577.**
$$\|f\|_{1,\alpha}=\max\Big\{\sup_x|f(x)|,\ \sup_{x,\mu}|(D^\eta_{A,\mu}f)(x)|,\ \sup_{x,x',\mu}\frac{|U(A(\Gamma_{x,x'}))(D^\eta_{A,\mu}f)(x')-(D^\eta_{A,\mu}f)(x)|}{|x-x'|^\alpha}\Big\},\quad 0<\alpha<1,$$
so a $C^{1,\alpha}$-type norm: sup of $f$ and of the covariant derivative, and a covariant Hölder seminorm of exponent $\alpha$ of the derivative. $\Gamma_{x,x'}$ is a shortest contour.

**Unit-lattice effective operators (1.13)–(1.14).** For $\Lambda\subset\Omega^{(k)}$, with $X|_\Lambda=\chi_\Lambda X\chi_\Lambda$:
$$C^{(k)}_\Lambda(\Omega,A)=\big((\Delta^{(k)}(\Omega,A)+aL^{-2}P(A))|_\Lambda\big)^{-1},\qquad \Delta^{(k)}(\Omega,A)=a_kI-a_k^2Q_k(A)G_k(\Omega,A)Q_k^*(A).$$
Probabilistic meaning (§3 of E3): after $k$ Gaussian block-spin steps the effective Gaussian action on the block variable $\psi$ is $\tfrac12\langle\psi,\Delta^{(k)}\psi\rangle$ ((3.1) ff.); $C^{(k)}$ is the covariance of the $(k{+}1)$-st fluctuation field. $P(A)$ is the covariant projection on the unit lattice attached to $L$-blocks (the operator $Q(A)$ of (3.1), averaging over $L$-blocks $B(z)$ of the unit lattice; UNCERTAIN on exact normalisation; see (3.1)–(3.3)).

## 2. Main results

**Theorem (= Prop. 2.1 of [1]), p. 573, (1.9)–(1.10).** For $\alpha<1$ there exist positive constants $\delta_0,c_0,R_0$, independent of $A,k,\Omega$ and depending on $d,M$ only ("$d$ only" in the statement; $R_0$ and the allowed $M$ depend on $M$, $n_0$ by (2.19)), $c_0$ also on $\alpha$, such that for $e$ sufficiently small, $A$ a regular vector field (1.7), and any $f:\Omega\to\mathbb R^N$:
$$\frac{1}{|x-x'|^{\alpha}}\Big|U(A(\Gamma_{x,x'}))\big(D^\eta_{A,\mu}G_k(\Omega,A)f\big)(x')-\big(D^\eta_{A,\mu}G_k(\Omega,A)f\big)(x)\Big|\le c_0\exp\!\big(-\delta_0\,\mathrm{dist}(\{x,x'\},\mathrm{supp}f)\big)\|f\|_\infty\tag{1.9}$$
for $x,x'\in\Omega$ with $\mathrm{dist}(\{x,x'\},\Omega^c)\ge R_0$. Also
$$|(D^\eta_{A,\mu}G_k(\Omega,A)f)(x)|,\ |(G_k(\Omega,A)f)(x)|\le c_0\exp(-\delta_0\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathrm{supp}f))\|f\|_\infty\tag{1.10}$$
for $x\in\Omega$, $\mathrm{dist}(x,\Omega^c)\ge R_0$. For $\Omega\subset\Omega_0$ and $\delta G_k(\Omega,\Omega_0,A)=G_k(\Omega,A)-G_k(\Omega_0,A)$ (1.11), the same bounds hold (the text says "(1.5) and (1.6)", i.e. the numbering of Part I) with the extra factor
$$\exp\big(-\delta_0\mathrm{dist}(\mathrm{supp}f,\Omega^c)-\delta_0\mathrm{dist}(\mathrm{supp}f,\Omega^c)\big)\tag{1.12}$$
(as printed; the two terms are surely $\mathrm{dist}(\mathrm{supp} f,\Omega^c)$ and $\mathrm{dist}(\mathrm{supp}f',\Omega^c)$ in the two-point version, i.e. UNCERTAIN misprint). For simple $\Omega$ (e.g. rectangular parallelepipeds) the restriction $\mathrm{dist}(\{x,x'\},\Omega^c)\ge R_0$ can be dropped (p. 573; proved at the end of §2, p. 579).

*Lower bound (1.8), p. 573:* "there exists a strictly positive $\gamma_0$, independent of $\eta$, $\Omega$, $A$, such that for $e$ sufficiently small and $A$ regular, $-\Delta^{\eta,N}_{A,\Omega}+aP_k(A)\ge\gamma_0I$". This is asserted in the introduction; it follows from the proof of Lemma 2.1 ((2.28) for the cube with $A=0$ and the perturbation argument, (2.26)–(2.27)); there is no separate statement. The constant for a cube with $A=0$ is explicit: $G_k(\Box,0)\le c_0I$ with $c_0^{-1}=\min\{\pi^2,a_k\}$ (2.27)–(2.28).

**Proposition 2.3 of [1], p. 574, (1.15)–(1.20).** There exist $\delta_0,c_0,\gamma_0,\gamma_1>0$ depending on $d$ and $M$ only such that for arbitrary $\Lambda\subset\Omega^{(k)}$ ($\Lambda$ a sum of big blocks) and $e$ small:
$$\gamma_0I\le\Delta^{(k)}(\Omega,\Lambda)+aL^{-2}P(\Lambda)\le\gamma_1I\tag{1.15}$$
$$|C^{(k)}_\Lambda(\Omega,\Lambda;x,x')|\le c_0\exp(-\delta_0|x-x'|),\quad x,x'\in\Lambda\tag{1.16}$$
(the printed (1.15) reads $\gamma_0I\le\Delta^{(k)}(\Omega,\Lambda)+aL^{-2}P(\Lambda)\le\gamma_1I$, with $\Lambda$ in place of $A$ in the second slot), and, for $\delta C^{(k)}_\Lambda(\Omega,\Lambda)=C^{(k)}_\Lambda(\Omega,\Lambda)-C^{(k)}(\Omega,\Lambda)$ (1.17),
$$|\delta C^{(k)}_\Lambda(\Omega,\Lambda;x,x')|\le c_0\exp\big(-\delta_0(|x-x'|+\mathrm{dist}(x,\Lambda^c)+\mathrm{dist}(x',\Lambda^c))\big)\tag{1.18}$$
(UNCERTAIN: in (1.13)–(1.18) the printed notation mixes $A$ (vector field) and $\Lambda$ (a set); the pages show $C^{(k)}_A(\Omega,A)$ with $A\subset\Omega^{(k)}$ a set and the same letter for the vector field, a notational collision in the printed text, and I have written $\Lambda$ for the set). For $\Omega\subset\Omega_0$ and $\delta C^{(k)}_\Lambda(\Omega,\Omega_0,A)=C^{(k)}_\Lambda(\Omega,A)-C^{(k)}_\Lambda(\Omega_0,A)$ (1.19):
$$|\delta C^{(k)}_\Lambda(\Omega,\Omega_0,A;x,x')|\le c_0\exp\big(-\delta_0(|x-x'|+\mathrm{dist}(x,\Omega^{(k)c})+\mathrm{dist}(x',\Omega^{(k)c}))\big).\tag{1.20}$$

**Proposition 3.1′ of [2], p. 574, (1.21)–(1.22).** Let $\Omega^{(k)}$ be a sum of unit blocks (any subset of $\mathbb Z^d$) and $A$ satisfy $|\partial^\eta_\mu A|\le O(1)p(e)$, $p(e)=a_0(1+|\log e^{-1}|^p)$ (1.21). There is $\gamma_0>0$ depending on $d$ only such that for $e$ small
$$\langle\phi,\Delta^{(k)}(\Omega,A)\phi\rangle\ge\gamma_0\Big(\sum_{\langle x,x'\rangle\subset\Omega^{(k)}}|U(A(\langle x,x'\rangle))\phi(x')-\phi(x)|^2+m^2\sum_{x\in\Omega^{(k)}}|\phi(x)|^2\Big)-O(1)e^{2-\alpha}\sum_{x\in\Omega^{(k)}}|\phi(x)|^2\tag{1.22}$$
for arbitrary $\alpha>0$, with an $O(1)$ constant depending on $\alpha$ and the other constants but independent of $\Omega,k,A,\phi$. (Hence (3.29) of [2].) Here "$\Omega^{(k)}$ is a sum of unit blocks" and $\phi$ is on the unit lattice. For $A=0$ ($e=0$) the error term vanishes (p. 591 remark after (4.7)).

**Theorem on unit-lattice operators, p. 594, §5 (proved in §5; pp. 594–597).** Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb Z^d$ and $A$ a symmetric operator on $L^2(\Omega)$ of $\mathbb R^N$-valued functions with positive constants $\gamma_0,c_0,\delta_0$ such that
$$A\ge\gamma_0I,\qquad|A(x,x')|\le c_0e^{-\delta_0|x-x'|},\quad x,x'\in\Omega.\tag{5.6}$$
Then there are $c_1,\delta_1>0$ such that, for $\Lambda\subset\Omega$, $C_\Lambda=A_\Lambda^{-1}$ ($A_\Lambda=\chi_\Lambda A\chi_\Lambda$), $C_\Omega=A^{-1}$:
$$|C_\Lambda(x,x')|\le c_1e^{-\delta_1|x-x'|},\ x,x'\in\Lambda\tag{5.7}$$
$$|\delta C_\Lambda(x,x')|\le c_1e^{-\delta_1(|x-x'|+\mathrm{dist}(x,\Lambda^c)+\mathrm{dist}(x',\Lambda^c))},\quad\delta C_\Lambda=C_\Lambda-C_\Omega.\tag{5.8}$$
If $B$ is a perturbation such that $A+B$ satisfies (5.6) and in addition
$$|B(x,x')|\le c_0e^{-\delta_0|x-x'|+\mathrm{dist}(x,\Omega^c)+\mathrm{dist}(x',\Omega^c)},\tag{5.9}$$
(printed with the sign pattern as shown; UNCERTAIN: surely $e^{-\delta_0(|x-x'|+\mathrm{dist}(x,\Omega^c)+\mathrm{dist}(x',\Omega^c))}$, i.e. $B$ is localised near the boundary of $\Omega$ and decays away from it) then
$$|A_\Lambda^{-1}(x,x')-(A+B)_\Lambda^{-1}(x,x')|\le c_1e^{-\delta_1(|x-x'|+\mathrm{dist}(x,\Omega^c)+\mathrm{dist}(x',\Omega^c))},\ x,x'\in\Lambda.\tag{5.10}$$
Constants: $\delta_1=\frac12\delta_2M^{-1}$ with $\delta_2=\frac14\delta_0$ (below (5.15) and (5.21)); $M$ is chosen so large that $\gamma_0^{-1}\alpha e^{d\delta_2}c_2^{2d}<1$ (before (5.21)), where "$\alpha$" is the number in (5.15) ($\alpha=O(M^{-1})$) and $c_2$ is the constant of (5.20) (Fourier estimate of the convolution of $2n$ exponentials). $c_1$ is a function of $\delta_0,\gamma_0,c_0$ (p. 597).

*Lemmas used.* Lemma 2.1 (p. 577): for a set $\Box$ that is a sum of at most a few big blocks and $A$ regular, $\|G_k(\Box,A)f\|_2,\ \|D^\eta_{A,\mu}G_k(\Box,A)f\|_2,\ \|G_k(\Box,A)D^{\eta*}_{A,\mu}f\|_2,\ \|D^\eta_{A,\mu}G_k(\Box,A)D^{\eta*}_{A,\nu}f\|_2\le c_2\|f\|_2$ (2.15). Lemma 2.2 (p. 577–578): for $\Box$ a rectangular parallelepiped (sum of few big blocks), $\tilde A$ regular and constant near $\partial\Box$, for $e$ small and $\alpha<1$: $\|G_k(\Box,\tilde A)f\|_{1,\alpha}\le c_1\|f\|_\infty$ (2.16), $c_1=c_1(d,\alpha)$, and $\|G_k(\Box,\tilde A)f\|_q,\ \|D G f\|_q,\ \|G D^{\eta*}f\|_q\le c_2\|f\|_p$ (2.17) for $1\le p,q\le\infty$, $\frac1p-\frac1{p_1}\le\frac1q\le\frac1p$, $p_1>d$, $c_2=c_2(d,p_1)$. Lemma 2.4 (p. 582): for $\alpha<1$ there exist $c_0,\delta_0,c_1$ such that for every $j\ge0$ and every rectangular parallelepiped $\Box\subset L^{-j}\mathbb Z^d$ built of big blocks: $|(G_j(\Box)Q_j^*)(x,y)|,\ |(\partial_\mu^{L^{-j}}G_j(\Box)Q_j^*)(x,y)|\le c_0e^{-\delta_0|x-y|}$ (2.35); the Hölder difference quotient of $\partial G_jQ_j^*$ in the first variable (exponent $\alpha$) is $\le c_1e^{-\delta_0\mathrm{dist}(\{x,x'\},y)}$ (2.36); $|C^{(j)}(\Box;y,y')|\le c_0e^{-\delta_0|y-y'|}$ (2.37). Corollary 2.3 (p. 580): for $f,f'$ scalar field configurations on $\Omega$,
$|\langle f,G_k(\Omega,A)f'\rangle|,\ |\langle f,D G f'\rangle|,\ |\langle f,GD^*f'\rangle|,\ |\langle f,DGD^*f'\rangle|\le c_0e^{-\delta_0\mathrm{dist}(\mathrm{supp}f,\mathrm{supp}f')}\|f\|_2\|f'\|_2$ (2.30), with the extra factor $e^{-\delta_0(\mathrm{dist}(\mathrm{supp}f,\Omega^c)+\mathrm{dist}(\mathrm{supp}f',\Omega^c))}$ for $\delta G_k(\Omega,\Omega_0,A)$ (p. 581). The Corollary needs no restriction on supports of $f,f'$ relative to $\partial\Omega$ and is "a bit stronger than Prop. I.2.1" in that respect.

**Inductive hypotheses.** None; this is a self-contained estimate paper, but several steps are imported from Part I–III (see §7).

## 3. Proof architecture

**§1 (pp. 571–574).** Statements. Reduction: it suffices to prove the theorem for $f$ supported in a unit cube $\Lambda_0=B^k(y_0)$ and sum (p. 575 top).

**§2. Generalized random-walk representation; proof of the Theorem (pp. 574–584).** Mechanism, in order:

1. *Parametrix.* Let $h(x)=\prod h_{j_\mu}(x_\mu)$ be a smooth partition of unity subordinate to cubes $\Box_j=\Omega\cap\{$sum of big blocks with $Mj$ as a vertex$\}$ (cube of side $2M$ centred at $Mj$ if $Mj$ is not on $\partial\Omega$, a union of fewer than $2d$ big blocks if on $\partial\Omega$); $h_j(x)=h(x/M-j)$ with $h\in C_0^\infty(]-\frac23,\frac23[)$, $h=1$ on $[-\frac13,\frac13]$, $\sum_jh_j^2=1$ (p. 575). Modified fields $\tilde A_j=A_0+\theta_jA'$ ($A=A_0+A'$, $A_0$ constant, $A'$ regular and small: $|A'|,|\partial A'|\le c'e^{\beta-1}$ ($c'$ depending on $c,M$) (2.23)), equal to $A$ on $\{|x-Mj|\le\frac34M\}$ and constant near $\partial\Box_j$ (so Neumann boundary conditions on $\Box_j\cap\Omega$ are the conditions of $\Omega$ on $\partial\Omega$). Set $G_0=\sum_jh_jG_k(\Box_j,\tilde A_j)h_j$ (2.2).
2. *Commutator calculus (2.3)–(2.9).* Discrete Leibniz rules $D^\eta_A(h\phi)=h(b_+)D^\eta_A\phi+(\partial^\eta h)(b)\phi(b_-)$ (2.3), summation by parts (2.4)–(2.5), and $P_k(A)h\phi=h P_k(A)\phi+R_k(A,\partial^\eta h)\phi$ (2.8). Because $h_j$ has vanishing normal derivative on $\partial\Box_j$ and is nonzero only on the part of $\partial\Box_j$ lying in $\partial\Omega$, (2.6) holds and
$$\big(-\Delta^{\eta,N}_{A,\Omega}+m_k^2+P_k(A)\big)G_0=I-R,\qquad R=\sum_jK_jG_k(\Box_j,\tilde A_j)h_j,\tag{2.9}-(2.11)$$
with $K_j$ a first-order operator whose coefficients are $\partial^\eta h_j=O(M^{-1})$ and $\Delta^\eta h_j=O(M^{-2})$ (2.10) (the sign convention for the terms in (2.9) as printed; the $a_k$ in front of $P_k$ is $a_k$, consistent with (1.6)).
3. *Neumann series.* $\|R\|=O(M^{-1})$ in suitable norms (Lemma 2.1 gives $L^2$-norm smallness), so $G_k(\Omega,A)=G_0(I-R)^{-1}=\sum_nG_0R^n$ (2.12), a *random-walk representation*: paths $\omega=(\omega_0,\dots,\omega_n)$ of cube labels with $|\omega_{i+1}-\omega_i|_\infty\le1$ (neighbouring cubes only, because $G(\Box_j)h_jK_{j'}G(\Box_{j'})=0$ if $|j-j'|_\infty>1$):
$$G_k(\Omega,A)=\sum_\omega h_{\omega_0}G_k(\Box_{\omega_0},\tilde A_{\omega_0})h_{\omega_0}K_{\omega_1}G_k(\Box_{\omega_1},\tilde A_{\omega_1})h_{\omega_1}\cdots K_{\omega_n}G_k(\Box_{\omega_n},\tilde A_{\omega_n})h_{\omega_n}.\tag{2.13}$$
4. *Local estimates (Lemmas 2.1, 2.2).* For each step one needs: $L^2$ bounds (Lemma 2.1) and sup/Hölder/$L^p\to L^q$ bounds (Lemma 2.2) for the local propagators, each with norm $c_2\cdot O(1)M^{-1}$ per step $K_{\omega}G(\Box_\omega)h_\omega$.
5. *Proof of the Theorem from the lemmas (pp. 578–579).* For $x,x'$ at distance $\ge R_0$ from $\Omega^c$ and $|x-x'|\le1$, bound term by term in (2.13) in the $\|\cdot\|_{1,\alpha}$ norm. Split the paths into $n\le n_0$ and $n>n_0$ with $R_0=\mathrm{diam}(\bigcup_{\omega}\bigcup_{i\le n_0}\Box_{\omega_i})+2M$ (2.19). For $n\le n_0$ all cubes are interior, and Lemma 2.2 ($L^\infty\to L^\infty$ for each factor) gives $\sum c_1(c_2O(1)M^{-1})^n\|f\|_\infty$ (2.20). For $n>n_0$ the first steps use the $L^{p_1}\to L^\infty$ and $L^{p}\to L^q$ estimates of (2.17) with $\frac1{p_1}=\frac1{2n_0}$, $p_1=2n_0>d+1$, $n_0=d$, interpolated through the chain $L^\infty\to L^{p_1}\to L^{p_1/2}\cdots\to L^2$ (the figure p. 583), the middle uses $L^2\to L^2$, to again control the sum in $L^\infty$ (2.21). Counting: at most $2^d(3^d)^{n-1}2^d$ paths of length $n$ with endpoints in prescribed positions, and $n\ge M^{-1}\mathrm{dist}(\{x,x'\},\mathrm{supp}f)-2$ is needed to connect supp $f$ to $x$; so the sum is $\le\frac{2^{d+1}}{e^2}e^{-M^{-1}\mathrm{dist}(\{x,x'\},\mathrm{supp}f)}\|f\|_\infty$ provided $3^dc_2O(1)M^{-1}\le e^{-1}$ (2.22). Thus $\delta_0\approx M^{-1}$. The difference $\delta G_k(\Omega,\Omega_0,A)$ is obtained by subtracting the two representations: terms with all $\Box_{\omega_i}$ interior to $\Omega$ coincide and cancel; the surviving terms have a cube meeting $\partial\Omega$, which gives the extra factor (1.12) (restriction $n\ge(2M)^{-1}(\ldots)-3$). For rectangular $\Omega$ all $\Box_j$ are cubes, Lemma 2.2 applies to every factor and the restriction $\mathrm{dist}\ge R_0$ is unnecessary.
6. *Proof of Lemma 2.1 (pp. 579–580).* Expand $G_k(\Box,A)$ in $A'$ about the constant configuration $A_0$ using Part I formula (I.3.16) (not reproduced); $G^{1/2}_k(\Box,A_0)(-\Delta^{\eta,N}_{A,\Box}+m_k^2+a_kP_k(A))G_k^{1/2}(\Box,A_0)=I-G^{1/2}_k V_kG^{1/2}_k$ (2.24) with $V_k$ a small first-order perturbation, $\|G^{1/2}V_kG^{1/2}\|_{2,2}\le O(1)e^\beta$ (2.25) after reduction to $A_0=0$ by a gauge transformation, hence (2.26) a convergent Neumann series (for $e$ small). The three needed bounds for $A_0=0$ are quadratic-form facts: on each unit block $\Delta$, $-\Delta^N_\Delta\ge\pi^2$ on the orthogonal complement of constants; $P_k$ is a projection onto constants; hence $\langle\phi,(-\Delta^N_\Delta+a_kP_k)\phi\rangle\ge\min\{\pi^2,a_k\}\|\phi\|^2_{L^2(\Delta)}$ (2.27), and since $-\Delta^N_\Box\ge\sum_{\Delta\subset\Box}-\Delta^N_\Delta$ (fewer bonds), $0<G_k(\Box,0)\le c_0I$, $c_0^{-1}=\min\{\pi^2,a_k\}$ (2.28); the derivative bound follows from $\|\partial^\eta_\mu G^{1/2}f\|^2\le\langle f,G^{1/2}(-\Delta^N+m^2+aP)G^{1/2}f\rangle=\|f\|^2$ (2.29). This is the heart of the $L^2$ part; elementary.
7. *Proof of Lemma 2.2 (pp. 581–583).* Same decomposition $A=A_0+A'$ where now $A_0$ is the constant to which $\tilde A$ equals near $\partial\Box$, so $A'$ has compact support in $\Box$; $G_k(\Box,\tilde A)=\sum_{n\ge0}G_k(\Box,A_0)(V_kG_k(\Box,A_0))^n$ (2.31). The term $D^{\eta*}_{A_0}F_{1,k}(-A')$ in $V_k$ is a first-order operator with small coefficients because of the compact support of $A'$ (2.32): this is the only place where constancy of $\tilde A$ near $\partial\Box$ is used. With $D^\eta_A=U(A')D^\eta_{A_0}+F_{1,k}(A')$ the series converges in $\|\cdot\|_{1,\alpha}$ (2.33). The case $A_0=0$ (or constant) reduces by gauge transformation to the scalar one-component operator $G_k(\Box)=G_k(\Box,0)$ (applied to each component; $1$ = identity on $\mathbb R^N$), and for this the proof uses the *RG equation (2.43) of [1] rescaled to the $\eta$-lattice*:
$$G_k(\Box)=C^{(0),\eta}(\Box)+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}a_j^2(L^j\eta)^{-4}G^\eta_j(\Box)Q_j^*C^{(j),L^{-j}\eta}(\Box)Q_jG_j^\eta(\Box)\tag{2.34}$$
(the factor $(L^j\eta)^{-4}$ as I read it; UNCERTAIN, since (2.38)–(2.41) use $a_j^2(L^j\eta)^{1-\alpha}$, $a_j^2L^j\eta$, so the printed power in (2.34) looks inconsistent with later formulas). This decomposes the Neumann Green's function over the $k$ scales; it is the multiscale decomposition of the propagator (compare P9 (4.17) / the Gaussian RG identity in the O(N) series). Lemma 2.4 gives uniform bounds for each scale, then the Hölder bound (2.38)–(2.39) follows since the scale-$j$ term contributes $(L^j\eta)^{1-\alpha}$, with geometric sum $\sum_j(L^{-j})^{1-\alpha}$, and the sums $\sum_xe^{-\delta_0|x|}$ converge: $\|G_k(\Box)f\|_{1,\alpha}\le c_1'\|f\|_\infty$, with $c_1'$ built from $c_0,c_1,\sum_xe^{-\delta_0|x|},\sum_j(L^{-j})^{1-\alpha}$ (p. 583). For $L^p\to L^q$: sup-norm case ($p=q=\infty$) is (2.16); $p=q=1$ by duality; $1<p=q<\infty$ by Riesz–Thorin (Ref. 6); $L^{p_1}\to L^\infty$ for $G$, $\partial G$, $GD^*$ from (2.40)–(2.41) with Hölder's inequality ($p_1>d$ is needed so that $\sum_j(L^j\eta)^{1-d/p_1}$ converges: precisely $\sum_j(L^{-j})^{1-d/p_1}<\infty$); then duality ($p_1'^{-1}+p_1^{-1}=1$) and interpolation fill the parallelogram of the figure on p. 583: $\frac1p-\frac1{p_1}\le\frac1q\le\frac1p$.
8. *Proof of Lemma 2.4 (pp. 584–586).* (2.37) is the special case of Prop. 2.3 for $A=0$ (§5 of E3, which uses only Corollary 2.3). (2.35)–(2.36): for rectangular $\Box$ built from unit blocks, express the Neumann Green's function by *multiple reflection* from the free-boundary propagator $G_j$ on $\xi\mathbb Z^d$ ($\xi=L^{-j}$), (2.42), so it suffices to prove the bounds for $G_j$ on the full lattice. Fourier transform (2.43): $(-\Delta^\xi+m_j^2+a_jQ_j^*Q_j)\phi_0=f$ becomes (2.44)–(2.46) with $\Delta^\xi(p)=\sum_\mu|e^{-i\xi p_\mu}-1|^2/\xi^2+m_j^2$ and $u_j(p)=\prod_\mu\frac{e^{-ip_\mu}-1}{e^{-i\xi p_\mu}-1}\cdot\xi^{-1}$ (the Fourier symbol of the block-sum, so $Q_j^*$ couples momenta $p'+l$, $l=2\pi n$, $|n_\mu|\le\frac{L^j-1}2$); the solution is an explicit rank-one-type formula in the sums over the aliases $l$. For $G_jQ_j^*$ (2.47)–(2.48) the kernel is an explicit integral over the Brillouin zone $[-\pi,\pi]^d$ of an integrand whose bound (2.50)–(2.51) comes from $|u_j|\sim\prod|p'_\mu|/|p'_\mu+l_\mu|$, $\Delta^\xi(p'+l)\ge O(1)\frac{|p'|^2+m_j^2}{\ldots}$, and the denominator is bounded below: $a_j\sum_l|u_j|^2\Delta^1(p')/\Delta^\xi+\Delta^1(p')\ge a_j|u_j|\Delta^1/\Delta^\xi\ge O(1)>0$ (2.50). The Hölder difference (2.49) is bounded by an integrand uniformly bounded in $p'$ (2.51) using $|e^{i(p'+l)\cdot(x-x')}-1|/|x-x'|^\alpha\le O(1)|p'+l|^\alpha$ and $\sum_l(1+|l|)^{-1-(1-\alpha)/d}<\infty$. Exponential decay then follows from analytic continuation of the integrand in a complex neighbourhood of $[-\pi,\pi]^d$, claimed independent of $j$, then shifting the contour in the direction $x'-y$ (p. 586). The statement "this neighbourhood can be chosen independently of $j$ and the expression is bounded also there" is asserted as "more troublesome, but equally elementary" without writing it out (p. 586).

**§3 (pp. 586–589). Proof of Prop. 2.3.** Explicit formula for $C^{(k)}_\Lambda(\Omega,A)$ by Gaussian integration: write $C^{(k)}$ as a Gaussian moment $\langle\psi(y)\psi^*(y')\rangle$ for the density $\exp[-\frac12aL^{d-2}\sum_z|(Q(A)\psi)(z)|^2-\frac12\langle\psi,\Delta^{(k)}\psi\rangle]$; represent $\Delta^{(k)}$ as the Gaussian-integral effective form of a joint measure in $(\psi,\phi)$ with $\phi$ on $\Omega$ ((3.1)); gauge-transform the $\psi$-integral blockwise, perform the elementary Gaussian block integrals using the general formula (3.3) (moments of $\sum x_j$ under a one-block Gaussian), and use the free flow $a_{k+1}=aa_k/(aL^{-2}+a_k)$ to get
$$C^{(k)}_\Lambda(\Omega,A;y,y')=\Big\langle\big(-\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{a_k}L^{-2}q_{k+1}(y)+q_k(y)\big),G_k(\Omega,\Lambda,A)\big(-\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{a_k}L^{-2}q_{k+1}(y')+q_k(y')\big)\Big\rangle-\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{a_k^2}L^{-2}P(A;y,y')+\tfrac{\delta_{y,y'}}{a_k}\tag{3.8}$$
with $G_k(\Omega,\Lambda,A)=(-\Delta^{\eta,N}_{A,\Omega}+m_k^2+a_kP_k(A)(\Omega\setminus B^k(\Lambda))+a_{k+1}L^{-2}P_{k+1}(A)B^k(\Lambda))^{-1}$ (3.5): on $\Lambda$ the projection is onto $L$-block-constants ($B^{k+1}(z)$, $z\in\Lambda'$), outside $\Lambda$ on unit-block constants. The functions $q_k,q_{k+1}$ (3.7) have $L^2$ norms $1$ and $L^{-d/2}$ and supports in $B^k(y)$, $B^{k+1}(z(y))$. Hence (1.16), (1.17)–(1.18) are consequences of Corollary 2.3, which was proved for $G_k(\Omega,A)$ but works with trivial changes for $G_k(\Omega,\Lambda,A)$; the only change is that $\min\{\pi^2,a_k\}$ is replaced by $\min\{\pi^2L^{-2},a_{k+1}L^{-2}\}$ on $L$-blocks, so the bounds on $C^{(k)}$ depend on $L$ (p. 588). The upper bound in (1.15) is elementary ((5.1)). The lower bound in (1.15) is from the identity $\Delta^{(k)}=a_kI-a_k^2QGQ^*$ and $G_k(\Omega,\Lambda,A)-G^\eta_{k+1}(\Omega,\Lambda,A)=\ldots$ (3.9), with $G_k-G_{k+1}$ having decay (3.10), proving (1.14) (the domain-change statement) — this is the L² estimate for $\delta C$.

**§4 (pp. 589–593). Proof of Prop. 3.1′ (lower bound for $\Delta^{(k)}$).** Write $\langle\phi,\Delta^{(k)}\phi\rangle=\sum_x[a_k|\phi(x)|^2-a_k^2\phi(x)\cdot(Q_k(A)(\ldots)^{-1}Q_k^*)\phi(x)]$ using $G_k\le((a_kP_k+m^2)|_\Omega)^{-1}$ (4.1); one block at a time this is a one-block Gaussian integral (4.2)–(4.3) giving $\ge\sum a_km^2/(a_k+m^2)|\phi|^2\ge\frac{a_k}{a_k+O(1)}m^2\sum|\phi|^2$ (4.4), the mass part. For the gradient part, split the bond set of $\Omega^{(k)}$ into $2d$ families $B_i$ such that each point lies on at most one bond of each family (even/odd bonds in each direction), apply Neumann bracketing to the pair of blocks $\Delta(x,x')=B^k(x)\cup B^k(x')$ to get (4.5), (4.6): it suffices to prove the *two-block inequality*
$$a_k|\phi(x)|^2+a_k|\phi(x')|^2-a_k^2\langle\phi,Q_kG_k(\Delta(x,x'),A)Q_k^*\phi\rangle\ge\gamma_0'|U(A(\langle x,x'\rangle))\phi(x')-\phi(x)|^2-O(1)e^2p^2(e)(|\phi(x)|^2+|\phi(x')|^2)\tag{4.7}$$
with $\gamma_0'$ independent of $k,\langle x,x'\rangle,A$ ($\gamma_0=\frac12\min\{\frac1{2d}\gamma_0',\frac{a_k}{a_k+O(1)}\}$). Expand $A=A_0+A'$ on $\Delta(x,x')$ and treat first-order terms in $e$ (4.8): after gauging away $A_0$ these are bounded using properties of $\phi^{(k)}=a_kG_k(\Delta(x,x'),A_0)Q_k^*(A_0)\phi$, namely $\phi^{(k)}(z)=\frac1{1+m^2}\phi(x)+O(U\phi(x')-\phi(x))$, $|\partial^\eta\phi^{(k)}|=O(U\phi(x')-\phi(x))$ (4.9), quoted "from II.2.75"; $\phi(x)\cdot q\phi(x)=0$ ($q$ antisymmetric) kills the leading term; with Young ($\delta>0$ arbitrary, constant $\delta^{-1}e^2p^2(e)$) one gets (4.10)–(4.11). Then it remains to prove (4.12), i.e. (4.14) for $A_0=0$: with $\phi'=\phi''+$ shift, the inequality is that the Gaussian integral (4.15)–(4.16) equals $\mathrm{const}\cdot\exp(-\frac12A_0|\psi|^2)$ with $A_0\ge\gamma_0''>0$ depending only on $d,a$. This is shown by the Fourier transform in $\psi=\phi''_k(x')-\phi''_k(x)$ ("duality transformation", p. 593): $A_0^{-1}=\eta+\langle f_{\langle x,x'\rangle},A^{-1}f_{\langle x,x'\rangle}\rangle$ (4.21), where $f$ is the bond-dipole $\sum_b\eta^{d-1}(\delta^\eta(\cdot-b_+)-\delta^\eta(\cdot-b_-))$ and $\langle f,A^{-1}f\rangle\le\langle f,G_k(\Delta)f\rangle+\langle f,G_k(\Delta')f\rangle=2\sum_{y,y'\in\Delta_1}\eta^{2(d-1)}G_k(\Delta;y,y')$, $\Delta_1$ a face of the block (4.22). This is bounded by a constant depending on $d,a$ only by the $L^2$-bound (2.28) (UNCERTAIN: the text says "a constant depending on $d$ and $a$ only" and does not spell out the bound; it follows from $\|G\|\le c_0$ and $\eta^{2(d-1)}\#\Delta_1^2\sim1$).

**§5 (pp. 593–597). Theorem on unit lattice operators.** First proves (1.11)–(1.12) of Prop. 2.3 again from Prop. 3.1′: $\Delta^{(k)}(\Omega,\Lambda)+aL^{-2}P(\Lambda)\ge\gamma_0\langle\phi,\phi\rangle-O(e^{2-\alpha})$ (5.1)–(5.3) (decompose $A$ on each block into constant plus regular, gauge to the Neumann Laplacian plus projection on constants: bounded below by $\frac12\gamma_0\min\{\pi^2L^{-2},aL^{-2}\}$), the kernel decays by Corollary 2.3 (5.4), and a change of the domain gives (5.5); these are exactly the hypotheses (5.6), (5.9) of the abstract theorem, so Prop. 2.3 follows. The abstract theorem is proved by the same partition-of-unity scheme: cubes $\Box_j=\Lambda\cap\{x:-M\le x_\mu-Mj_\mu<M\}$ ((5.11)), $C=\sum_jh_jC_jh_j$, $C_j=C_{\Box_j}$ (5.12); $A_\Lambda C=I-R$ with $R=\sum_{j,j'}R_{j,j'}C_{j'}h_{j'}$ and $R_{j,j}=-\Box_j[A,h_j]\Box_j$, $R_{j,j'}=-(1-\Box_{j'})h_j^2Ah_{j'}$ for $j\ne j'$ (5.13)–(5.14); $|R_{j,j'}(x,x')|\le c_0e^{-\delta_0|x-x'|}O(1)|x-x'|/M$ etc. gives $\|R_{j,j'}\|\le\alpha e^{-\delta_2|j-j'|}$, $\alpha=O(M^{-1})$ arbitrarily small for large $M$ (5.15), and $\|R\|\le\gamma_0^{-1}\alpha\sum_je^{-\delta_2|j|}$ small (5.16). Then $C_\Lambda=\sum_nCR^n$ with the random-walk expansion
$$C_\Lambda=\sum_\omega h_{\omega_0}C_{\omega_0}h_{\omega_0}R_{\omega_1,\omega_2}C_{\omega_2}h_{\omega_2}\cdots R_{\omega_{2n-1},\omega_{2n}}C_{\omega_{2n}}h_{\omega_{2n}},\quad\max_\mu|\omega_{2i,\mu}-\omega_{2i+1,\mu}|\le1\tag{5.17}$$
(the paths are "arbitrary indices $j$ but with the restrictions (5.17)"); sums over $j$'s factorize over components with $|j|=\sum|j_\mu|$, and the sum of $2n$ factors $e^{-\delta_2|j|}$ is the convolution $g_{2n}=g_1^{*2n}$, whose Fourier transform is $(1-e^{-2\delta_2})^n/((1-e^{-ip-\delta_2})(1-e^{ip-\delta_2}))^n$ (5.19), giving $|g_n(j)|\le c_2^ne^{-\frac12\delta_2|j|}$ (5.20); choosing $M$ with $\gamma_0^{-1}\alpha e^{d\delta_2}c_2^{2d}<1$ gives (5.21): $|C_\Lambda(x,x')|\le c_1e^{-\frac12\delta_2M^{-1}|x-x'|}$, i.e. (5.7) with $\delta_1=\frac12\delta_2M^{-1}$. (5.8) follows by subtracting the representations for $\Lambda$ and $\Omega$: terms with all cubes disjoint from $\Lambda^c$ cancel; the others carry the factor $e^{-\frac12\delta_2M^{-1}(\mathrm{dist}(x,\Lambda^c)+\mathrm{dist}(x',\Lambda^c)+|x-x'|)}$ (5.22)–(5.23). (5.10) similarly from the difference of the representations (5.24), using $R(A)_{j,j'}-R(A+B)_{j,j'}=-R(B)_{j,j'}$ and (5.9) in the form $\|R(B)_{j,j'}\|\le\alpha e^{-\delta_2|j-j'|}e^{-\delta_0(\mathrm{dist}(\Box_j,\Omega^c)+\mathrm{dist}(\Box_{j'},\Omega^c))}$ (5.25), $C(A)_j-C(A+B)_j=A_{\Box_j}^{-1}B_{\Box_j}(A+B)^{-1}_{\Box_j}$ (5.26), $\|C(A)_j-C(A+B)_j\|\le\gamma_0^{-2}c_0'e^{-2\delta_0\mathrm{dist}(\Box_j,\Omega^c)}$ (5.27), and the resulting estimate (5.27) ff.

## 6. Constants and parameters

Order of choice, as it can be reconstructed:
1. $d,N$, $L$ (small integer $>1$), $\alpha\in(0,1)$ (Hölder exponent), $a\in$ compact neighbourhood of 1 ("close to 1", never stated precisely), $m^2\ge0$.

**On the $\alpha$-dependence of the Hölder constant (relevant to P2's $C_\alpha=O(\alpha^{-d-1})$).** E3 states only that $c_1$ (Lemma 2.2) and $c_0$ depend on $\alpha$ and $d$; *no explicit power of $\alpha$ is stated anywhere in E3*. From the proof, the $\alpha$-dependence of $c_1$ comes from (i) the geometric sum $\sum_{j\ge0}(L^{-j})^{1-\alpha}=(1-L^{-(1-\alpha)})^{-1}\sim((1-\alpha)\ln L)^{-1}$ (2.39), and (ii) the lattice sum in (2.51), $\big(\sum_{l\in\mathbb Z}(1+|l|)^{-1-(1-\alpha)/d}\big)^d\sim(d/(1-\alpha))^d$. Together $c_1\lesssim O(1)(1-\alpha)^{-d-1}$ as the Hölder exponent $\alpha\to1$. This is a reconstruction from the printed estimates (not a statement in the paper). It matches P2's $O(1)\alpha_{P2}^{-d-1}$ if P2's exponent is $1-\alpha_{E3}$: P2 (1.15) bounds the Hölder quotient of $\partial^\eta\phi$ by $K_1C_\alpha(L^j\eta)^{-2+\alpha}\delta$ and divides by $|x-x'|^{1-\alpha}$ (UNCERTAIN: read from the OCR), so P2's $\alpha$ is the deficit from exponent 1, as in this reconstruction. So P2's attribution is consistent with E3 only through this implicit dependence, not as a quoted statement.

1. **The statements of Part I are in the introduction as "Theorem (Prop. 2.1 of [1])", but the proof uses unproven formulas of [1] and [2]**: (I.3.15), (I.3.16), (I.3.44) (expansion of $G_k(\Box,A)$ in $A'$, giving (2.24)), (II.2.75) (bound (4.9)), Lemma II.2.4 (gauge transformation). These are not reproduced; (2.25) is stated as "we can easily prove". Reproducing them is gauge-specific.
2. **Analytic strip uniform in $j$ in Lemma 2.4** (p. 586): "It is more troublesome, but equally elementary, to prove that this neighbourhood can be chosen independently of $j$ and that the expression is bounded also in this neighbourhood." This is the key input giving $j$-uniform exponential decay of $G_jQ_j^*$ and its Hölder derivative. Not written out; a Combes–Thomas argument would replace it.
3. **Printed formula (2.34) vs (2.38)–(2.41)**: power of $L^j\eta$ and $a_j$ inconsistent (the later formulas have $a_j^2L^j\eta$ and $(L^j\eta)^{1-\alpha}$). Probably a misprint in (2.34).
4. **Notation collisions**: letter $A$ is both the vector field and a set in (1.13)–(1.20) (set $\Lambda$ here), and $\alpha$ is both the Hölder exponent and the small number $O(M^{-1})$ in (5.15)–(5.27). Also "(1.5) and (1.6)" on p. 573 refers to Part I numbering, and (1.12) is printed with a duplicated $\mathrm{supp}f$ (the second should be $\mathrm{supp}f'$ in the two-point version).
5. **(5.9) as printed** has the signs of the distance terms apparently misplaced (it should be $-\delta_0(\ldots)$ on all three terms); (5.25) uses the corrected form.
6. **"$d$ only"** versus "$d,M$": the Theorem says the constants depend on $d$ only, Prop. 2.3 says $d,M$; $M$ itself is chosen depending on $d$ and $c_2$, so this is consistent but never says so; also $R_0$ depends on $M,n_0$.
7. **Dependence on $L$ and $a$** is not tracked; Prop. 2.3 constants depend on $L$ (p. 588) via $\min\{\pi^2L^{-2},a_{k+1}L^{-2}\}$; in the O(N) setting $L$ is fixed, so harmless.
8. **(1.7) "regular field"** constant: $|\partial A|\le ce^{\beta-1}$, with $e$ small and $\beta>0$ a gauge-theory scaling; irrelevant for $A=0$.
9. **Prop. 3.1′ first-order terms (4.8)–(4.11)** are quoted from [2],[3]; the proof says "formally we could rely on the estimates of the third paper, but they were derived in a different setting, so let us repeat this part of the argument", then still refers to II.2.75 for (4.9).
10. **No statement for variable (non-constant) potentials or complex potentials.** The operator contains only a constant $m^2$ and $aP_k$. P2/P3 need $-\Delta^D+Q^*aQ+\nu+\alpha(x)$ with $\alpha$ real or small complex, $|\alpha|\lesssim(L^j\eta)^{-2}\delta$ varying with scale (see §9).
11. **Boundary conditions**: Neumann only (and a combination Neumann/projection in (3.5)); P2's Green's function is for *Dirichlet* conditions on $\Omega_1$ (P2: $-\Delta^{D,\eta}_{\Omega_1}$). Dirichlet is not in E3 (see §9).

## 8. Preliminary formalization notes

Self-contained and formalizable: (a) §5 abstract theorem (symmetric positive exponentially local operators on $\ell^2(\mathbb Z^d\supset\Omega)$ have exponentially decaying inverse, localized dependence on the domain): needs only finite-dimensional linear algebra on $\mathbb R^{N\Omega}$ (for finite $\Omega$ on a torus), the geometric series, and a convolution estimate on $\mathbb Z^d$; the cleanest piece of the paper (the one that would become a reusable lemma). (b) The $L^2$ part (Lemma 2.1, (2.27)–(2.29), Corollary 2.3): quadratic-form bounds, Neumann bracketing, partition-of-unity calculus on the discrete torus. Hardest: Lemma 2.2/2.4 sup-norm and Hölder estimates uniform over $k$ scales, with explicit Fourier analysis of aliased block sums (2.42)–(2.51) (the paper itself leaves the $j$-uniform complex strip unproved); Mathlib has Fourier series and `Real.pi`-type inequalities but no discrete multi-dimensional Fourier machinery for this; Riesz–Thorin is in Mathlib's `MeasureTheory` interpolation development (guess; status uncertain).

## 10. Relevance to the formalization

**Interfaces.** For the O(N) series the usable output of E3 is a family of Green's function statements for the scalar operator $H=-\Delta^{\eta,\bullet}_\Omega+Q^*aQ+\nu+\alpha$ on tori/domains. The statements that would become interfaces are: (i) $L^2$ lower bound and $\|GD^*\|_{2,2}$ style bounds uniform in $k$ ((1.8), (2.15), (2.28)–(2.29)); (ii) off-diagonal $L^2$ exponential decay (2.30) (Corollary 2.3), which is all that P3 §3 and P4 need for the $L^2$-type localization of the resolvent and of $C^{(k)}$; (iii) sup-norm, Hölder and domain-comparison bounds (1.9)–(1.12), $C_\alpha=O((1-\alpha)^{-d-1})$ for P2; (iv) the abstract §5 theorem for unit-lattice operators (5.6)–(5.10), (5.17) (P3 §3, P4 §2). (iv) is both easy and reusable *as stated* and I would take it as a lemma with its own statement (for finite $\Omega$, i.e. tori, uniformly in volume).

**Reusable / gauge-specific.** Reusable: partition-of-unity commutator calculus (2.1)–(2.13); Neumann bracketing and spectral-gap bound (2.27)–(2.29); the Gaussian-integration formula (3.8) for the effective covariance (equivalent, in the scalar case, to the Gaussian RG identity $C^{(k)}$ and the free flow $a_{k+1}$); the scale decomposition (2.34) (this is the multiscale decomposition used in P9 (4.17) and P2 (4.2)–(4.4)); the two-block lower bound (4.7)/(4.14) for $e=0$ (the proof of $\Delta^{(k)}\ge\gamma_0(\ldots)$ via bond families and Fourier duality (4.21)–(4.22)). Gauge-specific and irrelevant: $U(A)$, $q$, $e$, Prop. 3.1′'s first-order terms in $A'$ (4.8)–(4.11), the reduction to constant $A_0$ by gauge transformations, (2.24)–(2.26), (2.31)–(2.33), the $p(e)$ regularity (1.21).

**Different, more standard proofs (suggestions, not claims).** The needed bounds for the scalar operator can likely be obtained without the specific Fourier/aliasing computations of (2.42)–(2.51):
1. *Exponential decay (all norms $L^2$, then $\ell^\infty$):* a **Combes–Thomas argument**: conjugate $H$ by $e^{\epsilon\theta\cdot x}$ with $|\epsilon|\le c\min\{1,\sqrt{\text{gap}}\}$ at the *unit* scale; the commutator is a first-order perturbation of size $O(\epsilon)$ relative to $\partial^\eta$ and $O(\epsilon^2)$ relative to the gap, so $H_\epsilon$ is invertible with the same bound, giving $\ell^2$ off-diagonal decay at rate $\delta_0\sim\epsilon$ (with Cauchy–Schwarz on local averages and bounds on $\alpha$ for the $\alpha$-dependence). The gap is supplied by P2/P3's lower bounds ($\gamma_0$, the term $Q^*aQ$ at the right scale, the $\nu$ term). This replaces §2 steps 1–5 and the §5 theorem (for the decay part).
2. *Sup-norm and Hölder estimates:* **discrete elliptic regularity** (De Giorgi–Nash–Moser / Schauder for the finite-difference Laplacian with bounded potentials): the discrete Laplacian with potential has Green's function bounds $|G(x,y)|\lesssim|x-y|^{2-d}$ (scale-invariant) uniformly in the potential's sign when bounded below by the mass; first-difference Hölder estimates follow from discrete Calderón–Zygmund/Schauder estimates, or by comparison with the free lattice Green's function plus Neumann series in the potential $\alpha$ (as in P2's own (3.31)–(3.43)), combined with the scale decomposition (2.34) of the block-averaging term. For $d\ge3$ the scale-by-scale bound $\sum_j(L^j\eta)^{1-\alpha}$ in (2.39) is a short geometric sum.
3. *Dependence on the block term $Q^*aQ$:* treat $Q^*aQ$ as a bounded nonnegative operator of range one per block and use a **Neumann/resolvent series with block decomposition** (the P3 (2.2) cube partition) or the **Schur complement / Gaussian-integral identity** (3.8)–(3.9), which gives $G$ in terms of $G^{\text{free}}=(-\Delta+\nu)^{-1}$ and a finite-rank (block-count) correction; the decay of $(\ldots)^{-1}$ then follows from decay of the free resolvent and a small rank-one-per-block correction. This is the cleanest way to deal with scalar $A=0$ and might reproduce E3's (2.34) in one line.
4. *Spectral/positivity inputs* ((1.8), (2.28), (4.7)–(4.22)) are elementary and, for $A=0$, can be done in a few lines with Neumann bracketing and the Poincaré inequality on a unit cube.

All four are suggestions; I have not verified that they give the exact uniformities in $\alpha$, $\nu$, $\lambda$ that P2–P7 need (these require tracking $(L^j\eta)^{-2}$-scale weights and complex $\alpha$ with $|\alpha|\le c(L^j\eta)^{-2}$, i.e. a *large* perturbation of the potential relative to unit-scale quantities; the Combes–Thomas argument has to be done scale by scale, and this is where [2b]'s (2.57) and P3's (2.5)–(2.8) do the work). E3 alone does not provide those weighted bounds; it provides the unit-scale lower bound, decay and Hölder pieces for a *constant* mass.
