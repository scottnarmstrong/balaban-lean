## 0. Identification

T. Balaban, "Propagators and renormalization transformations for lattice gauge theories. II", Commun. Math. Phys. 96 (1984) 223-250. Bundle key E5 (`sources/BF01240221.pdf`). 28 pages. Received January 12, 1984, revised June 28, 1984; communicated by A. Jaffe; supported by NSF PHY-82-03669 (Harvard).

Summary. The paper is the multiscale extension of Paper I (E4). Paper I studies the Gaussian quadratic form $\langle\partial A,\partial A\rangle$ for lattice gauge fields $A$ (bond variables) with one averaging restriction ($L$-block averages). Here the restrictions are imposed on a nested sequence of domains $\Omega_1\supset\Omega_2\supset\dots\supset\Omega_k$ (p. 224, (2.1)-(2.4)), with block averages of order $j$ prescribed on the layer $\Lambda_j=\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+1}$ ("restrictions of different scales on disjoint subdomains", Introduction p. 223). Such sequences are the "large-field region" geometry of the upper bound in [2] (p. 224, p. 229). The paper (i) solves the associated gauge-fixed variational problem and writes the Green's function operators $G=\Delta_a^{-1}$, $QGQ^*$, $H=GQ^*(QGQ^*)^{-1}$ (Section A, (2.5)-(2.35)); (ii) proves, by a random-walk expansion over cubes of mixed scales with a partition of unity, exponential decay and regularity bounds for the scalar gauge-fixing operator $G'=\Delta_a'^{-1}$ and for $(Q'G'^2Q'^*)^{-1}$ (Section B, (2.37)-(2.88), Lemma 2.1, Props. 2.2, 2.3); (iii) does the same for $G$, $(QGQ^*)^{-1}$ and $H$ (Section C, Props. 2.5-2.7, Cor. 2.8, (2.89)-(2.151)); (iv) proves a lower bound for the covariance of the Gaussian integral in a $(k+1)$-st renormalization step (Lemma 2.4, (2.152)-(2.157)). The authors' summary (p. 249): "This Corollary and Proposition 2.6 are our main technical results."

## 1. Setting and notation

Lattice. $T_\eta=\eta\mathbb Z^d/(\text{period})$, a torus, $\eta>0$; $|x-y|=\sum_\mu|x_\mu-y_\mu|$ ($\ell^1$) (p. 223). $L$ is the block factor of Paper I; $L^j\eta$ is the length scale of order-$j$ blocks. $B^j(y)$ is the block of $T_\eta$ of side $L^j\eta$ attached to $y\in T^{(j)}_{L^j\eta}$ (the lattice of block centres of spacing $L^j\eta$); for a set $A\subset T^{(j)}_{L^j\eta}$, $B^j(A)=\bigcup_{y\in A}B^j(y)$. (Block conventions are those of Paper I; not restated here: UNCERTAIN on centre vs. corner.) Sets of sites are identified with the set of bonds having at least one endpoint in the set (p. 224).

Multiscale geometry (p. 224, (2.1)-(2.4)). A sequence $\Omega_1\supset\dots\supset\Omega_k$, $\Omega_j\subset T_\eta$, with
$$\Omega_j=B^j(\Omega^{(j)}_j),\quad \Omega^{(j)}_j\subset T^{(j)}_{L^j\eta}\ \text{a sum of big blocks},\quad (L^j\eta)^{-1}\operatorname{dist}(\Omega_j^c,\Omega_{j+1})>RM\tag{2.2}$$
($M$ the size of big blocks, $R$ a big positive integer "to be fixed later"; some $\Omega_j$ may equal $T_\eta$). Layers:
$$\Lambda_j=\Omega^{(j)}_j\setminus\Omega^{(j)}_{j+1}\ (1\le j\le k-1),\quad \Lambda_k=\Omega^{(k)}_k,\quad \Lambda_0=\Omega_1^c,\qquad \Omega_1=\bigcup_{j=1}^kB^j(\Lambda_j),\ T=\bigcup_{j=0}^kB^j(\Lambda_j)\tag{2.3-2.4}$$
with $B^0(\Lambda_0)=\Lambda_0$. This is the same geometry as the generating sets $\mathbb B_k$ of P2 (1.1)-(1.3) (P2 review: $(L^j\eta)^{-1}\operatorname{dist}(\Omega_j^c,\Omega_{j+1})>RM$ (1.1)).

Objects (gauge theory).
- $A$: gauge field on bonds; $\partial$ the lattice gradient (exterior derivative), $\partial^*$ its adjoint; $\Delta=\partial^*\partial+\partial\partial^*$ on $T_\eta$ (on 0-forms $\Delta=\partial^*\partial\ge0$, the positive lattice Laplacian). Functional (2.5): $A\mapsto\sum_p\eta^d|(\partial A)(p)|^2$ over plaquettes $p$; boundary data (2.6): $A=B_0$ on $\Lambda_0$, $Q_jA=B_j$ on $\Lambda_j$ ($Q_j$ the order-$j$ block-average on bonds).
- Gauge transformations $A^\lambda=A-\partial\lambda$ with $\lambda=0$ on $\Lambda_0$, $Q'_j\lambda=0$ on $\Lambda_j$ (2.7); $Q'_j$ the order-$j$ block average on sites (scalar). Space $N(Q')$ (2.10); $R$ the orthogonal projection of $L^2(T_\eta)$ onto $\Delta N(Q')$; $P=I-R$.
- Poincare-type inequality (2.11): $\langle\lambda,\Delta\lambda\rangle\ge\pi^2\sum_{j=1}^k(L^j\eta)^{-2}\sum_{x\in B^j(\Lambda_j)}\eta^d|\lambda(x)|^2$ for $\lambda\in N(Q')$ (cited from [3, 2.26, 2.27]).
- Scalar operator (2.14): $\langle\lambda,Q'^*aQ'\lambda\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^k\sum_{y\in\Lambda_j}a_j(L^j\eta)^{d-2}|(Q'_j\lambda)(y)|^2$, with $a_{j+1}=aa_j/(aL^{-2}+a_j)$, $a_1=a$ ("see [1, 2.13 and 2.15]"; this is the free flow of P1 (2.9)-(2.11), P4 (2.9)). The value of $a_0$ (layer $\Lambda_0$, outside $\Omega_1$) is not given in the pages read: UNCERTAIN. $\Delta'_a=\Delta+Q'^*aQ'$, $G'=\Delta_a'^{-1}$.
- Vector (bond) operator (2.19): $\Delta_a=\partial^*\partial+\partial R\partial^*+Q^*aQ=\Delta-\partial P\partial^*+Q^*aQ$, $G=\Delta_a^{-1}$; $(QA)(b)=(Q_jA)(b)$ for $b\in\Lambda_j$, $(Q_0A)(b)=A(b)$ (2.20). Gauge-fixed solution (2.35): $A=HB=GQ^*(QGQ^*)^{-1}B$.
- Cube covering (pp. 229, 239): $\mathscr D$ = family of cubes $\square$ of size $2ML^j\eta$ (for a block set $B^j(\Lambda_j)$, centred at $y\in\Lambda_j$) and of size $2M\eta$-type at scale 0, covering $T_\eta$ ($T_\eta=\bigcup_{\square\in\mathscr D}\square$), with functions $h_\square$ (Paper I (1.118), rescaled) satisfying $\sum_\square h_\square^2=1$ (2.36). Each cube is "connected with a $L^j\eta$-scale", i.e. a cube $\square\subset B^j(\Lambda_j)$ or one also meeting $B^{j+1}(\Lambda_{j+1})$. The enlarged cubes $\tilde\square$, $\tilde\square^2$, $\tilde\square^3$ are sums of $4^d$, $6^d$,... big blocks (pp. 238-239).
- Local operators: $G'(\square)$ = inverse of $\Delta'_a$ with boundary condition on $\partial\square$ (Neumann, as in [3]) (2.37); $G_\square=(\Delta-\partial P_\square\partial^*+Q^*aQ)^{-1}$ on the torus $T_\square$ obtained by identifying the cube $\tilde\square^3$ with a torus (2.89)-(2.90), only two scales present.

Distance (p. 231, (2.45)-(2.47)). $\mathfrak B=\bigcup_{j=0}^k\Lambda_j$. For a contour $\Gamma$ on $T_\eta$, $|\Gamma|=n\eta$ with $n$ the number of bonds. Admissible contours are those whose part inside $B^j(\Lambda_j)$ consists of lattice bonds of that layer (wording of the paper: "a part of $\Gamma$ contained in $B^j(\Lambda_j)$ consists of bonds of the lattice $\Lambda_j$"; precise meaning UNCERTAIN). Then
$$d(y,y')=\inf_{\Gamma_{y,y'}}\sum_{j=0}^k(L^j\eta)^{-1}\,|\Gamma_{y,y'}\cap B^j(\Lambda_j)|,\qquad y,y'\in\mathfrak B,\tag{2.46}$$
extended to $x\in B^j(y)$, $x'\in B^{j'}(y')$ by $d(x,x')=d(y^j(x),y^{j'}(x'))$. Each bond in layer $j$ costs $L^{-j}$. $d$ satisfies the triangle inequality (2.54) (concatenate contours) and the lower bound (2.48) obtained by decomposing a contour at the interfaces $\Sigma_j$ between $B^j(\Lambda_j)$ and $B^{j-1}(\Lambda_{j-1})$ (2.47).

Constants. $\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3,\delta_4>0$ (decay rates, "dependent on $d$ and $L$ only" or absolute), $\gamma_0,\gamma_1>0$ (lower bounds of quadratic forms, (2.76), (2.110), (2.118), (2.157); not to be confused with the decay rate called $\gamma_0$ in P3), $c_0(\alpha)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\alpha\delta_0|z|}$, $c_1(\alpha)=12c_0^d(\tfrac12\alpha)$.

## 2. Main results

The paper has no numbered Theorem. Its statements are Lemma 2.1, Propositions 2.2, 2.3, 2.5, 2.6, 2.7, Corollary 2.8, and Lemma 2.4. Hypotheses common to all: the geometry (2.1)-(2.2), $a$ as described, "$M$ sufficiently large", $R M$ large (2.59).

**Lemma 2.1 (p. 234).** For $0<\alpha<1$, $c_1(\alpha)=12c_0^d(\tfrac12\alpha)$, and $RM$ satisfying
$$\tfrac14\alpha\delta_0RM>2d\log c_0(\tfrac12\alpha)+1\tag{2.59}$$
we have, for $y\in\Lambda_j$, $y'\in\Lambda_{j'}$:
$$e^{-\alpha\delta_0d(y,y')}\le e^{-\alpha\delta_0RM\max\{|j-j'|-1,0\}},\tag{2.60}$$
$$\sup_{y\in\mathfrak B}\sum_{y'\in\mathfrak B}e^{-\alpha\delta_0d(y,y')}\le c_1(\alpha),\tag{2.61}$$
$$\sum_{y_1,\dots,y_{n-1}\in\mathfrak B}e^{-\alpha\delta_0d(y,y_1)}\cdots e^{-\alpha\delta_0d(y_{n-1},y_n)}\le c_1(\alpha)^n,\tag{2.62}$$
$$\sum_{y_1,\dots,y_{n-1}}e^{-\delta_0d(y,y_1)}\cdots e^{-\delta_0d(y_{n-1},y_n)}\le c_1(\alpha)^n\,e^{-(1-\alpha)\delta_0d(y,y_n)}.\tag{2.63}$$

## 3. Proof architecture

**Section A (pp. 223-229): variational problem and algebra, no analysis.** (i) Geometry (2.1)-(2.4). (ii) The functional (2.5) with restrictions (2.6) is gauge invariant under (2.7); gauge fixed by choosing in each orbit the element minimizing $\|\partial^*A^\lambda\|^2$ (2.8), i.e. $R\partial^*A=0$ (2.12). Existence/uniqueness of $\lambda_0$ uses positivity of $\Delta$ on $N(Q')$ via (2.11). (iii) The operator $R$ is represented by (2.17), $Rf=f-G'Q'^*(Q'G'^2Q'^*)^{-1}Q'G'f$, by Lagrange multipliers for (2.13)-(2.16), requiring $G'=\Delta'^{-1}_a$ and $Q'G'^2Q'^*$ positive. (iv) The minimizer of (2.5)-(2.6) under (2.12) is found with multipliers $\lambda,\omega$ in $h(A,\lambda,\omega)$ (2.18): $A=G\partial R\lambda+GQ^*\omega$ (2.22), and the Faddeev-Popov manipulations (2.28)-(2.30), (2.32)-(2.33) show $R\partial^*G\partial R=R$, $R\partial^*GQ^*=0$, $QG\partial R=0$; so $\lambda=0$ and $\omega=(QGQ^*)^{-1}B$, giving (2.35). The only hypothesis used is positivity of $\Delta_a$, $a>0$, "to be proven later" (p. 228). (v) The same with Dirichlet boundary conditions on a neighbourhood $\Omega^c\supset\Omega_1^c$ (pp. 228-229): replace $G$ by $G(\Omega)=(\Delta_a\restriction_\Omega)^{-1}$, and one gets the extra terms $G(\Omega)Q^*(QG(\Omega)Q^*)^{-1}QG(\Omega)\partial^*\partial\Omega^cB-G(\Omega)\partial^*\partial\Omega^cB$. (vi) Remark (p. 229): the $Q^*aQ$ term is an effective mass that interpolates from $O(1)$ on $\Omega_k$ up to $+\infty$ outside $\Omega_1$: "technically much more convenient than the usual local boundary conditions like Dirichlet or Neumann, which give unpleasant singularities connected with sharp edges". So Dirichlet outside $\Omega_1$ is the limit of infinite mass on $\Lambda_0$, consistent with the constraint $\lambda=0$ on $\Lambda_0$ in (2.7). Nothing in this section is analytic estimation.

**Section B (pp. 229-238): the scalar operator $G'$.** This is the genuinely relevant section. Steps.
1. Approximate inverse (2.37)-(2.38). With $G'(\square)$ local inverses on cubes of the covering and $\sum h_\square^2=1$ (2.36): $\Delta'_aG'_0=I-\mathbf R$, $\mathbf R=\sum_\square K(h_\square)G'(\square)h_\square$, $K(h)$ the commutator (2.39). The commutator involves only first derivatives of $h$ (of size $(ML^j\eta)^{-1}$) and the $Q^*aQ$-commutator, hence an overall factor $M^{-1}$.
2. Local bound (2.40)-(2.44). Rescale a cube of scale $L^j\eta$ to the $L^{-j}$ lattice. If $\square\subset B^j(\Lambda_j)$ only one scale is present and $G'(\square)$ is the single-scale operator of [3], Lemma 2.2 (decay (1.12)-type bounds). If $\square$ meets both $B^j(\Lambda_j)$ and $B^{j+1}(\Lambda_{j+1})$ the paper writes $G'(\square)$ by a Gaussian-integral identity (2.41)-(2.42) as
$$e^{\frac12\langle f,G'(\square)f\rangle}=e^{\frac12\langle f,G'_j(\square)f\rangle+\frac12\langle f,G'_j(\square)Q'^*_jC_\Lambda^{(j)}(\square)Q'_jG'_j(\square)f\rangle}$$
($G'_j(\square)$ the order-$j$ single-scale operator, $C^{(j)}_\Lambda(\square)$ the covariance of an auxiliary Gaussian integral (2.41)), and deduces the exponential decay (2.43) from the properties of $G'_j(\square)$, $G'_j(\square)Q'^*_j$, $C^{(j)}_\Lambda(\square)$ in [3] (Lemmas 2.2, 2.4, Prop. 2.3 there). Thus the single-cube exponential bound is imported from [3], not proved here. UNCERTAIN: the exact statements in [3] are not in the bundle.
3. The distance $d$ (2.45)-(2.48). Single-scale exponential decay on each layer is converted to decay in a single metric by using contours that cross layer interfaces; (2.48) bounds $d(y,y')$ below by the sum of unit-scale distances between the interface crossing points.
4. $L^\infty$ convergence (2.49)-(2.51), composition (2.52)-(2.56). $\mathbf R$ has $L^\infty$ norm $O(M^{-1})$ and an exponentially decaying kernel with respect to $d$. For products $R_1\cdots R_n$ of operators with such kernels the triangle inequality (2.54) turns $e^{-\delta_0d(y,y_1)}\cdots e^{-\delta_0d(y_{n-1},y')}$ into $e^{-(1-\alpha)\delta_0d(y,y')}e^{-\alpha\delta_0(\cdots)}$, leaving the sum (2.56) $\le(\sup_y\sum_{y'}e^{-\alpha\delta_0d(y,y')})^n$.
5. Lemma 2.1 (2.57)-(2.59). The sum in (2.56) is estimated using (2.48) and the fact that two consecutive surface points $y'_l,y_{l+1}$ lie on different interfaces $\Sigma_j$ with $(L^{j_{l,l+1}}\eta)^{-1}|y'_l-y_{l+1}|>RM$ (2.57), so that crossing from layer to layer costs $e^{-\frac12\alpha\delta_0RM}$ per crossing (this is where the separation assumption $\operatorname{dist}(\Omega_j^c,\Omega_{j+1})>RML^j\eta$ is used), while the unit-scale sums give $c_0(\tfrac12\alpha)^{d(2m+1)}$ for $m$ crossings; the condition (2.59) on $RM$ makes the sum over $m$ converge, giving $c_1(\alpha)=12c_0^d(\tfrac12\alpha)$.
6. Prop. 2.2. $G'=G'_0\sum\mathbf R^n$, bounds (2.65)-(2.66); derivative and Hölder versions "similar" (p. 234, text before Prop. 2.2): the weight $(L^j\eta)^2$ is replaced by $L^j\eta$ and $(L^j\eta)^{1-\alpha}$. Not written out; relies on the single-scale Hölder bounds of [3].
7. Prop. 2.3 and (2.68)-(2.88): the operator $Q'G'^2Q'^*$ is an operator on the sites $\mathfrak B$ with $L^2(\mathfrak B)$ scalar product (2.69); bounded by (2.68) and (2.58); its inverse is constructed as in Section 5 of [3] (unit-lattice operators): local inverses $C_\square$ of the cube-restricted operators with a larger cube $\tilde\square$ (so that the operator is $Q'G'(\tilde\square)^2Q'^*$), lower bound via Bessel's inequality (2.72)-(2.78) and the explicit Fourier formula (2.75) from [3] (2.45): $Q'_jG'_jQ'^*_j\ge2\gamma_0$ (absolute, $a=1$), glued by $C=\sum h_\square C_\square h_\square$ with $Q'G'^2Q'^*C=I-\mathbf R$ (2.82) and $\|\mathbf R\|$ small by the same estimates (2.83)-(2.85).

**Section C (pp. 238-250): the bond-field operators.** (2.89)-(2.141): the same scheme for $G_\square$ on tori with two scales (2.90)-(2.94), through a Gaussian-integral representation (2.95)-(2.119), gauge transformation $\lambda=H'_j\mu$ preserving the quadratic form (2.98)-(2.104), operators $\Delta_j'=H_j'^*\Delta^2H_j'$ (2.107) with explicit Fourier form (2.108), final identity (2.129)-(2.131). ## 6. Constants and parameters

## 7. Weak spots and open points

1. Single-cube bounds are not proved here: the exponential bound (2.43) for $G'(\square)$ on cubes touching one or two layers is quoted from [3] (Lemmas 2.2, 2.4, Prop. 2.3 there) after a Gaussian-integral rewriting (2.41)-(2.42). The statement and hypotheses used from [3] (what boundary conditions, whether $a$ general, whether complex or massive perturbations) cannot be checked from the bundle. E3 is the real source of the local estimate that P3 calls (2.5) (via "[2b] (2.33)").
2. The derivative and Hölder bounds in (2.67), (2.136)-(2.140) are asserted "similar" to the $L^\infty$ case (p. 234). The Hölder norm is not defined in this paper.
3. Prop. 2.2 is stated for $a=1$ only. P2 requires $\tfrac12<a<\tfrac32$ with $a(\mathbb B_k)\in[a(1-L^{-2}),a]$ (P2 (4.8)); the extension is plausible (the proofs use only positivity and uniform constants) but is not stated.
4. No mass term $\nu$ and no complex potential $\alpha$ appear. The operator $\Delta'_a=\Delta+Q'^*aQ'$ already contains the "mass" $Q'^*aQ'$, which has size $(L^j\eta)^{-2}$ on layer $j$ and equals $+\infty$ (Dirichlet) outside $\Omega_1$ only as a limit (p. 229); no proof is given that the bounds are uniform as the outside mass goes to infinity, though (2.2) is meant to make this true. The value $a_0$ in (2.14) for the layer $\Lambda_0$ is not specified in the pages read.
5. Numbering irregularities: (2.53) is absent; (2.55) is printed twice (p. 232 and p. 233; the first is presumably (2.53)); Corollary 2.8's inequality is printed "(1.151)" for (2.151); "$d_e$" exponents in (2.142), (2.149), (2.151) are unclear; (2.59) vs the remark after Lemma 2.1 (constants 1 vs 8).
6. The distance $d$ is defined through "admissible contours" described only in words (p. 231), with an interface structure $\Sigma_j$; (2.46) sums over $j=0,\dots,k$ including $j=0$ (layer $\Lambda_0$ outside $\Omega_1$). P3's (2.4) sums only $j=1..k$ (inside $\Omega_1$) with $|\Gamma\cap\Omega_j\cap\Omega^c_{j+1}|$. These agree for paths inside $\Omega_1$; the difference at layer 0 matters for localization near $\partial\Omega_1$.
7. The weights in (2.67) use the scale $L^j$ of the TARGET point $x$, whereas P3 (2.5) has $(L^{j'}\eta)^2$ at the SOURCE scale. Conversion costs a factor $L^{2|j-j'|}$ which is absorbed by $e^{-\alpha\delta_0RM\max(|j-j'|-1,0)}$ from (2.60) at the price of a smaller rate; the paper states this convertibility explicitly only for (2.68) ("the choice of powers ... is purely conventional", p. 235).
8. Section C and the last pages are gauge-field specific; the proofs of Props. 2.6, 2.7 are given as "reasoning in the same way".
9. The proof of Prop. 2.3 expansion (2.86) has an index structure I could not read reliably (the printed "$R_{\square_1,\square_2}\cdots$" may be a misprint for a product over consecutive pairs).
10. Two uses of the symbol $R$ ($R$ as the gauge projection of (2.10) and $R$ as the error operator in (2.38)); the paper says "where the operator $R$ was defined" for (2.38). Since $\Delta'_aG'_0=I-R$ with $R=\sum K G'h$, this is a different operator from the projection $R$ of (2.12) despite the same name (so for the sake of reading I used $\mathbf R$). UNCERTAIN whether Balaban intends a relation between them (Paper I has "$R=I-P$").

## 9. What the O(N) series uses from this paper

General adaptation. The O(N) series needs the scalar operator, acting componentwise on $\mathbb R^N$-valued fields,
$$G(\alpha)=(-\Delta^{D,\eta}_{\Omega_1}+Q^*(\mathbb B_k)a(\mathbb B_k)Q(\mathbb B_k)+\nu+\alpha)^{-1}\quad(\text{P2 (3.22)}),$$
with $\Delta^{D,\eta}_{\Omega_1}$ the Dirichlet Laplacian ($-\Delta\ge0$ convention), $\nu\in[0,1]$, $\alpha$ a (possibly complex, possibly $\lambda$-sized) potential. The operator closest to it here is the gauge-fixing operator $G'=(\Delta+Q'^*aQ')^{-1}$ of Section B: it is scalar, uses the same multiscale averaging $Q'_j$ on layers $\Lambda_j$, the same weights $a_j(L^j\eta)^{-2}$ (P2's $a(\mathbb B_k)$), the same geometry (2.1)-(2.2) (P2 (1.1)), with Dirichlet realised as $\lambda=0$ on $\Lambda_0$ (2.7). Everything specific to gauge fields (bond variables, $\partial P\partial^*$, Faddeev-Popov, $H$, $QGQ^*$, Lemma 2.4, Props. 2.5-2.7, Cor. 2.8) is irrelevant. Needed adaptation, in order of importance: (a) add the potential $\nu+\alpha\ge$ small or complex perturbation: not covered (see item 2 below); (b) general $a\in(\tfrac12,\tfrac32)$ and $a_j$ in $[a(1-L^{-2}),a]$ instead of $a=1$; (c) allow the constant $a_0$/infinite mass outside $\Omega_1$ and Dirichlet exactly (via $G(\Omega)$ remark, p. 248, asserted); (d) $\lambda\to\infty$ (P3 handles it separately).

For the O(N) series (large fields in scope), E5 is a source of (a) the exact geometry of the multiscale generating set and the distance $d$ (Lemma 2.1) and (b) the architecture of the random-walk expansion over mixed-scale cubes. Statements that would become interfaces (frozen hypotheses of the formalization, to be later proved): the weighted $L^\infty$, derivative, and Hölder bounds on $G(\alpha)$ (P2 (3.17)); the local exponential bound with complex $\alpha$ on cubes (P3 (2.5)); the convergence of the expansion (P3 (2.8)-(2.11)). Gauge-specific and irrelevant: Section A's gauge fixing and Faddeev-Popov algebra, $H$, $QGQ^*$, Props. 2.5-2.7, Cor. 2.8, Lemma 2.4, (2.152)-(2.157). Reusable arguments: partition of unity with commutator $K(h)=O(M^{-1})$; composition of kernels and the triangle inequality for $d$; Lemma 2.1.

Bounds the O(N) series needs, precisely (scalar operator $G(\alpha)=(-\Delta^D_{\Omega_1}+Q^*aQ+\nu+\alpha)^{-1}$ on generating sets $\Omega_1\supset\dots\supset\Omega_k$ with (2.2), uniform in $k$, $\eta$, $a\in(\tfrac12,\tfrac32)$ (with layer coefficients in $[a(1-L^{-2}),a]$), $\nu\in[0,1]$, $\lambda\in[1,\infty]$):

- (N1) Weighted sup bound: $|G(\alpha)f|_{0}\le B_0|f|_{2}$ (weights $(L^j\eta)^\sigma$ on $\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+1}$) for $|\alpha|_2<\gamma_1$ ($\alpha$ complex).
- (N2) Gradient bound $|\partial^\eta G f|_1\le B_0|f|_2$; $\Delta$-bound $|\Delta^{D}Gf|_2\le B_0|f|_2$ (follows from the equation and N1).
- (N3) Hölder bound $|\partial^\eta Gf|_{(\alpha),2-\alpha}\le C_\alpha B_0|f|_2$, $C_\alpha=O(\alpha^{-d-1})$, $0<\alpha\le1$ (P2 p. 611).
- (N4) Local exponential decay of the Neumann Green's function on cubes of size $3ML^j\eta$ (P3 (2.5)) with complex $\alpha$, rate $\gamma_0$ in the multiscale distance $d$, constants independent of $M$ (after rescaling).
- (N5) Convergence of the random-walk expansion with the commutator bound $\|K_z\|\le4dM^{-1}(\cdot)$ (P3 (2.7), (2.9)-(2.11)) and extension to complex decoupling parameters $|s_\square|\le e^{\kappa_1}$.
- (N6) $|\lambda G(\lambda)f|_2\le O(1)|f|_2$ uniformly in $\lambda>0$ (P3 (2.34); proved there).
- (N7) For $\nu\downarrow0$ and the last steps: decay of the unit-lattice operators $(aL^{-2}Q^*Q+\Delta^{(k)})^{-1}$ and $G_j$ at rate $\delta(\sqrt\nu+L^{-j})$ (P4, P9). Not from E5.

Possible standard proofs (suggestions, not claims).
1. Coercivity is elementary. On a block of layer $j$, the Poincare inequality gives $\|u-Q_ju\|^2\le C(L^j\eta)^2\|\partial u\|^2$, and the penalty $a_j(L^j\eta)^{-2}\|Q_ju\|^2$ controls the mean, so $\langle u,(-\Delta^D+Q^*aQ+\nu)u\rangle\ge c\sum_j(L^j\eta)^{-2}\|u\|^2_{\text{layer }j}$ with $c$ depending on $d,L$ and $\min a_j$. This replaces (2.11), (2.76) and the Fourier formula (2.75). It also shows that $\alpha$-perturbations with $|\alpha|_2$ small preserve invertibility (a Neumann series in $L^2$).
2. L2 exponential decay by Agmon/Combes-Thomas with the multiscale weight. Conjugate by $e^{\varepsilon\varphi}$ with $\varphi(x)=d(x,x_0)$ (2.46), which is Lipschitz with constant $(L^j\eta)^{-1}$ per unit length on layer $j$ and changes by $O(1)$ over a block of layer $j$. In the quadratic-form version, the weighted form differs from the unweighted one by $\varepsilon^2|\partial\varphi|^2\sim\varepsilon^2(L^j\eta)^{-2}$ (first-order terms cancel in the real part), which is dominated by the local coercivity $c(L^j\eta)^{-2}$ of step 1 for small $\varepsilon=\varepsilon(d,L)$, uniformly in $k$; the $Q^*aQ$ term is handled since $e^{\varepsilon\varphi}$ varies by at most $e^{O(\varepsilon)}$ over a block. This gives an $L^2$ bound with weights $e^{\varepsilon d}$ and would supply (N4) with $\nu,\alpha$ and general $a$ at once, avoiding the appeal to [3] Lemma 2.2. [Suggestion.]
3. $L^2\to L^\infty$, gradient and Hölder bounds by local discrete elliptic regularity (Caccioppoli plus local Sobolev/Moser or Schauder) at scale $L^j\eta$, since after rescaling by $L^j\eta$ the equation is $-\Delta u+c\,u=f$ with bounded coefficients plus the block-average term, a bounded operator of norm $O(1)$ at that scale. This gives (N1)-(N3) from (N4)-type $L^2$ decay, with the Hölder constant $C_\alpha$ from the standard discrete Schauder constant. [Suggestion; the dependence $O(\alpha^{-d-1})$ would have to be re-derived.]
4. The random-walk expansion (N5) is then a Neumann series for $I-\mathbf R$ with $\|\mathbf R\|\le O(M^{-1})$ and exponentially weighted kernels; its proof is elementary once (N4) and Lemma 2.1 are available, and complex decoupling parameters $s_\square$ are just bounded multipliers of the terms. Alternatively decoupling can be obtained by Dirichlet-Neumann bracketing or by a direct Lieb-Robinson-free Feshbach/Schur-complement argument with Agmon decay; I have not checked that these give analytic dependence on the $s_\square$ needed in P3 Prop. 2.1. [Suggestion.]
5. Lemma 2.1 and (2.46) can be reformulated without "admissible contours" as a weighted graph distance (edge weight $L^{-j}$ on a bond in layer $j$), as P3 (2.4) does; the three counting statements (2.60)-(2.63) then reduce to a geometric-series estimate on the lattice using (2.2) alone. This is a self-contained early target.

Honest limits: E5 provides no bound with a $\sqrt\nu$-dependent rate (needed in P9) and nothing for the $\lambda$-large regime; both would come from the same Agmon/coercivity scheme with $\nu$ added to the mass, but the uniformity in $\lambda$ must be checked separately (P3 (2.31)-(2.34)).
