Summary. The paper fixes the model (O(N) spins with a quartic Ginzburg-Landau action in the form (1.5), including lambda = infinity, the Heisenberg model) and defines a sequence of block-spin ("renormalization") transformations T with Gaussian kernel and averaging operator Q, alternating with rescalings S, applied in a small-field domain (characteristic functions chi). It states a list of inductive hypotheses (H.1)-(H.9) describing the form of the k-th effective density rho_k (a "minimal configuration" phi_k solving a variational problem, an effective action E_k that is a sum of localized pieces E^(j), analytic on a family of domains, O(N)- and Euclidean-invariant, exponentially localized, and renormalized by conditions (H.6),(H.7)). The "Main Theorem" (Theorem 2.2) asserts that for beta large these hold for all k (proof deferred to this paper plus two later papers). The proof in this paper is only one step of the induction: Section 3 shows that, given (H.1)-(H.7), the effective action E_k has a uniform bound proportional to the volume (Corollary 3.11, eq. (3.131)). That is the "renormalization analysis": expand localized terms around constant configurations, isolate relevant/marginal terms, show everything else is irrelevant (carries a small factor), and show the renormalization conditions kill the marginal terms. Existence of the minimal configurations phi_j, psi_k^(j) (variational problems) is deferred to P2; the inductive proof of the Theorem (construction of the new E^(k+1), renormalization equations for beta_k, a_k, lambda_k, v_k, definition of chi^(j)) is deferred to the "third paper" (P4/P5). Role: foundation of the series; supplies (H.1)-(H.9), the spaces Phi, Xi, Psi, and the notation used by P2-P7.

## 1. Setting and notation

### 1.1 The model (Sect. 1, pp.103-107)
Lattice: Z^d, tori T = {x in Z^d : -L_mu <= x_mu < L_mu}, periodic b.c. (p.103). Spin phi(x) in R^N. Measure (1.1): $d\mu(\phi)=\rho(\phi)d\phi$, $\rho=\exp[-\beta A(\phi)-E]$, E = log Z.

Original action (1.2): $A(\phi)=\frac12\sum_{\langle x,x'\rangle\in T}|\phi(x)-\phi(x')|^2+\frac\lambda8\sum_x|\phi(x)|^4-\frac\mu2\sum_x|\phi(x)|^2-\sum_x h\cdot\phi(x)$. After rescaling phi so that the minimum of the potential is at $|\phi|=1$ and h unit vector (eqs. (1.3)-(1.4), with $\rho=1-2\nu/\lambda$ and the minimum at phi=h), the used form is (1.5) (p.104):
$$A(\phi)=\tfrac12\|\partial\phi\|^2+\tfrac\lambda8\||\phi|^2-1\|^2+\tfrac\nu2\|\phi-h\|^2,\quad \lambda\in[1,+\infty],\ 0<\nu\le 1,\ h\in S^{N-1}.$$
Norms $\|\cdot\|$ are l^2 norms on the lattice; $\|\partial\phi\|^2=\sum_{\langle x,x'\rangle}|\phi(x)-\phi(x')|^2$ (each nearest-neighbour bond once). $\lambda=\infty$ is the limit (1.6): $\exp[-\beta(\frac12\|\partial\phi\|^2+\frac\nu2\|\phi-h\|^2)-E_\infty]\prod_x\delta(|\phi(x)|^2-1)$, the Heisenberg (N=3) / O(N) model. Constants rescaled into E.

Symmetries (1.7) p.105: $A(R\phi;Rh,\lambda,\nu)=A(\phi;h,\lambda,\nu)$, R in O(N); $A(r\phi;h,\lambda,\nu)=A(\phi;h,\lambda,\nu)$ for lattice Euclidean transformations r ($(r\phi)(x)=\phi(rx)$). The first two terms are O(N)-invariant, the third breaks it to the stabilizer of h. Heuristic "spin wave picture" (p.105) for $d\ge3$, $N\ge2$: unique state for $\nu>0$, nonzero magnetization $m_\nu h$, exponential decay of truncated functions at rate $\sim\sqrt\nu$, as $\nu\to0+$ power-law decay of transversal components given by $(-\Delta)^{-1}$. These are the goals of the series, not proved here. Refs: [5] Frohlich-Simon-Spencer (infrared bounds), [9] Zinn-Justin.

Scalings (pp.106-107). For the epsilon-lattice $T_\varepsilon=\{\varepsilon x\}$, $\phi(\varepsilon x)=\phi(x)$ (spin does NOT scale, trivial factor $\varepsilon^0$), $\beta A(\phi;h,\lambda,\nu)=\beta\varepsilon^{2-d}A^\varepsilon(\phi;h,\lambda\varepsilon^{-2},\nu\varepsilon^{-2})$ (1.9), so (1.10): $\beta^\varepsilon=\beta\varepsilon^{2-d}$, $\lambda^\varepsilon=\lambda\varepsilon^{-2}$, $\nu^\varepsilon=\nu\varepsilon^{-2}$. For d>2, scaling lowers temperature, raises the barrier lambda and the field nu.

### 1.2 Renormalization transformation (pp.107-109)
For a density $\rho(\phi,\beta)$ (1.11): $(T\rho)(\psi)=\int d\phi\,t(\psi,\phi;\beta,a)\rho(\phi,\beta)$, $t=\prod_{y\in T_L^{(1)}}t(y;\psi,\phi;\beta,a)$,
$$t(y;\psi,\phi;\beta,a)=\exp\Big[-\tfrac12\beta aL^{d-2}|\psi(y)-(Q\phi)(y)|^2+\tfrac N2\log\tfrac{\beta aL^{d-2}}{2\pi}\Big],\quad (Q\phi)(y)=\sum_{x\in B(y)}L^{-d}\phi(x).$$
B(y) = L-block centred at y; $T_L^{(1)}$ = new lattice of block centres; a ~ 1 (a in (1/2, 3/2) suggested, p.107). Normalization $\int d\psi\,(T\rho)=\int d\phi\,\rho$. Q is block averaging (the "linear averaging operator"; no smoother averaging is used in this paper). The transformation depends on beta; beta is rescaled and renormalized after each step (so one does a product of T with different running beta_k, a_k). Scaling transformation S rescales L-lattice of new spins to unit lattice, not changing spin values. Index k = number of steps; $\rho_k=S^{(k-1)}T^{(k-1)}\rho_{k-1}=(ST)_k\rho_0$ (1.12). Generating functional for correlation functions: $\langle g,\phi\rangle$ added to the exponent, g complex-valued on T (p.108).

First step (1.13)-(1.14): $A_1(\psi,\phi;h,aL^{-2},\lambda_0,\nu_0)=\frac12aL^{-2}\|\psi-Q\phi\|^2+\frac12\|\partial\phi\|^2+\frac{\lambda_0}8\||\phi|^2-1\|^2+\frac{\nu_0}2\|\phi-h\|^2$. The $\phi$ integral is to be done by saddle point, but critical points are uncontrolled globally; so one restricts to small-field regions (1.15) (pp.108-109): $|\psi(y)-(Q\phi)(y)|<\beta_0^{-1/2}p(\beta_0)$, $|(\partial\phi)(b)|<\beta_0^{-1/2}p(\beta_0)$, $||\phi(x)|^2-1|<\lambda_0^{-1}\beta_0^{-1/2}p(\beta_0)$, $|\phi(x)-h|<\nu_0^{-1/2}\beta_0^{-1/2}p(\beta_0)$, with $p(\beta_0)=(A_0+\log\beta_0)^{p_0}$, $p_0$ an even integer, $A_0>0$. Large-field region (complement, "X") is bounded crudely by $\exp[-\frac18p^2(\beta_0)|X|]$ — not treated in P1-P2 (treated in P7 per the paper's own plan: "In the last paper"; see SOURCES). The small-field densities (1.16): $\rho_k=\chi_kS^{(k-1)}T^{(k-1)}\chi^{(k-1)}\cdots S^{(0)}T^{(0)}\chi^{(0)}\rho_0$, with characteristic functions $\chi_k,\chi^{(j)}$ of small-field spaces (defined precisely only later; $\chi_k$ explicit in (2.30), $\chi^{(j)}$ "in the third paper"). This paper only treats the case "whole lattice is small field" (p.109).

### 1.3 Rescaled actions, minimal configurations (Sect. 2, pp.109-113)
Let $\eta=L^{-k}$; spin configurations $\phi$ live on the lattice $T_\eta$, new spins $\psi$ on $T_1^{(k)}$ (the unit lattice of centres of k-blocks, blocks containing $L^{kd}$ sites of $T_\eta$). (2.1) p.110:
$$A_k(\psi,\phi;h,a,\lambda,\nu)=\tfrac a2\|\psi-Q_k\phi\|_1^2+\tfrac12\|\partial^\eta\phi\|^2+\tfrac\lambda8\||\phi|^2-1\|^2+\tfrac\nu2\|\phi-h\|^2,$$
norms on the $\eta$-lattice except the first, on the unit lattice. $Q_k$ = k-fold block average (composition of the $Q$'s). Variational problem (2.2): $\inf_\phi A_k(\psi,\phi;\ldots)$. Its (unique, in small-field domain) minimizer is $\phi_k=\phi_k(\psi;h,a,\lambda,\nu)$, analytic in $\psi,h$ and the coefficients; existence/regularity deferred to P2 ("next paper", p.110, p.111). Scaling law (2.3)-(2.4): $A_j(\psi,\phi;h,a,\lambda,\nu)=(L^j\eta)^{-(d-2)}A_j^\eta(\psi,\phi;h,a(L^j\eta)^{-2},\lambda(L^j\eta)^{-2},\nu(L^j\eta)^{-2})$ and $\phi_j(x;\psi,h,a,\lambda,\nu)=\phi_j(L^j\eta x;\psi,h,a(L^j\eta)^{-2},\lambda(L^j\eta)^{-2},\nu(L^j\eta)^{-2})$ (lattice rescaling only; spin values unchanged).

Connecting problem (2.6): $\inf_{\psi_j}\{\frac12a_{k-j}\|\psi_k-Q_{k-j}\psi_j\|_1^2+A_j^\eta(\psi_j,\phi_j;h,a_j(L^j\eta)^{-2},\lambda_j(L^j\eta)^{-2},\nu_j(L^j\eta)^{-2})\}$ with solution $\psi_k^{(j)}$ (existence in P2). Composition identity (2.7)-(2.8) (p.111-112): $(2.6)=\inf_\phi A_k(\psi_k,\phi;h,\frac{a_{k-j}a_j}{a_j+a_{k-j}(L^j\eta)^2},\lambda_j(L^j\eta)^{-2},\nu_j(L^j\eta)^{-2})$, and $\phi_j\circ\psi_k^{(j)}=\phi_k$ with the combined coefficient. Requiring that this coefficient equal $a_k$ (so that the identities hold for the actual flow) gives (2.9)-(2.11):
$$\lambda_j=\lambda_k(L^j\eta)^2,\ \nu_j=\nu_k(L^j\eta)^2,\quad \frac{a_{k-j}a_j}{a_j+a_{k-j}(L^j\eta)^2}=a_k,\quad a_j=a_k\frac{1-L^{-2k}}{1-L^{-2j}}.$$
Here (as p.112 notes) coefficients $a_j,\lambda_j,\nu_j$ for $j<k$ are defined from the k-th ones ("ideal flow matched at step k"); the index k is suppressed. Explicit critical point (2.13)-(2.14): $\psi_k^{(j)}=Q_j\phi_k+\frac{1-L^{-2j}}{1-L^{-2k}}(L^j\eta)^2Q_{k-j}^*(\psi_k-Q_k\phi_k)$ ($Q^*$ adjoint of Q). Notation (2.12): $\psi_k^{(j)}(\psi_k;h,a_{k-j},a_j(L^j\eta)^{-2},\lambda_k,\nu_k)$; depends on h via $\nu_kh$.

### 1.4 Analyticity domains (pp.113-115, 123-124)
Fix a lattice $T_\xi$, $\xi=L^{-j}$. (2.15) real space:
$$\Phi_j(\sigma,\varepsilon;\lambda,\nu)=\{(\phi,h):|\partial^\xi\phi|<\sigma\varepsilon,\ |\Delta^\xi\phi|<\sigma^2\varepsilon,\ |\alpha|<\sigma^2\varepsilon,\ |\phi-h|<\tfrac{\sigma\varepsilon}{\sqrt\nu},\ \||h|^2-1|<\tfrac{\sigma^2\varepsilon^2}{\nu}\},\ \alpha=\tfrac\lambda2(|\phi|^2-1).$$
(Pointwise bounds at every site/bond.) Complex extension (2.16) p.114: $\Phi^c_j(\sigma,\varepsilon;\lambda,\nu)=\{(\phi+\phi',h+h'):(\phi,h)\in\Phi_j(\sigma,\varepsilon;\lambda,\nu),|\phi'|<\varepsilon,|\partial^\xi\phi'|<\sigma\varepsilon,|\Delta^\xi\phi'|<\sigma^2\varepsilon,|\delta\alpha|<\sigma^2\varepsilon,|h'|<\frac{\sigma^2\varepsilon}\nu,|\phi\cdot h'|<\frac{\sigma^2\varepsilon^2}\nu\}$, $\delta\alpha=\frac\lambda2((\phi+\phi')^2-\phi^2)$ (complex square, not modulus). Consequences (2.17)-(2.19): $|\phi\cdot\partial^\xi\phi|<5\xi\sigma^2\varepsilon$ if $\lambda_j\xi^2>5/6$, $\varepsilon<1/10$. Used with $\lambda=\lambda_j$, $\nu=\nu_j$, written $\Phi_j^c(\sigma,\varepsilon)$ (subscript j = scale; $\lambda_j\xi^2$ near 1 for lambda ~ 1).

Spaces of pairs $(\psi_j,h)$ on $T_1^{(j)}$ (2.20)-(2.21) p.115: $\Xi_j(\sigma,\varepsilon)=\{(\psi_j,h):(\phi_j(\psi_j;h,a_j,\lambda_j,\nu_j),h)\in\Phi_j(\sigma,\varepsilon;\lambda_j,\nu_j),\ |\psi_j-Q_j\phi_j(\psi_j;\ldots)|<\sigma^2\varepsilon\}$, $h\in S^{N-1}$; complex version $\Xi_j^c$: $(\psi_j+\psi_j',h+h')$ with $(\phi_j(\psi_j+\psi_j';h+h'),h+h')=(\phi_j(\psi_j,h)+\delta\phi_j,h+h')\in\Phi_j^c(\sigma,\varepsilon;\lambda_j,\nu_j)$ and $|\psi_j'-Q_j\delta\phi_j(\psi_j')|<\sigma^2\varepsilon$ (analytic extension of $\phi_j$ asserted, "described in the next paper", P2). $a_j,\lambda_j,\nu_j$ are the k-dependent ideal-flow constants (2.9),(2.11).

Lemma 2.1 (p.116): $\psi_k^{(j)}$ maps $\Xi_k^c(\sigma,\varepsilon)\to\Xi_j^c(L^j\eta\sigma,\varepsilon)$ analytically, $\varepsilon$ small, $\sigma\le1$. Proof: "immediately from the composition property (2.8), (2.14), (2.9)-(2.11) and definitions" (one-line; uses the existence/analyticity of phi_j from P2).

Elementary spaces (Sect. 3, pp.121-124) in terms of psi directly. (3.13):
$\Psi_j(\sigma,\varepsilon)=\{(\psi_j,h):|\partial^1\psi_j|<\sigma\varepsilon,\ ||\psi_j|^2-1|<\sigma^2\varepsilon,\ |\nu_j(1-(\psi_j)_0\cdot h)|<\sigma^2\varepsilon^2,\ |\nu_j(h^2-1)|<\sigma^2\varepsilon^2\}$, with $(\psi)_0=\psi/|\psi|$. (3.14): $\Psi_j^c(\sigma,\varepsilon)=\{(\psi_j+\psi_j',h+h'):(\psi_j,h)\in\Psi_j(\sigma,\varepsilon),|\psi_j'|<\varepsilon,|\partial^1\psi_j'|<\sigma\varepsilon,|(\psi_j+\psi_j')^2-\psi_j^2|<\sigma^2\varepsilon,|\nu_jh'|<\sigma^2\varepsilon,|\nu_j(\psi_j)_0\cdot h'|<\sigma^2\varepsilon^2\}$. Simplified spaces (3.18)-(3.19) with $\delta=\sigma\varepsilon$: $\bar\Psi_j(\delta)=\{|\partial^1\psi_j|<\delta,||\psi_j|^2-1|<\delta,|\nu_j(1-(\psi_j)_0\cdot h)|<\delta^2,|\nu_j(h^2-1)|<\delta^2\}$; $\bar\Psi_j^c(\delta,\varepsilon)=\{\ldots,|\psi_j'|<\varepsilon+\delta,|\partial^1\psi_j'|<\delta,|(\psi_j+\psi_j')^2-\psi_j^2|<\delta,|\nu_jh'|<\frac{\delta^2}{\varepsilon},|\nu_j(\psi_j)_0\cdot h'|<\delta^2\}$. "$\Psi_{const}$" = restrict to constant configurations, dropping derivative conditions. Lemma 3.1 (3.16): $\Xi_j(\sigma,\varepsilon)\subset\Psi_j(3\sigma,\varepsilon)$, $\Xi_j^c(\sigma,\varepsilon)\subset\Psi_j^c(3\sigma,\varepsilon)$ for small $\varepsilon$ [proved pp.121-122 via (3.1)-(3.12)]. ### 1.5 Localization (p.117)
Large cubes of side $M=L^m$ (m large integer); $T_1\subset\mathbb Z^d+(\frac12,\ldots,\frac12)$, a point x identified with the unit cube centred at x. Partition $\pi_j$ of $\mathbb R^d$ into cubes of the lattice $L^jM\mathbb Z^d$ (rescaled to scale $\xi$); $\pi_j'$ = cover by LM-cubes (unions of $L^d$ cubes of $\pi_j$-type, overlapping). $\mathcal D_j$ = class of connected unions X of open cubes of $\pi_j'$ (2.26) (viewed as open subsets of $\mathbb R^d$ and as lattice subsets). Tree size (2.27): $d_j(X)=\inf\{M^{-1}|\Gamma|:\Gamma$ continuous tree graph in X meeting every cube $\square\subset X$, $\square\in\pi'_{j}\}$ (length of shortest tree, in units of M); scale-dependent, X rescaled together with the lattice.

## 2. Main results

### 2.1 The inductive hypotheses (all pp.110-120). Statement is verbatim-precise up to my transcription.

**(H.1)** p.110. $\rho_k(\psi_k)=\chi_k\exp[\mathscr A_k+\mathscr F_k(\psi_k,h,g)]$, $\mathscr A_k=-\beta_kA_k(\psi_k,\phi_k;h,a_k,\lambda_k,\nu_k)+\mathscr E_k(\psi_k,h)-E_k$, with $\psi_k$ a spin configuration on $T_1^{(k)}$, $\phi_k=\phi_k(\psi_k;h,a_k,\lambda_k,\nu_k)$ the minimizer (2.2), coefficients $\beta_k,a_k,\lambda_k,\nu_k$ determined by "inductive renormalization equations" (third paper). Leading behaviour (2.5): $\beta_k\approx\beta L^{k(d-2)}$, $a_k\approx a\frac{1-L^{-2}}{1-L^{-2k}}$, $\lambda_k\approx\lambda L^{2k}$, $\nu_k\approx\nu L^{2k}$. [(2.5) says $\beta_k\approx\beta L^{k(d-2)}$ as printed; by (1.10) with $\varepsilon=L^{-k}$ this is the scaling $\beta\varepsilon^{2-d}$. OK.]

**(H.2)** p.113. There exist $\mathscr E^{(j)}(y;\psi_j,\nu_jh)$, $\psi_j$ spin on $T_1^{(j)}$, with $\mathscr E_k(\psi_k,h)=\sum_{j=1}^k\mathscr E^{(j)}(\psi^{(j)}_k(\psi_k,\nu_kh),\nu_jh)$ and $\mathscr E^{(j)}(\psi_j,\nu_jh)=\sum_{y\in T_1^{(j)}}\mathscr E^{(j)}(y;\psi_k,\nu_jh)$ [as printed; second sum is over y with argument $\psi_j$]. Each $\mathscr E^{(j)}$ is rescaled to scale $L^j\eta$, with no change of form.

**(H.3)** p.115. $\mathscr E^{(j)}(y;\psi_j,\nu_jh)$ is defined on $\Xi_j(1,\varepsilon_j)$ and has an analytic extension to $\Xi_j^c(1,\varepsilon_j)$, where
$$\varepsilon_j=\alpha_{0,k}\,\xi^{\frac{d-2}2-\gamma}(A_0+\log\xi^{-1})^p,\quad \xi=L^{-j},\quad 0\le\gamma<\gamma_0\le\tfrac{d-2}2.$$
$\gamma$ is a free parameter in $[0,\gamma_0)$: larger $\gamma$ = larger analyticity domains but smaller uniform bound range. For $\gamma=0$: $p=p_0$, $A_0$ as in (1.15) (matches the small-field domain). For $\gamma$ near $\gamma_0$, take $p=0$ (no logs; used in the rest of the series "for simplicity"). $\alpha_0$ small. Later in Sect.3, $\gamma_0=\min\{\frac{d-2}2,\frac12\}$ is shown to be admissible (pp.136, 147; see Sect 7 on the extra constraint $\gamma_0\le1$ mentioned on p.130).

**(H.4)** p.116. Symmetries: $\mathscr E^{(j)}(ry;r\psi_j,\nu_jh)=\mathscr E^{(j)}(y;\psi_j,\nu_jh)$ for Euclidean transformations r of the lattice $T^{(j)}$ (translations, reflections, coordinate permutations; $(r\psi)(y)=\psi(r^{-1}y)$), and $\mathscr E^{(j)}(y;R\psi_j,\nu_jRh)=\mathscr E^{(j)}(y;\psi_j,\nu_jh)$, $R\in O(N)$. Also for the analytic extensions: the second equality on $\Xi_j^c(1,\varepsilon_j)$, the first on the subspace of constant $h+h'$. Consequence (2.23)-(2.25): translation invariance for constant $\psi_j$ makes $\mathscr E^{(j)}(y;\psi,\nu_jh)$ independent of y, giving the effective potential $\mathscr V^{(j)}(\psi,\nu_jh)=\mathscr E^{(j)}(y;\psi,\nu_jh)$ evaluated on constant $\psi$.

**(H.5)** p.117-118. ## 3. Proof architecture

**Sect. 1 (pp.103-109), Introduction.** Model, scalings, the T-transformation, motivation for small-field restrictions. No theorems.

**Sect. 2 (pp.109-120), inductive description.** The key structural ideas:
1. The effective density is, in small-field, the exponential of $-\beta_kA_k(\psi_k,\phi_k(\psi_k))$ (the classical action along minimal configurations: the "leading" Gaussian-like term; $\phi_k$ is the classical solution = background field) plus an effective action $\mathscr E_k$ of lower order.
2. $\mathscr E_k$ is a sum over earlier steps j of terms $\mathscr E^{(j)}$ evaluated at $\psi_k^{(j)}$, the minimizer of the intermediate variational problem (2.6); this makes the analytic structure transparent (new variable psi_j replaced by its classical value in terms of $\psi_k$). Requirement that the minimizers compose (2.8) forces the ideal-flow equations (2.9)-(2.11), solvable explicitly.
3. Analyticity domains $\Xi$, scaled by $\sigma$ so that Lemma 2.1 (compositional property of $\psi_k^{(j)}$) holds: $\psi_k^{(j)}$ maps $\Xi_k^c(\sigma,\varepsilon)$ to $\Xi_j^c(L^j\eta\sigma,\varepsilon)$. This is the "pseudo-scaling": higher j means smaller $\sigma$ (finer derivative scale), same $\varepsilon$; so after composition a function of size $\varepsilon_j$ evaluated at fluctuation of size $\varepsilon_k$ is small by the factor $(L^j\eta)^{\text{power}}$.
4. Localization expansions (H.5): $\mathscr E^{(j)}$ = sum over connected unions X of LM-cubes, exponentially decaying in the tree size of X. Standard (Balaban gauge theory papers [2d]); here only assumed.
5. Renormalization conditions (H.6),(H.7) on relevant (potential: $\mathscr V=0,\partial_h\mathscr V\cdot e_1=0,\partial_\psi\mathscr V\cdot e_1=0$ at $(e_1,\nu e_1)$) and marginal ($\|\partial\psi\|^2$-coefficient, via a second-moment sum) parts. Remaining "irrelevant" terms carry extra small factors of the form $(L^j\eta)^{\alpha_i}$.
6. Dimension count: with $d\ge3$ and the dimension of $\mathscr E^{(j)}$ in $\xi=L^{-j}$ units of $\varepsilon_j\sim\xi^{\frac{d-2}2-\gamma}$, a localized term contributes $\varepsilon^{4}\cdot$ so that after four orders the result is smaller than the volume factor.
(H.1)-(H.9) are stated; Theorem 2.2 asserts them; no proof here.

**Sect. 3 (pp.121-153), renormalization analysis.** Aim: for given $\mathscr E^{(j)}$ with (H.3)-(H.7), bound $\mathscr E_k$ by const·volume.
(a) 3.1-3.12, Lemma 3.1 (pp.121-123): elementary but fiddly estimates showing $\Xi\subset\Psi$: the nonlinear $\phi_j$-based conditions imply direct conditions on $\psi_j$ (derivatives, $|\psi|^2-1$, $\nu(1-\psi_0\cdot h)$...). Uses (2.15) conditions and a discrete-Taylor expression (3.2) of $\psi\cdot\partial\psi$ via $Q\phi,\partial\phi$ along shortest lattice paths $\Gamma_{x,x'}$. Routine.
(b) Lemma 3.2 (p.124): tower of inclusions; condition on $k-j\ge n_1$ ("a gap") ensures $\psi_k^{(j)}$ is small enough that constant configurations built from $\psi^{(j)}_k(y)$ stay in the domain where $\mathscr E^{(j)}$ is analytic with room; $n_1$ defined in (3.62)/(p.126), $L^{n_1}\ge7C_0K_1\frac1\alpha$. For the last $n_1$ steps (j close to k), no smallness, only crude bound $E_0K_0|T_1^{(j)}|$ (3.131 second sum).
(c) Decomposition (3.23), p.125: $\mathscr E_k=\sum_{j\le k-n_1}\sum_y\mathscr V^{(j)}(\psi_k^{(j)}(y),\nu_jh)+\sum_{j\le k-n_1}\sum_{y,X}[\mathscr E^{(j)}(y,X;\psi_k^{(j)},\ldots)-\mathscr E^{(j)}(y,X;\psi_k^{(j)}(y),\ldots)]+\sum_{j>k-n_1}\sum_y\mathscr E^{(j)}(y;\psi_k^{(j)},\ldots)$: potential part + gradient (non-constant) part + near-k part.
(d) Effective potential (pp.125-130): by O(N)-invariance (H.4) reduce to $f(u,v,v^2w^2)$ of three invariants $u=|\psi|$, $v=\nu^{?}$... precisely $\mathscr V(\psi,\nu h)=f(|\psi|,\nu(\psi_0\cdot h),\nu^2(|h|^2-(\psi_0\cdot h)^2))$ (3.28), via rotations $R_1,R_2$ (3.24)-(3.25) and (3.27). The function f is analytic on the domain (3.26) and bounded by $E_0K_0$ (2.29), so Cauchy estimates (3.30) on the polydisc (3.29)/(3.31) give Taylor remainders (3.32)-(3.33); (H.6) says $f=\partial_uf=\partial_vf=0$ at $(1,\nu_j,0)$ [(3.34)-(3.35)], so $f$ is $O(\ (u-1)^2,\ (u-1)v',\ v'^2,\ w^2)$: quadratic. Lemma 3.3 gives analytic extension on $\Psi_{const}^c$; Proposition 3.4 / Corollary 3.5: after substituting $\psi_k^{(j)}(y)$, the potential is $O((L^j\eta)^{d+\alpha_2})$ — irrelevant. This needs $\gamma<1$ and $\alpha_2=2(1-\gamma)>0$.
(e) Localized terms, non-constant configurations (pp.130-150). Taylor expand $\mathscr E(y,X;\psi,\nu h)-\mathscr E(y,X;\psi(y),\nu h)$ to 4th order in $\delta\psi=\psi(x)-\psi(y)$ around the constant configuration $\psi(y)$ (3.51). Geometric control: $|\delta\psi(x)|\le|\Gamma_{y,x}||\partial^1\psi|$ with a path inside X of length $\le2dLM+dMd(X)$ (3.54); this is why the tree size $d(X)$ and a restriction $d(X)\le\frac1{2dM}\frac{\delta_0}\delta$ (3.59) appear, and why large X are handled separately (no expansion, using the crude bound with $e^{-\kappa d}$ and the loss $e^{d}$, (3.66)). Fourth-order term: Cauchy estimate in $\tau$ (3.60), radius $r_0$; yields $e^{-(\kappa-1)d(X)}$ and four powers of smallness: irrelevant (Lemma 3.7). Terms of orders 1-3 are treated by O(N)-symmetry (3.67)-(3.88): with $\psi_t$ reduced to $\psi_0$-direction, the derivative tensors have the structure (3.73) for n=1 ($v_1,v_2$), (3.78) for n=2 ($v_0,v_{1,1},v_{1,2},v_{2,1},v_{2,2}$), obtained from invariance under the stabilizer of $e_1,e_2$ and reflection; the 3rd-order term is simply estimated (3.103) as irrelevant. Splitting $\delta\psi\cdot R\delta\psi$ into $Z_1(x,y,\psi)=-\frac12|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|^2+\frac12|\psi(x)|^2-\frac12|\psi(y)|^2$ (3.82) and $Z_2$ (3.83),(3.85) (marginal, then irrelevant remainders $R_1$ (3.86)). All terms are collected in the big identity (3.89); the first three sums are irrelevant (3.92)-(3.95). Left: four terms (3.96), which are the relevant/marginal contributions: $\frac12\langle v_1,\psi^2(x)-\psi^2(y)\rangle$, $\langle v_2,\nu h\cdot\psi_0(x)-\nu h\cdot\psi_0(y)\rangle$ — antisymmetric in $x,y$ (cancel on summation by symmetry (3.113) of $v_\sigma(x,y)$) — and the $v_0$, $v_1,v_2$ gradient terms. Discrete Taylor formula (3.97)-(3.98) (from [2d]) replaces $\psi(x)-\psi(y)$ by $(x-y)\cdot\nabla\psi(y)+R_2$, remainder with second derivative, giving relevant/marginal term $\frac12(v_0-v_1+\nu v_2)((x-y)\cdot\nabla\psi)^2$ plus irrelevant remainders (3.99)-(3.102).
(f) Sums over X, thermodynamic constants (pp.147-152): split $d(X)\le d(\rho)$ ($\rho=\sigma\frac\varepsilon{\varepsilon_0}$-dependent) and $>d(\rho)$ (3.116). For $d(X)>d(\rho)$ everything is irrelevant by the exponential decay (tail $e^{-d(\rho)}$ gives the small factor, (3.117)-(3.119)). For $d(X)\le d(\rho)$ the sums over X are taken to the thermodynamic limit ($\mathscr D_j(\mathbb Z^d)$) giving $v_\sigma^{(\infty)},v_\sigma^{(\rho)}$; by Euclidean symmetry the matrices $v^{(\infty)}_{\sigma;\mu,\nu}$ are proportional to the identity (3.115): coefficient $v_\sigma^{(\infty)}=\frac1d\sum v^{(\infty)}_{\sigma;\mu,\mu}$; symmetric in $x,y$ so the antisymmetric terms sum to 0 (3.113). Thus the only surviving marginal term is $\frac12(v_0^{(\infty)}-v_1^{(\infty)}+\nu v_2^{(\infty)})\|\partial\psi\|^2$ (3.114). Ward-Takahashi identity (3.122)-(3.124), obtained by differentiating the O(N)-invariance $\mathscr E(R\psi,\nu Rh)=\mathscr E(\psi,\nu h)$ along $\exp(\lambda A)$, with $A_{21}=-A_{12}=1$, evaluated at $(e_1,\nu e_1)$: relates $v_0^{(\infty)}-v_1^{(\infty)}+\nu v_2^{(\infty)}$ to the second-moment sum in (H.7) (computed in (3.121)-(3.125) using $(x_1-y)\cdot(x_2-y)=-\frac12|x_1-x_2|^2+\frac12|x_1-y|^2+\frac12|x_2-y|^2$, translation invariance, and (H.6) for the first derivative terms); hence it vanishes by (H.7): the coefficient of $\|\partial\psi\|^2$ is zero. This is the logical heart: (H.6) and (H.7) are exactly what is needed for the three relevant/marginal contributions to disappear, and the Ward identity explains why (H.7) with the $e_2\otimes e_2$ transversal component also kills the longitudinal marginal coefficient (the symmetry relates the two).
(g) Proposition 3.10 and Corollary 3.11 assemble: replace $\psi$ by $\psi_k^{(j)}$, use $|\partial^2\psi_k^{(j)}|<K_1C_{\alpha_1}(L^j\eta)^{2-\alpha_1}\varepsilon_k$ (3.107) (from properties of $\phi_k$ deferred to P2 and (2.14)), get (3.128), sum over j (a convergent geometric series, uses $\alpha_3=2(\gamma_0-\gamma)-\alpha_1>0$, so $\gamma<\gamma_0$ strictly and $\alpha_1$ small), giving the uniform volume bound (3.131).

Genuine difficulties: (i) the bookkeeping of the analytic domains so that every composition and Taylor expansion stays inside the domain of analyticity (Lemmas 3.2, 3.6); (ii) the symmetry analysis of derivative tensors under the stabilizer subgroup (3.67)-(3.79); (iii) the Ward identity link between (H.6)/(H.7) and the coefficients. Routine: Lemma 3.1, Cauchy estimates, geometric sums. The central logic (what the inductive hypotheses are, how effective actions are renormalized) is more important than the estimates here.

## 7. Weak spots and open points

1. Theorem 2.2 is only a statement; not proved here. Its proof is distributed over "three papers"; since the series grew, check the final location. Constants' order of choice is not specified in P1. This is the most important gap for formalization: the hypotheses (H.1)-(H.9) cannot be formalized as stated without the precise dependence of $\chi^{(j)}$, $\beta_k$ etc. from later papers.
2. $\chi^{(j)}$ (the intermediate small-field characteristic functions) are undefined here (p.120: "technical, postponed to third paper"). (H.1) with $\chi_k$ defined in (2.30) only describes the last restriction.
3. The existence/analyticity of $\phi_k$, $\psi_k^{(j)}$ (even their uniqueness in the small-field domain), the bound (3.107) $|\partial^2\psi_k^{(j)}|<K_1C_{\alpha_1}(L^j\eta)^{2-\alpha_1}\varepsilon_k$, and the analytic extension of $\phi_j$ in (2.21) are cited from "the next paper" (P2) and are not proved in P1. Lemma 2.1 and 3.1-3.2 depend on them.
4. (H.1)'s $E_k$, normalization constants and the $\lambda=\infty$ case: the case $\lambda=\infty$ requires $\delta(|\phi|^2-1)$ constraints, but the whole analysis uses $\Phi_j(\sigma,\varepsilon;\lambda,\nu)$ with $\alpha=\frac\lambda2(|\phi|^2-1)$ bounded: for $\lambda=\infty$ the meaning of the inductive hypotheses (e.g. $\chi_k$ with $|\alpha_k|$, $\lambda_k=\infty$) is not explained. Claimed ("all constructions and proofs are uniform for $\lambda\in[1,+\infty]$", p.104) without details.
5. (H.9) has uncertain printed details: the norm of g ("$\|g\|_{l^1}<1$" reading), the exponent in the bound $M_0\xi^{\gamma-2\alpha_1}$ (p.120), and the claim that the sum is $\le M_0K_0\beta^{-1/2}$ ("small for $\beta$ large") — I cannot reconcile these precisely from the image alone. Marked uncertain.
6. $\alpha_{0,k}=\alpha_0(1+\sum_{n=-k}^\infty\frac1{n^2})$ in (H.3): the printed lower limit makes no sense as written (n=0 term). Read as a convergent perturbation of $\alpha_0$; UNCERTAIN.
7. Inconsistencies in the choice of $\gamma_0$: p.115-116 say $0\le\gamma<\gamma_0$; p.130 gives $\gamma_0=\min\{1,\frac{d-2}{2}\}$ (needed for $\alpha_2>0$); p.136 requires $d-4\gamma>0$; p.145-147 improve to $\gamma_0=\min\{\frac{d-2}2,\frac12\}$ (because of the second-derivative term $\sigma^{3-\alpha_1}\varepsilon^2$ in (3.100)); p.145: the author says that an improvement up to $\min\{\frac{d-2}2,1\}$ is possible with an additional symmetry analysis "quite straightforward, but lengthy" (omitted). So the admissible range in the final form is $\gamma_0=\frac12$ for $d\ge3$ ("$\gamma_0=\frac12$ for $d\ge3$", p.147).
8. Many bounds are asserted "similarly"/"in the same way": e.g. (3.77)-(3.81) from (3.73)-(3.79) (Taylor remainder with difference quotients), the bounds for $v_0^{(\rho)},v_2^{(\rho)}$ (p.150), (3.100)-(3.103). The third-order term is estimated crudely (p.145-146). Numeric constants are explicitly non-optimized, may contain slips; the proofs are formulas rather than prose. Place where I suspect typos: (3.79) trace formula with $e_N\otimes e_N$ (used to define $v_{2,2}$), (3.86), (3.93) (the constant 18 vs 12), (3.105), (3.129), (3.131) (garbled middle line).
9. The localization expansion (H.5) is stated for $\mathscr E^{(j)}(y,X;\cdot)$ depending on restrictions to X, but the analytic extension relevant for expansions uses configurations extended by constants outside X (3.58) with the characteristic functions $\chi_X$; the logic of why $\mathscr E(y,X;\psi(y))$ is meaningful for configurations defined on all T is only implicit.
10. Different instances of the same constants: $K_\alpha$ appears in (3.49) and as $K_a$ in (3.65) with different values; sign/exponent of $L$ powers are not consistent in a few places (e.g. $L^{1+\alpha}$ vs $L^{3/2}$ vs $L^{1+\alpha/2}$ — relates to different choices of $n_1$) — UNCERTAIN, would need to be re-derived.
11. (H.6),(H.7) are stated for $j\le k$ and the terms at $e_1,\nu_je_1$ with $\lim_{T_\xi\to\xi\mathbb Z^d}$ (thermodynamic limits of localized terms); the existence of these limits and their independence of the torus is not discussed.

## 8. Preliminary formalization notes

Self-contained and formalizable early (guesses, no Mathlib check):
- Definitions of lattice, tori, action (1.5), block average Q, kernel T, scalings (1.9)-(1.10); explicit solutions (2.9)-(2.14) of the linear-algebra part of the ideal flow (algebraic identities, $Q^*$, composition of block averages).

- Ward identity (3.122)-(3.124): differentiate $\mathscr E(R_t\psi,\nu R_th)=\mathscr E(\psi,\nu h)$; needs differentiability in Fréchet sense on complex domains (holomorphic functions on finite-dim spaces; Mathlib has `DifferentiableOn`/`AnalyticOn` and Cauchy estimates in one variable (circle integrals) and some several-variable analytic API; several-variable Cauchy estimates probably need development).
- Tree-size $d_j(X)$ and $K_0<\infty$ summation (2.28): combinatorial counting of connected unions of cubes with given tree length; moderate.

Infrastructure needed: finite-dimensional complex analysis (polydisc Cauchy estimates, Taylor with integral remainder), lattice Taylor formula along unit paths, symmetry-reduction of invariant tensors under stabilizers in O(N) (could be done by explicit matrices for rank <= 2), discrete convex geometry (cone/tree path constructions, p.131-133), an abstract notion of "localized function with exponential decay in tree size".
