## 0. Identification

T. Balaban, "The large field renormalization operation for classical N-vector models", Commun. Math. Phys. 198 (1998) 493-534. Bundle key P7. 42 pages (received 2 Feb 1998, accepted 12 Feb 1998). Only 15 references.

Summary. After one RG step (the fluctuation integral and scaling, P4-P6), the new density $\rho_{k+1}$ has a "small field" part satisfying all inductive hypotheses, and a "large field" part $\rho'_{k+1}(Z_{k+1},\mathbb A_{k+1})$ summed over large-field regions and multi-indices (eq. (1.1), p. 493). The hypothesis (H.6) of P5 (a lower bound on the function $K(Z,\mathbb A\cap Z)$ controlling how many future steps the large-field smallness factor can still pay for, inequality (1.30) of P5) is the only inductive hypothesis that P4-P6 cannot propagate (Intro, p. 493; p. 495). The paper defines an operation $R^{(k)}$ on densities which does not change $\int d\psi_k\rho_k$ (eq. (1.7)/(1.8)) and which removes exactly those large-field terms for which some component $Z$ of the region has $K(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)=0$ ("components of the second kind"). For these components it (i) introduces new large-field restrictions (new characteristic functions) that give a very small factor, or (ii) if none apply, replaces the large-field density on $Z$ by a small-field expression times a normalised ratio of integrals over a small domain $Z_0$ (cancellation of main actions), and exponentiates the remainder through a polymer (Mayer) expansion whose activities are small. The result is $R^{(k)}\rho_k$ satisfying (H.1)-(H.7) of P5, with new correction terms added to the $\mathcal R^{(k)}_m$ of the effective action. This completes the inductive proof, hence the whole RG flow (Theorem 5, p. 497). Section 4 treats the last RG step (integration of $\rho_{k_f}$ over $\psi_{k_f}$) and gives the generating functional of correlation functions (4.17), leaving details to [7]=P9.

Takes from earlier: P1 (definition of the model, normalization (1.12)), P2 (variational problems, background configuration formulas, Prop. 1.1, 3.1, 4.1), P3 (localization expansions, exponential decay (2.55),(2.56), Props. 2.1, 4.3), P4 (RG equations, renormalization transformations, last step in small field case Sect. 6), P5 (inductive hypotheses (H.1)-(H.7), spaces (1.42), Theorems 1-3, formulas (1.24)-(1.35)), P6 (large field definitions (3.1)-(3.3), (3.5)-(3.9), Prop. 3.1, 3.2, fluctuation measure analysis). Supplies to later: P9 (correlation functions) and the claim that the full inductive description is closed (Theorem 5).

IMPORTANT caveat on this review: the paper is heavily dependent on formulas (and definitions of $\mathbb T_k$, $\mathbb A_k$, $K$, $\rho'_k$, $\chi_k$, $\tilde\chi_k$, the spaces $\tilde\Psi,\Xi$) from P5/P6, which are not reproduced here. This paper defines almost nothing from scratch; it is an "analogous modifications" paper. A faithful Lean statement of the objects cannot be written from P7 alone.

## 1. Setting and notation

Everything is relative to scale $k<k_0$ (before the final step). The torus has unit lattice $T^{(0)}$-type structure; $T^{(k)}_1$ is the scale-$k$ unit lattice ($L^{k}$-blocks of unit sites), $\Lambda_j$ the lattice at scale $j$, $\Delta_k(y)$ the block (unit cube of $T^{(k)}_1$) of the point $y$, $\pi_k$ the partition into large cubes at scale $k$ (the "large cubes" of side $M$ in the $L^{-k}$ scale; $M$ = size of large cube), $\mathbb B_k$ generating sets (sets where background configurations are minimised), $\Omega_k,\Omega_k^c$ small/large field domains (with $\Omega'_{k+1}=\Omega_{k+1}$ when no large field regions), $W_k,U_k$ localization domains, "~" = enlargement by one layer of cubes (so $X^{\sim3}$ is three layers, $X^{\approx}$ etc.), $\mathcal D_k(\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega_k^c)$ the class of localization domains modulo large field.

Constants: $\beta_k=\beta L^{k(d-2)}$-type running couplings (after $k$ steps, see (2.10): $\beta_{j+1}=\beta_k(L^{j+1}\eta)^{d-2}$ etc.), $a_k,\lambda_k,\nu_k$ (with $\nu_{k+1}\approx\nu_kL^2$), $\varepsilon_k$ (analyticity radius) and $\delta_k$ (small field constant, $\delta_k=\beta_k^{-1/2}A_1(\log\beta_ka)^{p_0}$-type, cf. (2.25),(2.61)), $R_j$/$[R_j]$ (scale-dependent size factors of large field regions, $R_k\sim R_0(\log\beta_k a)^{d}$-type; precise definition is in P5/P6, not here), $p_0(\beta_k), p_1(\beta_k),p_2(\beta_k)$ (powers of $\log\beta_k$: $p_2\ge 2d+2$, $2p_0>2d+1$, $2p_0-4\ge 2d+2$), $H_k=H_0\log\beta_k$, $K_0,B_0,\kappa,\gamma_0,\alpha,c_5,C_6,K_1,K_2,K_3,K_6,A_0,A_1$ etc.

Densities: $\rho_k=\sum_{Z_k,\mathbb A_k}\rho_k(Z_k,\mathbb A_k)$ (1.3), with
$$\rho_{k+1}=\sum_{Z_{k+1},\mathbb A_{k+1}}\rho'_{k+1}(Z_{k+1},\mathbb A_{k+1})\chi_{k+1}(Z^c_{k+1})\exp[\mathcal A_{k+1}(Z^c_{k+1})+\mathcal F_{k+1}(Z^c_{k+1})+\mathcal R^{(k+1)}(Z_{k+1})]\quad(1.1,\ p.493).$$
$\chi$ and the exponential are the "small field part" ($\chi_{k+1}$ = characteristic function of small field conditions on $\psi_{k+1}$ in $Z^c$). $\rho'$ is the large field density, built inductively by (3.1)-(3.3) of P6 from integral operators $\mathbb T_k(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)$ (operators acting on functions of $\psi,\psi'$; products over $j=k-1..k_1$ of $\chi_{j+1}T^{(j)}\chi'_{j+1}\exp[-\beta_{j+1}A^*_{j+1}\ldots]$, eq. (2.6) p. 499) times $\exp\mathcal B^{(j)}$ boundary terms. $\mathcal A,\mathcal E,\mathcal F,\mathcal R$ = main action, effective action, generating functional and its remainder ($\mathcal R^{(k)}_m$ = function whose $m$ more steps of expansion are tracked), $g$ = source variable, $h$ = external-field unit vector ($|h|=1$).

The function $K(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)$ (defined in P5/P6, inductively by (3.9),(3.10) of P6, with $\kappa_\kappa$-notation) is non-negative and measures how many further RG steps the large-field smallness (inequality (1.30) of P5) will persist. $K>0$: "component of the first kind"; $K=0$: "second kind" (p. 495). (H.6) of P5 says all components of $Z_k$ are of the first kind; this fails to be preserved by one step in general because on a component with no new large-field regions $\kappa^{(\kappa)}$ decreases by $O(1)\log\beta_k+\text{const}$ per cube (p. 495).

Bounds quoted from P5 and used in the Introduction (p. 494, eq. (1.2)): for $Z_k=\bigcup_{i=1}^nX_i$ connected components,
$$\sum_{\mathbb A_k}\int d\psi_k\lceil_{Z_k}\rho'_k(Z_k,\mathbb A_k)\le\prod_i\exp(-2p_2(\beta_k)-H_k|X_i|)\cdot\sup\chi\exp|\mathcal B^{(k)}|\cdot\exp\Big\{\sum_{X\in\mathcal D_k(\mathrm{mod}\Omega_k^c),X\cap Z_k\ne\emptyset,X\cap Z_k^c\ne\emptyset}B_0\exp(-\kappa d_k(X\,\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega_k^c))\Big\}$$
$$\le\prod_i\exp(-2p_2(\beta_k)-H_k|X_i|+B_0K_0M^{-d}|X_i|)\le\prod_i\exp(-2p_2(\beta_k)-\tfrac12H_k|X_i|),$$
using $H_k=H_0\log\beta_k$, $H_0>2B_0K_0M^{-d}$. Here these bounds follow from (H.5), (H.7), (1.26),(1.29)-(1.31) of P5. So the large-field densities are tiny: factors $\exp(-2p_2(\beta_k))\sim\exp(-c(\log\beta_k)^{2d+2})$ per component plus volume decay $\exp(-H_k|X|)$, which are used to make a convergent polymer expansion of "gas of weakly interacting polymers with exponentially decaying interaction potentials".

## 2. Main results

### Theorem 1 (recalled, [5,6])
(p.493) For $\rho_k$ satisfying (H.1)-(H.7) of P5 (and P4), the step $S^{(k)}T^{(k)}$ gives $\rho_{k+1}$ of the form (1.1) satisfying all the hypotheses for $k+1$ with the possible exception of (H.6) of [5]. The small field parts satisfy (H.2)-(H.4) [5] and (H.2)-(H.7) [4]. This is cited, not proved here.

### Theorem 2 (p.497; reformulation of Theorem 2 of [5] with $k+1\to k$)
Under the assumptions of Theorem 1 [5,6] and for $k<k_0$ there exists a transformation $R^{(k)}$ defined on densities $\rho_k=S^{(k-1)}T^{(k-1)}\rho_{k-1}$ in the image of $S^{(k-1)}T^{(k-1)}$ considered on the space (1.42) [5] for index $k-1$ (defined "in a general way by (1.3)-(1.6)") such that
1. $R^{(k)}\rho_k$ satisfies all the inductive hypotheses (H.1)-(H.7) [5], i.e. belongs to the space (1.42) [5];
2. normalization $\int d\psi_kR^{(k)}\rho_k=\int d\psi_k\rho_k$ (1.8);
3. its effect is to remove from the sum (1.3) the terms for which $Z_k$ has components $Z$ with $K(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)=0$;
4. a stronger normalization: $R^{(k)}$ determines uniquely a transformation $\mathcal R^{(k)}$ of the extended space of effective actions and generating functionals which is the identity on $\{\mathcal E^{(j)}\}_{j\le k},\{\mathcal M^{(j)}\}_{j\le k},\{\mathcal R^{(k)}_n\}_{n<m}$, and generates a new function $\mathcal R''^{(k)}_m(\psi_k,g)$ which is added to $\mathcal R^{(k)}_m(\psi_k,g)$; the sum has the same properties and the bounds required by (H.4) [5].
5. For $k\ge k_0$ take $R^{(k)}=\mathrm{id}$, $\mathcal R''^{(k)}_m=0$.
(The proof is Sects. 2-3.)

### Theorem 5 (p.497; combination of Thm 1 [5,6], Thm 2 [5, and above], Thm 4 [6])
Under the assumptions of Theorem 1 [5,6], for $k<k_0$ the transformation $R^{(k+1)}S^{(k)}T^{(k)}$ maps the space (1.42)[5] into the space determined by $k+1$, $BL^{d-2}$, $\bar\nu L^2$, $L$ with remaining constants unchanged, and satisfies the conclusions of Theorem 1 [4,5,6]. For $k\ge k_0$ the transformations applied to an initial density $\rho_{k_0}$ yield densities of the forms (1.1) [6], satisfying (H.1)-(H.5), (H.7) [5], with integral operators $\mathbb T_k(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)$ satisfying inequality (1.31) [5] with $\ell=1$, as long as $\nu_k\le1$. Hence the densities $\{\rho_k\}$ of the whole RG flow obtained by applying the RG transformations to the initial density $\rho_0$ of (1.1) [1] satisfy all conditions with constants $\beta=\beta_0$, $a=1$, $\lambda=\lambda_0$, $\nu=\nu_0$, $B L^{k(d-2)}$, $\bar\nu L^{2k}$, with $B$ sufficiently large and $\bar\nu$ sufficiently small.

## 3. Proof architecture

### Section 1 (pp.493-498): Introduction, outline
Explains (p.494-495) why (H.6) is the issue: the function $\kappa^{(\kappa)}$ defined in (3.9) of P6 has on components with no new large field regions only negative contributions $-(O(1)\log\beta_k+\text{const})|Z_k^c\cap Z_{k+1}|$, so after sufficiently many steps it becomes negative and (1.30) [5] and with it (1.2) fail. By Prop. 3.2 of P6, the inequality is lost only after very many steps, by which time the initial large field region has been shrunk by scaling to a region of tiny size with only small field restrictions on all added layers. So we then treat the large-field part like a fluctuation integral: extract the small field expression, make a polymer expansion of the remainder, exponentiate it. Structure of $R^{(k)}$: (1.3) classification of components, (1.4) transformed representation, (1.5) reference densities, (1.6) definition, (1.7) normalization. The definition (1.6) is simple; the substance is writing $R^{(k)}\rho_k$ as a density satisfying all hypotheses; two issues: the cancellation of numerator and denominator main actions, and analyticity of the resulting localized expressions (non-linear non-local restrictions from characteristic functions).

Definition (1.6), p.496:
$$R^{(k)}\rho_k=\sum_{Y_k,Z_k,\mathbb A''_k}\rho_{k,0}(Z_k)\frac{\int d\psi_k\lceil_{Z_{k,0}}\rho''_k(Y_k,Z_k,\mathbb A_k'')}{\int d\psi_k\lceil_{Z_{k,0}}\rho_{k,0}(Z_k)},\qquad \rho_{k,0}(Z)=\chi_{Z_0}\exp[-\beta_kA^*_k(Z;\psi_k,\phi_k(\mathbb B_k(U);\tilde\psi_k))]$$
($U=Z^\approx$; $Z_{k,0}$ the union of the $Z_0$ over components $Z$ of $Z_k$). Because the bracket in $\{\}$ integrates (over $Z_{k,0}$) to $\int d\psi_k\lceil_{Z_{k,0}}\rho''_k$, (1.7) holds identically. The introduction of $\chi_{Z_0}$ puts a restriction on $\psi_k$ on $Z_0$ (where no restriction otherwise exists) - the main purpose.

### Section 2 (pp.498-517): Detailed definition of $R^{(k)}$ and the cancellation problem
Works with one component $Z$ of the second kind ($Z_k=Z$ w.l.o.g.).

(a) Geometry (pp.498-499, (2.1)-(2.5)). Because $K(Z,\ldots)=0$, the conditions of Prop. 3.2(ii) [6] hold: $Z_j$ for $k_1\le j\le k$ are rectangular parallelepipeds with diameters in the $L^{-j}$-scale below the critical size $12M[R_{j-1}]$; no large field regions $P,Q,R,S$ in the new layers. Distances (2.1): $\mathrm{dist}_j(Z_j,\Omega_{j+1})>2(L+1)M+M[R_{j-1}]+LM[R_j]$. Since $L^{-(k-j)}LM[R_j]$ is decreasing as $j$ decreases, there is a smallest $k_2$ with this term $\ge1$; then $L^{-(k-k_2)}LM[R_{k_2}]<\frac98L$ and (2.2) $\mathrm{diam}_k(Z_{k_2})<\frac{27}{2}$, $\mathrm{dist}_k(Z_{k_2},\Omega_{k_2+1})>1$. $Z_0$ = smallest union of unit cubes of $T_1^{(k)}$ containing $Z_{k_2}$ (2.3); $\mathrm{diam}_k(Z_0)<15$, $\mathrm{dist}_{k_2}(Z_0,\Omega^c_{k_2+1})>2(L+1)M+M[R_{k_2-1}]$ (2.4). Auxiliary $Z_0''$: union of cubes of $\pi_k$ meeting $Z_{k_3}$ with $k_3$ the smallest index with $L^{-(k-k_3)}L[R_{k_3}]\ge1$; diam $<15M$ (2.5).

(b) Structure of $\mathbb T_k(\mathbb A_k)$ (2.6): product over $j=k-1,\ldots,k_1$ of $\{\chi_{j+1}(\Omega^{\sim-3}_{j+1}\cap Z_{j+1})T^{(j)}(\Omega^c_{j+1})\chi'_{j+1}(\ldots)\chi_j(Z_j^c\cap(\Omega_{j+1}^{\sim3})^c)\exp[-\beta_{j+1}A^*_{j+1}(Z^c_j\cap Z_{j+1};\ldots)-E_j|Z^c_j\cap\Omega^c_{j+1}|_\xi-E_j'''|\Omega_{j+1}\cap Z_j|_\xi]\}\,\mathbb T_{k_1}(Z_{k_1},\mathbb A_{k_1})\exp[\sum_{j}\mathcal B^{(j)}(Z_j^c\cap Z_{j+1})]$. The $T^{(j)}$'s compose (composition formulas Sect. 4 of [2], Lemma 4.1 [2], (1.36),(1.37) of [4]) into (2.7): a product of two transformations localized in $Z_0$ and $Z_0^c\cap\Omega_k^c$, giving the Gaussian-type kernel (2.9) with constants (4.6),(4.7) [2]; $\int d\psi_k\lceil_{Z_0}$ acts only on the first transformation (2.8).

(c) Renormalizing the actions (2.10)-(2.17). The sum of main actions at scales $j+1$ is converted to scale-$k$ coefficients by $k-j-1$ applications of the renormalization decomposition ((2.31) [6]), errors bounded as (2.11); get (2.12) with boundary function $C^{(j+1)}$; define the new generating set $\mathbb B_{k_1,k}$ (2.13) and expansion (2.14); $C_{k_1,k}$ analytic on (1.32)[5], bounded by (2.15). The exponential becomes (2.16): $\exp[-\beta_kA^*_k(Z^c_{k_1}\cap Z_k;\psi,\phi_{k_1,k})+C_{k_1,k}]$. The combined quadratic form + action (2.17) is $\ge A^*_k(Z_0^c\cap Z_k;\psi_k,\phi_{k_1,k})$ and Prop. 3.1 [6] ((3.5),(3.6)) applies.

(d) New small factors from large field restrictions ("new large field regions") (pp.501-505). This is the combinatorial heart of getting smallness. Four successive decompositions of unity:
 1. (2.18): $1=\tilde\chi_k+\tilde\chi_k^c$ on $Z_0^c\cap(\Omega_k^c)^{\sim3}$ ($\tilde\Psi$ = the space where unit-bond derivatives $<\delta$ and $||\psi|-1|<\delta$). On the $\tilde\chi^c_k$ term: either some derivative $\ge3\delta_k$, so (3.5)[6] gives $\exp(-\frac92\gamma_0p_0^2(\beta_k))$, or all derivatives are small but some $||\psi_k(z)|-1|\ge3\delta_k$; connect $z$ to a point $z_0\in\partial(\Omega_k^{\sim-3}\cap T_1^{(k)})$ by a segment (length $<6M[R_{k-1}]$) inside the region, accumulate $<6M\cdot3\delta_k$, get $||\psi_k(z)|-\ldots|<c_5$ and apply the second part of Prop. 3.1 [6], inequality (3.6) [6]. Combine factors (combinatorial factor $\exp O(1)|Z_0^c\cap(\Omega_k^c)^{\sim3}|$, the exponential factor of size (2.15)) to bound by $\exp(-4\gamma_0p_0^2(\beta_k))$ for $2p_0>2d+1$, $A_0$ large. Then the multi-index $\mathbb A_k$ is enlarged by the new large field region and $Z$ becomes of the first kind (bound $\exp(-\kappa_k(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)-4\gamma_0p_0^2(\beta_k))$).
 2. second decomposition (2.1)[6] with $\Omega''^{\sim-3}_{k+1}$ replaced by $Z_0''^c\cap(\Omega_k^c)^{\sim3}$; sum over $S_k''\subset Z_0''^c\cap(\Omega^c_k)^{\sim3}$; factors $\exp(-\frac{\gamma_0}{2\cdot6^dK_1^2}p_0^2(\beta_k)\frac1{M^d}|S_k''|)$ as in (3.8)[6]; $S''_k\ne\emptyset$ gives first kind.
 3. Reference vector $\psi_0=\tilde\psi/|\tilde\psi|$, $\tilde\psi$ = average of $\psi_k$ over $\partial(Z_0^c\cap T_1^{(k)})$ (2.19), $|\psi_k(z)-\psi_0|<90d\delta_k$ on that boundary (2.20). Introduce $\psi^0_{k_1}$ and new characteristic functions $\chi^0_{k_1,1/2}$ (same as $\chi_{k_1}$ but with $\frac12\delta_{k_1}$ and $\psi_{k_1}\to\psi_{k_1}^0$) on $U^c_{k_1}\cap U^{\sim6}_{k_1}$, decomposition over regions $Q''_{k_1}$. This is the delicate case: if the third inequality in (2.21) ($|\psi_{k_1}(y)-\psi_0|\ge K_1^{-1}\frac12\delta_{k_1}$) holds (and neither of the other two), then chain of points $y_{k_1+1},y'_{k_1+1},\ldots,y_k$ through the scales (2.22)-(2.28) gives $|\psi_{k_1}(y)-\psi_0|<K_1^{-1}\frac12\delta_{k_1}(\ldots)$ (2.29) unless one of the bounds (2.22) (with $c$ given by (2.30), $c=(64K_1LMR_{k_1})^{-1}$) fails; failing gives a small factor $\exp(-\frac12\gamma_0(64K_1LMR_{k_1})^{-2}p_0^2(\beta_{k_1}))$. Here the index $n_1=k-k_1$ is chosen as the smallest satisfying (2.31): $n_1\log L>\frac1\alpha\log\beta_{k_1}a$, so $\beta_{k_1}$ and $\beta_k$ are polynomially comparable (2.32). 2. The proof is mostly sketched: "obviously the same way" for $\chi'_{j+1}$ (p.515), "similar remarks should be made for other operations" (p.502), the polymer expansion bound "almost completely standard, we will discuss a similar bound in the section on fluctuation integrals" (p.528), the bounds of the exponentials in (3.9) are "obtained by inspection". No explicit statement of a cluster-expansion convergence theorem.
3. The constant bookkeeping in (2.29)-(2.33) is long; $K_1$ appears in both $\delta_{k_1}K_1^{-1}$ and constants; potential typos: (2.29) vs (2.30) (the $c$ choice $\frac12$ for the first term); (2.26) uses $L^{-1/3}<3/4$; the equation after (2.31) writes the strength "$>(\alpha/d)^p$" - accepted. ## 8. Preliminary formalization notes

- Hard dependency: formalizing P7 requires the exact definitions of $\rho'_k$, $\mathbb T_k$, $K$ (P5/P6), and the full spaces (1.42); P7 itself contains no self-contained theorem that can be formalized in isolation. The whole paper is an "inductive-step" argument.
- Self-contained-looking parts (formalizable relatively early): the geometry of $Z_0$ and indices $k_1,k_2,k_3$ (2.1)-(2.5) (arithmetic and rectangular domains); the abstract normalization property (1.7) (algebraic identity for ratio of integrals); the elementary lower bound $\langle\delta\psi,(-\Delta^1)\delta\psi\rangle\ge\frac1{36}\lVert\delta\psi\rVert^2$ with Dirichlet conditions (2.37)-(2.38); chain-of-points estimates (2.22)-(2.30) and inequality (2.25), (2.31)-(2.32); the $O(N)$ coset-averaging argument (Haar measure invariance, local analytic sections).
- Needs infrastructure: Gaussian-type and finite-dimensional integrals on $\mathbb R^{N|Z_0|}$ with analytic dependence, Cauchy estimates on polydiscs, implicit-function theorem (Mathlib has it for Banach spaces, analytic version partially), Mayer/polymer expansion with exponentiation (GUESS: not in Mathlib; must be built, e.g. Kotecky-Preiss type convergence criterion for polymer gases with localization domains $\mathcal D_k(\mathrm{mod}\Omega_k^c)$), Haar measure on $O(N)$ (Mathlib has Haar measure on compact groups; $O(N)$ as `Matrix.orthogonalGroup`; coset integration needs work).
- Hardest: Lemma 2.5 and the analytic-extension Lemma 3.1 (huge bookkeeping of constants and nested domains); the polymer expansion with non-small boundary terms using only the large field smallness; the interplay of the new restrictions with all earlier hypotheses (multi-index bookkeeping). The paper's own arguments are sketches ("obviously", "similar"), so a formalization would require reconstructing the constant chase and probably re-deriving statements that are not given here.
