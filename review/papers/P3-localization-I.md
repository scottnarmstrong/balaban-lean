## 0. Identification

T. Balaban, "Localization Expansions. I. Functions of the 'Background' Configurations", Commun. Math. Phys. 182 (1996) 33-82. Received 17 July 1995, accepted 2 January 1996. Bundle key P3. 50 pages (82 printed pages minus 32 = 50 PDF pages; p. 82 is the reference list only; the text ends p. 81).

Summary. P1 sets up the renormalization-group (RG) flow and assumes, as inductive hypothesis (H.5), that effective-action terms $\mathcal E^{(j)}(X;\psi_j)$ have "localization expansions" (sums over localization domains with exponential decay in a linear size of the domain). When the next step is done, one has to evaluate such terms at the composition of the old spin variable $\psi_j$ with the background fields $\phi_k,\alpha_k$ (the minimizers of the variational problems of P2), and to show the result again has a localized expansion. This paper does that for the "background" part. It (i) introduces interpolation (decoupling) parameters $s_\square\in\mathbb C$ attached to large cubes into the minimizers $\phi_k,\alpha_k$ via a generalized random-walk expansion of the Green's operator $G(\alpha)$ (taken from Balaban's 1982/83 papers) and proves that $\delta\phi_k(s),\delta\alpha_k(s)$ are analytic in $|s_\square|\le e^{\kappa_1}$ (Prop. 2.1, 2.2, 2.3); (ii) does the same for the fluctuation covariance square root $C^{1/2}_\Lambda$ and the operator $\log$ via a random-walk expansion of the resolvent (Prop. 3.1); (iii) assembles these, with the composition formula (4.21) of P2 and a minimal generating set $\mathbb B_j(X)$ determined by the localization domain $X$, to prove Prop. 4.3: the function $\mathcal E(X;\psi^{(j)}_k(\psi^{(k)}_k(\theta)+\psi'))$ has an expansion over domains $Y\in\mathscr D(\mathbb B_{k+1})$, $Y\supset X$ with bound $E\exp(-\kappa_0 d_j(X)-2\kappa\tilde d_{k+1}(Y))$. It takes from P1 the definitions/inductive hypotheses and spaces $\Psi^c$, $\Xi^c$, and from P2 the variational equations, Prop. 1.1, 3.1 (existence/bounds of minimizers), the composition formula (4.21), Lemmas 4.3, 4.4, and the Green's function $G(\alpha)$. It supplies the "localized background-field" input to the fluctuation-integral analysis in the next paper ([P4] presumably, and P5/P6; the paper says "next paper"). The proofs are given at a very condensed, partly schematic level ("it is easy to see", "analogous").

## 1. Setting and notation

All objects are those of P1, P2; they are recalled only partially here (Introduction, pp. 33-36). UNCERTAIN: many definitions are cited by number only (e.g. (1.1)-(1.3), (2.27) of P1 or P2), so the following is a reconstruction from usage in this paper.

- Lattice: $T_\eta$ = unit lattice rescaled by the microscopic spacing $\eta$; $T^{(j)}=T_{L^{-j}}$-scale lattice, $\xi=L^{-j}$ ("original lattice $T_\xi$, $T^{(j)}$ unit lattice $T^{(j)}_1$", p. 34). $L$ = integer block ratio. Scaled sizes: $L^j\eta$-cubes, $ML^j\eta$-cubes (large cubes, $M=L^m$), $M_1=L^{m_1}$ (small-large cube size used in this paper; the RG size $M\ge M_1$, p. 68).
- Generating set $\mathbb B_k$ = sequence of domains $\{\Omega_1,\dots,\Omega_k\}$ (P2 (1.1)-(1.3)), $\Omega_0=T$, $\Omega_{j+1}$ the next. $\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+1}$ is a union of large cubes of scale $L^{-j}$ (partition $\pi_j$, cover $\pi'_j$). $\mathbb B_j\prec\mathbb B'_k$ is the ordering (P2 (4.1)).
- Fields: $\psi$ = spin-type variable at scale $j$, $\phi\in\mathbb R^N$ ($N$-component) minimizing configuration, $\alpha$ the Lagrange-type variable for the constraint $\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(\phi^2-1)$; $\phi_k=\phi(\mathbb B_k;\psi),\ \alpha_k=\alpha(\mathbb B_k;\psi)$, $\phi_k=\phi_0+\delta\phi_k$, $\alpha_k=\alpha_0+\delta\alpha_k$ with $(\phi_0,\alpha_0)$ the approximate solutions of P2 (3.2),(3.3) (p. 36). $h$ = auxiliary unit-vector-like field with $|h^2-1|$ small; $\psi\mapsto\psi(\mathbb B_j,\mathbb B_k;\psi_k)$ the composition ("$\psi^{(j)}_k$").
- Averaging operations $Q=Q(\mathbb B_k)$, $Q_j$ (one-block averaging); $Q^*aQ$ the quadratic form term, $a=a(\mathbb B_k)$, $a_j$.
- Operator $G(\alpha)=(-\Delta^{D,\eta}_{\Omega_1}+Q^*aQ+v+\alpha)^{-1}$ (P2 (3.22), see (2.3) p. 37 for local versions $G(\square_z,\alpha)$ with Neumann boundary on the part of $\partial\square_z$ inside $\Omega_1$). Distance $d(y,y')=\inf_\Gamma|\Gamma|_{\mathbb B_k}$, $|\Gamma|_{\mathbb B_k}=\sum_{j=1}^k(L^j\eta)^{-1}|\Gamma\cap\Omega_j\cap\Omega^c_{j+1}|$ (2.4, p. 37). Scaled sizes $d_j(X)$ (P1 (2.27)), $\tilde d_j(Y)$ (1.3, p. 34): $\inf\frac1M|\Gamma|_{\mathbb B_j}$ over tree graphs $\Gamma\subset Y$ meeting all cubes of the covers $\pi'_n$ ($n\le j$) in $Y\cap\Omega_n\cap\Omega^c_{n+1}$.
- Localization domains $\mathscr D(\mathbb B_j)$: connected domains $Y\subset T$ with $Y\cap\Omega_n\cap\Omega_{n+1}^c$ in $\mathscr D_n$, $n\le j$ (1.2, p. 34). "Polymer" = such a domain $Y$ (or the pair $(X,Y)$ with $X\subset Y$).
- Interpolation / localization parameters: one complex number $s_\square$ per large cube $\square$ outside $X_1$ (2.16, p. 41); $s\equiv1$ restores the original function; $s_\square=0$ on a family $\mathscr C$ makes the function independent of the data in $\bigcup_{\mathscr C}\square$.
- Analyticity domains: polydisc $\{s:|s_\square|\le e^{\kappa_1}\}$; spaces $\Phi^c,\Xi^c_j,\tilde\Psi^c$, $\Psi^c_j(X;C_0L^j\eta,\varepsilon_k)$ of P1, restated on pp. 73-74 (4.25)-(4.28) (generalizations of P1 (2.15),(2.16),(2.20),(2.21)). Real configurations $(\psi,h)$ extended to complex $(\psi+\psi',h+h')$ with $\psi',h'$ small complex perturbations.
- Cube partitions of unity $\{h_z\}$ (2.2, p. 37), with $\sum h_z^2=1$, $|\partial h_z|<2(ML^j\eta)^{-1}$, subordinate to cubes $\square_z$ of size $3ML^j\eta$ centered at points of the lattice $T^{(j+m)}_{ML^j\eta}$.
- Fluctuation operator: $C^{(k)1/2}_\Lambda=\big((aL^{-2}Q^*Q+\Delta^{(k)})\!\restriction_\Lambda\big)^{-1/2}$, $\Lambda=\Omega^{(k)}_{k+1}\subset T^{(k)}_1$, with $\Delta^{(k)}$ given by (3.1), (3.6)-(3.7) (p. 59-61); $\beta_k$ inverse temperature at step $k$; $\psi=\beta_k^{-1/2}C^{(k)1/2}_\Lambda\psi$ ("$\psi'$" in (1.1),(4.24)), variable $\psi$ "fluctuation", $|\psi|<p_1(\beta_k)=A_1(\log\beta_k)^{p_1}$ (4.23) (discussed in the next paper).

## 2. Main results

The paper has no numbered "Theorem". The main statements are Propositions 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 3.1, Lemma 4.1, Lemma 4.2, Proposition 4.3, and the target statement (1.4)-(1.5).

**Target statement (p. 34, (1.4)-(1.5)).** For $X\in\mathscr D_j$, $X\subset\Omega_{k+1}$, $\mathcal E^{(j)}(X;\psi_j)$ a term of the P1 (H.5) expansion,
$$\mathcal E\big(X;\psi^{(j)}_k(\psi^{(k)}(\theta)+\beta_k^{-1/2}C^{(k)1/2}_\Lambda\psi)\big)=\sum_{Y\in\mathscr D(\mathbb B_{k+1}):Y\supset X}\mathcal E(X,Y;\theta,\psi),\qquad |\mathcal E(X,Y;\theta,\psi)|<E_0\exp(-\kappa_0d_j(X)-2\kappa\tilde d_{k+1}(Y)),$$
terms depending on $\theta,\psi$ only through their restrictions to $Y$ (analyticity domains postponed to Section 4).

**Proposition 2.1 (p. 56).** Consider solutions $\delta\phi_k,\delta\alpha_k$ of the system (3.20) of P2 defined on the space (3.21) of P2. There exist constants $B_1,K_3,c_6$ such that if $B_1c_6\le\frac18$, $c_6\le\gamma_2$, and $B_1\delta_0\le\frac18$, $B_3\delta_1\le\frac12c_6$, the solutions extend to analytic functions $\delta\phi_k(s),\delta\alpha_k(s)$ on $\{|s|\le e^{\kappa_1}\}$ with $\delta\alpha_k(s)$ solving (2.30)/(2.43), $\delta\phi_k(s)$ given by (2.16), coinciding with $\delta\phi_k,\delta\alpha_k$ at $s=1$. If $s_\square=0$ for $\square\in\mathscr C$ then $\chi_{X_0}\delta\phi_k(s)$ depends only on the variables restricted to the connected component of $Y_0^c=(\bigcup_{\mathscr C}\square)^c$ containing $X_0$ ($Y_0=\bigcup_{\mathscr C}\square$ as printed), $\delta\alpha_k(s)=0$ on $Y_0$ and $\chi_{\Delta(y)}\delta\alpha_k(s)$ depends on the component of $Y_0^c$ containing $\Delta(y)$. Bounds (2.54)-(2.56): $|\delta\phi_k(s)|<K_3\delta_1$, $|\partial^\eta\delta\phi_k(s)|_1<K_3\delta_1$, $|\partial^\eta\delta\phi_k(s)|_{(\alpha),2-\alpha}<C_\alpha K_3\delta_1$ ($0<\alpha\le1$), $|\Delta^{D,\eta}_{\Omega_1}\delta\phi_k(s)|_2,\ |\delta\alpha_k(s)|_2<K_3\delta_1$, and the differences $|\chi_{\Delta(y)}(\delta\phi_k(s)-\delta\phi_k(1))|<\exp(-\frac14\gamma_0d(y,(X_1^c)^{\sim2}))K_3\delta_1$ (same for the other norms). One may take $K_3=4B_3^2$ (p. 56). On $X_0$ the exponential is $\le\exp(-\frac14\gamma_0r_1M)$ (2.57).

**Proposition 2.2 (p. 57).** Solutions of P2's (1.14) (the actual variational equations) with the hypotheses of P2 Prop. 1.1: there exist $c_7,K_4$ with $\delta\le c_7$ implying analytic extensions $\phi_k(s)=\phi_0+\delta\phi_k(s)$, $\alpha_k(s)=\alpha_0+\delta\alpha_k(s)$ (2.58), satisfying the bounds (1.15),(1.16) of P2 Prop. 1.1 with $K_4=(2K_3+1)C_3$ replacing $K_1$, and the differences $\phi_k(s)-\phi_k$, $\alpha_k(s)-\alpha_k$ bounded by $\exp(-\frac14\gamma_0d(y,(X_1^c)^{\sim2}))K_4\delta$ (or $\exp(-\frac14\gamma_0r_1M)K_4\delta$ on $X_0$), except the first and sixth bounds on $\Omega_j\cap X_0$. Same localization property. Note: the extended $\phi_k(s)$ do NOT satisfy P2's (1.14) (in particular $\alpha=\frac\lambda2(\phi^2-1)$ fails for $s\ne1$) (p. 57).

**Proposition 2.3 (pp. 58-59).** For $\psi(\mathbb B_j,\mathbb B'_k)$ on $\tilde\Psi^c(\mathbb B'_k;\delta,\varepsilon)$, $\delta\le c_7$ as in Prop. 1.1 of P2: an analytic extension $\psi(\mathbb B_j,\mathbb B'_k;s)$ exists, given by P2 (4.21) in terms of $\phi(\mathbb B'_k;s)$, satisfying P2 (4.29),(4.37) with $K_4$ for $K_1$, $|\psi(s)-\psi|<\exp(-\frac14\gamma_0d(y,(X_1^c)^{\sim2}))K_4\delta<\exp(-\frac14\gamma_0r_1M)K_4\delta$ on $X_0\cap\mathbb B_j$, same localization.

**Proposition 3.1 (p. 67).** The operator $C^{(k)1/2}_\Lambda$ given by (3.4),(3.9),(3.6),(3.22),(3.16) has a localization extension, analytic in $(\phi_0,\alpha_0)$ on (3.2) ($\delta_0$ small) and in $s\in\{|s|<e^{\kappa_1}\}$, satisfying $|C^{(k)1/2}(s)f|\le B_5|f|$ (3.24), the bound corresponding to (2.60) with $K_4\delta\to B_5|f|$, and the localization property: if $s=0$ on $\mathscr C$ and $Y_1$ is the component of $(\bigcup\mathscr C)^c$ containing $X_1$, then $\chi_{Y_1}C^{(k)1/2}(\phi_0,\alpha_0,s)f$ depends on $\phi_0,\alpha_0,s,f$ restricted to $Y_1$. Similar statements for the $\log$ operator (3.22) are asserted but not stated ("we will need only its trace", p. 67).

**Lemma 4.1 (p. 74).** For $\varepsilon$ small enough and $\mathbb B_j\prec\mathbb B'_k$: $\psi(\mathbb B_j,\mathbb B'_k):\Xi^c_k(\mathbb B'_k;\sigma(\mathbb B'_k),\varepsilon)\to\Xi^c_j(\mathbb B_j;\sigma(\mathbb B_j,\mathbb B'_k)\sigma(\mathbb B'_k),\varepsilon)$ with $\sigma(\mathbb B_j,\mathbb B'_k;x)=L^nL^{-p}$ for $x\in(\Omega_n\setminus\Omega_{n+1})\cap(\Omega'_p\setminus\Omega'_{p+1})$ (4.30). Stated as a generalization of Lemma 2.1 of P1; follows "by inspecting the definitions".

**Lemma 4.2 (p. 77).** The function (4.32), $\mathcal E(X;\psi(\mathbb B_j(X),\mathbb B_k(X);\psi(\mathbb B_k(X),\mathbb B_{k+1};\theta,s)+\delta\psi(\mathbb B_k(X),\mathbb B_k;\psi',s)))$, is defined and analytic on the space (4.33) $\Xi^c_{k+1}(\mathbb B_{k+1};1,\varepsilon')\times\{\psi':|\psi'|<\delta_1\}\times\{s:|s|\le e^{\kappa_1}\}$ with $\varepsilon'\le\varepsilon_{k+1}$ and $\delta_1$ satisfying the conditions (4.38) (here $\varepsilon_j=\alpha_{0,k}\xi^\alpha\ (1+\sum_{n=k}^\infty n^{-2})$-type formula, with $\alpha=\frac{d-2}2-\gamma$, $0<\gamma<\gamma_0=\min\{1,\frac{d-2}{2}\}$; exact form of $\varepsilon_j$ at p. 76 is UNCERTAIN, the printed formula is $\varepsilon_j=\alpha_{0,k}\xi^\alpha_0(1+\sum_{n=k}^{+\infty}\frac1{n^2})$-like), and satisfies the bound (4.31) $|\mathcal E(X;\psi_j)|<E\exp(-(\kappa-1)d_j(X))$.

**Proposition 4.3 (p. 81) (the main result).** Let $\mathcal E(X,\psi_j)$ be analytic on a complex space containing one of $\Xi^c_j(\mathbb B_j(X);1,\varepsilon_j)$, $\Psi^c_j(X;C_0L^j\eta,\varepsilon_k)$ with the condition (3.107) of P1, and satisfy (4.31). Then (1.1) has a localization expansion (4.51)
$$\mathcal E(X;\psi^{(j)}_k(\psi^{(k)}_k(\theta)+\psi'))=\sum_{Y\in\mathscr D(\mathbb B_{k+1}):X_1\subset Y}\mathcal E(X,Y;\theta,\psi'),$$
whose terms are analytic on (4.46) $\Xi^c_{k+1}(\mathbb B_{k+1}(Y);1,\varepsilon')\times\{\psi':|\psi'|<\delta_1\}$ with $\varepsilon',\delta_1$ as in (4.38), depend only on $\theta,\psi'$ ($\theta,\psi$) restricted to $Y$, and satisfy (4.52): $|\mathcal E(X,Y;\theta,\psi')|<E\exp(-\kappa_0d_j(X)-2\kappa\tilde d_{k+1}(Y))$. Conditions on constants: $\frac13\kappa-1\ge\kappa_0$, i.e. $\kappa\ge3\kappa_0+3$ (pp. 80-81), $\kappa_1\ge4d(2L+1)^d\kappa$ and $\kappa_1\ge 8dm_0\log(2L+1)$ ($M/M_1=L^{m_0}$, $m_0\ge$ with $L^{m_0}\ge14d$).

**Inductive hypothesis (H.5) of P1 (used, modified p. 74-75).** The paper does not list (H.1)-(H.n). ## 3. Proof architecture

**Section 1 (pp. 33-36), Introduction.** Describes the problem. Terms $\mathcal E(X;\psi_k^{(j)}(\ldots))$ are non-local because $\phi_k(x;\psi),\psi^{(j)}(y;\psi)$ depend on $\psi$ on the whole lattice. Three-step strategy for localizing $\psi^{(j)}_k$: (a) the auxiliary variable $\lambda$ (P2) with $\lambda$ only through $1/\lambda$; (b) the composition formula P2 (4.39) and the representation P2 (4.21) express $\psi^{(j)}_k$ as a composition of a function localized near $X$ with a nonlocal function entering only through "boundary conditions of the local one" and satisfying more relaxed conditions in spaces $\tilde\Psi^c$; (c) localizing $\phi(\mathbb B_k)$ itself, where the second nonlinear variational equation P2 (1.14) needs care. The sections are arranged in reverse order: Sect. 2 localizes $\phi(\mathbb B_k),\psi(\mathbb B_j,\mathbb B'_k)$ by interpolating parameters and analytic extension; Sect. 3 does the same for $C^{(k)1/2}_\Lambda$; Sect. 4 derives (4.16) and constructs (1.4). Remark p. 35-36: $M=L^m$ here is a free parameter, $M^{-1}$ small is a controlling factor of convergence; need not equal the RG large cube size.

**Section 2 (pp. 36-59): localization of the background configurations. This is the genuine core.**

*2.1 Representation and random-walk expansion of $G$ (pp. 36-40).* Write $\phi_k=\phi_0+\delta\phi_k$, $\alpha_k=\alpha_0+\delta\alpha_k$. $\phi_0,\alpha_0$ are almost-local functions of $\psi+\psi',h+h'$ (P2 (3.2),(3.3)); $\delta\phi_k,\delta\alpha_k$ solve P2 (3.20). $\delta\phi_k$ is written (via P2 (3.23) and the identity (3.25) of P2) as the four-term resolvent expansion (2.1) in $G(\alpha_0)$, $G(\alpha_0+\delta\alpha)$ with data $Q^*a\delta\psi+f_1$ and $\phi_0\delta\alpha$. Covering of $\Omega_1$ by cubes $\square_z$ of size $3ML^j\eta$ centered on points of $\Lambda^{(m)+}_j=(\Omega_j^\sim\setminus\Omega_{j+1})\cap T^{(j+m)}_{ML^j\eta}$, with the quadratic partition of unity (2.2). Local Green's functions $G(\square_z,\alpha)$ (2.3) (Neumann boundary condition inside $\Omega_1$). The key analytic input is the exponential bound (2.5): for complex $|\alpha|_2<\gamma_1$,
$$L^j\eta|\chi_{\Delta_j(y)}\partial^\eta G(\square_z,\alpha)\chi_{\Delta_{j'}(y')}f|+|\chi_{\Delta_j(y)}G(\square_z,\alpha)\chi_{\Delta_{j'}(y')}f|\le B_0e^{-\gamma_0d(y,y')}(L^{j'}\eta)^2|\chi_{\Delta_{j'}(y')}f|.$$
This is "the bound (2.33) of [2b]" (cited; not reproved, except the $\lambda G$ version, see below). The generalized random walk expansion (2.8)
$$G(\alpha)=\sum_\omega h_{\omega_0}G(\square_{\omega_0},\alpha)h_{\omega_0}K_{\omega_1}G(\square_{\omega_1},\alpha)h_{\omega_1}\cdots K_{\omega_n}G(\square_{\omega_n},\alpha)h_{\omega_n},$$
with $K_z=K(h_z)=2\nabla h_z\cdot\nabla+\Delta h_z-[Q^*aQ,h_z]$ (2.6), $\omega_i$ centers of cubes with overlapping supports, is the expansion cited from Prop. 2.2 of [2b]. Counting walks and using $\|K_z\|\le4dM^{-1}(\ldots)$ (2.7) gives (2.9)-(2.11): $|\chi_{\Delta(y)}G(\alpha)f|_2\le2^{d+2}B_0c_1(d,\frac14\gamma_0)|f|_2$, under $8dB_0c_1(d,\frac14\gamma_0)(2L+1)^dM^{-1}\le1$; the number of walks of length $n$ from a fixed cube is $\le2^{d+1}(2L+1)^{nd}$; $c_1(d,\gamma)=12c_0^d(\gamma)$, $c_0(\gamma)=\sum_xe^{-\gamma|x|}$. Note the remark at the end p. 39 that bounds are written with $M^{-n}$ and $\exp(-\frac12\gamma_0d)$ which could be improved to $M^{-n/2}$ for later use. A sum over walks with $\bigcup\square_{\omega_i}=X$ gives the (H.5)-type expansion for $G(\alpha)$ itself, but "it is not enough for our purposes" (p. 40).

*2.2 Generalized walks for $\delta\phi_k(\delta\alpha)$ (pp. 40-44).* Replace $\delta\alpha_k$ by an arbitrary $\delta\alpha$ in (2.1) and expand both $G(\alpha_0)$ and $G(\alpha_0+\delta\alpha)$; because products of expansions need two kinds of vertices, walks $\omega=(\omega_0,\dots,\omega_n)$ with $\omega_i=(z_i,\sigma_i)$, $\sigma_i\in\{0,1\}$ where $\sigma_i=1$ means the operator $K_{z_i}$ is replaced by multiplication by $-\delta\alpha h_{z_i}$; at most two such replacements; $\vartheta_i\in\{0,1\}$ chooses $\alpha_0$ or $\alpha_0+\delta\alpha$ (2.12); the terminal function $g_{\sigma}$ is $Q^*a\delta\psi+f_1$ or $-\phi_0\delta\alpha$. Result (2.13). The interpolation: one parameter $s_\square$ for each large cube $\square$ of the complementary scale decomposition $\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+1}$ outside $X_1$ (cubes not contained in $X_0$); (2.16): $\delta\phi_k(\delta\alpha,s)=\sum_\omega h_{z_0}G(\square_{z_0},\alpha_0)h_{z_0}(KG)(\tilde\omega)g_{\sigma_0}\prod_{\square\subset X_\omega\cap X_1^c}s_\square$, $X_\omega=\bigcup\square_{z_i}$. It is a polynomial in $s$, equal to $\delta\phi_k(\delta\alpha)$ at $s=1$, and for $s=0$ gives the sum over walks with $\square^\sim_{z_0}\cap X_0\ne\emptyset$, $X_\omega\subset X_1$. Domain-geometry condition (2.14): $X_0,X_1\in\mathscr D_k$, $X_0^{\sim r_0}\subset X_1$, $r_0\ge5$ integer. The bound (2.17) for $|\chi_{\Delta(y)}\delta\phi_k(\delta\alpha,s)|$ follows with the factor $\exp\kappa_1\sum_{j}(ML^j\eta)^{-d}|X_\omega\cap X_1^c\cap\Omega_j\cap\Omega^c_{j+1}|$ from $|s_\square|\le e^{\kappa_1}$. Pages 42-44 prove geometrically that this exponential is beaten by $e^{-\frac12\gamma_0d(y,\omega)}$ if $M\ge2(7L)^d\kappa_1/\gamma_0$ (2.19): a path $\Gamma$ is decomposed into pieces of $\mathbb B_k$-length $\le M$, each lying in a cube of size $7ML^j\eta$ hence in two consecutive scales, and the number of cubes contained in $X_1^c$ is $\le(7L)^d|\Gamma|/M$. The output (2.21): $\exp(-\frac12\gamma_0 d(y,(X_1^c)^{\sim2})+(7L)^d\kappa_1)$ when $X_\omega\cap X_1^c\ne\emptyset$; (2.22) gives $d(y,(X_1^c)^{\sim2})>(r_0-4)M$ when $\Delta(y)\subset\square_{z_0}$, $\square_{z_0}\cap X_0\ne\emptyset$, so $r_0\ge5$ makes the factor $\le1$. Results (2.23): $|\delta\phi_k(\delta\alpha,s)|<2^{d+3}B_0c_1e^{(7L)^d\kappa_1}(\frac52\delta_1+\frac32|\delta\alpha|_2)$, and difference to $s=1$ bounded with additional $\exp(-\frac12\gamma_0d(y,(X_1^c)^{\sim2}))$.

*2.3 The nonlinear equation for $\delta\alpha_k$ (pp. 44-56).* P2's second nonlinear equation (3.20) reads $\phi_0\cdot\delta\phi_k(\delta\alpha)+\frac12\delta\phi_k\cdot\delta\phi_k-\frac1\lambda\delta\alpha=\frac1\lambda\alpha_1$ (2.24). The sums then become sums over tree graphs: $t_1=(\tau,\omega)$ (one trunk, one vertex, one branch) and $t_2=(\tau,\omega',\omega'')$ (trunk + two branches). Introducing $s$ gives (2.30), which is of the form (3.43) of P2:
$$\delta\alpha+H_1(s)\delta\alpha+H_2(s;\delta\alpha)=H_0(s)\ (2.43).$$

**Section 4 (pp. 67-81): the localization expansion (1.4).**
(a) Identities (4.1) for the composed background function. Naively one could insert $s$ everywhere, but the resulting functions would not lie in the space $\Psi^c_j(X;C_0L^j\eta,\varepsilon_k)$ because interpolation destroys the algebraic structure/equations (p. 68). So a more elaborate representation is built (pp. 68-71): a minimal sequence of domains $\{\Omega'_1,\dots,\Omega'_k\}$ determined by $X$ and $M_1$: $X_1=$ union of $L^2M$-cubes (cover $\pi'_{k+1}$) containing an $LM_1L^j\eta$-cube of $\pi'_j$ contained in $X$ (4.2); $X'$ = union of cubes meeting $X$; $\Omega'_k$ = union of $LM_1$-cubes containing a $LM_1L^j\eta$-cube in $X$ (4.4); $\Omega'_{k-1}=\Omega'^{\sim5L}_k$ (4.5), and iteratively $\Omega'_n=\Omega'^{\sim5L}_{n+1}$ (4.6) with layers of $M_1L^{j}\eta$-cubes; $X_0=X'^{\sim4L}$ so that $\Omega_1(X)\subset X_0$ (4.7) and $X_0^{\sim r_0}\subset X_1\subset\Omega_k$ (4.8) when $4LM_1+r_0M_1\le LM$, e.g. $r_0=5$, $6M_1\le M$. The resulting generating set $\mathbb B_k(X)$ (also $\mathbb B_j(X)$). Constants are chosen so that the conditions of Sect. 2 hold for $M_1$, then $M=L^{m_0}M_1$ with $m_0$ later.
(b) Composition identities (4.9)-(4.16): $\phi(\mathbb B_k;\psi)=\phi(\mathbb B_k(X);\psi(\mathbb B_k(X),\mathbb B_k;\psi))$ etc.; using P2 (4.21) for each $p\ge j$ one derives $\psi(\mathbb B_j,\mathbb B_k;\psi)=\psi(\mathbb B_j(X),\mathbb B_k(X);\psi(\mathbb B_k(X),\mathbb B_k;\psi))$ on $\Omega_j(X)^{(j)}$ (4.15), and the key formula (4.16)
$$\psi(\mathbb B_j,\mathbb B_k;\psi(\mathbb B_k,\mathbb B_{k+1};\theta)+\psi')=\psi(\mathbb B_j(X),\mathbb B_k(X);\psi(\mathbb B_k(X),\mathbb B_{k+1};\theta)+\delta\psi(\mathbb B_k(X),\mathbb B_k;\psi'))\ \text{ on }X\cap T^{(j)},$$
where $\delta\psi$ is defined by (4.17) from $\delta\phi(\mathbb B_k;\psi')$, the solution of P2 (3.20) with $\delta\psi=\psi'$, $\phi_0=\phi(\mathbb B_{k+1};\theta)$, $\alpha_0=\alpha(\mathbb B_{k+1};\theta)$, $f_1=0$, $\alpha_1=0$. Thus the external (non-local) function is only the small linear correction $\delta\psi(\ldots;\psi')$ and the interpolation $s$ can be put into the internal functions on the right side (the internal functions are in spaces of size $\tilde\Psi^c(\mathbb B_{k+1};\ldots)$); this is the point of the construction. Introduction of $s$ (4.18): $\delta\phi(\mathbb B_k;\psi',s)$.

Lemma 4.2.

By the localization property the term for $\mathscr C$ depends only on the component $Y_1$ of $X_1\cup\bigcup_{\mathscr C}\square$ (4.42) containing $X_1$ (other components are killed by the derivative), so one gets a sum over connected domains (4.43). Routine versus difficult. Routine: counting of walks, Neumann series, polydisc/Cauchy estimates, geometric inequalities of domain sizes (long but elementary). Genuine difficulties: (1) choosing the interpolation so that it respects the singular nonlinear equation for $\delta\alpha_k$ (Section 2.3), (2) the non-uniform-in-$\lambda$ operator $\lambda G(\square,\lambda)$, (3) arranging the composition (4.16) so that the localization parameters enter only through functions that remain in the spaces required by P1's (H.5), (4) keeping the powers of $M,M_1,L,\kappa_1$ consistent (condition chain (4.38),(2.19),(4.49),(4.50)).

## 7. Weak spots and open points

- Many proofs are sketches. The convergence of the random walk expansion (2.8) rests entirely on [2b] (Prop. 2.2) and the exponential bound (2.5) on [2b] (2.33): neither is in the bundle. For the $x$-dependent operator $G_{k,x}$ of Sect. 3 the paper itself says the proofs of [2a] "do not extend directly" ("although they can be easily extended", p. 63) and replaces them by $L^2$ arguments without proof.
- The Gaussian integration giving (3.8)-(3.9) is not shown ("elementary Gaussian integrations", p. 62). Index restrictions in the 12-operator walk (3.17) are not formulated ("we do not need to formulate them explicitly", p. 65). The expansion for the third term (3.15)-(3.16) and the bound (3.18)-(3.19) are asserted with numerical constants (48, 8^n) that cannot be checked from the text.
- The treatment of (2.43): "We repeat the reasoning leading to Lemma 3.2 of [3]" (p. 52). The uniqueness/analyticity of $\delta\alpha_k(s)$ with respect to the extra parameters is not argued.
- Prop. 2.3 is "the same as the proofs of (4.29),(4.37) of P2".
- The log-operator statements in Sect. 3 are not formulated as a proposition ("Obviously similar conclusions can be formulated for the second operator", p. 67); later papers need only a trace.
- The reduction (p. 40) "it is not enough for our purposes" shows that the usual random-walk expansion with domain sums is not used directly; the analyticity-in-$s$ route replaces it. The statement of the final domains uses a tree-graph geometry (pp. 79-80) with printed inequalities including factors like $L^{-1}L^j\eta$, whose dimensional consistency I could not verify (UNCERTAIN).
- Inconsistencies / possible typos noted: (i) abstract date "2 January 1996" fine; (ii) in Prop. 2.1 the statement "$Y_0^c$" and "$Y_0=\bigcup\square$" appear to be as printed; (iii) the condition on $M$ appears as $M^{-1}$ in Sect. 2 and $M^{-1/2}$ on p. 68 (where $8dB_0c_1(2L+1)^dM^{-1/2}\le1$ and $M\ge4(7L)^d\kappa_1/\gamma_0$), whereas (2.19) says $M\ge2(7L)^d\kappa_1/\gamma_0$; the change is intended (p. 39 remark) but the consolidated conditions are never listed; (iv) "$r_1=r_0-4$ ... we may take $r_1=4$" implies $r_0=8$, versus "$r_0\ge5$ which we assume from now on"; (v) $\varepsilon_j$ formula at p. 76 is hard to read; (vi) the printed (4.47) "$\kappa_1\ge\log(1+\frac1{\sqrt e-1})$" is unclear.
- The paper modifies the inductive hypothesis (H.5) (spaces $\Xi^c_j(\mathbb B_j(X);1,\varepsilon_j)$ instead of $\Xi^c_j(1,\varepsilon_j)$, p. 74-75) and the lower exponent $(\kappa-1)$ in (4.31). Whether the stronger hypothesis can be re-established at each step is not shown here.
- Proposition 4.3 is proved under the simplifying assumption $X\subset X_1\subset\Omega_{k+1}$; the general case ("variations such as $X_1\subset\Omega_k$", "$\psi'$ as an independent complex variable") is deferred (p. 81).
- The bound $|\psi|<p_1(\beta_k)$ for the fluctuation variable and the choice of $\beta_k$ large relative to the constants are deferred to the next paper.
- The "next paper" on fluctuation integrals is not identified in this paper beyond "the next paper"; candidates in the bundle are P4 ($\text{II}$ RG equations) and P5/P6. (UNCERTAIN.)
- Also: the (H.5) normalization with factor $\kappa_0 d_j(X)$ and $2\kappa\tilde d_{k+1}(Y)$: the relation to the exact form in P1 is only qualitative here.

## 8. Preliminary formalization notes

- Most self-contained: abstract content of Section 2.1 (partition of unity, random-walk expansion convergence given the local bound (2.5) and commutator bound (2.7)), counting of walks (2.9)-(2.11), geometric inequalities (2.18)-(2.22), and Section 4's combinatorics (4.41)-(4.50): interpolation identity, partial resummation, exponent inequalities. These are elementary but long; the geometric inequalities depend on the definition of $d(\cdot,\cdot)$ through $\mathbb B_k$ which must be formalized with the exact scales.
- Infrastructure needed: lattice Laplacians with Neumann/Dirichlet boundary conditions on cubes and their resolvent bounds with exponential off-diagonal decay (not in Mathlib; this is the dependence on [2a,2b]; also needs (2.5), which is an external theorem, treat as an axiom/interface or formalize from scratch via a random-walk expansion of Neumann Laplacians), holomorphic functional calculus for finite-dimensional (lattice) operators (finite-dimensional positive operators: spectral calculus and integral representations of $z^{-1/2}$, $\log$ exist partly in Mathlib), analytic functions of finitely many complex variables with Cauchy estimates (Mathlib has one-variable and some multi-variable Cauchy formulas; guess), contraction mapping/analytic implicit function theorem in Banach spaces (Mathlib has implicit function theorem for $C^1$; analytic version is a guess), norm spaces $|\cdot|_1,|\cdot|_{(\alpha),2-\alpha},|\cdot|_2$.
- Hardest: Section 2.3 (singular equation for $\delta\alpha_k$ together with the localization property and the differential estimates (2.46)-(2.52)), Section 3 (12-term walk, Gaussian algebra) and the compatibility of domains/analyticity spaces between P1, P2 and this paper (the chain of constants in (4.38)). Formalizing these requires a precise statement of the objects of P1/P2 first. This paper is statement-heavy and bound-heavy; recommend treating Prop. 2.1-2.3, 3.1, and 4.3 as the frozen interface and Lemma 4.2/(4.41)-(4.50) as separate formalization targets with the random-walk bounds as hypotheses.
