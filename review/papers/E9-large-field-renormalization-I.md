## 0. Identification

T. Balaban, "Large field renormalization. I. The basic step of the R operation", Commun. Math. Phys. 122 (1989) 175-202. Bundle key E9 (SOURCES.md: cited by P5 [5c], P6 [6c]). 28 pages. Received June 5, 1987, revised June 10, 1988; communicated by A. Jaffe. Supported by NSF DMS-86 02207. Setting: four-dimensional (and d<4) lattice gauge theory (plaquette variables $U(\partial p)$, gauge field $V$, Wilson action); the abstract says the R operation "removes the main obstacle to prove the ultraviolet stability of four-dimensional gauge field theories".

Summary. After some RG steps the density $\rho_k(V)=\sum_Z\rho(Z,V)$ is a sum over large field regions $Z$ (E8 (2.18) [III]). In a gauge theory in $d=4$ the bare coupling $g_0$ decays only logarithmically ($g_0^{-2}\sim a+b\log\varepsilon^{-1}$), so the smallness $\exp(-p_0(g_0))$ of a large plaquette (0.1) is not a positive power of $\varepsilon$ and controls only a small fraction of the steps; for a component that has survived a number of steps without acquiring new large fields it is exhausted and the component must be renormalized. The remedy (Intro, pp. 175-177): replace the large field expression on such a component by a small field expression, in such a way that the integral of the density is unchanged. Schematically (0.3): multiply the density on the "good" part $Z''$ by a normalized ratio of integrals over the "bad" part $Z'$. E9 carries out the first stage: it (a) singles out the components to be renormalized (small, rectangular, no new large fields for $N$ steps), (b) undoes $N$ fluctuation integrals near the boundary of these components one at a time, creating a new, much smaller large field region $Z''_j$ and new boundary terms, with new characteristic functions chosen so that all earlier restrictions become automatic, (c) integrates all field variables on a box $\Lambda$ (diameter at most $100M$ in the unit lattice scale), whose minimum over gauge orbits is Prop. 1, (d) fixes a gauge on $\Lambda$ by a tree built from the rectangular-parallelepiped structure and introduces a fluctuation field around the new background $U_0$, and (e) writes the result as the operation $\mathbb R'$ (1.100) with the normalization property (1.102). The remaining steps (extract $\exp A_k$, localize, polymer expansion, exponentiate) are announced for the next paper (p. 202).

## 1. Setting and notation

### 1.1 Model and objects as used here (gauge theory, d = 4 or d < 4)

Lattice: $T_\eta$ with spacing $\eta=L^{-k}$ inside a fixed torus; $T^{(j)}$, $T_{L^{-j}}$ the lattices of spacing $L^{-j}$ ($j$th scale); $L$ the block factor; $M$ the size of the "large cubes" (an $M$-cube is $M$ blocks of the corresponding scale on a side); $M_1$ a smaller cube size, $M/M_1$ a power of $L$. Cubes of the lattice at scale $j$: "$LM_2R_j$-cubes" etc. (the partition into cubes whose size carries the buffer width $R_j$).

Fields: gauge field $V$ (group elements on bonds, $G$-valued; complexification $G^c$, Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, $\mathfrak g^c$); $V_j$ = the $j$th component of the gauge field (variable at scale $j$), $A_j$ = "axial/auxiliary" variable of the $j$th step (the field $A_j$ integrated against the Gaussian $\exp[-\frac12\langle A_j,C^*\Delta^{(j)}_{A_{j+1}}CA_j\rangle]$ in (1.25)); $U(\partial p)$ the plaquette variable. Averaging operations $M^j$, $M^\bullet$, $Q^*_j$; $U(\mathbb B,\cdot)$ a background configuration determined by a determining set $\mathbb B$ (here a sequence of sets $\{\Gamma_j\}$). $A(U)$ is the Wilson action; $A(\zeta,U)$ the Wilson action with a cutoff function $\zeta$ in the sum over plaquettes.

Density: $\rho_k(V)=\sum_Z\rho(Z,V)$ (0.2), more precisely the expansion (2.18) of [III]: sum over sequences $\{\Omega_j,\Lambda_j\}$ (small field domains $\Omega_j$ and large field data), each term containing $\chi_k(\Omega_k^{\sim 4})\,\mathbb T_k(Z_k)\exp A_k$, with $Z_k=\Omega_k^c$ the large field region, a union of connected components (here "k" is used instead of "k+1"). $\mathbb T_k(Z_k)$ is the large field operator (the integral operators left undone inside the large field region); it factorizes over components, (1.1):
$$\mathbb T_k(Z_k)=\mathbb T_k(Z_k\cap Z^c)\mathbb T_k(Z)=\mathbb T_k(Z_k\cap Z^c)\prod_{i=1}^m\mathbb T_k(X_i),\quad Z=\bigcup_{i=1}^mX_i\ \text{(disjoint components)}.$$

Small field domains and regions: $\Omega_j$ domains that are unions of cubes; $\Omega_j^{\sim}$ the enlargement by one layer of cubes ($\sim n$: $n$ layers); admissible sequences; $Z_j$ the large field region at scale $j$ (so $Z_j\cap$ stuff transported by scaling); $\Omega^{\prime\prime}_j$, $Z''_j$ the new sequence (1.10)-(1.12).

Characteristic functions (the "seven groups", (1.3)-(1.9), p. 178), appearing in $\chi_{j+1}$, $\zeta$, $\chi_h$ of (1.2):
- (1.3) $\chi(\{\sup_{p\subset\square}|U_{j,\square}(V_j,\partial p)-1|<\varepsilon_j(L^{k-j}\eta)^2\})$ for $\square\subset(\Omega_j^{\sim-4}\setminus\Omega^{\sim}_{j+1})\cap Z$ (small plaquette variables of the background at scale $j$);
- (1.4) the same with $j+1$ and a $\square'$, for $\square'\subset(\Omega^{\sim-4}_{j+1}\setminus\Omega^{\sim}_{j+2})\cap Z$;
- (1.5) $\chi(\{|V_j(y,x)-1|<\varepsilon_j\})$ (gauge fixing bonds, $y$ block centre, $x\in B(y)$);
- (1.6) $\frac1z\exp[-\frac1{g_j^2}(1-\mathrm{Re\,tr}V_j(y,x))]$ (the Wilson-type factor on the same bonds);
- (1.7) $\chi(\{\sup_{b\in(\square)^{*}}|V_j(b)(V^{(j)}_\square(b))^{-1}-1|<2\delta_j\})$ (fluctuation field small);
- (1.8) $\chi(\{\sup|\exp ig_jA_j(b)\,V^{(j)}_{Z_{j+1}\setminus Z_j}(b)(V^{(j)}_\square(b))^{-1}-1|<2\delta_j\})$;
- (1.9) $\chi(\{\sup|A_j(b)|<g_j^{-1}\delta_j\})$ for the last layer.
Here $\varepsilon_j$ is the small-field plaquette bound and $\delta_j$ the fluctuation bound ($\delta'_j=g_jA_1p_1(g_j)$ below). (I transcribe the shape of these conditions; the printed exponents and tilde-decorations on the index sets are dense and I did not re-derive them: UNCERTAIN in detail, exact in structure.)

Constants of the coupling flow: $g_j$ running coupling at scale $j$, $p_0(g)=A_0(\log g^{-2})^{p_0}$, $p_1(g)=(\log g^{-2})^{p_1}$ with $p_1<p_0$, $\delta'_j=g_jA_1p_1(g_j)$, $\beta_0$ (a constant in factors $1+\beta_0$ that comes from [III], controlling the ratio of couplings at different scales; used with $\beta_0\le\frac12$, p. 192; UNCERTAIN what exactly it is), $\beta\in(0,\frac12]$ and $L_0$ (parameters in (1.24)).

## 2. Main results

There is no numbered theorem. The statements, in order of importance (all as printed):

**(R-1) The heuristic definition (0.3), p. 176.** For $\rho(V)=\sum_Z\rho(Z,V)$ (0.2), decompose each large field region $Z=Z'\cup Z''$ ($Z''$: components for which the small factors from the large field control some number of next steps; $Z'$: remaining components, which require renormalization), and set
$$(\mathbb R\rho)(V)=\sum_Z\rho(Z'',V)\,\frac{\int dV\lceil_{Z'}\,\rho(Z,V)}{\int dV\lceil_{Z'}\,\rho(Z'',V)}.\tag{0.3}$$
The paper says "We will prove that the densities are positive and the integration domains in the integrals above are nonempty, hence the denominators are positive and the operation $\mathbb R$ is well defined." (p. 176.) This is not proved in E9 (see Sect. 7). The normalization property
$$\int dV\,(\mathbb R\rho)(V)=\int dV\,\rho(V)\tag{0.4}$$
follows at once from (0.3): the denominator and the factor $\int dV\lceil_{Z'}\rho(Z'',V)$ combine, i.e. integrating $\rho(Z'',V)\cdot[\text{ratio}]$ over $V\lceil_{Z'}$ gives $\int dV\lceil_{Z'}\rho(Z,V)$ (my one-line verification; the paper only asserts it). The intended shape of the rest is (0.5)-(0.6):
$$\sum_{Z'\subset Z''^c}\frac{\int dV\lceil_{Z'}\rho(Z'\cup Z'',V)}{\int dV\lceil_{Z'}\rho(Z'',V)}=\exp\sum_X\mathbb R(X,V),\ X\cap Z''^c\ne\emptyset,\qquad(\mathbb R\rho)(V)=\sum_{Z''}\rho(Z'',V)\exp\sum_X\mathbb R(X,V).\tag{0.5, 0.6}$$
These are announced, not constructed, in E9. The text says the actual procedure differs from (0.3), (0.5): the denominators are not integrals of whole densities $\rho(Z'',V)$ but "the integrals of some parts of these densities", "the parts localized in neighborhoods of the domains $Z'$, so they do not depend on the large field regions $Z''$, and are determined by small field effective actions only" (p. 177).

**(R-2) Definition of the operation $\mathbb R'$, (1.100) (p. 201).** After the preparatory equivalence of Sect. 1 (below), with the sum over $Z_k\setminus Z$ left with its old operator $\mathbb T''_k(Z_k)$ and the sum over the allowed forms of the operation factorized over the components $X_1,\dots,X_n$ of $Z$,
$$(\mathbb R'\rho_k)(V_k)=\sum_{Z_k}\Big(\sum_{\{\Omega_j,Z_j\}}\mathbb T''_k(Z_k)\Big)\Big\{\sum_{n\ge0}\sum_{\{X_1,\dots,X_n\}}\prod_{i=1}^n\Big[\frac1{N_i}\sum_{\{\Omega^c_{i,j},Z_{i,j}\},G_i,T_i}\chi_{k,\Lambda_i}\frac{\delta_{G_i}(V'_k)\chi(\Lambda_i)\exp\big[-\frac1{g_k^2}A(\zeta_i,U_{k,X_i}(V'_kV_{\Lambda_i}))\big]}{\int dV'\lceil_{\Lambda_i}\,\delta_{G_i}(V')\chi(\Lambda_i)\exp\big[-\frac1{g_k^2}A(\zeta_i,U_{k,X_i}(V'V_{\Lambda_i}))\big]}$$
$$\cdot\int dV_k\lceil_{(\Omega''^{\sim2}_{i,h+1})^c\cap\Omega_{i,h}}\chi_{k,1/2}((\Omega''^{\sim2}_{i,h+1})^c\cap\Omega_{i,h})\,\mathbb T_h(Z_{i,h})\int dV'\lceil_{\mathbb B_i}\delta_{T_i}(V')\chi'_i\Big]\exp A''_k\Big\}$$
with $G_i$ a graph in $\Lambda_i$ fixing the axial gauge, $\zeta_i\in C^\infty_0(X_i)$ equal to 0 to 1 in a neighbourhood of $\partial X_i^{\sim2}$ (printed "$X_i^{\sim-2}$": UNCERTAIN which), and
$$\chi(\Lambda_i)=\chi\Big(\Big\{\Big|\tfrac1i\log V'(b)\Big|<M_0\varepsilon_k\ \text{for } b\in\Lambda_i\Big\}\Big).\tag{1.101}$$
UNCERTAIN: the printed (1.100) is a long display and I am not sure which integrals sit inside the bracket $[\cdots]$ and exactly where $\exp A''_k$ sits; the structure (a quotient "reference density at the new background $V_\Lambda$ over its integral over the fluctuation field $V'$ on $\Lambda_i$", multiplied by an integral of the large field density with the new restrictions) is certain. Because of the gauge fixing, (1.100) is not Euclidean invariant; E9 averages over families of graphs: replace the graph by its images under the $d!$ coordinate permutations and $2^d$ reflections in subsets of coordinates, so that $N_i=1/(2^dd!)$.

**(R-3) Normalization of $\mathbb R'$, (1.102) (p. 201):**
$$\int dV_k\,(\mathbb R'\rho_k)(V_k)=\int dV_k\,\rho_k(V_k).$$
Asserted, with the comment "In fact, the $\mathbb R'$-operation changes essentially the initial density $\rho_k$ only in a neighborhood of the large field region; the changes are decaying exponentially fast with the distance to the region." The justification is the structure (R-2): the quotient integrates to 1, and all preparatory steps are equalities of integrands or integrations over variables that the quotient does not touch. Not written out. "$\mathbb R'$ is not a complete $\mathbb R$-operation, but is a basic part of it" (p. 201).

**Proposition 1 (p. 194).** For a configuration $V_k\lceil_{Z\cap\Lambda^c}$ satisfying the regularity condition $|\partial V_k-1|<\varepsilon$ on the domain $Z\cap\Lambda^c$, with $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently small, there exists exactly one critical orbit (under the group of gauge transformations defined on $\Lambda$) of the function $V_k\lceil_\Lambda\mapsto A(U_{k,Z}(V_k))$ (1.77). An element of the orbit is a minimum of that function; it is denoted $V_\Lambda=V_\Lambda(V_k\lceil_{Z\cap\Lambda^c})$ and satisfies
$$|V_\Lambda(\partial p')-1|<B_5M^5\varepsilon\quad\text{for }p'\in\Lambda.\tag{1.78}$$
The orbit-valued function $V_\Lambda(V_k\lceil_{Z\cap\Lambda^c})$ has an analytic extension to $G^c$-valued configurations $\mathbb V_k=V'_kV_k=\exp iB'\,V_k$ satisfying the same regularity condition as $V_k$, with $B'\in\mathfrak g^c$ small, $|B'|<\varepsilon$. The constant $B_5$ is determined by the geometry (condition (i)); "the power $M^5$ is not the optimal one, in fact we can take just $M$, but we will not prove it" (p. 195). The proof is not given in E9: the paper says it will be given in the next paper ("We will prove later the following theorem", p. 194; "We will discuss it together with the proof of Proposition 1", p. 195). The sketched strategy: minimize first with the averages $M^k(U''_k)$ fixed, then remove the restrictions on the averages (p. 194).

**Preparatory equivalence (pp. 177-200), the content of Sect. 1 before $\mathbb R'$.** The density $\rho_k$ is replaced by an "equivalent" density (equal integral over $V_k$) obtained from $\rho_k$ by: the new decompositions of unity and exclusions of components (these produce additional large field regions and so remove components from $Z$), $N$ partial integrations, and one integration over $V_k$ on $\Lambda$ (summary at p. 200). Two exact identities are proved in detail:

$$\chi^{(n+1)}_k\chi(Z_{j+1}\cap\Omega_{j+1})\Big(\prod_{\square'\subset\Omega_{j+1}\setminus\Omega_{j+1}^{\sim-1}}\chi^{(j)}_{\square'}\Big)\zeta(\Omega^c_{j+1})\chi'_j\chi^{(n)}_k\cdot\Big(\prod\prod\chi(\{|V_j(x,y)-1|<\varepsilon_j\})\tfrac1z\exp[-\tfrac1{g_j^2}(1-\mathrm{Re\,tr}V_j(x,y))]\Big)=\chi^{(n+1)}_k\Big(\prod_{\square'\subset Z_{j+1}\cap\Omega_{j+1}}\chi^{(j)}_{\square'}\Big)\chi'_j\Big(\prod\prod\tfrac1z\exp[\cdots]\Big),$$
for $j+1<k$; for $j+1=k$ the right side carries an extra factor $\chi_k(\Omega_k^{\sim-4})$. (UNCERTAIN on the exact index sets of the products; the content is "the new restrictions imply the old ones".)

(b) **(1.89), p. 198, proved pp. 198-200.** With $\beta\le\frac14$:
$$\chi_k(\Omega_k^{\sim-4})\chi_{k,\Lambda}\chi_{h,1/2}((\Omega''^{\sim2}_{h+1})^c\cap\Omega_h)\chi'_k\chi_h(\Omega_h\cap Z_h)\chi''_k=\chi_k(\Omega_k^{\sim-4})\chi_{k,\Lambda}\chi_{h,1/2}((\Omega''^{\sim2}_{h+1})^c\cap\Omega_h)\chi'_k,$$
i.e. $\chi''_k=1$ and $\chi_h(\Omega_h\cap Z_h)=1$ under the new restrictions. Here $\chi''_k$ is the characteristic function of the conditions (1.24) for the new sequence. The proof uses $N\le O(1)(\log g_k^{-2})^\nu$ with $\frac12\nu\le p_0-p_1-1$, and $N$ large enough that $L^{-N}N^{1/2}O(1)B_3^2B_5M^6<\alpha\varepsilon_h$ (p. 198).

**Bound (1.70), p. 192.** Each term of the localized boundary contribution $\mathbb C^{(n)}_k(X,(\mathbb U,\mathbb J))$ extends analytically to the space $\tilde U^{c(n)}_k(X,\tilde\alpha_0,\tilde\alpha_1)$ and satisfies $|\mathbb C^{(n)}_k(X,(\mathbb U,\mathbb J))|\le C_0\exp(-(1+3\beta)\kappa\,d_m(X))$ for $X\in\mathbb D_m$ in the representation (1.63). ## 3. Proof architecture

The construction is a pipeline of exact manipulations. I follow the order of the paper.

**A. Setup and selection of components (pp. 177-178; (1.1)-(1.2)).** Each RG step adds at least ten layers of $MR_k$-cubes around a large field region, so a component has size $>20MR_k$; after rescaling by $L^{-1}$ and ten new layers the size exceeds $40MR_k$ (since $R_k$ decreases by $L^{-1}$ in some steps); the minimal size is about $42\frac{L}{L-1}MR_k$. "Components of almost minimal size": the class of components $Z$ satisfying (i) contained in a cube of size $100MR_k$; (ii) in the preceding $N$ steps no new large field regions were created inside it (and earlier regions inside satisfy (i) at their scales). "According to our rule of construction of the large field regions, for such a component all the regions connected with the last $N$ steps are rectangular parallelepipeds" (p. 177): this is used throughout, in the gauge-fixing construction (pp. 195-196) and implicitly in the variational Prop. 1. $\mathbb T_k(Z)\exp A_k$ is written as the product of the last $N$ one-step operations (1.2): $\chi_k(\Omega_k^{\sim-4})\prod_{j=k-1}^{h}\mathbb T^{(j)}(Z_{j+1})\chi_h(\Omega\setminus\Omega^\sim_{j+1})\mathbb T_h(Z_h)\exp A_k$, $h=k-N$, in which the first and last characteristic functions are the simplest (no earlier large field restrictions occur, by (ii)).

**B. New region sequence (pp. 178-180; (1.10)-(1.17)).** Purpose (p. 179): "to get a new, much smaller large field region $Z$, in order to be able to fit the constructions of this section to the inductive assumptions on the effective action"; also to choose a convenient gauge fixing. Define $N_0$ (smallest positive integer with $L^{-N_0+1}MR_{k-N_0+1}=M$ in the $M$-cube normalization), $k_0=k-N_0$, assume $N>N_0$. New regions (as printed): $Z''_k=(Z'_{k-N_0})^{\sim3}=(\Omega^{\sim5}_{k-N_0+1})^c\cap Z$ and $Z''_{k-N_0+1}=(Z'_{k-N_0})^\sim=(\Omega^{\sim7}_{k-N_0+1})^c\cap Z$ (UNCERTAIN on the superscripts; the page text is clear only that these are obtained from complements of enlargements of $\Omega_{k_0+1}$), completed to a sequence whose complements form an admissible sequence of domains with partitions into $M$-cubes; $Z''_j=(\Omega_j^{\sim5})^c$ for $j=k-N_0,\dots,k-N+1=h+1$ and $Z''_j=Z_j$ for $j=h,\dots,1$ (1.11); $\Omega''_j=(Z''^c_j\cap Z)\cup(\Omega_j\cap Z^c)$ for $j=k,\dots,h+1$ and $\Omega''_j=\Omega_j$ for $j\le h$ (1.12); generating set $\mathbb B''_k=\{\Gamma''_j\}$, $\Gamma''_j=(\Omega''_j\setminus\Omega''_{j+1})^{(j)}$, $\Gamma''_k=(\Omega''_k)^{(k)}$, $\Gamma''_0=\Omega''^c_1$ (1.13). ## 6. Constants and parameters

None of the constants is fixed to a number; the conditions are scattered and phrased "sufficiently small/large". What appears, with the constraints I could read:
- $g_0$, $g_j$: running couplings; $d<4$: $g_0=g\varepsilon^{1/2(4-d)}$; $d=4$: $g_0\sim(a+b\log\varepsilon^{-1})^{-1/2}$ (p. 175).
- $p_0(g)=A_0(\log g^{-2})^{p_0}$ with $p_0$ a positive integer (small field threshold); $p_1(g)=(\log g^{-2})^{p_1}$, $p_1<p_0$; $A_0$ large, $A_1$ small relative to it: needed to make $9B_3\frac{A_1p_1(g_j)}{A_0p_0(g_j)}(1+\beta_0)(\dots)$ "arbitrarily small by choosing $\gamma$, or $A_1/A_0$ sufficiently small" (p. 186). The conditions on $M,\gamma,A_0,A_1$ do not depend on $i,j,k$.
- $M$ large: so that $\delta(M/M_1)\ge2$ (p. 186) and $\exp(-4\delta(m-k_0)M)$ is small (p. 200), and "$M$ large" in (1.98); $M_1$ with $M/M_1$ a power of $L$.
- $N_0$: defined by $L^{-N_0+1}R_{k-N_0+1}=1$, giving $L^{N_0-1}=R_{k-N_0+1}\le(L+1)(N_0+1)^{\rho_0}R_k$ hmm printed $\rho_0$ for a power exponent (UNCERTAIN symbol; it is the exponent in $R_k$'s $\log$-power from [III]); $N_0=O(\log g_k^{-2})$.
- $N>N_0$ with $N\le O(1)(\log g_k^{-2})^\nu$, integer $\nu$ with $\frac12\nu\le p_0-p_1-1$, and $N$ large enough that $L^{-N}N^{1/2}O(1)B_3^2B_5M^6<\frac12\alpha$ (p. 198).
- $\alpha$: absolute constant, $\frac1{12}$ in (1.96), $\le\frac18$ in (1.52)/(p. 200), fixed "later"; $\beta\in(0,\frac12]$ (e.g. $\frac12$), $\beta\le\frac14$ for (1.89), $\beta_0\le\frac12$; $L_0$: $2\le L_0\le\frac12L$ (as printed), with $L_0^2\le\frac13L$ for (1.52), and $L_0^{2N_0}\le(\frac13)^{N_0}L(L+1)(N_0+1)^{\rho_0}R_k$-type bound; $C\le2$.
- $\varepsilon_j$, $\delta_j$, $\delta'_j=g_jA_1p_1(g_j)$, $\kappa$ (decay rate in (1.70): $\exp(-(1+3\beta)\kappa d_m(X))$), $M_0$ (in (1.101); UNCERTAIN, a fixed constant), $B_3,B_5$ geometric, $\delta$ (decay rate of $\mathbb H$, "scaled distance in $M_1$-cubes" (1.47)).
Order of choice: not stated. The remark on p. 186 that the conditions on $M,\gamma,A_0,A_1$ are independent of scales is the only statement about order.

## 7. Weak spots and open points

1. No theorem is proved. The whole paper is an exact preparatory construction; the part of R that does the renormalizing (cancellation of main actions, polymer expansion, exponentiation, bounds on $\mathbb R(X,V)$) is in E10. A reader of E9 alone cannot verify that $\mathbb R'\rho_k$ has the inductive form.
2. Positivity of the denominators and nonemptiness of the integration domains (0.3) are announced ("We will prove") but nothing in E9 proves them; for the actual denominator in (1.100) positivity is trivial only if the integrand is positive (the Wilson weight is, so it is plausible), but the domain of $V'$ on $\Lambda_i$ (restriction $\chi(\Lambda_i)$, gauge fixing) being non-null is not discussed.
3. Prop. 1 is stated without proof; also the claim that the minimum orbit's analytic extension satisfies the same regularity. The constant $M^5$ is non-optimal "but we will not prove it".
4. The normalization (1.102) is asserted; with the Euclidean-covariant averaging over $d!2^d$ gauge-fixing graphs it still holds (each graph gives an exact normalization), but the paper does not say this.
5. The printed $N_0$: "smallest positive integer such that $L^{-N_0+1}MR_{k-N_0+1}=M$. It is easy to see that either there is exactly one such integer, or ..." (p. 179); equality cannot hold in general for integer $N_0$. I read it as the first index at which the buffer layer $L^{-(k-j)}MR_j$ has size at most one $M$-cube; UNCERTAIN. (Compare P7 (2.1)-(2.3) where $k_2$ is the smallest index with $L^{-(k-k_2)}LM[R_{k_2}]\ge1$.)
6. Superscripts of the tilde operations (e.g. $\Omega^{\sim5}$, $\Omega^{\sim7}$, $X^{\sim-2}$) in (1.10), (1.100), and the printed index sets in the products of (1.29) are hard to read on the page images; I describe their role, not their exact form.
7. (0.1) is printed as $\exp[-\frac1{g_0^2}(1-\mathrm{Re\,tr}U(\partial p))]\le\exp(-p_0(g_0))=g_0^{A_0(\log g_0^{-2})^{p_0-1}}$; the last equality holds only up to a constant factor in the exponent, and one would expect $p_0^2$ from $1-\mathrm{Re\,tr}U\gtrsim|U-1|^2$ with $|U-1|\ge g_0p_0(g_0)$. UNCERTAIN whether the exponent is $p_0$, $p_0^2$ or $\frac12p_0$; the O(N) papers use $\exp(-c\,p_0^2(\beta_k))$ (P7 (2.33)).
8. Cross-reference to E10: Prop. 1 and the cancellation are in E10; I have not read E10, so statements about what E10 contains are inferred from the last page of E9 and from the P5/P6 citations ([6d] for $\kappa$, $K$, Prop. 3.2).
9. The abstract and Sect. 0 call this "the basic step"; P6/P5 do not cite E9 for any specific item (Sect. 9).

## 8. Preliminary formalization notes

E9 offers nothing that could be formalized as a stand-alone theorem. Its content is a chain of exact identities whose formalization requires the gauge-theory setting (not part of the project). The abstract algebra (0.3)-(0.4), (1.102) (replace a density by a normalized ratio of integrals; normalization by Fubini) is trivial and is the interface idea; the substantial parts (1.29), (1.89) are inequality bookkeeping that P7 redoes in its own setting. See Sect. 10.

## 10. Relevance to the formalization

- E9 cannot be the source of any theorem statement to be frozen: it has none (a Proposition with deferred proof and a definition). The statement-level source for the R operation is P7 Thm 2 (= P5 Thm 2); E9 only explains why P7's construction has the shape it has, and the statement (1.102)/P7 (1.7) is the interface: "the operation $R^{(k)}$ preserves $\int d\psi_k\rho_k$ identically".
- Reusable as a roadmap (not as proof text): the order of operations (select small components with no new large fields for $N$ steps; undo or compose the last $N$ steps; new restrictions that make the old ones automatic (E9 (1.29), P7 Lemma 2.5); minimize on a small box (Prop. 1 $\leftrightarrow$ P7 Lemma 2.1); new fluctuation field and half-width restriction; normalized ratio). The precise bookkeeping chain of "new restrictions imply old restrictions" in E9 (1.44)-(1.52), (1.90)-(1.98) is a worked example of the argument that P7 sketches at Lemma 2.5, and a formalizer reconstructing P7 Lemma 2.5 can use E9's explicit inequality chain (replace plaquette variables by the $\partial\psi$, $\Delta\phi$, $\alpha$ conditions, and the boundary-layer decay of $\mathbb H$ by the decay of the Green's function bounds of P3).
- Gauge-specific and irrelevant for O(N): gauge fixing on nested rectangular parallelepipeds (tree graphs, axial/Landau gauge, Faddeev-Popov), averaging over $d!2^d$ gauge-fixing graphs for Euclidean invariance, orbit-valued minimizer and its analytic extension (Prop. 1), the plaquette characteristic functions (1.3)-(1.9), the gauge-invariance remarks, the logarithmic running coupling and $p_0(g)$-vs-$\varepsilon$ discussion for $d=4$ (in the O(N) series $\beta_k\sim\beta L^{k(d-2)}$ grows geometrically, and the smallness $\exp(-cp_0^2(\beta_k))$ is still not enough for more than $\sim\log\beta_{k_1}a$ steps, which is why R is needed in both cases).
- Not here: Green's function / propagator estimates. E9 only quotes (from E6 refs [12], [14]) the exponential decay of the function $\mathbb H$ and the regularity estimates (1.65) of [14]. So there is no propagator proof in E9 to replace by Combes-Thomas or a Neumann series; the corresponding O(N) estimates are P2/P3 (with E3, E5).
- Hardest to formalize if one follows E9-style: the successive $N$-step peeling with boundary terms accumulating (E9 (1.61)-(1.70)); the P7 composition route (one-shot composition of the $N$ steps via P2 Sect. 4) is shorter, so the formalization should follow P7, not E9, for the construction, and use E9 only to understand the intended logic and the geometry (i), (ii), and the thresholds.
- Suggestion (not a claim): since the normalization identity and the "replace large field density by reference density times normalized ratio" structure are purely measure-theoretic, an early Lean interface could state them abstractly (densities on a product space, a measurable subset, a positive reference density with finite positive integral on the sub-factor, and the identity $\int R\rho=\int\rho$), independent of any of the field-theoretic content; E9 (0.3)-(0.4) and P7 (1.6)-(1.7) are both instances.
