## 0. Identification

T. Balaban, "Renormalization Group Approach to Lattice Gauge Field Theories. II. Cluster Expansions", Commun. Math. Phys. 116 (1988) 1-22. External key **E7**. 22 pages (21 of text, p. 22 has two lines of text and the reference list). Received 23 Sept 1985, revised 5 Oct 1987.

Summary. In E6 (Part I) the RG transformation of lattice gauge theory in four dimensions is applied to a small-field effective density; the fluctuation-field integral (E6 (I.2.12), (I.2.13)) is shown to be a small perturbation of a quadratic form and the renormalization is done. What is missing is the representation of the new effective action $\mathbb E^{(k+1)}$ as a sum of terms $\mathbb E^{(k+1)}(X)$ localized in domains $X\in\mathcal D_{k+1}$, analytic in the configurations on $X$ only, exponentially small in the tree length $d_{k+1}(X)$ (E6 (I.1.18)). E7 supplies it by two successive expansions (p. 1, Introduction):

1. Section 1 (pp. 2-11): a "decoupling" (generalized random-walk, Cauchy-formula) localization of the nonlocal terms of the fluctuation action (nonlocal because the propagators and the minimizers $H$, $\tilde G$, $H_0$, $A_0$ are nonlocal). Output: Lemmas 1 and 2 (pp. 9, 11): the fluctuation action is $\sum_{Y\in\mathcal D_k}V_k(Y,B)$, each term depending on the configuration only in $Y$, analytic, exponentially small in $d_k(Y)$.
2. Section 2 (pp. 11-22): an exponentiated cluster (polymer) expansion of the integral $\int d\mu_{C^{(k)}}(B)\,\chi_k\exp\sum_YV_k(Y,B)$ in the localized interaction. A Mayer expansion with decoupling parameters $t(Y)$ (2.1), a second localization of the propagators with parameters $s(\Delta)$ (2.8), a polymer expansion with activities $H(Z)$ (2.11), exponentiation (2.12)-(2.13), and the activity bound Lemma 3 (2.38). Output: the localized $\mathbb E^{(k+1)}(X)$ with the bound (2.41); this finishes the proof of Theorem I.3 (p. 22).

## 1. Setting and notation

Everything is imported from E6 and not restated; I record what E7 uses. The model is lattice gauge theory in $d=4$ (the powers $M^{-4}$, $(L^j\eta)^4$ and $(LM)^4$ in the paper are volumes in four dimensions), with gauge group $G$ and a Higgs-free pure gauge action ("Wilson action" with plaquette sums, p. 10).

- Lattices and scales. $T_\eta^{(k)}$ lattices of spacing $L^k\eta$ (the unit lattice $T_1^{(k)}$ carries the fluctuation field $B$; $T^{(k+1)}$ is the coarser lattice). $M$ is the size of the cubes of the partition $\pi_k$ of the torus (cubes of $\pi_k$ are "$M$-cubes", written $\square$, $\Delta$); $\pi_{k+1}$ consists of the $LM$-cubes (scale of $T^{(k+1)}$); $M_1\ll M$ is the size of smaller cubes ("$M_1$-cubes") used in the random-walk expansions of propagators (p. 3); $R_1M_1$-cubes form the partition $\sigma_k$ of the lattice $T_\eta$ used for decoupling, with $R_1$ a power of $L$ with $R_1M_1M^{-1}\le1$ (p. 3). Tilde means enlargement ($\tilde\square$, $\tilde Z$: adjoin neighbouring cubes; footnote on p. 13: a tilde over $X_0$ refers to $M_1$-cubes, a tilde over $Z_0$ to $M$-cubes).
- Localization domains. $\mathcal D_k$: connected unions of cubes of $\pi_k$ (connected in the sense: any two cubes are joined by a chain of cubes of $\pi_k$ in the domain, successive ones sharing a $(d-1)$-dimensional wall, pp. 4, 11). Linear size function $d_k(X)$: the (1/M)-normalized length of the shortest tree graph connecting the cubes of $X$ (consistent with (2.30): $(3\cdot2^3)^{-1}M^{-4}|X|\le d_k(X)\le M^{-4}|X|-1$, p. 18; here $|X|$ is the number of lattice points/bonds counted in units for which $M^{-4}|X|$ is the number of $M$-cubes up to a constant; UNCERTAIN: the exact normalization of $|X|$ is in E6). The scaling inequality (1.31): $d_j(X)\ge(L^j\eta)^{-1}d_k(X_0)$ for the localization domain $X_0$ of a term $X$ (p. 9). Between consecutive scales (2.36): $2d_k(Z_i)\ge L\,d_{k+1}(Z_i')$ (p. 19), $Z_i'$ the smallest domain of $\mathcal D_{k+1}$ containing $Z_i$.
- Fields. Gauge configurations $(U,J)$ (external gauge fields) in analyticity spaces $\mathcal U^c_{k+1}(\cdot,\alpha_0,\alpha_1)$ (E6 conditions (I.i)-(I.iv)); the fluctuation field $B$ on bonds $b$ of $T_1^{(k)}$ (the bonds are indexed by $b$); $g_k$ is the running coupling constant. Small-field restriction: $\chi_k=\prod_b\chi(|B(b)|<\varepsilon_1/g_k)$, i.e. $g_k|B|<\varepsilon_1$ (pp. 6, 12).
- Operators. $\mathbb H_k(B')=H_0B'+A_0-HD(H_0B'+A_0)$ (1.2), the (nonlinear, nonlocal) function representing the minimizer; $D(A')$ solves $D(A')=C(A'-HD(A'))$ (1.3); $A_0$ solves $A_0+\tilde G(\frac\delta{\delta A'}V)(H_0B'+A_0)=0$ (1.4); $H,\tilde G,H_0$ propagators, $C$ a given operator, all from E6/[13,15]. $B'=g_kCB-h\tilde D(g_kCB)$ (1.19). $\Delta_k=\Delta_k(\sigma)$ the quadratic form $\mathbb\Delta$ of the action; covariance $C^{(k)}$ and measure $d\mu_{C^{(k)}}(B)$; $(C^{(k)})^{1/2}$ via the resolvent integral (2.7).
- Decoupling parameters. $s(\Delta)\in\mathbb C$, one per cube $\Delta$ of a partition $\sigma_0$; $t(Y)\in[0,1]$ or complex, one per localization domain $Y$ (2.1); $t_\square$ one extra parameter for one distinguished cube; $\tau(Y)$ the complex variable of Cauchy integrals (2.14). Parameters of the analyticity domain: $\kappa$ (decay rate), $\kappa_1$ (radius of the $s$-circles: $|s|\le e^{\kappa_1}$), $\delta_0$ (decay rate of propagator random walks), $\delta$, $\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\varepsilon_3$, $\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_6$, $C_1,\dots,C_4$, $B_0$, $E_0$, $q$, $\gamma_2$.

## 2. Main results

There is no theorem numbered in this paper. The statements are:

**Lemma 1 (p. 9, formulas (1.33)-(1.36)).** The second expression in the fluctuation action ((I.2.12)),
$$\mathbf E_k\big(U_k(\exp iB'V^{(k)})\big)-\mathbf E_k\big(U_k(V^{(k)})\big)=\sum_{Y\in\mathcal D_k}V_k'(Y,U_{k+1},B),$$
where each $V_k'(Y,U,J,B)$ is defined and analytic on $\mathcal U^c_{k+1}(Y,(1+\beta)\alpha_0,(1+\beta)\alpha_1,\alpha_0)\times\{B:|B|<\varepsilon_1g_k^{-1}\text{ on }Y\}$, depends only on the restriction of the configurations to the interior of $Y$, and satisfies (1.35) $V_k'(Y,U_{k+1},B)=V_k'(Y,U_{k+1},J_{k+1},B)$ and there are absolute constants $C_1,C_2,q$ with
$$|V'_k(Y,\mathbf U,\mathbf J,B)|\le E_0\varepsilon_1C_1M^q\exp(C_2\kappa_1)\exp\big(-(1-2\delta)\kappa d_k(Y)\big).\quad(1.36)$$
Hypotheses (p. 9): $\frac1{16}(\kappa_1-1)\ge(1-2\delta)\kappa$, $0<\delta<1$.

**Lemma 2 (p. 11, (1.41)-(1.43)).** The fluctuation field action satisfies $\mathbf P^{(k)}(g_k,U_{k+1},B)+\{\cdots\}=\sum_{Y\in\mathcal D_k}\mathbf V_k(Y,U_{k+1},B)$, each term defined and analytic on the space (1.34), satisfying (1.35), and equal to a sum of two terms
$$\mathbf V_k(Y,B)=\tfrac12\langle Q(Y,B)B,B\rangle+\mathbf V_k''(Y,B),$$
with matrix elements of the quadratic form obeying
$$|Q(Y,B,b,b')|\le C_3\varepsilon_1M^4\exp C_2\kappa_1\,\exp\!\big(-\tfrac18(\kappa_1-1)d_k(Y)-\tfrac12(\kappa_1-1)M^{-4}|Y|\big)\ \ (b,b'\in Y)\quad(1.43)$$
and $\mathbf V_k''$ satisfying (1.36). All terms are gauge invariant under simultaneous gauge transformations in a small neighbourhood of the $G$-valued ones (p. 11).

**Lemma 3 (p. 20, (2.38)).** Under the restrictions on the constants $M,\kappa,\kappa_1,\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_4,\alpha_6,\gamma_2,\varepsilon_1$ listed in Sect. 2 (see section 6 below), the activity $H(Z)$ of the polymer expansion, for a localization domain $Z\in\mathcal D_{k+1}$, is analytic in the configurations $(\mathbf U,\mathbf J)$ on $\mathcal U_{k+1}^c(Z,\alpha_0,\alpha_1)$, depends on them only through $Z$ and
$$|H(Z)|\le C_3\varepsilon_1\exp\big(-(1-8\delta)\tfrac12L\kappa\,d_{k+1}(Z)\big).$$
(The constant $C_3$ is redefined at (2.37) as $C_3=2(L+2)^4O(1)2E_0C_1\varepsilon_1\alpha_4^{-1}\alpha_6^{-1}M^q\exp C_2\kappa_1$; $C_3\varepsilon_1$ is denoted in (2.38) as printed, "$C_3\varepsilon_1$" with $C_3$ containing $E_0C_1\alpha_4^{-1}\dots$ -- UNCERTAIN whether the printed formula (2.38) has $\varepsilon_1$ in addition to the $\varepsilon_1$ already contained in $C_3$ of (2.37); as printed (2.38) reads $C_3\varepsilon_1\exp(\dots)$ and (2.37) has $\varepsilon_2$ in front, so the second reading is $C_3=\dots E_0C_1\alpha_4^{-1}\alpha_6^{-1}M^q\exp C_2\kappa_1$ times $\varepsilon_1$ -- consistent with (2.41). I did not recompute.)

**Main conclusion (pp. 20-22, (2.12)-(2.13), (2.39)-(2.41)).** The series
$$\mathbb E^{(k+1)}(X)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac1{n!}\sum_{(Z_1,\dots,Z_n):\ \bigcup Z_i=X}\rho^T(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)H(Z_1)\cdots H(Z_n),\qquad X\in\mathcal D_{k+1},\quad(2.13)$$
converges absolutely and
$$|\mathbb E^{(k+1)}(X)|\le O(1)\,C_3\varepsilon_1\exp\big(-(1-10\delta)\tfrac12L\kappa\,d_{k+1}(X)\big).\quad(2.41)$$
With $\delta=\frac1{10}(1-2L^{-1})$, so that $(1-10\delta)\frac12L=1$, this is $\le O(1)C_3\varepsilon_1\exp(-\kappa d_{k+1}(X))$. Choosing $\varepsilon_1$ (hence $O(1)C_3\varepsilon_1\le\frac12E_0$) one gets (E6 (I.1.18)) with $\frac12E_0$ in place of $E_0$ for the terms of $\mathbb E^{(k+1)}$ (p. 21). The remaining part of the new effective action, $[\log Z^{(k)}(U_{k+1})-\log Z^{(k)}(1)]$, is localized by the generalized random-walk expansion of [16] (formula (63) there) and satisfies (E6 (I.1.18)) with $\kappa$ replaced by $\delta_0M$ and an absolute constant (p. 21). Gauge invariance extends to gauge transformations near $G^c$-valued ones, so the expressions are constant on orbits (pp. 21-22). This completes the proof of the inductive hypotheses for $A_{k+1}$, hence of Theorem I.3 (p. 22).

## 3. Proof architecture

### 3.1 Section 1: localization of the fluctuation action (pp. 2-11)

Mechanism (pp. 2-5). Terms of the fluctuation action (E6 (I.3.7), (I.3.21), (I.3.34)) are analytic and local functions of $\mathbf U,\mathbf J,B$ evaluated at a nonlocal argument: $\mathbf A=(t\zeta_\square+t_\square\zeta_\square)\mathbf H_k(B')$ (1.1), where $\mathbf H_k$ is the minimizer function of (1.2)-(1.4). Nonlocality arises only through three ingredients: the operators $H,\tilde G,H_0$ (propagators) and the nonlinear equations (1.3), (1.4) for $D,A_0$. These are solved by contraction mappings, so their locality properties follow from those of the propagators.

(a) Random-walk decomposition of propagators (1.6)-(1.7): each propagator is $\sum_\omega R_0(X_0)R_{\alpha_1}(X_1)\cdots R_{\alpha_n}(X_n)$ over walks $\omega$ through simple localization domains ($M_1$-scale), with the bound $O(1)O(M_1^{-1/2})^{|\omega|}M_1^{-1/2|\omega|}\exp(-\delta_0d(\omega,y,y'))$ per walk (the printed (1.7) is $O(1)O(M_1^{-1/2})^{|\omega|}M_1^{-1/2|\omega|}\exp(-\delta_0d(\omega,y,y'))$; I read it as in the paper, formula partly hard to read: UNCERTAIN). The decay constant is $\delta_0$ ([13], Theorem 3.1; (3.107), (3.108) there).

(b) Decoupling parameters (p. 3). Choose a partition $\sigma_0$ of the lattice into cubes of size $R_1M_1$ and attach a complex $s(\Delta)$ to each cube $\Delta\in\sigma_0$; a walk $\omega$ is multiplied by $s(\Delta_1)\cdots s(\Delta_m)$, $\Delta_i$ the cubes of $\sigma_0$ meeting the localization domain of $\omega$. This defines $H(s),\tilde G(s),H_0(s)$ equal to the true operators at $s=1$ (1.8).

(c) Fundamental theorem of calculus (1.9) (p. 4), the Mayer-type identity of cluster expansions:
$$\mathbf E(\square_0,\dots)=\sum_{\sigma\subset\sigma_0}\prod_{\Delta\in\sigma}\int_0^1ds(\Delta)\frac\partial{\partial s(\Delta)}\mathbf E\big(\dots H(s),\tilde G(s),H_0(s)\dots\big)\Big|_{s(\sigma^c)=0}.$$
Key observation (pp. 4-5): for $s(\sigma^c)=0$ the kernels of $H(s),\tilde G(s),H_0(s)$ vanish unless both arguments lie in one component of the set $Y(\sigma)$ (the union of the cubes in $\sigma$ together with $\tilde\square^4$, the fixed enlarged cube). Equations (1.3), (1.4) then split into separate equations on the components; the function depends on the data only through the component $Y_0$ of $Y(\sigma)$ that contains $\tilde\square^4$; derivatives with respect to $s$ in other components vanish. So the sum reduces to (1.10), a sum over connected domains $Y_0\ni\tilde\square^4$ with parameters $s(Y_0)$, and the term depends only on propagators and $B$ restricted to $Y_0$ ("connected" here is defined on p. 4 via chains of cubes sharing a $(d-1)$-dimensional wall; a "modified" connectedness for the component containing the cube).

(d) Cauchy estimates in $s$ (pp. 5-7). Each term is estimated by the Cauchy formula on the circles $|s(\Delta)|=e^{\kappa_1}$. Needed: the functions $\mathbf H(s(Y_0)),\tilde G(s(Y_0)),H_0(s(Y_0))$ are analytic and bounded by $B_0e^{16\kappa_1}$ on the polydisc. Proof (1.11): $B_0|X|\sup_\omega M_1^{-1/2|\omega|}\exp(-\delta_0d(\omega))e^{m\kappa_1}$ where $m$ is the number of the parameters attached to the walk; for $m>2^4$ the decay $\delta_0d(\omega)\ge\delta_1mM$ beats $e^{m\kappa_1}$ when $\delta_1M\ge\kappa_1$ (this is why $M$ is much larger than $\kappa_1$), for $m\le2^4$ one gets $e^{16\kappa_1}$. Then the nonlinear equations: change of variables $A=A'-H(s(Y_0))D(\dots)$ (1.12); the right side of (1.3) is a contraction on $|A'|<\varepsilon_2$ if $4C_2B_0e^{16\kappa_1}\varepsilon_2\le1$ (1.13)-(1.14); (1.4) is a contraction with the bounds (1.15)-(1.16) under $2B_0e^{16\kappa_1}\varepsilon_3\le\varepsilon_2$; hence $\mathbf H_k(s(Y_0),B')$ is analytic in $s(Y_0),B'$ and $|\mathbf H_k|\le4B_0e^{16\kappa_1}|B'|$ (1.17)-(1.18), and for $B'$ in terms of $B$ (1.19)-(1.21).

(e) A second Cauchy estimate in the parameter $t_\square$ (pp. 7-8): the function must be analytic in $t_\square$ on a disc, the radius of which follows from (1.21) and E6 (I.3.31): $|t_\square|\le(8B_0C_1e^{16\kappa_1}\alpha_2^{-1}g_k|B|)^{-1}$ (1.22). Combining $(t_\square$-derivative at $0$ by Cauchy, the $s$-derivatives by Cauchy$)$ gives (1.23)-(1.24):
$$|(1.23)|\le8B_0C_1e^{16\kappa_1}\alpha_2^{-1}g_k|B|\,E_0\Big(\frac{\alpha_1}{\alpha_3}\Big)^5(L^j\eta)^5\exp\big(-(\kappa_1-1)M^{-4}|Y_0\setminus\tilde\square^4|\big)\exp(-\kappa d_j(X)).$$

(f) Resummation to a fixed domain $Y$ (pp. 8-9): sum over $\square_0,Y_0,j,X$ with fixed $Y=Y_0\cup\square_0$; uses (1.25) (adding and subtracting $\frac18\kappa_1d_k(\square_0)$), the convergence-of-cluster-expansion estimate (1.26) $\sum_{X\in\mathcal D_j,X\supset\square'}\exp(-\kappa d_j(X))\le O(1)$ ("used many times in convergence proofs of cluster expansions, e.g. [48,50,40,26,3]", p. 8), counting of subsets $Y_0$ of the family of cubes in $\square_0\setminus\tilde\square^4$ (the sum is bounded by $\exp(8\cdot12^3\exp(-\frac12(\kappa_1-1)))\le e$, (1.27)), and the volume bound (1.28) $M^{-4}|Y|\le3\cdot2^3d_k(Y)\le\exp\frac1{16}(\kappa_1-2)d_k(Y)$. Result (1.29): $|\sum(1.23)|\le E_0\varepsilon_1O(M^q)\exp O(1)\kappa_1\exp(-\frac1{16}\kappa_1d_k(Y))$. Then the same for the other terms (pp. 8-9), and the scaling inequality (1.31) with the factor $L^j\eta$ to sum over $j$ (p. 9, (1.30)-(1.32)). This gives Lemma 1.

(g) The $1/g_k^2$ problem (pp. 9-11). The first term $\mathbf P^{(k)}(g_k,U_{k+1},B)$ of the action has the prefactor $1/g_k^2$ in front of the Wilson action, so one cannot just use the bound (1.36) for the whole. The action is decomposed over plaquettes by a partition of unity $\sum\zeta_\square=1$ (1.37), each plaquette term is localized by (1.10) (1.38) and bounded by (1.39); expanded in $B'$ to second order (terms of zero and first order vanish) the coefficients are the second derivatives, bounded by (1.40), and the second-order term is written as the quadratic form $\frac12\langle Q(Y,B)B,B\rangle$; the remainder is a bounded polynomial in $B$ only (no $1/g_k^2$), obeying (1.36). Lemma 2 collects these.

Routine versus difficult. The structural idea ((b)-(c): decoupling parameters, locality of solutions of the equations at $s(\sigma^c)=0$) is the heart; the bounds (d)-(g) are routine but long. All substantive inputs (properties of propagators, contraction estimates for $D$, $A_0$: "Sect. C, E [15]", "Theorems 3.1-3.10 [13]", "Proposition 4, inequality (98) [15]", "(55), (57), (80) [15]") are cited, not reproved.

### 3.2 Section 2: the cluster expansion (pp. 11-22)

Step 1: Mayer expansion with decoupling parameters in the interaction (pp. 11-12). With Lemma 2, the integral to be expanded is $\int d\mu_{C^{(k)}}(B)\chi_k\exp[\sum_{Y\in\mathcal D_k}V_k(Y,B)]$. Introduce parameters $t(Y)\in[0,1]$ and write
$$\int d\mu_{C^{(k)}}(B)\chi_k\exp\Big[\sum_{Y}\mathbf V_k(Y,B)\Big]=\sum_{\mathbf D\subset\mathcal D_k}\prod_{Y\in\mathbf D}\int_0^1dt(Y)\int d\mu_{C^{(k)}}(B)\chi_k\exp\Big[\sum_Yt(Y)\mathbf V_k(Y,B)\Big]\prod_{Y\in\mathbf D}\mathbf V_k(Y,B)\quad(2.1)$$
(the standard formula: $e^{\sum V}=\sum_{\mathbf D}\prod_{Y\in\mathbf D}\int_0^1dt(Y)\partial_{t(Y)}\dots$ evaluated at $t=0$ off $\mathbf D$, then re-evaluated; it is the "forest-free" Mayer expansion). Each term is localized in $Y_0=\bigcup_{Y\in\mathbf D}Y$ (2.2) because the $V$ outside $\mathbf D$ are multiplied by $t=0$.

Step 2: the small-field characteristic function (2.3) (p. 12). $\chi_k$ is split as $\chi_{k,Y_0}\chi_{k,Y_0^c}$ and the second factor is rewritten by inclusion-exclusion over the set $Y_0^{c*}=\{b\in T^{(k)}:b\subset Y_0^c\}$ of bonds:
$$\chi_{k,Y_0^c}=\sum_{P\subset Y_0^{c*}}(-1)^{|P|}\chi^c_{k,P},\qquad\chi^c_{k,P}=\prod_{b\in P}\chi\Big(\Big\{|B(b)|\ge\frac{\varepsilon_1}{g_k}\Big\}\Big).$$
The point: outside $Y_0$ the integrand is then only the Gaussian times characteristic functions of "large field bonds", which cost $\exp(-\frac{\varepsilon_1^2}{2g_k^2}\gamma_2|P|)$ (2.22) (see Step 5), so only finitely many $P$ matter. Let $Z_0\in\mathcal D_k$ be the smallest localization domain containing $Y_0$ and $P$ (the bonds of $P$ lie in the interior of $Z_0$). Resummation over $\mathbf D,P$ giving the same $Z_0$ defines $F(Z_0,B)$ and
$$(2.1)=\sum_{Z_0\in\mathcal D_k}\int d\mu_{C^{(k)}}(B)F(Z_0,B).\quad(2.4)$$

Step 3: conditioning (2.5)-(2.6) (pp. 12-13). Because the covariance $C^{(k)}$ is not a product measure, the term with $Z_0$ is written (as in [6], (2.28)) as the ratio
$$\frac{\int dB|_{Z_0^c}\exp(-\frac12\langle B,C^*\Delta_kCB\rangle)F(Z_0,B)}{\int dB|_{Z_0^c}\exp(-\frac12\langle Z_0^cB,C^*\Delta_kCZ_0^cB\rangle-\frac12\langle Z_0B,C^*\Delta_kCZ_0B\rangle)},$$
integrated against the normalization, which reduces to an integral over $B|_{Z_0}$ with the conditional covariance $C^{(k)}(Z_0)$ (the restriction of $C^{(k)}$ to $Z_0$, obtained by integrating out $Z_0^c$) times an explicit Gaussian factor depending on $B'$ at $\partial Z_0$. The linear change of variables $B'=(C^{(k)})^{1/2}X$ makes the measure on $Z_0$ the standard Gaussian product measure $d\mu_0(X)=\prod_b dX(b)e^{-|X(b)|^2/2}(2\pi)^{-1/2\cdots}$; the integrand becomes the function $G(Z_0,X,C^{(k)}(Z_0),(C^{(k)}(Z_0))^{1/2},\Delta_k)$ (2.6). Thus
$$(2.1)=\sum_{Z_0\in\mathcal D_k}\int d\mu_0(X)\,G(Z_0,X,C^{(k)}(Z_0),(C^{(k)})^{1/2}(Z_0),\Delta_k).$$
$G$ depends on the external fields through all of $T_\eta$ (via $\Delta_k$, $C^{(k)}(Z_0)$, $(C^{(k)})^{1/2}(Z_0)$), so it is localized again.

Step 4: second localization of the operators (pp. 13-14). Use $\pi_{k+1}$-cubes ($LM$-scale) with parameters $s(\Delta)$, $\Delta\in\sigma_0$ ($\sigma_0$ = family of $LM$-cubes disjoint from the interior of $\tilde Z_0$ or of $Z_0'$, the smallest $\mathcal D_{k+1}$-domain containing $\tilde Z_0$). The generalized random walk expansions of [13] are used for $\Delta_k$ and $C^{(k)}(Z_0)$; for $(C^{(k)})^{1/2}(Z_0)$ the resolvent representation (2.7)
$$(C^{(k)})^{1/2}=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty dx\,x^{-1/2}(xI+C^*\Delta_kC)^{-1}=\frac1\pi\int_0^{\gamma_1}\cdots+\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n}{n+1/2}\gamma_1^{-n-1/2}(C^*\Delta_kC)^n$$
is used, with the resolvent expanded in random walks with an additional nonnegative, bounded, almost local operator $\tilde G_3(x)$ replacing $\tilde G_2$ in [13] (3.185) (method of [16] Sect. C, determinant (63)). The decoupling identity (1.10) is then applied:
$$\int d\mu_0(X)G=\sum_{Z\supset Z_0'}\prod_{\Delta\in Z\setminus Z_0'}\int_0^1ds(\Delta)\frac\partial{\partial s(\Delta)}\int d\mu_0(X)G(Z_0,X,C^{(k)}(Z_0,s(Z)),(C^{(k)})^{1/2}(s(Z)),\Delta_k(s(Z)))=\sum_ZH(Z,Z_0)\quad(2.8)$$
with $Z$ ranging over domains in $\mathcal D_{k+1}$ each connected component of which contains a component of $Z_0'$ (the sum is over unions of components: $Z$ need not be connected). Then (2.9): $(2.1)=\sum_ZH(Z)$, $H(Z)=\sum_{Z_0:Z_0'\subset Z}H(Z,Z_0)$, and (2.10): $H(Z)=H(Z_1)\cdots H(Z_n)$ if $Z=Z_1\cup\dots\cup Z_n$ is the decomposition into connected components (the measure is a product, the derivatives in $s$ factorize over components).

Step 5: polymer expansion (2.11) and exponentiation (2.12)-(2.13) (p. 14).
$$(2.1)=\sum_{\{Z_1,\dots,Z_n\}}H(Z_1)\cdots H(Z_n)=1+\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac1{n!}\sum_{(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)}\prod_{i<j}\zeta(Z_i,Z_j)H(Z_1)\cdots H(Z_n),$$
where $\zeta(Z,Z')=0$ if $Z\cap Z'$ contains a cube or a wall of a cube and $\zeta(Z,Z')=1$ otherwise. "If the activities $H(Z)$ are sufficiently small, then the polymer expansion can be exponentiated according to the well-known formula, see [36,60,26,25,67,50]" (p. 14):
$$\mathbb E^{(k+1)}=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac1{n!}\sum_{(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)}\rho^T(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)H(Z_1)\cdots H(Z_n),\quad\rho^T(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)=\sum_{g\in C_n}\prod_{\{i,j\}\in g}(\zeta(Z_i,Z_j)-1),\ \rho^T(Z)=1,$$
$C_n$ the set of connected graphs on $\{1,\dots,n\}$. The localized terms are (2.13): $\mathbb E^{(k+1)}(X)=\sum_n\frac1{n!}\sum_{(Z_1,\dots,Z_n):\bigcup Z_i=X}\rho^T H\cdots H$, $X\in\mathcal D_{k+1}$. Note: $\rho^T\ne0$ only for $\zeta$-connected collections, so $X$ is a connected domain automatically. The convergence is proved by the estimates of the activities, not by a general theorem stated in the paper: the paper quotes the "paper [26]" (Cammarota) estimates for the sums (2.40).

Step 6: analyticity of the activities (pp. 14-15). $H(Z)$ is a sum of many terms (over $Z_0,\mathbf D,P$), each an analytic function of $(\mathbf U,\mathbf J)\in\mathcal U^c_{k+1}(X,\alpha_0,\alpha_1)$ and of the complex parameters $\sigma(Z)$, $\tau$; the potentials extend to the larger space $\mathcal U^c_{k+1}(Y,(1+\beta)\alpha_0,(1+\beta)\alpha_1,\alpha_0)$ ("with constants $\alpha_0',\alpha_1'$ much bigger"), so one can restrict to the smaller space and get analyticity of $H(Z)$ and of $\mathbb E^{(k+1)}(X)$ (p. 15). The generic term of $H(Z)$ is written as (2.14): Cauchy integrals in $s(\Delta)$ (circles $|\sigma(\Delta)|=e^{\kappa_1}$) and in $t(Y)$ (variable $\tau(Y)$, radius to be chosen) times
$$\int d\mu_0(X)|_Z\exp\!\big(-\tfrac12\langle\Gamma_k(Z_0,\sigma(Z))X,C^{(k)}(Z_0,\sigma(Z))\Gamma_k(Z_0,\sigma(Z))X\rangle\big)\int d\mu_{C^{(k)}(Z_0,\sigma(Z))}(B)\exp(-\langle B,\Gamma_k(Z_0,\sigma(Z))X\rangle)(-1)^{|P|}\chi_{k,Y_0}(B)\chi^c_{k,P}(B)\exp\Big[\sum_{Y\in\mathbf D}\tau(Y)\mathbf V_k(Y,B)\Big].$$
For complex parameters the operators are non-symmetric and the Gaussian measure complex; the paper handles this by perturbation: for $(\mathbf U,0)$ the operators are symmetric and the measure positive; in general $\mathbf U=U'U$ with $U'=\exp iL^{-1}\eta A'$, $A'$ small, so everything is a small perturbation. The first estimate is (2.15): absolute values with the real parts, $|\det C^{(k)}/\det\mathrm{Re}\,C^{(k)}|^{1/2}$ factors, and the factor $\exp(-(\kappa_1-1)(LM)^{-4}|Z\setminus Z_0'|)$ from the $s$-circles.

Step 7: bound for a single term (pp. 15-17). (a) The difference between the operators with $\sigma(Z)$, $\mathbf U,\mathbf J$ general and those with $\sigma=0$, $\mathbf U=\mathbf U,\mathbf J=0$ is a quadratic form with matrix elements $|R_1(b,b')|\le(O(1)e^{-1/3\delta_0M}+O(\alpha_0+\alpha_1))\exp(-\frac12\delta_0|b_--b'_-|)$ (2.16) (here $M$ much larger than $\kappa_1$, $e^{-1/3\delta_0M}e^{16\kappa_1}<1$), so determinants are bounded by $\exp((O(1)e^{-1/3\delta_0M}+O(\alpha_0+\alpha_1))|Z_0|)$ (2.17), and the other quadratic forms (with $R_2,R_3$) by (2.21). (b) The potentials: with the radius $|\tau(Y)|^{-1}=E_0\varepsilon_1C_1\alpha_4^{-1}M^q\exp C_2\kappa_1\exp(-(1-3\delta)\kappa d_k(Y))$ (2.18) and the quadratic-form bound (1.42), (2.19): $|\tau(Y)||\mathbf V_k(Y,B)|\le\alpha_4M^{-4}\exp(\dots)|B(b)||B(b')|$ sums; resummed over $Y\in\mathbf D$ containing a given point, (2.20) gives the quadratic form bounded by $O(1)\alpha_4(\sum_{b\subset Y_0}|B(b)|^2+M^{-4}|Y_0|)$ (small $\alpha_4$ absorbs it into the Gaussian). (c) The characteristic functions: $\chi_{k,Y_0}\chi^c_{k,P}\le\exp(-\frac12\gamma_2\frac{\varepsilon_1^2}{g_k^2}|P|+\frac12\gamma_2\|PB\|^2)$ (2.22), $\gamma_2$ small. (d) Gaussian integral (2.23)-(2.25): everything is dominated by $\int d\mu_0(X)|_Z\exp\frac12\alpha_5\|ZX\|^2\int d\mu_{C^{(k)}(Z_0,0)}\exp(\dots)$ with $\alpha_5=O(1)e^{-1/3\delta_0M}+O(\alpha_0+\alpha_1)+O(\alpha_4)+\gamma_2$; this is a Gaussian integral evaluated exactly: (2.24) with $\det(C^{(k)}(Z_0,0)^{-1})/\det(C^{(k)}(Z_0,0)^{-1}-\alpha_5I)$ factors $\le\exp O(1)\alpha_5|Z_0|$, and (2.25) $\int d\mu_0\exp\frac12O(\alpha_5)\|ZX\|^2=\prod_{b\in Z}(1-O(\alpha_5))^{1/2}\le\exp(O(\alpha_5)|Z|)$ (assumes $\alpha_5\|C^{(k)}(Z_0,0)\|<\frac12$). Collected: (2.26):
$$|(2.14)|\le\exp(-(\kappa_1-1)(LM)^{-4}|Z\setminus Z_0'|)\Big[\prod_{Y\in\mathbf D}2E_0\varepsilon_1C_1\alpha_4^{-1}M^q\exp C_2\kappa_1\exp(-(1-3\delta)\kappa d_k(Y))\Big]\exp\Big(-\frac1{2}\gamma_2\frac{\varepsilon_1^2}{g_k^2}|P|\Big)\exp O(1)\alpha_5|Z|$$
(as printed up to the exact numerical factors in the exponent of $|P|$; the paper writes $\frac12\gamma_2\varepsilon_1^2g_k^{-2}|P|$ in (2.22) and $\exp(-\frac12\gamma_2\varepsilon_1^2g_k^{-2}|P|)$ in (2.26); UNCERTAIN of the numerical constant: it is $\frac12\gamma_2$ in (2.22) and shown as $\frac12\gamma_2$ in (2.26); I did not recheck).

Step 8: resummation to get $H(Z)$ (pp. 17-20). The sums over $\mathbf D,P,Z_0$ at fixed $Z$ are done in this order: (i) over $\mathbf D$ with fixed $Y_0$: it factorizes over the connected components $Y_i$ of $Y_0$; extract $\alpha_6\exp(-\delta\kappa d_k(Y))$ from each factor; use (2.27) $\sum_{Y\in\mathbf D_i}(d_k(Y)+5)\ge d_k(Y_i)+5$ (tree-length is subadditive up to a constant per overlap) to get (2.28) $\prod_Y(\dots)\le\prod_Y\alpha_6\exp(-\delta\kappa d_k(Y))\cdot2E_0\varepsilon_1C_1\alpha_4^{-1}\alpha_6^{-1}M^q\exp C_2\kappa_1\exp(-(1-4\delta)\kappa d_k(Y_0))$, assuming $2E_0\varepsilon_1C_1\alpha_4^{-1}\alpha_6^{-1}M^q\exp C_2\kappa_1\exp5\kappa\le1$; and the sum over all $\mathbf D$ of the product is $\le1$ (2.29) ("inequalities of this type were proved many times, e.g. by a simple modification of the argument in [26]"); (ii) over $P$ with fixed $Z_0$: the large-field factor $\exp(-\frac1{20}\gamma_2\varepsilon_1^2g_k^{-2}M^{-4}|Z_0\setminus Y_0|)\exp(-\frac1{10}\gamma_2\varepsilon_1^2g_k^{-2}|P|)$ uses $|P|\ge\frac12M^{-4}|Z_0\setminus Y_0|$ (because $Z_0$ is the smallest domain containing $Y_0\cup P$, one bond of $P$ connects at most two cubes of $Z_0\setminus Y_0$) and the sum over $P$ is bounded by (2.31), assuming $g_k$ small ($\gamma_2^2$ small): $\frac1{20}\gamma_2\varepsilon_1^2g_k^{-2}-4M^4\exp(-\frac1{10}\gamma_2\varepsilon_1^2g_k^{-2})\ge4\kappa$ (2.33) (using (2.32): $\sum d_k(Y_i)+4M^{-4}|Z_0\setminus Y_0|\ge d_k(Z_0)$); gives $\max\{\varepsilon_2,\exp(-\frac1{20}\gamma_2\varepsilon_1^2g_k^{-2})\}\exp(-(1-4\delta)\kappa d_k(Z_0))$, $\varepsilon_2=2E_0\varepsilon_1C_1\alpha_4^{-1}\alpha_6^{-1}M^q\exp C_2\kappa_1$; (iii) over $Y_0\subset Z_0$: bounded by $\exp(\exp(-\frac1{20}\gamma_2\varepsilon_1^2g_k^{-2})M^{-4}|Z_0|)$ (2.34); so (2.35): $|\sum_{\mathbf D,P}(2.14)|\le\exp(-(\kappa_1-1)(LM)^{-4}|Z\setminus Z_0'|)[\prod_i\varepsilon_2\exp(-(1-5\delta)\kappa d_k(Z_i))]\exp O(1)\alpha_5|Z|$; (iv) over $Z_0$ with fixed $Z_0'$ ($Z_0'$ the $\mathcal D_{k+1}$-hull), where (2.36) $2d_k(Z_i)\ge Ld_{k+1}(Z_i')$ converts $d_k$ to $d_{k+1}$ with the gain $\frac12L$, using (1.28) with $\kappa\to\delta\kappa$ and an additional sum over $(L+2)^4$ cubes touching a fixed $LM$-cube; result (2.37): $|\sum_{\mathbf D,P,Z_0}(2.14)|\le\exp(-(\kappa_1-1)(LM)^{-4}|Z\setminus Z_0'|)\prod_i2(L+2)^4O(1)\varepsilon_2\exp(-(1-7\delta)\frac12L\kappa d_{k+1}(Z_i'))\exp O(1)\alpha_5|Z|$; (v) over $Z_0'$ (the sum over $Z\setminus Z_0'$): by (2.34)-type bound $\exp(\exp(-\frac12(\kappa_1-1))(LM)^{-4}|Z|)$, with $\alpha_5$-terms $(LM)^4\alpha_5$, $(LM)^4\alpha_0$ etc. bounded; (2.30) $(3\cdot2^3)^{-1}M^{-4}|Y|\le d_k(Y)\le M^{-4}|Y|-1$ absorbs the volume factors into $d_{k+1}$. Lemma 3 follows with $C_3=2(L+2)^4O(1)2E_0C_1\varepsilon_1\alpha_4^{-1}\alpha_6^{-1}M^q\exp C_2\kappa_1$ (up to the printed form).

Step 9: bound for $\mathbb E^{(k+1)}(X)$ (pp. 20-21). Each factor $H(Z_i)$ in (2.13) is replaced by the bound (2.38); the exponential $\exp(-\delta\frac12L\kappa d_{k+1}(Z_i))$ is extracted from each and the rest is estimated using (2.27) and the condition $\bigcup Z_i=X$ with $X$ connected: (2.39)
$$|\mathbb E^{(k+1)}(X)|\le\exp(-(1-9\delta)\tfrac12L\kappa d_{k+1}(X))\exp(-5\kappa)\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n!}\sum_{(Z_1,\dots,Z_n):\bigcup Z_i=X}|\rho^T(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)|\prod_iC_3\varepsilon_1\exp5\kappa\exp(-\delta\tfrac12L\kappa d_{k+1}(Z_i)).$$
"To the above sum we can repeat all the considerations and bounds of the paper [26] for $\kappa$ sufficiently large and $\varepsilon_1$ sufficiently small" gives (2.40): the sum is $\le\sum_{Z=X}C_3\varepsilon_1\exp5\kappa\exp(-\frac12\delta L\kappa d_{k+1}(Z))O(1)\exp2(LM)^{-4}|Z|$ ... and then (2.41). No proof of the bound $\sum|\rho^T|\prod\le\dots$ is given; it is cited from Cammarota [26] (= E1 in this project, CMP 85, 517-528) and the exponentiation references. So the Kotecky-Preiss / Cammarota step is not proved in E7.

Step 10: the remaining term $\log Z^{(k)}(U_{k+1})-\log Z^{(k)}(1)$ (p. 21): localized by the random-walk expansion of [16]; bounds (E6 (I.1.18)) with $\kappa\to\delta_0M$ and an absolute constant; $M$ chosen large so that $\delta_0M\ge\kappa$.

Step 11: gauge invariance (pp. 21-22): the terms are analytic in $(\mathbf U,\mathbf J)$ and gauge invariant under $G$-valued gauge transformations, hence under $G^c$-valued transformations near $G$ (analyticity), hence constant on intersections of orbits with the configuration space; extended as constants to whole orbits. Gauge-specific.

## 7. Weak spots and open points

1. Style. The paper is short and terse. Section 1 does one example term (the last term of (I.3.34)) and says that all other terms are "quite general" and "can be bounded similarly using (I.4.5), (I.4.22), (I.4.36), (I.5.44) and other inequalities" (pp. 8-9, 11). Lemma 2's second-order Taylor argument (1.37)-(1.40) is done on one term. The reader cannot check the completeness of Lemma 1 and Lemma 2 from this paper alone.
2. The geometric inequalities (2.27), (2.30), (2.32), (2.36) are stated without proof ("simple, but awkward, geometric and combinatorial considerations", p. 19; "by a simple geometric argument", p. 18; (2.30) quoted as "useful"). They are exactly the combinatorial inputs that a formalization must supply; the counterpart (4.11) of P6 is proved in full there (P6 pp. 25-26), but for $|X|\le(3LM)^d(1+d_k(X))$, not for (2.27), (2.32), (2.36).
3. Convergence of the polymer expansion: the paper does not state a convergence theorem. It says "the polymer expansion can be exponentiated according to the well-known formula" (p. 14) and that "we can repeat all the considerations and bounds of the paper [26]" (p. 21). The precise hypothesis under which (2.12) is the log of (2.11), and the proof of the tree-graph bound $\sum|\rho^T|\prod|H|$, are not given. The incompatibility relation $\zeta$ (sharing a cube or a wall) is specific: polymers touching along a lower-dimensional face are compatible.
4. The nonsymmetric/complex Gaussian estimate (p. 15, (2.15)): the "perturbative argument" is stated qualitatively ("U = U'U, $A'$, J small"); the passage from (2.14) to (2.15) (replacing the complex covariance by the real part with determinant ratio) is not justified in detail.

## 8. Preliminary formalization notes

- The abstract polymer-expansion core (Steps 1-2, 5, 8-9) is self-contained and formalizable. What needs to be proved once: see section 10 below for the precise statement.
- Self-contained pieces: (i) the Mayer expansion with decoupling parameters (2.1) and (1.9): purely algebraic identity (fundamental theorem of calculus / Boole-type identity for $F(1)$ in terms of the derivatives at $s(\sigma^c)=0$); (ii) the inclusion-exclusion (2.3) and the "conditioning" trick (2.5); (iii) the polymer expansion (2.11) and the exponentiation (2.12); this is a Kotecky-Preiss / Cammarota theorem and would be proved abstractly (it is not in Mathlib; a guess); (iv) the Gaussian integral computations (2.23)-(2.25); (v) combinatorial lemmas on tree-length and domains (2.27), (2.30), (2.32), (2.36), (1.26), (1.28): finite combinatorics on a cubic lattice, not in Mathlib.
- Infrastructure: finite-dimensional Gaussian integrals with complex-symmetric perturbations; analytic functions of many complex variables with Cauchy estimates on polydiscs (Mathlib: one variable, partly several; a guess that this needs development); lattice cubes/domains/tree-length; absolutely convergent combinatorial series with the Ursell functions.
- Hardest: Section 1 (needs everything about the propagators and the minimizer, none of which is proved in E7); Lemma 2's bookkeeping (gauge-specific). For O(N) these are replaced by P2, P3.

## 10. Relevance to the formalization

Contributions. E7 is the cleanest short account in the corpus of the two-stage "decoupling + polymer" scheme, and the model for what P3 Prop. 4.3 and P6 §4 do. For a Lean formalization it is useful as a design document, not as a dependency. Its gauge-specific parts (Lemma 2 plaquette/ $1/g_k^2$ cancellation, gauge-orbit extension, $\mathcal U^c$ spaces) are irrelevant; its Section 1 (propagator random walks, contraction for $\mathbf H_k$) is the gauge counterpart of P2/P3 and is not reusable beyond the pattern.

**The abstract polymer-expansion statement to prove once.** Formulate, with no reference to Gaussians or to analyticity, an abstract cluster-expansion theorem with the form that E7 (2.11)-(2.13), (2.38)-(2.41), P6 (4.46)-(4.51), and (according to BIG_PICTURE §5 item 4) P7 (3.27)-(3.28), (4.14) and P9 (4.4)-(4.6) use:

*Data.* A countable set $\mathcal P$ of polymers (finite connected unions of cubes of a given partition of a torus $\Lambda$; "connected" in a fixed sense: wall-connected as in E7, or connected mod a given set $\Omega^c$ as in P6), with a symmetric reflexive incompatibility relation $Z\not\sim Z'$ (E7: $Z\cap Z'$ contains a cube or a wall of a cube; in the P6/P7 setting: the corresponding relation for domains modulo $\Omega^c$), a size function $d:\mathcal P\to[0,\infty)$ (tree length), a volume $|Z|\in\mathbb N$ (number of cubes), and complex activities $H(Z)$ (in the application analytic functions of external parameters, with values in a Banach space of analytic functions; so state the theorem for activities with values in a normed commutative algebra, or prove it for numerical activities on a polydisc and apply it pointwise and with Cauchy estimates).

*Hypotheses (counting inputs).* (C1) Entropy bound: for a cube $\square$ and $n\ge0$, $\#\{Z\in\mathcal P:Z\ni\square,\ d(Z)\le n\}\le e^{c_0(1+n)}$, equivalently $\sum_{Z\ni\square}e^{-\kappa d(Z)}\le C(\kappa)$ with $C(\kappa)\to0$ as $\kappa\to\infty$ (E7 (1.26), P3/P6 summation over $\mathcal D$). (C2) Volume versus tree length: $c_1^{-1}|Z|\le1+d(Z)\le c_2|Z|$ (E7 (2.30), P6 (4.11), the part $|Z|\le c(1+d(Z))$). (C3) Subadditivity under connected unions: if $Z=\bigcup Z_i$ is connected then $d(Z)\le\sum_i(d(Z_i)+c_3)$ (E7 (2.27), (2.32); P6 (4.44)). (C4) Scale-change inequality, when polymers of two scales are compared: $d_{k}(Z)\ge\frac12Ld_{k+1}(Z')$ (E7 (2.36)). (C5) Activity bound: $|H(Z)|\le a\,e^{-\kappa d(Z)}$ for each polymer, with the multiplicative property $H(Z)=\prod H(Z_i)$ over $\sim$-components for non-polymer sets (E7 (2.10)).

*Conclusion.* There is $a_0(\kappa,c_i)>0$ such that for $a\le a_0$ and $\kappa\ge\kappa_0$: (i) the polymer partition function $\Xi=\sum_{\{Z_i\}\text{ pairwise compatible}}\prod H(Z_i)$ (finite volume) is $\ne0$ and $\log\Xi=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n!}\sum_{(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)}\rho^T(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)\prod H(Z_i)$, absolutely convergent; (ii) the localized terms $\mathbb E(X)=\sum_n\frac1{n!}\sum_{\bigcup Z_i=X}\rho^T\prod H$ satisfy $|\mathbb E(X)|\le C a\,e^{-\kappa'd(X)}$ for any $\kappa'<\kappa$ minus the constant lost to (C1)-(C3) (E7 (2.41): exactly $\kappa'=(1-10\delta)\frac12L\kappa$ at the coarser scale, using (C4)); (iii) $\sum_{X\ni\square}|\mathbb E(X)|e^{\kappa'd(X)}\le Ca$; (iv) lower bound $|\Xi|\ge e^{-C a|\Lambda|}$-type (P6 (4.51): $|(4.46)|>e^{-1}e^{-d(Z)}$ for the restricted gas); (v) for expectations with marked point (P6 (4.35)-(4.54)): the ratio $\langle F(Y_0)\rangle=\sum_{Z\supset Y_0}\mathcal E(Y_0,Z)$ with $|\mathcal E(Y_0,Z)|\le Ca\,e^{-6\kappa_0d(Y_0)}e^{-(\kappa'-\cdot)d(Z)}$ follows from (i)-(iii) by the same expansion with one marked polymer. Stating it with a general compatibility relation and a general "connected" structure (so that wall-connectedness, E7, and connectedness mod $\Omega^c$, P6 p. 44, both instantiate it) is advisable. The convergence theorem to prove is Kotecky-Preiss (or Brydges' version in E11, or Cammarota E1); a Lean proof of Kotecky-Preiss for abstract polymer systems is the most economical way (this is a suggestion; I did not check any Lean library).

*Interface statements* that a formalization would freeze: (a) Lemma 1/2 analogue = P6 Lemma 4.1 (localized interaction $\mathcal U(Y)$ with the bound (4.28)); (b) the abstract polymer lemma above; (c) the identity $F(1)=\sum_{\mathscr C}\prod\int ds\,\partial_sF|_{s(\mathscr C^c)=0}$ (E7 (1.9), P3 Prop. 4.3); (d) the Gaussian bound (E7 (2.23)-(2.25)): for a Gaussian with covariance a bounded perturbation of identity, $\int d\mu_0\exp\frac12\alpha\|ZX\|^2\le\exp(O(\alpha)|Z|)$, a trivial finite-dimensional statement.

Reusable arguments: the splitting of $\chi_k$ outside the support by inclusion-exclusion (2.3) together with the counting $|P|\ge\frac12M^{-4}|Z_0\setminus Y_0|$ (2.31); the conditioning-on-$Z_0^c$ ratio (2.5); the consideration that the activity is absolutely summable over the auxiliary data $(\mathbf D,P,Z_0)$ by the "product of $\alpha_6\exp(-\delta\kappa d(Y))$ and (2.29)" trick, where the small constant $\alpha_6$ turns the free sum over $\mathbf D$ into a geometric series; the use of (2.36) to gain the factor $\frac12L$ so that the decay rate does not deteriorate from scale to scale (the same phenomenon as P6/P3's $2\kappa$).

Not reusable / irrelevant: gauge invariance and orbit extension, plaquette decomposition, the $G$-valued configurations, the propagators of E5/[13] with gauge fixing.

Green's function aspects. E7 itself proves no propagator estimate; it uses [13] (random-walk expansion with decay $\delta_0$) as a black box, with $M_1$-cubes and the factor $O(M_1^{-1/2})^{|\omega|}$ per step. The scalar counterpart is P3 §2 (Neumann series / random walks with $K_z=2\nabla h_z\cdot\nabla+\Delta h_z-[Q^*aQ,h_z]$ of norm $O(M^{-1})$) and P2. For the O(N) formalization, the properties required of the propagators are exactly: (i) locality: each propagator has a representation as a sum over walks of localized operators, with exponentially decaying kernels, analytic in the decoupling parameters $s$ on $|s|\le e^{\kappa_1}$, and vanishing on cross-component kernels when $s(\sigma^c)=0$; (ii) the contraction estimates for the minimizer equations. These are stated as interfaces in E7 and would be the same interface in the scalar case. A Combes-Thomas / Neumann-series proof with a block decomposition would supply (i) (suggestion, not a claim); the locality at $s=0$ is a structural property that must be built into the construction, not derived from decay alone.
