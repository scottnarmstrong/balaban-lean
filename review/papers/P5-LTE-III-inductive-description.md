Summary. P4 gave an inductive description of the effective densities in the small-field case only. P5 extends the description to the full case, with large-field domains. It does four things.
1. It states seven inductive hypotheses H1-H7 (pp.488-497). They describe the densities $\rho_k$ as a sum over large-field regions $Z_k$ of (small-field exponential on $Z_k^c$) times a new term $\mathcal R^{(k)}$, with large-field densities $\rho'_k$ given by integral operators $\mathbb T_k$ and boundary terms $\mathcal B^{(k)}$.
2. It states the four main theorems of the whole series (pp.499-500). These say that the renormalization step $S^{(k)}T^{(k)}$ followed by a new large-field renormalization operation $R^{(k+1)}$ maps the space of densities (1.42) into itself with $k\to k+1$, $B\to BL^{d-2}$, $\bar\nu\to\bar\nu L^2$. They also say that a final stretch of $k_0$-to-end steps without $R$ keeps the hypotheses H1-H5, H7. Consequences for the generating functional are sketched (pp.500-501).
3. It carries out the transformation $T^{(k)}$ (Sect. 2, pp.501-513): new small-field region $\Omega_{k+1}$, new characteristic functions, decomposition of $\psi_k$ into approximate background plus fluctuation, rewriting of the action, change of variables, determinant term.
4. It classifies the contributions of the fluctuation integral and states localization-expansion propositions (Sect. 3, pp.513-521, Props. 3.1-3.4). Proofs are deferred.

Role. It takes the small-field machinery from P1 and P4, the variational problems from P2, and localization expansions of background functions and operators from P3. It supplies the statement of Theorems 1-4. Per p.501, "We prove Theorems 1,4 in the first two papers, Theorem 2 in the third paper, and Theorem 3 is a simple conclusion", where "first two" and "third" refer to the two following papers, which I read as P6 and P7 respectively (the abstract/intro of P5 says "this and the following two papers"; the paper-specific list in SOURCES.md is consistent with P6 = "Renormalization and localization expansions II", P7 = "The large field renormalization operation"). The Introduction (p.486) confirms that the third paper contains the operation $R^{(k)}$ and that "Sect. 4" of the third paper does the final integration. The paper says "in the following paper" (p.486) that the Sect. 4 final integration is in the third paper and the "forthcoming last paper... written together with Michael O'Carroll" (p.501) is the spin-wave/correlation-function paper (this I take to be P9, with P8 as its short companion). ## 1. Setting and notation

### 1.1 Lattices, scales, cubes
- Torus $T\subset\mathbb R^d$ (continuum), lattices $T^{(j)}$ at scale $L^j\eta$ with $\eta=L^{-k}$ on the scale-$k$ rescaled picture (p.488, $\|\cdot\|_{\Omega^*}$ with $\eta=L^{-k}$; $T_\eta$ the lattice of mesh $\eta$). $L$ is the blocking factor, $M$ the fixed integer size of "large cubes" (in units of unit cubes), $L^m=M$ with $m$ a positive integer (p.491). $\pi_j$ = partition of the torus into $M$-cubes of scale $\xi=L^{-j}$ ("large cubes"); $\pi'_k$ is a cover by $L^2M$-cubes (p.493).
- Distances in $|x|_\infty=\max_\mu|x_\mu|$, $\mathrm{dist}_j$ (p.487).
- Operations on domains: $X^\sim$ adds one layer of large cubes in the proper scale; $X^{\approx}$ adds a thick layer (p.487, (1.2)):
$$X^{\approx}=\bigcup\{\square\in\pi_j:\mathrm{dist}_j(\square,X)\le MR_{j-1}\}=X^{\sim([R_{j-1}]+1)}=\bigcup_{\square\in\pi_j,\square\subset X}R_j(\square),\quad R_{j-1}=R_0(\log a\beta_{j-1})^2,$$
$$R_j(\square)=\bigcup\{\square'\in\pi_j:\mathrm{dist}_j(\square',\square)\le MR_{j-1}\}.\quad (1.3)$$
$X^{\sim3}$, $X^{\approx2}$, $X^{\sim 3+\frac{L-1}2}$ etc. are used in Sect. 2.
- $\mathcal D_k$: class of localization domains (unions of large cubes of $\pi_k$, connected components). $\mathcal D_k(\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega^c)=\{X\in\mathcal D_k:$ every component of $\Omega^c$ is contained in $X$ or disjoint from $X\}$ (1.33). Modified linear size $d_k(X\,\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega^c)$ (1.34): $\inf\{\frac1M|\Gamma|_\eta:\Gamma$ connected tree graph in $X$ meeting every cube of the cover $\pi'_k$ that meets $X\cap\Omega\}$; equals $d_k(X)$ for $X\subset\Omega$. Bound (1.35): $\sum_{X\in\mathcal D_k(\mathrm{mod}\Omega^c):\square\subset X}e^{-d_k(X\mathrm{mod}\Omega^c)}<K_0$ for $\square\in\pi_k,\square\subset\Omega$.

### 1.2 Fields and actions (taken from P1/P4, restated)
- $\psi_k$ = spin fluctuation variable at scale $k$ (N-vector valued field on $T^{(k)}$), $h$ external (magnetic-type) field, $g$ source field; $\phi_k=\phi(\mathbb B_k(W_k);\tilde\psi_k)$, $\psi_k^{(j)}=\psi(\mathbb B_j(W_k),\mathbb B_k(W_k);\tilde\psi_k)$, $W_k=(Z_k^c)^{\approx2}$, $\tilde\psi_k=Q^*(T_1^{(k)},\mathbb B_k(W_k))\psi_k$ (1.4). These are the solutions of the variational problems of P2 ((1.6),(1.9),(4.14),(4.15) of [2]) restricted to the neighbourhood of the small-field region $Z_k^c$ with boundary conditions from $\mathbb B_k((Z_k^c)^{\approx2})$.
- $\mathbb B_k$ = generating set determined by a sequence of domains $\{\Omega_j\}$ satisfying (1.1)-(1.3) of P2 (here made more restrictive, Sect. 2). $\psi=\psi_j$ on $\Lambda_j=(\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+1})^{(j)}$, $\Omega_0=\Omega_1^\sim$, $\Omega_{k+1}=\emptyset$ for the $k$-step (p.492).
- Main action restricted to domain $\Omega$ (1.5):
$$A_k^*(\Omega,\mathbb B_k;\psi,\phi)=\tfrac12\langle\psi-Q(\mathbb B_k)\phi,a(\mathbb B_k)(\psi-Q(\mathbb B_k)\phi)\rangle_{\Omega\cap\mathbb B_k}+\tfrac12\|\partial^\eta\phi\|^2_{\Omega^*}+\tfrac{\lambda_k}8\||\phi|^2-1\|_\Omega^2+\tfrac{\nu_k}2\|\phi-h\|^2_\Omega,$$
with $\|\partial^\eta\phi\|_{\Omega^*}^2=\sum_{\langle x,x'\rangle\subset\Omega\cap T_\eta}|\partial^\eta\phi(\langle x,x'\rangle)|^2+\frac12\sum_{\langle x,x'\rangle\in\mathrm{st}(\Omega)}|\partial^\eta\phi|^2$, $\mathrm{st}(\Omega)=\{\langle x,x'\rangle: x\in\Omega\cap T_\eta,x'\in\Omega^c\cap T_\eta\}$ (boundary bonds counted with weight 1/2 on each side).
- Constants $\beta_k,a_k,\lambda_k,\nu_k$ (flow); $\beta_ka_k$, $\lambda_k$ evolve by the free flow (1.40) (see Sect. 6).
- Flow case split: $\nu_k\beta_k\le1$ (1.9) holds iff $k\le k_0$ (p.490). Then the $\nu_k$ term and $h$ are dropped from the variational problems (the configurations $\phi_k,\psi^{(j)}_k$ do not depend on $\nu_k,h$) and the $\nu_k$-term is treated as a perturbation. For $k>k_0$ the full variational problems are used. $k_0$ is the unique index with $\beta_{k_0}\nu_{k_0}>\frac78L^{-d}$, $\beta_{k_0+1}\nu_{k_0+1}<\frac98L^d$ (p.490). The "incomplete to complete" switch of configurations is absorbed into the renormalization at step $k_0$.
- Characteristic function of a cube (1.19): $\chi_k(\square)=\chi(\{|\psi_k-Q_k\phi_k(\square;\tilde\psi_k)|,|\partial^\eta\phi_k|,|\Delta^\eta\phi_k|,|\alpha_k|<\delta_k$ on $\square^\sim$, $|\phi_k-h|<\delta_k/\sqrt{\nu_k}$ on $\square^\sim$, $|h^2-1|<\delta_k^2/\sqrt{\nu_k}\})$, $\alpha_k=\frac{\lambda_k}2(\phi_k^2-1)$. For $k\le k_0$ the last two conditions are omitted and $\nu_k=0$.

### 1.3 Multi-scale structure for large-field densities (p.492, (1.20))
- Two decreasing sequences: large-field regions $Z_j$ and small-field regions $\mathbb C_j$; domains $\Omega_j$ on which the $j$-th fluctuation integral is performed; $\Omega''_j\subset\Omega_j$ on which the fluctuation variable is small and integrated; $\psi'$ unintegrated fluctuation variable on $(\Omega_j\setminus\Omega''_j)\cap T^{(j)}$. Inclusions:
$$\Omega_1\supset\Omega''_1\supset Z_1^c\supset\Omega_2\supset\cdots\supset\Omega_j\supset\Omega''_j\supset Z_j^c\supset\Omega_{j+1}\supset\cdots\supset\Omega_k\supset\Omega''_k\supset Z_k^c.\quad(1.20)$$
- Multi-index $\mathbb A_k$ includes $\mathbb B_k$, $\{\Omega''_j\}$, $\mathbb C_k$ and other region sequences introduced in Sect. 2 of P5 and Sect. 3 of the next paper (the full definition is deferred, p.493). All elements of $\mathbb A_k$ are determined by their restrictions to $Z_k$, so $\mathbb A_k$ can be localized to components: $\mathbb A_k\cap Z$.
- Combinatorial factors $C(\mathbb A_k\cap Z)$, with $\sum C^{-1}(\mathbb A_k\cap Z)\le1$ (1.27) and (1.28)
$$C(\mathbb A_k\cap Z)\le\exp\Big(\tfrac13\tfrac1{M^d}|Z_1\cap Z|_1+\tfrac53\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\tfrac1{M^d}|Z_j^c\cap Z_{j+1}|_\xi\Big).$$
- Integer function $K(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)\ge0$ = maximal number of additional renormalization steps for which (1.30) with $\ell=2$ would still hold if no new large-field restrictions are created near $Z$ (p.495-6). Full definition: deferred to Sect. 3 of the next paper (Prop. 5.2 there).

## 2. Main results

### 2.1 Inductive hypotheses H1-H7 (final form, with large-field structure)

All "(H.x) [4]" refer to P4's hypotheses (H.1)-(H.7) of the small-field description, themselves refining P1's. I do not have P4's hypothesis texts at hand beyond what P5 recalls. P5 states only the modifications.

**H1 (p.488, (1.1)).** A density has the form
$$\rho_k(\psi_k,h,g)=\sum_{Z_k}\rho_k(Z_k;\psi_k,h,g)=\sum_{Z_k}\rho'_k(Z_k;\psi_k,h,g)\,\chi_k(Z_k^c)\exp\big[\mathcal A_k(Z_k^c;\psi_k,h)+\mathcal F_k(Z_k^c;\psi_k,h,g)+\mathcal R^{(k)}(Z_k^c;\psi_k,h,g)\big],$$
sum over $Z_k$ such that each component of $Z_k^c$ belongs to $\mathcal D_k$. (Small-field case of P4 / P1: just one term, $Z_k=\emptyset$, without $\rho'$ and without $\mathcal R$.) The new feature relative to P1/P4: sum over large-field regions, large-field densities $\rho'_k$, extra term $\mathcal R^{(k)}$, characteristic functions localized to $Z_k^c$ and with modifications depending on $k\lessgtr k_0$.

**H2 (pp.489-490).** There exist positive $\beta_k,a_k,\lambda_k,\nu_k$ satisfying the conditions of (H.1) [4], constants $E_k,\alpha_0,\gamma$, and functions $\mathcal E^{(j)}(\psi_j,h)$, $j=1..k$, satisfying (H.2)-(H.5) [4] (formulated on whole lattices), such that
$$\mathcal A_k(Z_k^c;\psi_k,h)=-\beta_kA_k^*(Z_k^c;\psi_k,\phi_k)+\mathcal E_k(Z_k^c;\psi_k,h)-E_k|Z_k^c|,\quad(1.6)$$
$$\mathcal E_k(Z_k^c;\psi_k,h)=\sum_{j=1}^k\mathcal E^{(j)}(Z_k^c;\psi_k^{(j)}(\tilde\psi_k),h),\quad(1.7)$$
$$\mathcal E^{(j)}(Z_k^c;\psi_j,h)=\sum_{y\in Z_k^c\cap T^{(j)}}\ \sum_{X\in\mathcal D_j:\,y\in X\subset(Z_k^c)^\approx}\mathcal E^{(j)}(y,X;\psi_j,h)-\sum_{y\in Z_k^c\cap T^{(j)}}\sum_{x\in(Z_k^c)^\approx\cap T^{(j)}}\Big[\tfrac12v_1^{(j)}(y,x)(\psi_j^2(x)-\psi_j^2(y))+v_2^{(j)}(y,x)(\nu_jh\cdot(\psi_j)_0(x)-\nu_jh\cdot(\psi_j)_0(y))\Big].\quad(1.8)$$
$v_1^{(j)},v_2^{(j)}$ on $T^{(j)}\times T^{(j)}$ are determined from the whole-lattice $\mathcal E^{(j)}(y;\psi_j,h)$ by (3.106) of P1. Configurations $\phi_k,\psi_k^{(j)}$: if $\nu_k\beta_k\le1$ (1.9), omit the $\nu_k$ term in the variational problems ($\nu_k=0$); otherwise the full definitions of [4]. Comments (p.490): the key point is that $\mathcal E_k(Z_k^c)$ is the restriction of the whole-lattice $\mathcal E_k$ minus the second sum in (1.8); terms in (1.8) do not depend on the domain $Z_k^c$ containing $Y$ (a term of $\mathcal E^{(k+1)}(z,Y)$ must depend only on the action localized in $Y$). The second sum in (1.8) is zero on the whole lattice (trace of an antisymmetric matrix, P1) but not on a restricted domain, and is needed to cancel the relevant/marginal terms so that the uniform bounds (3.128),(3.129) of P1 hold for the whole $\mathcal E_k$.

**H3 (p.490, (1.10)-(1.13)).** There exist $\mathcal F^{(j)}(\psi_j,h,g)$, $j=1..k$, satisfying (H.7) [4], with
$$\mathcal F_k(Z_k^c;\psi_k,h,g)=\langle\chi_{Z_k^c}g,\phi_k\rangle_1+\sum_{j=1}^k\mathcal F^{(j)}(Z_k^c;\psi_k^{(j)}(\tilde\psi),h,g)=\langle\chi_{Z_k^c}g,\mathcal M_k(Z_k^c;\psi_k,h,g)\rangle_1,$$
$$\mathcal F^{(j)}(Z_k^c;\psi_j,h,g)=\langle\chi_{Z^c_k}g,\mathcal M^{(j)}(Z_k^c;\psi_j,h,g)\rangle_1,\quad\mathcal M^{(j)}(x,Z_k^c)=\sum_{X\in\mathcal D_j:x\in X\subset(Z_k^c)^\approx}\mathcal M^{(j)}(x,X),$$
$$\mathcal M_k(x,Z_k^c;\psi_k,h,g)=\phi_k(x;\tilde\psi_k)+\sum_{j=1}^k\mathcal M^{(j)}(x,Z_k^c;\psi_k^{(j)}(\tilde\psi_k),h,g).$$

**H4 (p.491).** There are functions $\mathcal R_n^{(k)}(\psi_k,h,g)$, $n=1..m$, real analytic on $\Xi_k(1,\epsilon_k)\times\{g:\|g\|_{\ell^1}<1\}$ on the whole lattice, with symmetry
$$\mathcal R_n^{(k)}(R\psi_k,Rh,Rg)=\mathcal R_n^{(k)}(\psi_k,h,g)\ (R\in O(N)),\quad\mathcal R_n^{(k)}(r\psi_k,h,rg)=\mathcal R_n^{(k)}(\psi_k,h,g)$$
for Euclidean transformations $r$ of the torus leaving invariant the lattice $T^{(k+n)}_{L^n}$ (equivalently the rescaled partition $\pi_{k-m+n}$ of $T$ into $L^n$-cubes). They have localization expansions of the usual form (H.4) [4] (locality, analyticity, symmetry) with the constant $E_0$ replaced by $\beta_k^{-n-1}$. The restricted function is $\mathcal R^{(k)}(Z_k^c)=\sum_{n=1}^m\mathcal R_n^{(k)}(Z_k^c)$, $\mathcal R_n^{(k)}(Z_k^c)=\sum_{X\in\mathcal D_k:X\subset Z_k^c}\mathcal R_n^{(k)}(X)$. These are like $\mathcal E^{(k)}$ but with reduced Euclidean symmetry, dependence on $g$, and not "pre-localized" (not sums over points $y\in T_1^{(k)}$). The symmetry improves as $n$ decreases, and each renormalization step decreases $n$ by 1. After $m$ steps (with $L^m=M$) terms have all properties of $\mathcal E^{(k)}$ and are absorbed in $\mathcal E^{(k)}$. The powers of $\beta_k^{-1}$ are arbitrary to some degree; higher powers impose stronger restrictions on $\beta_k$ (p.491-2).

**Characteristic function** (not numbered as hypothesis): $\chi_k(Z_k^c)=\prod_{\square\in\pi_k,\square\subset Z_k^c}\chi_k(\square)$ (1.18), with $\chi_k(\square)$ as in (1.19).

**H5 (p.493, (1.21)).** The large-field density has the form
$$\rho'_k(Z_k;\psi_k;h;g)=\sum_{\mathbb A_k}\mathbb T_k(Z_k,\mathbb A_k;\psi_k,h,g)\exp\mathcal B^{(k)}(Z_k,\mathbb A_k;\psi,\psi',h,g),$$
sum over admissible multi-indices; the integral operator $\mathbb T_k(Z_k,\mathbb A_k)$ acts on functions of $\psi,\psi'$ and gives functions of $\psi_k$, with a real nonnegative kernel depending on $\psi_k,\psi,\psi',g$ restricted to $U_k^\sim$, $U_k=Z_k^\approx$, and on $\mathbb A_k$. The kernel is invariant under orthogonal transformations of the vector-valued variables $\psi,\psi',h,g$ and under Euclidean transformations leaving invariant $T^{(k+m)}_M$ or the partition $\pi_k$ (applied also to all domains in $\mathbb A_k$); it is invariant under all those transformations. The operators are defined explicitly and inductively (compositions of renormalization transformations restricted to $\Omega_j^c$, unintegrated fluctuation integrals restricted to $(\Omega_j\setminus\Omega''_j)\cap\Lambda_j$, characteristic functions, small-field densities restricted to $Z_{j-1}^c\cap Z_j$); the explicit definition is not given here (p.493). Properties derived from the definition ("consequences, not hypotheses", p.494):
- Factorization in space (1.22): if $Z_k=Z'\cup Z''$, $U'=Z'^\approx$, $U''=Z''^\approx$, $U'^\sim\cap U''^\sim=\emptyset$, then $\mathbb T_k(Z_k,\mathbb A_k)=\mathbb T_k(Z',\mathbb A_k\cap Z')\mathbb T_k(Z'',\mathbb A_k\cap Z'')$ (commuting operators). This is crucial for $R^{(k+1)}$.
- Factorization in levels (1.23): after the last fluctuation integral, $\mathbb T_k(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)=\mathbb T^{(k-1)}(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap(Z\cap Z_{k-1}^c))\mathbb T_{k-1}(Z\cap Z_{k-1},\mathbb A_{k-1}\cap(Z\cap Z_{k-1}))$ (may be destroyed where $R^{(k+1)}$ acts).
- Domain of integration (1.24): in $\psi$ contained in
$$\bigcap_{j=0}^{k-1}\bigcap_{\square\subset\pi_{j+1},\square\subset\Omega_{j+1}^\sim\cap Z_{j+1}}\tilde\Psi_j(\mathbb B_k\cap\square^\sim;3dL\delta_j)\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^k\bigcap_{\square\in\pi_j,\square\subset Z^c_j\cap(\Omega_{j+1}^\sim)^c}\tilde\Psi_j(\square^\sim;3\delta_j)$$
($\Omega^\sim_{k+1}=\emptyset$; spaces $\tilde\Psi$ as in Sect. 1 of [2]); in $\psi'$ contained in $\{|\psi'_j|<\text{const}\cdot p_0(\beta_j)$ on $\Omega_j\setminus\Omega''_j\}$ (1.25).
- Bound (1.26): $\int d\psi_k\upharpoonright_Z|\mathbb T_k(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)F|\le\exp(-\kappa_k(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z))\sup\chi|F|$, $F$ bounded function of $\psi,\psi'$, $\chi$ = characteristic function of the domains (1.24),(1.25). The function $\kappa_k$ is defined along with the operator (built from small factors from large-field restrictions), definition deferred to Sect. 3 of the next paper.
- Summed form (1.29): $\sum_{\mathbb A_k\cap Z}\int d\psi_k\upharpoonright_Z|\mathbb T_kF|\le\sup C(\mathbb A_k\cap Z)e^{-\kappa_k}\cdot\sup\chi|F|$. The suprema are over **admissible** multi-indices (a special subset of all multi-indices satisfying (1.1)-(1.3) of [2], (1.20), and conditions linked to bounds of the product in (1.29)).
- Key assumption (1.30): there are an integer $p_2\ge2d+2$ and large constants $A_2,H_0$ such that for all admissible $\mathbb A_k\cap Z$, with $\ell=1$ or $2$,
$$C(\mathbb A_k\cap Z)\exp(-\kappa_k(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z))\le\exp\big(-\ell p_2(\beta_k)-H_k|Z|_\xi\big),\quad p_2(\beta_k)=A_2(\log\beta_k)^{p_2},H_k=H_0\log\beta_k.$$
$p_2,A_2$ are expressible via $p_0,A_0,L,M,K_0,K_1$ (e.g. $p_2=p_0-2d-2$, p.495); $H_0$ expressible in terms of $E_0$, $B_0$, used in one bound only in Sect. 3 of the next paper. Combined (1.31): $\sum_{\mathbb A_k\cap Z}\int d\psi_k\upharpoonright_Z|\mathbb T_kF|\le\exp(-\ell p_2(\beta_k)-H_k|Z|_\eta)\sup\chi|F|$. The author states that this inequality "cannot be taken as a basis of the inductive analysis" because it does not behave simply under induction steps; instead $K$ (H6) is used.

**H6 (p.496, (1.32)).** For $k\le k_0$ and each connected component $Z$ of $Z_k$,
$$K(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)>0$$
for all admissible multi-indices in the sum (1.21). Slightly stronger than (1.30), easier to control; the large-field renormalization $R^{(k+1)}$ preserves it. Also required for earlier steps: $K(Z_j\cap Z,\mathbb A_k\cap(Z_j\cap Z))>0$ for all $j\le k$; this is one of the admissibility conditions. For this part the author uses combinatorial arguments from [5d] and says it is "quite universal" and model independent.

**H7 (p.497, (1.36)-(1.39)).** Boundary term:
$$\mathcal B^{(k)}(Z_k,\mathbb A_k;\psi,\psi',g)=\sum_{X\in\mathcal D_k(\mathrm{mod}\,Z_k^c):X\cap Z_k\ne\emptyset,X\cap Z_k^c\ne\emptyset}\mathcal B^{(k)}(X,\mathbb A_k;\psi,\psi',g),\quad(1.38)$$
(the printed text of the sum condition is "$X\cap Z_k\ne\emptyset$, $X\cap Z_k^c\neq\emptyset$"; read the formula (1.38) on p.497 for exact subscript, UNCERTAIN in OCR of the subscript on $\mathcal D_k$ which reads mod$\,\Omega_k^c$ in the printed formula), terms with the usual locality properties w.r.t. $\psi,\psi',g$, with analytic extensions to the spaces $\Xi^c(X,\mathbb B_k;\{\epsilon_j\})$ (1.36), the $\psi'$ domain (1.37) and $\{g:\|g\|_{\ell^1}<1\}$, satisfying
$$|\mathcal B^{(k)}(X,\mathbb A_k;\psi,\psi',g)|<B_0\exp(-\kappa\,d_k(X\,\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega_k^c)),\quad(1.39)$$
with $B_0$ sufficiently large, and the symmetry properties of H5. $B_0$ plays the role of $E_0$.
Analyticity space (1.36), for $X\in\mathcal D_k(\mathrm{mod}\Omega^c_k)$:

## 3. Proof architecture

### 3.0 Introduction (pp.485-488): the picture
The first transformation $T=T^{(0)}$ (P1 (1.11),(1.13)) is applied after splitting the domain of integration into subdomains defined by small-field conditions (1.15) of P1 on blocks, bonds and points of the unit lattice, e.g. $|\psi(y)-(Q\phi)(y)|<\beta_0^{-1/2}p_0(\beta_0)$ vs. $\ge$; $|(\partial\phi)(b)|<\beta_0^{-1/2}p_0(\beta_0)$ vs. $\ge$. Union of large cubes with large-field conditions = $Z_1$ (large-field region), complement $Z_1^c$ small-field. On $Z_1$ integration is left undone; on $Z_1^c$ the P4 operations are performed (fluctuation integral generating an effective action, renormalization). Next step: apply $T^{(1)}$ only on small-field regions, redo the decomposition on $Z_1^c$, obtain new large-field regions which are united with the neighbourhood of $Z_1$ to give $Z_2$ etc. The one new difficulty: the densities on large-field regions are controlled only by small factors from large-field restrictions; if no new restrictions appear near a component for many steps, the control is lost. The remedy is the new operation $R^{(k)}$ (third paper), to improve bounds on large-field densities. The author compares this to phase-space-cell analysis (Feldman-Magnen-Rivasseau-Seneor, Abdessalam-Rivasseau, Rivasseau) but multi-scale on many scales rather than one-scale (Brydges-Dimock-Hurd).

### 3.1 Section 1: inductive hypotheses and main theorems (pp.488-501)
Mechanism and differences from P1/P4 (see Sect. 2.1 above). Key structural points:
- **The $\mathcal E_k$ restriction (1.8)** with the added second sum: necessary because bounds on the whole lattice quantities ($\mathcal E_k$ of P4) are needed, but the actions are localized on $Z_k^c$.
- **The flow choice $c_{k+1}=0,d_{k+1}=b_{k+1}$ (p.498, (1.40)).** Of the several admissible choices of the P4 RG constants $c_{k+1},d_{k+1}$, here the "free flow" is chosen:
$$\beta_{j+1}a_{j+1}=\beta_{j+1,u}a_{j+1,u}=\beta_jL^{d-2}\frac{aa_j}{a_j+aL^{-2}},\quad\lambda_{j+1}=\lambda_{j+1,u}=\lambda_jL^2.$$
With $a_j=\frac{1-L^{-2k}}{1-L^{-2j}}a_k$ and $a=a_1=\frac{1-L^{-2k}}{1-L^{-2}}a_k$, one gets $a_{j+1,u}=\frac{1-L^{-2(k+1)}}{...}$ (formula in text, p.498) and the scaling relation
$$\beta_ja=\beta_ka_kL^{-(k-j)(d-2)}\frac{1-L^{-2k}}{1-L^{-2}}=\beta_ka(L^j\eta)^{d-2},\quad(1.41)$$
where $a$ is determined by $a_k$ (the constant $a$ in $T^{(j)}$ really depends on $j$ via a double index, "slightly unprecise old notation"). This allows all composition formulas of Sect. 4 of [2] to be used in the analysis of $R^{(k+1)}$. Also $p_0(\beta_j)=A_0(\log\beta_ja)^{p_0}$, $\delta_j=(\beta_ja)^{-1/2}p_0(\beta_j)$ are defined with $\beta_ja$ rather than $\beta_j$, which makes scale comparisons simple.
- **Case split at $k_0$.** For $k\le k_0$ ($\nu_k\beta_k\le1$) the field $h$ is not part of the variational problem. For $k>k_0$ it is. Only $\sim\log\beta_{k_0}$ steps follow the large-field renormalization (Theorem 4).
- **Product structure of $\mathbb T_k$ (H5)**: factorization (1.22)/(1.23), bounds (1.26)-(1.31), integer $K$ (H6). The design principle: (1.30) is the quantity one wants but it is not stable under induction; $K>0$ (the counter) is stable and implies (1.30) (Prop. 5.2 of the next paper, per p.495).
- **Multi-scale analyticity (1.36)-(1.37)** for boundary terms.

Difference with P1/P4 small-field version, summarised:
1. Sum over large-field regions $Z_k$ of $\rho'_k\chi_k\exp[\ldots]$ vs. single exponential. Actions, $\chi_k$, $\mathcal F_k$ localized on $Z_k^c$ with neighbourhoods $(Z_k^c)^{\approx}$, $(Z_k^c)^{\approx2}$.
2. New term $\mathcal R^{(k)}=\sum_{n=1}^m\mathcal R_n^{(k)}$ with reduced symmetry (Euclidean symmetry only w.r.t. coarser lattices $T^{(k+n)}_{L^n}$), $g$-dependent, bounds in terms of $\beta_k^{-n-1}$ rather than $E_0$.
3. The restricted action has the extra sum in (1.8) so $\mathcal E_k$ differs from the whole-lattice $\mathcal E_k$ by boundary-supported cancelling terms.
4. Case split $k\le k_0$ / $k>k_0$ built into the definitions of configurations and characteristic functions.
5. H5-H7 are entirely new (large-field densities, integral operators, boundary terms, counter $K$).
6. Weaker H6 in the image of $S^{(k)}T^{(k)}$; the extra operation $R^{(k+1)}$.
