## 1. Setting and notation

General setting (Sects. 1-3). A finite (or countable) set $\Lambda$ of "sites"; objects $X_w=\{1,2,\dots\}$ labelled by integers interacting via a Gibbs factor $\psi(X)=\exp(-V(X))$ on finite subsets. Usually $V$ is two-body (2.1): $V(X)=\frac12\sum_{ij\in X}v_{ij}$ (sum over ordered pairs including $i=j$, self-energy $v_{ii}$). $\mu$ is a finite measure on a space $\Omega$ (a product measure $d\mu(\phi)$ in the spin setting). $|X|$ is cardinality; on $\mathbb Z^d$ distances use the max norm (p. 135).

Connected parts $\psi_c$ (p. 135-136): $\psi_c(X)=\psi(X)$ for $|X|=1$; otherwise defined recursively by
$$\psi(X)=\sum_{\Pi\in\text{partitions of }X}\ \prod_{Y\in\Pi}\psi_c(Y).\tag{2.2}$$
Grand-canonical Mayer series (2.3a)-(2.3b): $Z=\sum_N\frac1{N!}\int d\mu(x_1)\cdots d\mu(x_N)e^{-V(x_1..x_N)}$, $\log Z=\sum_N\frac1{N!}\int d^N\mu\,(e^{-V})_c$ "as formal power series in $z$" ($d\mu\to z\,d\mu$); nothing is claimed about convergence at this point.

Spin systems on a finite lattice: $Z=\int\prod_{x\in\Lambda}d\mu(\phi_x)\psi(\Lambda)=\sum_\Pi\prod_{Y\in\Pi}\int d^Y\mu\,\psi_c(Y)$. With the normalisation (2.4) $\int d\mu(\phi)e^{-V(Y)}=1$ for $|Y|=1$ one gets the **polymer system** (2.5a)
$$\int d^\Lambda\mu\,\psi(\Lambda)=\sum_{N}\frac1{N!}\sum_{\substack{Y_1,\dots,Y_N\subset\Lambda\\ \text{disjoint}}}K(Y_1)\cdots K(Y_N),\qquad K(Y)=\int d^Y\mu\,\psi_c(Y)\ (|Y|>1),\ K(Y)=0\ (|Y|=1)\tag{2.5b}$$
(p. 136-137). Polymers $Y$ are arbitrary finite subsets, the hard-core interaction is $v'(X,Y)=0$ if $X\cap Y=\emptyset$, $=\infty$ otherwise (2.6), and (2.7)
$$\log\int d^\Lambda\mu\,\psi(\Lambda)=\sum_N\frac1{N!}\sum_{Y_1..Y_N}K(Y_1)\cdots K(Y_N)\,\psi'_c(Y_1,\dots,Y_N),$$
where $\psi'_c$ is the connected part of the hard-core Gibbs factor $e^{-V'}$. So Brydges' $\psi'_c(Y_1..Y_N)$ is exactly the "Ursell function" $\rho^T(Y_1,\dots,Y_N)$ of P6 (4.50).

Stability and decoupling (p. 138): $V$ is **stable** if $V(X)\ge0$ for all $X$ ("the standard definition allows $-\text{const}|X|$; ours is not less general since diagonal parts can be adjusted"); $V(X,s)=\frac12\sum v_{ij}s_{ij}$ ($s_{ij}=s_{ji}\in[0,1]$, self-energy terms unchanged) is a **convex decoupling** of $V(X)$ if it is a convex linear combination of potentials of the form $\sum_i V(Y_i)$ with $\bigcup Y_i=X$, $Y_i$ disjoint. A convex decoupling of a stable potential is stable.

Gaussian expansion setting (Sect. 4, p. 143-144). $d\mu(\phi)$ a normalised Gaussian measure on $\phi=(\phi_x)_{x\in\Lambda}$ with mean 0 and covariance $C_{xy}$; $\Lambda=\bigcup_{x'\in\Lambda'}x'$ partitioned into blocks; $F(\Lambda)=\prod_{x'\in\Lambda'}F_{x'}(\Phi_{x'})$ with $\Phi_{x'}=(\phi_x)_{x\in x'}$, normalised $\int d\mu\,F_{x'}=1$ (4.1). $\Delta_{x'y'}=\sum_{x\in x',y\in y'}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi_x}C_{xy}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi_y}$ (4.4); $d\mu_s$ the Gaussian with covariance $C_{xy}s_{xy}$, $s_{xy}=s_{x'y'}$ for $x\in x',y\in y'$ and $s_{xy}=1$ if $x,y$ lie in the same block.

Erice expansion setting (Sect. 5, p. 149ff). $d\mu=Z_0^{-1}d^\Lambda\phi\,e^{-S_0}$, $S_0=\frac12\sum A_{xy}(\phi_x-\phi_y)^2+\frac12m^2\sum\phi_x^2-\sum h_x\phi_x$ (p. 150), $A_{xy}\ge0$; $A_{xy}(s)=s_{x'y'}A_{xy}$ for $x\in x',y\in y'$, $x'\ne y'$, unchanged within a block; $d\mu_s$ obtained by replacing $A$ by $A(s)$ in numerator AND denominator (so the normalisation $Z_0(\Lambda,s)$ also depends on $s$, and gets differentiated: this "truncates" expectations, p. 156). $\Gamma\subset\{x'y'\}$ a graph of block bonds, $s_\Gamma$ = the vector with $s_{x'y'}=0$ off $\Gamma$, $\partial^\Gamma=\prod_{x'y'\in\Gamma}\partial/\partial s_{x'y'}$, $X(\Gamma)$ = union of blocks linked by $\Gamma$.

## 2. Main results

Everything below is as stated in the notes. "Proof" column: P = proved in text, S = sketch, E = left as exercise, R = referred to elsewhere.

**Thm 3.1 (p. 138-139), tree formula for the connected part [Battle-Federbush]** (P: Appendix A (formal part) and the "essential step" argument p. 139; the many-body version proved in Appendix B in outline).
For each tree graph $T$ on $X$ there is a probability measure $dp_T(s)$ on $s=(s_{ij})_{i,j\in X}$, $s_{ij}=s_{ji}\in[0,1]$, independent of $V$, such that for every (two-body) $V$
$$(e^{-V(X)})_c=\sum_{T\text{ on }X}\ \prod_{ij\in T}(-v_{ij})\int dp_T(s)\,e^{-V(X,s)},\qquad V(X,s)=\tfrac12\sum_{ij\in X}v_{ij}s_{ij},$$
and $dp_T$ is supported on $s$ for which $V(X,s)$ is a convex decoupling of $V(X)$ (equivalently, for every $V$). Printed as $(-v_{ij})_I$ in the first line; the subscript "I" is OCR noise. The identity is first proved with possibly signed measures via the graph expansion (3.1): $(e^{-V(X)})_c=\sum_{G\text{ connected on }X}\prod_{ij\in G}(e^{-v_{ij}}-1)\prod_{i\in X}e^{-v_{ii}/2}$ (Appendix A, proof (A.1)-(A.2)). Then positivity/normalisation of $dp_T$: compare the formula for a potential $V^{(T)}$ supported on a fixed tree with the graph formula (3.3), put $v_{ij}=\varepsilon$ on $T$, and compare leading orders in $\varepsilon$ to get $1=\int dp_T$ (p. 139). The positivity of $dp_T$ is attributed to Battle and Federbush [8, 9] ("suppose the representation of Theorem 3.1 holds for some measures which are possibly not probability measures..."); the text does not prove positivity separately, it argues from the "essential step" paraphrased on p. 139. (This is a sketch: the claim that $dp_T$ is a positive measure supported on convex decouplings is taken from [8, 9].)

**Cor 3.2 (p. 139), tree-graph inequalities** (P, immediate).
(a) $V$ stable $\Rightarrow$ $|(e^{-V(X)})_c|\le\sum_{T\text{ on }X}\prod_{ij\in T}|v_{ij}|$.
(b) $V$ repulsive ($v_{ij}\ge0$ all $i,j$, $v_{ii}=0$) $\Rightarrow$ $|(e^{-V(X)})_c|\le\sum_{T\text{ on }X}\prod_{ij\in T}|e^{-v_{ij}}-1|$.
Proof (b): replace $V(X,s)$ by $V^{(T)}(X,s)$ in the right side of Thm 3.1 (makes it bigger) and apply (3.3). (b) is attributed to older work [10, 11] (Rota-Wahrsch, Penrose).

**Thm 3.3 (p. 140), convergence of the Mayer expansion (2.3b)** (P, via (3.4)-(3.6)). The expansion converges if either (a) $V$ is stable and $e\sup_x\int d\mu(y)|v(x,y)|<1$, or (b) $V$ is repulsive and $e\sup_x\int d\mu(y)|e^{-v(x,y)}-1|<1$. Proof: Cor 3.2(a), Cayley's number of trees $N^{N-2}$, stripping outermost branches with $q:=\sup_x\int d\mu(y)|v(x,y)|$ (3.5), giving $|\log Z|$ bounded termwise by $\frac{N^{N-2}}{N!}\mu(\Omega)q^{N-1}$ (3.6), summable iff $qe<1$ (Stirling). In (3.6) the printed text reads "$|\log Z|\le Z\,\frac{N^{N-2}}{N!}\mu(\Omega)q^{N-1}$"; the leading "Z" is garbled (presumably $\sum_N$ and no $Z$). The text says Case (b) does not transfer to the polymer Mayer expansion (2.7) because its transcription (3.8), $e\sup_{x}\sum_{Y\cap x\neq\emptyset}|K(Y)||e^{-v(x,Y)}-1|<1$, can never hold for large $\Lambda$ (the sup over a hard core counts all polymers meeting a point and the volume).

**Improved counting (p. 141), "extra factorials"** (P; Appendix C). Sum over trees first at fixed incidence numbers $d_1..d_N$:
$$\Big|\sum_T(\cdot)\Big|\le\sum_{d}\frac{(N-2)!}{(d_1-1)!\cdots(d_N-1)!}\sup_{T,\,d\text{ fixed}}|(\cdot)|\tag{3.9}$$
(Cayley refined by degrees; the factors $(d_i-1)!$ are the Federbush-Battle "extra factorials" [14]). With it the criterion becomes
$$Q:=\sum_{d=0}^\infty\frac1{d!}\sup_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{Y\ni x}|K(Y)|\,|Y|^d<1\tag{3.10a}$$
(the factors $|Y|$ count the overlaps; translation-invariant case) $\sup_x\sum_{Y\ni x}|K(Y)|e^{|Y|}<1$ (3.10b). Remark p. 141: the $e^{|Y|}$ is "qualitatively" optimal: it is the partition function of small polymers inside a big polymer; the constant can be improved (ref. [15] p. 110, Fredenhagen).

**Thm 3.4 (p. 142, "implicit in [13]" = Cammarota), logarithm of a polymer gas** (S: proved in Appendix C by tree stripping, with the contribution of a fixed tree displayed in Fig. 5). Let
$$Z=\sum_N\frac1{N!}\sum_{\substack{X_1,\dots,X_N\subset\Lambda\\ \text{disjoint}}}K(X_1)\cdots K(X_N).$$
Then
$$\log Z=\sum_N\frac1{N!}\sum_{X_1..X_N\subset\Lambda}K(X_1)\cdots K(X_N)\,\psi_c(X_1,\dots,X_N)=:\sum_N I_N,\qquad |I_N|\le\frac1N\,Q^N|\Lambda|,$$
with $Q$ from (3.10). (The hard-core connected part is written $\psi_c$ here, $\psi'_c$ in (2.7).) Hypotheses: $\Lambda$ finite, $K$ arbitrary (complex) with $Q<\infty$. NOT stated: that $\sum_NI_N=\log Z$ as a convergent identity when $Q<1$ (the display is an identity of formal power series in $K$; the analytic identification for $Q<1$, i.e. $Z\ne0$ and the series converges to a branch of the log, is left implicit; UNCERTAIN whether Brydges regards it as obvious). Proof idea (App. C, p. 180-181): the tree graph in (C.1), $\prod_{ij\in T}(e^{-v_{ij}}-1)$ with $v_{ij}=\infty$ if $Y_i\cap Y_j\ne\emptyset$, forces polymers joined by a line of $T$ to overlap; sum over leaves first: $\sup_{y}\sum_{Y_3\ni y}|K(Y_3)||Y_3|^{d_3-1}=:Q(d_3)$, passing to the neighbour by summing the base point $y_3\in Y_2$ ($\to$ factor $|Y_2|$); the last polymer gives $|\Lambda|Q(d_1+1)\prod_{i\ge2}Q(d_i)$, then sum over $d_1..d_N$ with (3.9) gives $Q^N|\Lambda|/N$. Several steps ("this bound leads directly to (3.10a) and Thm 3.4") are stated, not written out.

**Thm 3.5 (p. 143), polymers with constituents** (E: "Exercise. Prove theorem 3.5"). Polymer $X=(x_1,\dots,x_p)\in(\Omega,\mu)$, $p$ arbitrary; pair interaction $v'(X,Y)=\sum_{x\in X,y\in Y}v(x,y)$ inherited from a **stable** $v$; $\psi'(X_1..X_N)=\exp(-V'(X_1..X_N))$; $d\mu'$ a measure on the space of polymers; $Z=\sum_N\frac1{N!}\int d\mu'(X_1)\cdots d\mu'(X_N)\psi'(X_1..X_N)$. Then $\log Z=\sum_N\frac1{N!}\int d^N\mu'\,\psi'_c$ with
$$\frac1{N!}\int d^N\mu'\,|\psi'_c|\le\frac1N\Big(Q\sup_x\int d\mu(y)|v(x,y)|\Big)^{N-1}\mu(\Omega),\qquad Q:=\sum_d\frac1{d!}\sup_x\int_{X'\ni x}d\mu'(X)|X|^d,$$
and if $V$ is repulsive $v$ may be replaced by $|e^{-v}-1|$ in the bound. (The printed prefactor reads "$\frac1{N'}$"; I read $\frac1N$ because of the exponent $N-1$ and Thm 3.4; UNCERTAIN.) Used by Gopfert-Mack [16, 17] for iterated (two-stage) Mayer expansions.

**Sect. 4, Theorem-like statement (4.2)-(4.4) (p. 144-145)**, expansion about a Gaussian (P for polynomial $F_{x'}$, then "by continuity" for $C^\infty$ functions with exponential bounds on derivatives; not rigorous as stated). With the setting of §1,
$$Z(\Lambda)=\int d\mu(\phi)F(\Lambda')=\sum_N\frac1{N!}\sum_{\substack{Y'_1..Y'_N\subset\Lambda'\\ \text{disjoint}}}K(Y'_1)\cdots K(Y'_N),$$
$$K(Y')=0\ \ (|Y'|=1),\qquad K(Y')=\sum_{T\text{ on }Y'}\int dp_T(s)\int d\mu_s\prod_{x'y'\in T}\Delta_{x'y'}\,F(Y')\ \ (|Y'|>1).$$
Proof: for polynomial $F$, $\int d\mu\,F=\exp(\frac12\sum_{x'y'\in\Lambda'}\Delta_{x'y'})F|_{\phi=0}$ (4.5) (heat-kernel-of-Laplacian identity), set $\frac12\sum\Delta_{x'y'}=:\psi(\Lambda)$ in the language of (2.2), take connected parts of the operator exponential (operators commute), use Thm 3.1, and re-express $\exp(\frac12\sum s_{x'y'}\Delta_{x'y'})$ as $\int d\mu_s$. The Gaussian measure $d\mu$ is NOT localised here: the covariance $C_{xy}$ is the full one (so the polymers still interact through $C$), only the cross-block parts of it are scaled by $s$.

**Sect. 4, simple-site convergence statement (p. 145-146), single-site blocks** (S). Assume (4.1), (I) $|\partial^rF_x(\phi)|\le\lambda^rr!$ for all $x$, $r\ge1$, all real $\phi$; (II) $\sup_x\sum_y|C_{xy}|\le c$. Then for $\lambda$ sufficiently small (depending on $c$) $Q<1$, hence by Thm 3.4 $\log Z(\Lambda)$ has a convergent expansion for every $|\Lambda|$. The proof (p. 145-146) uses $|K(Y)|\le\sum_{T\text{ on }Y}\prod_{xy\in T}|C_{xy}|\prod_{x\in Y}(\lambda^{d_x}d_x!)$ (Wick/integration by parts), then $Q\le\sum_{N\ge2}\frac1{N-1}C^{N-1}e^N\sum_{d_1..d_N}\prod(\lambda^{d_i}d_i)$. "From this it is clear that $Q<1$ if $\lambda$ is small depending on $C$." Variant (4.6)-(4.7): if $|\partial^rF_x|\le\lambda^r(r!)^p$ with $p>1$ the argument needs decay of $C_{xy}$: with $\Lambda\subset\mathbb Z^d$, $|C_{xy}|<Ce^{-m|x-y|}$ one has for any $p$: $\sum_{\text{distinct }y_1..y_{d_x}}\prod_k|C_{xy_k}|\le C_p^{d_x}(d_x!)^{-p}$ (4.7) ("easy to prove").

**Exercise (p. 147), block-wise Gaussian expansion.** Conditions $|\partial^rF_{x'}|\le\lambda^{|r|}r!$ for multi-indices $r$, $\sup_{x'}\sum_{y'}\sup_{x\in x',y\in y'}|C_{xy}|\le C_2$, $eC_2\lambda(1-\lambda)^{-|x'|}\le1$, $\int d\mu F_{x'}=1$ are claimed sufficient for convergence of the block-wise expansion (not proved here).

**Sect. 4, continuum $P(\phi)_2$ outline (p. 147-148), (4.8)** (S, very sketchy; refers to [3] pp. 156-157, 196). $|K(Y)|\le\sum_T\prod_{x'y'\in T}e^{-\mathrm{dist}(x',y')}\prod_{x'\in Y'}(d_{x'}!)^pe^{c|Y'|}$ via Cauchy-Schwarz, Neumann-Dirichlet bracketing (the convex decoupling lifts to $(1-\Delta)^{-1}$ bracketing), Wick's theorem; conclusion $Q<1$ for small coefficients of $P$ (large negative $c$). "An exercise with Cayley's theorem and (4.7)."

**Exercise (p. 149), sine-Gordon type** pressure expansion: $Z=\int d\mu\,e^{-\lambda V}$, $V=\sum_xv(\phi_x)$ with bounded analytic $v$ in a strip and $\sup_x\sum_y|C_{xy}|<\infty$; claim $|\Lambda|^{-1}\log Z$ has a convergent Taylor series in $\lambda$ uniformly in $\Lambda$. Remark: truncated expectations bounded by $|\langle V^{(1)},..,V^{(N)}\rangle|\le\sum_T\sup_s\int d\mu_s\prod_{ij\in T}\Delta_{ij}V^{(1)}\cdots V^{(N)}$.

**Sect. 5, the Erice expansion**, (5.2)-(5.3b) (p. 150-152) (P, short proof). With the setting of §1, normalising each $F(x')$ so that $Z(x')=1$ (hence $Z(\Lambda,\mathbf0)=1$):
$$Z(\Lambda)=Z(\Lambda,\mathbf0)\sum_N\frac1{N!}\sum_{\substack{\Gamma_1..\Gamma_N\\ \text{disjoint}}}K(\Gamma_1)\cdots K(\Gamma_N),\qquad K(\Gamma)=0\text{ if }\Gamma\text{ is disconnected},\tag{5.2}$$
$$K(\Gamma)=\int ds_\Gamma\,\partial^\Gamma Z(\Lambda,s_\Gamma)\Big/Z(\Lambda,\mathbf0),\quad\text{and for connected }\Gamma:\ K(\Gamma)=\int ds_\Gamma\,\partial^\Gamma Z(X(\Gamma),s_\Gamma)\Big/Z(X(\Gamma),\mathbf0).\tag{5.3a,b}$$
Proof: fundamental theorem of calculus in each $s_{x'y'}$ gives $Z(\Lambda)=1+\sum_\Gamma\int ds_\Gamma\partial^\Gamma Z(\Lambda,s_\Gamma)$ (sum over bond subsets $\Gamma$); if $\Gamma$ splits into connected components $\Gamma_i$ then $\partial^\Gamma Z$ factorises as $\prod_i\partial^{\Gamma_i}Z(\Lambda,s_{\Gamma_i})$ since the measure factorises when the $s$ across missing bonds vanish; and $Z(x')=1$ removes the rest (the "disjoint" of (5.2) means: components with no common block). Locality (5.3b): all $s_{x'y'}$ with $x'y'\notin\Gamma$ vanish so numerator and denominator factorise over $X(\Gamma)$ and its complement. Remarks (p. 152): the expansion localises more than Sect. 4 because all covariances are localised by Neumann boundary conditions on $\partial X(\Gamma)$; the price is a more complicated convergence proof; $d\mu$, $d\mu_s$ need not be Gaussian (any measure that factorises when the $s$ vanish suffices; Gaussianity is used for integration by parts to evaluate the $s$-derivatives).

**Normalisation factor (5.4)-(5.5) (p. 153)**: $Z_0(\Lambda)=Z_0(\Lambda,\mathbf0)\exp(\sum_\Gamma W(\Gamma))$, $W(\Gamma)=\int ds_\Gamma\,\partial_s^\Gamma\log Z_0(\Lambda,s\Gamma)$ [the printed "$s\mathbf r$" is OCR for $s_\Gamma$], $W(\Gamma)=0$ if $\Gamma$ is disconnected; for $\Gamma=\{b_1..b_N\}$, $W(\Gamma)=\int ds_\Gamma\langle\partial_{s_{b_1}}S_0;\dots;\partial_{s_{b_N}}S_0\rangle_{s_\Gamma}$ (truncated expectation of Gaussian "bond" energies $I_{x'y'}=\frac12\sum A_{xy}(\phi_x-\phi_y)^2$ (p. 156), a sum of closed-loop and open-line Feynman graphs, equivalently random walks visiting every bond of $\Gamma$ and returning).

**(5.6) (p. 154), bound for $W$** (R: BBIJ [20], sects. 12-13). For $A_{xy}=1$ nearest neighbours, $m>0$, blocks of side $L$, $mL\gg1$, $M:=\sup\{|\langle\phi_x\rangle_s|:s,x\text{ next to a face crossed by }\Gamma\}$:
$|W(\Gamma)|\le(1+M^2)\exp(-c_dmL|\Gamma|)$ if $|\Gamma|>d$; $\le c_mL^{d-1}(1+M^2)$ if $|\Gamma|\le d$.

**(5.11) (p. 155), main Erice bound** (R/S: "We refer the reader to ref. [20] sect. 14 for a detailed proof"; principles on pp. 155-156). Assumptions (5.7)-(5.10): $\Lambda\subset\mathbb Z^d$, $m>0$, $A_{xy}=1$ for nearest neighbours; $F_{x'}$ small in the sense $\sup_\Phi|\partial^r_\Phi F_{x'}|\le\lambda^{|r|}(|r|!)^p\prod_{x\in x'}c_1\exp|c_2\phi_x|$ (5.8) for $r\ne0$; $\sup_x|h(x)|e^{-m\,\mathrm{dist}(x,\text{faces crossed by }\Gamma)}\le1$ (5.9); $mL\gg1$ (5.10). Conclusion:
$$\Big|\int ds_\Gamma\,\partial^\Gamma Z(X(\Gamma),s_\Gamma)\Big|\le\lambda\,e^{-c_3|\Gamma|+c_4|X(\Gamma)|}\tag{5.11}$$
with $c_3$ arbitrarily large "by first taking the blocks large and then taking $\lambda$ small" (the printed constant "A" before the exponential is $\lambda$, OCR). To get a bound on $K(\Gamma)$ divide by $Z(X(\Gamma),\mathbf0)$ (a product of $Z(x')$, bounded uniformly in $\lambda$). Principles of proof (p. 155-156): the left side is bounded by $\sup_s|\int d\mu_{s_\Gamma}F(X(\Gamma))\prod_{x'y'\in\Gamma}(;I_{x'y'})|$ with truncation in the $I$'s; small by (1) smallness of derivatives of $F$, (2) large $h_x$ only far from $\Gamma$, (3) large blocks, so the $I$ factors are far apart on scale $m^{-1}$ and connected graphs of $I$'s pay exponentially small covariances; non-polynomial $F$ treated by integration by parts $\langle G\phi_x\rangle=\langle G\rangle\langle\phi_x\rangle+\sum_y\langle\phi_x;\phi_y\rangle\langle\partial G/\partial\phi_y\rangle$.

**Grouping principle (p. 157-159)** (P modulo estimates). Polymers labelled by $(\Gamma,s)$ ($s$ = "species"): $Z=\sum_N\frac1{N!}\sum_{(\Gamma_i,s_i)}\prod K(\Gamma_i,s_i)$. To a configuration associate the smallest union of blocks $C$ containing all blocks linked by some $\Gamma$; decompose into maximal components $C_1..C_M$ connected through faces, edges, corners ("no two $C_i$ touch"):
$$Z=\sum_M\frac1{M!}\sum_{\substack{C_1..C_M\subset\Lambda'\\ \text{no two touch}}}\bar K(C_1)\cdots\bar K(C_M),\quad\bar K(C):=\sum_N\frac1{N!}\sum_{(\Gamma_i,s_i)\text{ compatible with }C}\prod K(\Gamma_i,s_i).$$
Compatibility conditions (hard cores, species restrictions) are absorbed into the definition of $\bar K(C)$; finite-range interactions of range $<L$ are allowed. Needed: $|\bar K(C)|\le e^{-\bar c|C'|}$ with $\bar c$ large. Proof sketch: with $K_\alpha(\Gamma,s)=e^{\alpha|\Gamma|}|K(\Gamma,s)|$, $\alpha$ chosen so that $\inf\{\sum|\Gamma_i|\}\alpha\ge(\bar c+1)|C'|$ (a compatible family covers $C$, so $\sum|\Gamma_i|$ is large when $C$ is), one gets $|\bar K(C)|\le e^{-(\bar c+1)|C'|}Z(K\to K_\alpha)$ and $Z(K\to K_\alpha)\le\exp(|C'|\sup_{x'\in C'}\sum_{\Gamma,s:\Gamma\text{ links }x'}K_\alpha)$, so it suffices that
$$\sup_{x'}\sum_{\Gamma,s:\ \Gamma\text{ links }x'}|K(\Gamma,s)|e^{\alpha|\Gamma|}\le1\tag{5.13}$$
for large $\alpha$ (depending on $\bar c$). **Lattice counting facts (p. 159)** quoted from Glimm-Jaffe-Spencer [19] p. 219: the number of bond graphs $\Gamma$ with $|\Gamma|$ fixed linking a given block is $<\exp(\mathrm{const}\,|\Gamma|)$, and the number of connected unions $X'$ of blocks with $|X'|$ fixed containing a given block is $<\exp(\mathrm{const}\,|X'|)$. Hence $|K(\Gamma)|\le e^{-c|\Gamma|}$ with $c$ large implies (5.13), and $|K(X')|\le e^{-c|X'|}$ with $c$ large implies the Mayer convergence criterion $\sup_{x'}\sum_{X'\ni x'}|K(X')|e^{|X'|}<1$ (the lattice-polymer version (3.10b)).

**Higgs-type example, (5.14)-(5.24) (p. 159-166)** (S; details in [20] = Balaban-Brydges-Imbrie-Jaffe preprint). Model $Z=\int_\Omega d^\Lambda\phi\,e^{-S}$, $\Omega=[-\rho_0,\infty)^\Lambda$, $\rho_0=O(\lambda^{-1/2})$, $S=\frac12\sum(\phi_x-\phi_y)^2+\frac12m^2\sum\phi_x^2+\lambda\sum\phi_x^4$. Large-field/small-field decomposition ("small at $x$": $|\phi_x|\le p(\lambda)=|\log\lambda|^{d+1}$); partition of unity $1=\sum_{\Lambda_0}\chi_{\Lambda_0}\zeta_{\Lambda_0^c}$ isolating "holes" $R_0$, backing off by a buffer $r(\lambda)=\log^2\lambda$ to "superholes" $R_1$; **(5.14)**: $\sum_{R\subset R_1}Z_0^{-1}(R_1)\int d^{R_1}\phi\,e^{-S_0(R_1)}\zeta_R\le4^{|R_1|}e^{-c\,r(\lambda)^{-d}p^2(\lambda)|R_1|}$ (obtained by scaling $\phi\to2\phi$, $S_0\to4S_0=S_0+3S_0$ and using the cost of a large field encoded in $\zeta_R$; the printed exponent is hard to read and has $r(\lambda)^{-d}$; UNCERTAIN of the exact exponent; the statement is that superholes are rarer than any power of $\lambda$ for fixed $L$). Then $\bar K(C)$ is built from superhole activities $\rho(R,R_1,\phi)=e^{-S(R)}\zeta_RZ_0^*(R_1\setminus R)/Z_0(R_1)$ (5.16), the Erice expansion of the "$\Xi^*$" remaining Gaussian-times-small-perturbation integral, the normalisation clusters $(W^*-W)(\Gamma)$ and (5.19)
$$\bar K(C)=\sum_{R_1,\Delta,\Gamma}\ \prod_{R_1\in\mathbf R_1}\Big(\sum_{R\subset R_1}\int d^R\phi\,\rho(R_1,R,\phi)\,\Xi^*(R_1\setminus R)/\Xi_0(R_1)\Big)\prod_{\Delta}K^*(\Delta)\prod_{\Gamma}(W^*-W)(\Gamma).$$
Target (5.20): $|\bar K(C)|\le\exp(-\bar c|C'|)$ with $\bar c$ as large as wanted "by taking $L$ large followed by $\lambda$ small". The proof (5.21)-(5.24) reduces to $Z_\alpha(C)\le e^{|C'|}$ (5.21) by relaxing the covering constraint, using (5.6) for the $\Gamma$-sum (5.22), (5.11) for the $\Delta$'s ($K_\alpha(\Delta)\le\lambda e^{-c_1|\Delta|}$, $\sup_{x'}\sum K_\alpha\le c_2\lambda$ (5.23)), and the superhole bound (5.14); final $Z_\alpha(C)\le\exp|C'|$ if $\lambda$ small (p. 166). It is an outline: the key estimates are references to [20].

**Sect. 6, self-avoiding walk lace expansion (pp. 166-176)**: Thm 6.1 (lace formula for $\psi_c(I)$, with proof), (6.3)-(6.4), Thm 6.2 (for $d\ge5$, $\tau=\infty$, $\beta$ small, $N(k,T)\sim r^{-T}e^{-\frac12ATk^2}$; precise statement a scaling limit, not proved here, ref. **Appendix B (p. 177-180)**: many-body potentials. $V(X)=\sum_{b\subset X}V(b)/|b|!$ (sequences $b$ of elements of $X$); Thm 3.1 generalises (B.1)-(B.3) with $\psi_c(Y)=\sum_T\prod_{b\in T}(-v(b)/|b|!)\int dp_T(s)e^{-V(s)}$, $dp_T$ a probability measure on convex decouplings (Federbush-Battle [8]); Cor B.1: (a) stable: $|\psi_c(X)|\le\sum_T\prod_b|v(b)|/|b|!$; (b) repulsive without self-energies: $\le\sum_T\prod_b|e^{-v(b)/|b|!}-1|/|b|!$ (as printed). **Decay of correlations.** There is NO theorem on decay of correlations as such. The motivation is (1.2) $\langle\phi_i;\phi_j\rangle\le c_1e^{-c_2|i-j|}$ with $c_1,c_2$ independent of $|\Lambda|$, and the representation $\log Z(h)=\sum_{X\subset\Lambda}K(X,h)$ (1.4) with $K(X,h)$ analytic in $h$ and small for large/spread-out $X$; (1.3) $\langle\phi_i;\phi_j\rangle=\partial^2\log Z/\partial h_i\partial h_j|_{h=0}$. Exponential decay follows from (1.4) plus decay of $K$, but this deduction is only described in words (pp. 132-133, 160), and the Sect. 4 remark p. 149 bounds truncated expectations of products of local functions by the same tree sum.

**Forest formulas / BBF.** Brydges' notes contain only the Battle-Federbush TREE formula for ONE connected part (Thm 3.1); the "forest" formulas (Brydges-Kennedy 1987, Abdesselam-Rivasseau 1995) post-date these notes (1984) and are not here. The proof structure behind Thm 3.1 (decouple one vertex at a time, Appendix B) is the precursor.

## 3. Proof architecture

1. Sect. 2 is pure algebra: the polymer representation (2.5) of a spin partition function with product reference measure by expanding the Gibbs factor into connected parts over partitions; the "second expansion" (2.7) is the Mayer expansion of the resulting hard-core polymer gas ("if at first you do not succeed, then expand and expand again", p. 137). This is formal (power series in the activity).
2. Sect. 3 supplies the only analysis of convergence: the tree-graph representation (Thm 3.1) reduces $\psi_c$ (a signed sum over many connected graphs, "too many connected graphs", p. 138) to a sum over trees with a probability measure over convex decouplings, so that for stable $V$ one needs only $|e^{-V(X,s)}|\le1$ (stability) and Cayley's tree count; refining the count by degrees (3.9) gives the factorial gain that makes the polymer criterion $Q<1$ volume-independent (Thm 3.4) instead of the false (3.8).
3. Sect. 4 transfers the polymer machinery to a Gaussian reference measure by an exact formula: $\int d\mu F=\exp(\frac12\sum\Delta)F|_0$ and its block-decoupled version (4.2)-(4.4). The convergence proof needs smallness in the sup norm of derivatives of $F_x$ in the form $\lambda^rr!$ and a summability assumption on $C_{xy}$ (or decay with $r!^p$ derivatives), then the Wick-based bound on $K(Y)$ and Cayley.
4. Sect. 5 handles the situation where the Gaussian cannot be treated globally: decouple blocks by $s$ inside both numerator and denominator (so the Gaussian normalisation is differentiated and truncates expectations), expand by Taylor in the $s_{x'y'}$ (5.2)-(5.3), get locality (5.3b), expand the Gaussian normalisation similarly (5.4)-(5.5), group all expansions with mutual compatibility conditions into one polymer system indexed by connected unions of blocks $C$ (the "no two $C$ touch" hard core), and verify the diluteness condition $|\bar K(C)|\le e^{-\bar c|C'|}$ via a generic "relax the covering condition, make activities $e^{\alpha|\cdot|}$-larger, exponentiate" argument. The estimates special to the model (5.6), (5.11), (5.14) are sketched or referred to [20].
5. Appendix C proves Thm 3.4 by stripping outermost branches of a tree and summing the base point; Appendix A the formal identities; Appendix B the many-body extension.

The genuine analytic content in this paper: Thm 3.1 (positivity of $dp_T$, taken from Battle-Federbush), (3.9)-(3.10), and Thm 3.4. Everything in Sects. 4-5 is a template whose estimates are given in outline.

## 7. Weak spots and open points

1. Thm 3.1: positivity of $dp_T$ (it is a probability measure supported on convex decouplings) is not proved in these notes from scratch; the text says "the essential step in their arguments may be paraphrased as follows", and a reader must trust [8, 9] (Battle-Federbush). The identity is verified only formally.
2. Thm 3.4 asserts $\log Z=\sum I_N$ without stating that the series converges to $\log Z$ (only that each term is bounded when $Q<\infty$). Lean needs an explicit statement: if $Q<1$ then $Z\ne0$ and the series equals a (principal) logarithm; this follows by analytic continuation in a scaling $K\to zK$, $|z|\le1$, but is not written.
3. App. C proves the tree bound for one example (Fig. 5); the general inductive statement and the interchange of the sum over trees and over $Y$ are not written out. In particular, the roles of the two "$|Y|$" factors ($|Y|^{d-1}$ vs $|Y|^{d}$) are fixed by example and not proved in general.
4. The hypotheses of Thm 3.4 are for subsets of a finite set with disjointness. The O(N) series uses polymers as connected unions of cubes "modulo $\Omega^c$" (P3, P5, P6, P7) with a non-standard connectivity and compatibility relation ("$Y_1,Y_2\in\mathcal D_{k+1}(\mathrm{mod}\ \Omega^c_{k+1})$ connected if $Y_1\cap Y_2\cap\Omega_{k+1}\ne\emptyset$", P6 p. 36). Brydges treats only: arbitrary subsets with disjointness (Thm 3.4), and in Sect. 5 connected unions of blocks with "no two touch" (face/edge/corner). The "modulo $\Omega^c$" version is not treated and requires the abstract polymer-with-incompatibility form (Kotecky-Preiss style) rather than literal set disjointness.
5. Sect. 4 assumes a fixed finite Gaussian measure with global covariance $C_{xy}$ and uniformly bounded $\sum_y|C_{xy}|$; P6 does not use this expansion. Sect. 5 is the closest relative of P6 Sect. 4.4 (decoupling $t_Y$ attached to localization domains, factorisation over connected components of the union), but P6's decoupling is by $t_Y\mathcal U(Y;\psi)$ on a product measure with all covariance moved into the interaction; it does not require Neumann localisation of covariances.
6. (5.14) and (5.6), (5.11) are only outlined and referred to a preprint; the printed exponent in (5.14) is garbled in the scan. Printed constants: "A" for $\lambda$ in (5.11) (OCR). Only the structure is reliable.
7. The citations "[7] Brydges... see for a definition of $\rho^T$" (P6 p. 39) can be settled: the function $\rho^T(Z_1..Z_n)$ is the connected part of the hard-core Gibbs factor, $\psi'_c$ in (2.6)-(2.7) and $\psi_c$ in Thm 3.4. But E11 does not use the notation $\rho^T$ and does not state the explicit bound $|\sum_n\frac1{n!}\sum\rho^T\prod U|\le O(1)\varepsilon^{1/2}M^{-d}|Z|$ that P6 uses; it must be derived from Thm 3.4 with $\Lambda\to Z$ (see Sect. 9).
8. P5 [6]: no in-text citation found (see Sect. 0).
9. Some scan artefacts: stray OCR in "Erice" (printed "Erice"), subscripts "(.)_I", $\mathbf r$ for $\Gamma$; I flagged all uncertain formulas.

## 10. Relevance to the formalization

**What to formalize: one abstract polymer-expansion theorem** serving P6 §4 ((4.46)-(4.51)), P7 §3 ((3.26)-(3.29), (4.12)-(4.14)) and P9 §4 ((4.4)-(4.6), via P6/P7). Statement (to be proved in Lean, as an interface for the whole series; the form below is my own formulation; E11 supplies only the discrete-set special case Thm 3.4 with $K(Y)$ complex, and Kotecky-Preiss supplies the sharp criterion):

*Abstract polymer expansion.* Let $\mathcal P$ be a countable set (polymers) with a symmetric reflexive relation $X\sim Y$ ("incompatible"; $X\not\sim Y$ means compatible), a size function $d:\mathcal P\to[0,\infty)$ and $|\cdot|:\mathcal P\to[0,\infty)$, and $K:\mathcal P\to\mathbb C$ (activities). Suppose there are functions $a,b:\mathcal P\to[0,\infty)$ with
$$\sum_{Y\sim X}|K(Y)|\,e^{a(Y)+b(Y)}\le a(X)\quad\text{for every }X\in\mathcal P\tag{KP}$$
(Kotecky-Preiss). Then for every finite $\Lambda\subset\mathcal P$ (or the full set), with $Z(\Lambda)=\sum_{\{X_1..X_n\}\subset\Lambda\text{ pairwise compatible}}\prod K(X_i)$:
(i) $Z(\Lambda)\ne0$ and $\log Z(\Lambda)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n!}\sum_{(X_1..X_n)\in\Lambda^n}\varphi^T(X_1..X_n)\prod K(X_i)$, absolutely convergent, where $\varphi^T$ is the Ursell function of the incompatibility graph (= $\psi_c'$ of Brydges (2.6)-(2.7), $=\rho^T$ of P6 (4.50));
(ii) for every polymer $X_0$: $\sum_{(X_1..X_n):\ \text{some }X_i\sim X_0}\frac1{n!}|\varphi^T\prod K(X_i)|\,e^{\sum b(X_i)}\le a(X_0)$ (so in particular the sum of all cluster terms with support in a set $S$ is bounded by $\sum_{X\in S}a(X)$);
(iii) with the "localized logarithm" $\mathcal P(X):=\sum_{n}\frac1{n!}\sum_{(X_1..X_n):\ \bigcup X_i=X}\varphi^T\prod K(X_i)$ for connected $X$ (when the incompatibility relation is overlap of unions of cubes) one has $\log Z=\sum_X\mathcal P(X)$ and, if $d$ satisfies the superadditivity $d(X)\le\sum_i(d(X_i)+c_0)$ for clusters with connected union, $|K(Y)|\le\varepsilon e^{-\kappa d(Y)}$ with $\kappa\ge\kappa'+c_0+1$ and $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0(\kappa,\text{lattice constants})$, then $|\mathcal P(X)|\le C\varepsilon e^{-\kappa'd(X)}$;
(iv) (analyticity and symmetry) each $\mathcal P(X)$ is a convergent series of polynomials in $\{K(Y)\}_{Y\subset X}$; if the $K(Y)$ are analytic functions of external parameters on a domain, uniformly bounded as above, then so is $\mathcal P(X)$ with the same bound; symmetry invariance of $K$ passes to $\mathcal P$.

For P6/P7 one takes $\mathcal P$ = connected unions of cubes (in $\mathcal D_{k+1}$ or $\mathcal D_k(\mathrm{mod}\ \Omega^c)$), incompatibility = nonempty intersection modulo the relevant complement, $a(Y)=$ number of cubes (the form (3.10b)), $d$ = tree length. The criterion in Brydges' (3.10b) $\sup_x\sum_{Y\ni x}|K(Y)|e^{|Y|}<1$ is a special case of (KP): put $a(Y)=|Y|$, $b=0$; then $\sum_{Y\cap X\ne\emptyset}|K(Y)|e^{|Y|}\le\sum_{x\in X}\sup_x\sum_{Y\ni x}\dots\le|X|$. Brydges' (3.10a) is slightly stronger (sup outside, inside a sum over $d$), so E11's Thm 3.4 follows from (KP). The conclusion $|I_N|\le Q^N|\Lambda|/N$ of Thm 3.4 is weaker than (ii) (it gives only a size bound of the log, not the decay of the cluster sum).

**Relation to BKAR.** Brydges' Thm 3.1 is the Battle-Federbush tree formula; BKAR (Brydges-Kennedy 1987, Abdesselam-Rivasseau 1995) writes, for smooth $f:[0,1]^{E}\to\mathbb C$ ($E$ = edges of the complete graph on the vertex set),
$$f(\mathbf 1)=\sum_{F\text{ forest on }V}\int_{[0,1]^{F}}dw\ \partial^Ff(s^F(w)),$$
where $s^F_e(w)=\inf$ of the $w$-values along the path in $F$ connecting the ends of $e$ and $0$ if they are not connected in $F$. Applied to the Gibbs factor $f(s)=e^{-V(s)}$ of the hard-core polymer gas it gives a tree formula for the connected part of Brydges' (3.1) with the explicit $dp_T$ obtained as the push-forward of Lebesgue measure on $w\in[0,1]^{T}$ (for a tree $T$). In `/home/scott/KPZ/cumulants/Lean/Polymers/BKAR/Formula.lean` I looked only at the statements: `Polymers.BKAR.bkar_formula_forestIndex_cube_contributions` (paper-facing scalar form) says, for `ρ : (Edge V → ℝ) → ℝ` with `hρ : BKARContDiff ρ` and `V` a Fintype with decidable equality, that `ρ oneConfig = ∑ I : ForestIndex V, I.cubeContribution ρ`, with the variant `bkar_formula_nonempty` isolating the empty forest `ρ zeroConfig` and the variants (`bkar_formula`, `bkar_formula_reached_forests`, ...) indexed by `Forest.ActiveExtensionChoice V` and the order/support sectors `Forest.edgeSetOrders`. This is the (support-indexed, ordered-simplex) form of the forest formula for a function of edge parameters. I did not read its proofs or definitions of `cubeContribution`/`ForestIndex`; the match to the classical BKAR statement ($\sum_F\int dw\,\partial^Ff(s^F(w))$) is therefore UNVERIFIED by me and should be checked. If the identification holds, the route for Lean is: (1) BKAR for $f(s)=\psi(X;s)$ gives Thm 3.1 for free once $s^F(w)\in$ convex decouplings is shown; (2) apply it to the hard-core polymer gas to prove the abstract theorem (i)-(ii) with the KP criterion by the Brydges-Procacci / Fernandez-Procacci tree-graph or the Kotecky-Preiss induction (the induction proof of KP is independent of BKAR and may be shorter).
