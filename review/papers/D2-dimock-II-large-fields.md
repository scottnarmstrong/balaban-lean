**Headline findings (read this first).**
1. D2 is the phi^4_3 (scalar, d=3, one-component) model, **not** a gauge model. Its large-field set-up is modelled directly on Balaban's O(N) papers P1-P7 (it says "the proof follows especially [8]-[14]", p. 32, where [8]-[14] are P1, P2, P3, P4, P5, P6, P7; the notation dictionary on p. 84 maps to them). It cites **none** of Balaban's gauge large-field papers (CMP 119, 122:175, 122:355); its bibliography has only Balaban's Higgs and propagator papers [1]-[7], plus [8]-[14] = P1-P7.
2. D2 contains **no step counter K, no admissible multi-indices in P5's sense, and no R operation.** Remark 3 on p. 31 says explicitly that in Balaban's models the remainder gets contributions "from a recycling operation that converts some large field contributions back to small field contributions (the R-operation). This difficult step is not necessary for this model." D2 therefore cannot replace E10 (CMP 122:355) / P6 Prop 3.2 / P7 for the R operation.
3. D2 does **not** contain the estimates that make the large-field sum converge. It proves an exact inductive *representation* (Thm 3.1, pp. 28-30): a sum over nested small-field regions with characteristic functions, with the integrals over large-field variables left undone, plus the small-field analysis. The summation of the large-field contributions ("point (4)", p. 3) and the stability bound are in D3, section 3 "large field bounds" (D3 p. 19ff; not reviewed here). The abstract's "we control the large field contribution" means: the representation is set up and the small field/boundary parts are controlled.
4. What D2 does reprove in full is the part of the large-field calculus that is *geometric/analytic and model-independent*: the nested-region bookkeeping ($\Omega_k\supset\Lambda_k\supset\Omega_{k+1}$ with buffers), multiscale averaging and its Green's function with random-walk expansion, how to replace non-local minimizers and non-local fluctuation covariances by localized ones so that characteristic functions do not destroy analyticity, and how boundary terms between small and large field regions are kept small by buffers of width $r_k=(-\log\lambda_k)^r$ ("terms that survive many steps must have large extent and hence tiny value", p. 68).

## 0. Identification

J. Dimock, "The renormalization group according to Balaban II. Large fields", J. Math. Phys. 54 (2013) (DOI 10.1063/1.4821275 per SOURCES.md); arXiv:1212.5562v2. 94 pages. Bundle key D2.

Cited by the bundle papers? Grep of `papers/P*.md`, `BIG_PICTURE.md`: no bundle paper cites Dimock (P1-P9 predate 2011). BIG_PICTURE §5 recommends it as a secondary source. D2 itself cites P1-P7 and P9 (as [17]); it cites E3 (CMP 89) as [4] and E5 (CMP 96) as [6], Brydges Les Houches (= E11) as [18].

Summary. Setting: the phi^4 model on the torus $\mathbb T_M^{-N}=(L^{-N}\mathbb Z/LM\mathbb Z)^3$ with ultraviolet cutoff $N$, scaled to a unit lattice, density $\rho_0=\exp(-S_0-V_0)$ with small couplings $\lambda_0=L^{-N}\lambda$, $\mu_0=L^{-2N}\mu$ (pp. 1, 3). The goal (D1-D3 together) is a stability bound uniform in $N$. D1 treats the small-field region; D2 introduces the large-field decomposition at each RG step and proves an exact inductive representation of $\rho_k$ as a sum over nested pairs of regions (Thm 3.1); D3 proves the sums converge. Since the RG step here is exactly block averaging of a Gaussian-weighted density, many identities are exact (Lemma 2.1, Thm 2.1). D2 is a re-derivation (not a verbatim transcription) of the Balaban O(N) architecture P5-P7, simplified because the model has only one interaction $V=\varepsilon+\mu\phi^2+\lambda\phi^4$ and the flow has a clean smallness parameter $\lambda_k=L^k\lambda_0\to\lambda$.

Role: supplies a complete worked version of the "decomposition of unity -> small field region + large field region -> background field from a localized minimizer -> localized fluctuation integral -> polymer expansion -> scaling" cycle for a model in which every step is written out. Takes from D1 the global small-field theory (the functions $\mathcal E_k$, couplings flow (222)/(547), random walk expansion on the whole torus). Supplies to D3 the representation (201)-(202).

## 1. Setting and notation

**Model.** $\mathbb T_{M}^{-N}=(L^{-N}\mathbb Z/LM\mathbb Z)^3$, $\mathbb T^{-k}_{M+N-k}$ the torus after $k$ steps with lattice spacing $L^{-k}$ (so after $k$ steps $\mathbb T^{-N}\to\mathbb T^{-N+k}$ conventions, p. 1, 3). $L$ block factor, $M=L^m$ cube size (the big cube; the M-cubes are the "polymer cubes"). Density $\rho_0(\Phi)=\exp(-S_0^N-V_0^N)$, $S_0=\frac12\|\partial\Phi\|^2+\frac12\bar\mu_0\|\Phi\|^2$, $V_0=\varepsilon_0\mathrm{Vol}+\frac12\mu_0\|\Phi\|^2+\frac14\lambda_0\sum\Phi^4$ (p. 1, (3)-(4)). $\lambda_k=L^k\lambda_0$ (143); $\lambda\le e^{-1}$, $\bar\mu\le1$ (p. 29).

**RG step** (5)-(8), (199)-(200): $\tilde\rho_{k+1}(\Phi_{k+1})=\mathcal N^{-1}\int\exp(-\frac12 aL|\Phi_{k+1}-Q\Phi_k|^2)\rho_k(\Phi_k)d\Phi_k$, $Q$ = average over $L$-cubes; then scaling $\rho_{k+1}(\Phi_{k+1})=\tilde\rho_{k+1}(\Phi_{k+1,L})L^{-|\mathbb T|/2}$ with $\Phi_L(x)=L^{-1/2}\Phi(x/L)$ (6). The constant preserves the integral (8).

**Localized (multiscale) block averaging, §2.1 pp. 3-6.** Region sequence $\Omega=(\Omega_1\supset\Omega_2\supset\dots\supset\Omega_k)$, $\Omega_j$ a union of $L^{-(k-j)}M$ cubes; $\delta\Omega_j=\Omega_j-\Omega_{j+1}$, $\delta\Omega_k=\Omega_k$ (18)-(20). Fundamental fields $\Phi_{k,\Omega}=(\Phi_{1,\delta\Omega_1},\dots,\Phi_{k,\Omega_k})$ with $\Phi_{j,\delta\Omega_j}$ living on $\delta\Omega_j^{(j)}$ (the lattice of $L^j$-cube centres) (21)-(23): the field is progressively coarser away from the current region. $Q_{k,\Omega}\phi=([Q_1\phi]_{\delta\Omega_1},\dots,[Q_k\phi]_{\Omega_k})$ (27), weights $a_j^{(k)}=a_jL^{2(k-j)}$ (28), $\|a^{1/2}(\Phi_{k,\Omega}-Q_{k,\Omega}\phi)\|^2=\sum_{j<k}a_j^{(k)}\|\Phi_j-Q_j\phi\|^2_{\delta\Omega_j}+a_k\|\Phi_k-Q_k\phi\|^2_{\Omega_k}$ (29).

## 2. Main results

**Theorem 2.1 (p. 7), free flow with localized regions.** For $\rho_0$ Gaussian-weighted, after $k$ localized averaging steps
$$\rho_{k,\Omega}(\phi_{\Omega_1^c},\Phi_{k,\Omega})=Z_{k,\Omega}\exp\Big(-\tfrac12\|a^{1/2}(\Phi_{k,\Omega}-Q_{k,\Omega}\phi)\|^2_{\Omega_1}-\tfrac12\langle\phi,(-\Delta+\bar\mu_k)\phi\rangle\Big)\Big|_{\phi_{\Omega_1}=\phi_{k,\Omega}}$$
(41) with $\phi_{k,\Omega}=G_{k,\Omega}[Q^T_{k,\Omega}a\Phi_{k,\Omega}+[\Delta]_{\Omega_1,\Omega_1^c}\phi_{\Omega_1^c}]$ (42), $G_{k,\Omega}$ as in (43). Proved in full (pp. 7-8), by completing the square. Lemma 2.1 (p. 6): $\rho_{k,\Omega}=N^{-1}\int\exp(-\frac12\|a^{1/2}(\Phi_{k,\Omega}-Q_{k,\Omega}\phi)\|^2)\rho_0(\phi_{L^k})d\phi_{\Omega_1}$ (30), by induction using the one-step minimizer (33)-(35), $a_{k+1}=\ldots$ (the free flow of $a_k$ is from D1).

**Lemma 2.2 (p. 8)**: $S_k(\Omega_1,\Phi_{k,\Omega},\phi_{k,\Omega})=\frac12\langle\Phi,\Delta_{k,\Omega}\Phi\rangle+\frac12\langle\phi_{\Omega_1^c},[\Delta]_{\Omega_1^c,\Omega_1}G_{k,\Omega}[\Delta]_{\Omega_1,\Omega_1^c}\phi_{\Omega_1^c}\rangle$, $\Delta_{k,\Omega}=a-aQ_{k,\Omega}G_{k,\Omega}Q_{k,\Omega}^Ta$ (49)-(50).

**Lemma 2.3 (p. 9-10), one step of the free flow.** The joint minimum of $J_{\Omega^+}$ in $(\phi_{\Omega_1},\Phi_{k,\Omega_{k+1}})$ is at $\phi=\phi^0_{k+1,\Omega^+}$, $\Phi_k=\Psi_{k,\Omega_{k+1}}(\Omega^+)$ (55)-(57); $\phi^0_{k+1,\Omega^+}=\phi_{k,\Omega}(\phi_{\Omega_1^c},\Psi_{k,\Omega^+})$ (59); the minimal value is $S^0_{k+1}$ (60)-(61) (the same function with $k+1$ in place of $k$: the action does not change form). The quadratic expansion around the minimizer, (68)-(70), has leftover form $\frac12\langle Z,[\Delta_{k,\Omega}+\frac a{L^2}Q^TQ]_{\Omega_{k+1}}Z\rangle$. Scaling check (72)-(76).

**Lemmas 2.4, 2.5 (pp. 11-12)**: the same expansion for the action restricted to a region $\Lambda$ ($\Omega_1\supset\dots\supset\Omega_k\supset\Lambda\supset\Omega_{k+1}$), with the non-minimality error $R_{k,\Omega,\Lambda}$ (90) and boundary term $b_\Lambda(\partial\phi,Z)$ (83); here $\|\partial\phi\|^2_{*,\Lambda}$ counts half of each bond crossing $\partial\Lambda$ (78).

**Theorem 2.2 (p. 16), random walk expansion for $G_{k,\Omega}$.** Assume the separation condition $d(\Omega_j^c,\Omega_{j+1})\ge L^{-(k-j)}MR$ (96) and $M$ large. Then $G_{k,\Omega}=\sum_\omega G_{k,\Omega,\omega}$ (114), convergent, with, for $\Delta_y\subset\delta\Omega_j$, $\Delta_{y'}\subset\delta\Omega_{j'}$ ($L^{-(k-j)}$-cubes),
$$|1_{\Delta_y}G_{k,\Omega}1_{\Delta_{y'}}f|\le CL^{-2(k-j')}e^{-\frac14\gamma_0d_\Omega(y,y')}\|f\|_\infty,$$
and the same for $L^{-(k-j)}\partial G$ and $L^{-(1+\alpha)(k-j)}\delta_\alpha\partial G$ (115), $\gamma_0=O(L^{-2})$, $\frac12<\alpha<1$ (Hölder derivative). Multiscale distance $d_\Omega(x,y)=\inf_\gamma\sum_jL^{k-j}\ell(\gamma\cap\delta\Omega_j)$ (97); $\sum_ye^{-\delta d_\Omega(x,y)}\le O(1)\delta^{-3}$ (98). Proof sketched pp. 16-18 (partition of unity $h_z^2$, parametrix, commutator $K_z$ of size $M^{-1}$, Neumann series over walks; local inverse bounds Lemma 2.6 p. 14 cite E3 (CMP 89) [4] and Appendix D of D1; identity (108) from E5 [6] p. 230). Lemma 2.7 (p. 18): $|\phi_{k,\Omega}|,L^{-(k-j)}|\partial\phi_{k,\Omega}|,\ldots\le C(\|\phi\|_\infty+\|\Phi_{k,\Omega}\|_\infty)$ (133). Variations (A) local version (138), (B) weakening parameters $s_\square$, $|s_\square|\le e^{\kappa_1}\le M^{1/2}$, giving $G_{k,\Omega}(s)=\sum_\omega s_\omega G_{k,\Omega,\omega}$ (139)-(140), (C) same for the one-step Green's function $G^0_{k+1,\Omega^+}$ (141)-(142).

**Main Theorem 3.1 (pp. 28-30): representation of $\rho_k$ with large fields.** Let $0<\lambda\le e^{-1}$, $0<\bar\mu\le1$, $L$ sufficiently large, $M$ sufficiently large (depending on $L$), $\lambda_k$ sufficiently small (depending on $L,M$); $\varepsilon_k,\mu_k$ the coupling constants of D1. Then
$$\rho_k(\Phi_k)=Z_k\sum_\Pi\int d\Phi_{k,\Omega^c}\,dW_{k,\Pi}\;K_{k,\Pi}\,\mathcal C_{k,\Pi}\;\chi_k(\Lambda_k)\exp\big(-S^+_k(\Lambda_k)+E_k(\Lambda_k)+R_{k,\Pi}(\Lambda_k)+B_{k,\Pi}(\Lambda_k)\big)\tag{201}$$
with (202)
$$d\Phi_{k,\Omega^c}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\exp\big(-\tfrac12aL^{-(k-j-1)}|\Phi_{j+1}-Q\Phi_j|^2_{\Omega^c_{j+1}}\big)d\Phi^{(k-j)}_{j,\Omega_{j+1}^c},\quad
dW_{k,\Pi}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(2\pi)^{-|[\Omega_{j+1}-\Lambda_{j+1}]^{(j)}|/2}\exp\big(-\tfrac12L^{-(k-j)}|W_j|^2_{\Omega_{j+1}-\Lambda_{j+1}}\big)dW_j,$$
$$K_{k,\Pi}=\prod_{j=0}^k\exp\Big(c_j|\Omega_j^{c,(j-1)}|-S^{+,u}_{j,L^{-(k-j)}}(\Lambda_{j-1}-\Lambda_j)+\big(\tilde B_{j,L^{-(k-j)}}\big)_{\Pi_j}(\Lambda_{j-1},\Lambda_j)\Big),\qquad \mathcal C_{k,\Pi}=\prod_{j=0}^k\big(\mathcal C_{j,L^{-(k-j)}}\big)_{\Lambda_{j-1},\Omega_j,\Lambda_j}$$
(conventions $\Lambda_{-1},\Omega_0$ = whole torus; $\Pi_j$, $\Omega_j$ the truncation at level $j$). Properties:

Bare action $S_k^+(\Lambda_k)=S^+_k(\Lambda_k,\Phi_k,\phi_{k,\Omega(\Lambda_k^*)})$, $S_k^*=\frac{a_k}2\|\Phi_k-Q_k\phi\|^2_{\Lambda_k}+\frac12\|\partial\phi\|^2_{*,\Lambda_k}+\frac12\bar\mu_k\|\phi\|^2_{\Lambda_k}$, $V_k=\varepsilon_k\mathrm{Vol}+\frac12\mu_k\|\phi\|^2+\frac14\lambda_k\int\phi^4$ (210).

## 3. Proof architecture

### 3.0 Preview (§1, pp. 2-3)
Heuristic picture: insert $1=\sum_{\Omega_1}\zeta(\Omega_1^c,\Phi_0)\chi(\Omega_1,\Phi_0)$ (9) over unions $\Omega_1$ of $M$-cubes, where $\chi$ is the characteristic function of the small-field conditions $|\Phi_1-Q\Phi_0|\le p_0$, $|\partial\Phi_0|\le p_0$, $|\Phi_0|\le\lambda_0^{-1/4}p_0$ (10) with $p_0=(-\log\lambda_0)^p$, and $\zeta$ the characteristic function of "at least one inequality violated in every cube of $\Omega_1^c$". In a cube of $\Omega_1^c$ one of $\exp(-\frac a{2L^2}\|\Phi_1-Q\Phi_0\|^2)$, $\exp(-S_0)$, $\exp(-V_0)$ is bounded by $e^{-O(1)p_0^2}$; hence the large-field region carries $e^{-O(1)p_0^2|\Omega_1^c|_M}$ (p. 3), "sufficient to strongly suppress the contribution of any large field region and control the sum over $\Omega_1$". After $k$ steps: decreasing regions $\Omega_1\supset\dots\supset\Omega_k$ with an explicit leading action on $\Omega_k$. This is a heuristic only; the quantitative bounds are in D3. Four issues listed: small field region (D1), large field regions, coupling between them (D2), convergence of sums (D3).

### 3.1 §2 Localized block averaging (pp. 3-19)
Exact Gaussian computations with multiscale regions; random walk expansion with weakening parameters $s_\square$ (these are the decoupling parameters, same role as P3). The multiscale lattice makes the Green's function exponentially decaying in the scaled metric $d_\Omega$ with rate $\gamma_0=O(L^{-2})$ uniformly in $k$; $L^{-2(k-j')}$ prefactors encode the coarsening. Genuine difficulty: mixed cubes (touching $\Omega_1^c$, non-rectangular, mixed Dirichlet/Neumann) where only $L^2$ bounds survive (110)-(112); handled by $L^{-1}$-lattice norm equivalence. Compare P2 §4 / P4 (composition of averaging operators), P3 §2-3 (random walk expansion): D2 *proves* these for the scalar case, citing only E3/E5 for the single-scale local bounds (105) (E3 = CMP 89 [4]; E5 = CMP 96 [6] p. 230).

### 3.2 §3.1-3.3 Definitions and statement (pp. 20-31)
- Nested pair of regions per step: $\Lambda_0\supset\Omega_1\supset\Lambda_1\supset\Omega_2\supset\dots$ (145) with separation $d((\bar\Lambda_{j-1})^c,\Omega_j)\ge5[r_j]L^{-(k-j)}M$ and $d(\Omega_j^c,\Lambda_j)\ge5[r_j]L^{-(k-j)}M$ (148). So $\Omega_j$ is where the step-$j$ fluctuation integral is still to be done (the new variable $\Phi_j$ lives there), $\Lambda_j\subset\Omega_j$ is the *shrunken* region in which the field is small enough to expand; $\Omega_j-\Lambda_j$ is a collar where the new $W$-variables are large; the remaining large-field variables are left unintegrated.
- Buffers, enlargements, polymers with holes (149)-(167), pp. 20-22. Decay: $\sum_{X\in\mathcal D_k(\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega_k^c),X\supset\square}e^{-\kappa_0d_M(X,\mathrm{mod}\,\Omega_k^c)}\le K_0$ (151) (Appendix E); multiscale version $\sum_{X\supset\square}e^{-\kappa_*|X|_\Omega}\le e^{-\kappa_*/2}$ (153) (Appendix D, Lemma D.1, with $\kappa_*\ge O(\log L)$, via tree counting with $(2dL^{d-1})^{2n}$ paths).

### 3.5 Where the difficulties are
Routine: Gaussian algebra (§2.1-2.4), tree/polymer counting (Appendix D, E), scaling bookkeeping. Genuine: (i) random walk expansion with a multiscale Dirichlet problem, mixed boundary conditions (§2.5, 3.9); (ii) designing the objects so that characteristic functions depend locally on fields and do not spoil analyticity: this is what the localized minimizer $\Psi^{\rm loc}$ and localized covariance $(C^{1/2})^{\rm loc}$ achieve, at the price of $e^{-r_{k+1}}$ errors that must be shown tiny; (iii) proving that on $\Lambda_{k+1}^{**}$ the new characteristic functions imply the old ones (Lemma 3.13) so that the large-field conditions are not accumulated inside the active region; (iv) keeping the analyticity domain from shrinking repeatedly in the bulk (Def 3.4, Lemma 3.14).

## 7. Weak spots and open points

1. No large-field estimate. The quantitative statement that the unintegrated large-field regions are summable (the analogue of P5 (1.29)-(1.31)) is not in D2; it is D3 §3. Anyone using D2 as a source of the large-field *bounds* must read D3.
2. The explicit definition of $\mathcal C_{k,\Pi}$ is left to "the course of the proof"; it is reconstructible from (233)-(236), (324)-(330), (393) but nowhere written as one formula.
3. Many "similar to / as before / with the same result" passages: decoupling expansions in Lemmas 3.16, 3.17, 3.22, 3.23.
4. Theorem F.1's proof is a sketch (p. 91-92); it refers to D1 App. B.
5. Typos/unclear: the superscripts in (230); statement "Theorem 3.1 ... Let $\lambda_k$ sufficiently small" vs hypothesis on $\lambda$; the label "[12]" in "(2.41) in [12]" (p. 40); the chain $\Lambda_0\supset\Omega_1$ vs. "$\Omega_0=\emptyset$" in (144) (p. 20) versus "$\Lambda_{-1},\Omega_0$ full torus" (p. 29): the text says both. I read the former as a sequence convention and the latter as the convention used in the products (202).
6. Why the R operation is unnecessary is not explained in D2. A plausible reason (UNCERTAIN, my reading): the phi^4 flow has exactly $N$ steps with $\lambda_k=L^{k-N}\lambda$, the thresholds $p_k,r_k$ decrease from $(N\log L)^p$ to $O(1)$, and the single final step $N+1$ is handled by integrating everything at once (D3 p. 19ff: "no new large field region ($P_{N+1}=\emptyset$)"); an infrared tail with $\nu_k\beta_k\to$ large and mass-dependent number of steps, as in P5 §0 ($k_0,k_f$), does not occur. In O(N), by contrast, the number of steps after which a large-field component no longer pays is not controlled without K and R. Not verified.
7. The model is one-component with an explicit quartic potential, small coupling and no constraint; no sphere geometry, no reduced-symmetry terms $\mathcal R^{(k)}_n$, no field-strength flow $a_k$ beyond the free flow, no $\nu_k$ transition.

### 9.3 Can D2 stand in for the missing large-field papers?

Not for the large-field calculus proper. The coordinator's note says E8-E10 are now available (CMP 119, 243; CMP 122, 175; CMP 122, 355) and are being reviewed separately. I only glanced at the first pages of E10 (BF01238433.pdf): its abstract and §1 state that it performs "all the remaining operations defining R, like cancellations of the large terms in numerators and denominators in (1.100) [IV], localizations in disjoint large field regions, and exponentiations", and that "the main bounds are combinatorial bounds proving convergence of the whole expansion defined by the R operation" (p. 355); (1.1), p. 356, is the ratio $\chi_{k,1/2}\int dV'\Gamma\ldots/\int dV'\Gamma\ldots$ that P7 (1.6) adapts, with the cancellation (1.3)-(1.10) (pp. 356-358), the lower bound (1.7)-(1.9) on the quadratic form with axial gauge and $\mathrm{diam}\le100M$. None of this appears in D2. What D2 gives that is useful for the O(N) reconstruction:
1. A clean and complete model of the *structure* that P5-P7 only describe: the exact representation, the roles of $P,Q,R$ regions, buffers and the three types of boundary term, with all functional-analytic details (domains, bounds, localization) in one place for a case where positivity of the action is trivial.
2. The scalar/lattice Laplacian version of the Green's function machinery with block-averaging term $Q^Ta Q$ (Thm 2.2), which is exactly what P2/P3 cite from E3/E5 for O(N): $G=(-\Delta^D_\Omega+Q^*aQ+\nu)^{-1}$ in multiscale form. Needs adaptation only for $\nu\ne0$ mass in $\Omega$ ($\bar\mu_k$ plays this role in D2) and for $\lambda=\infty$ (constraint).
3. A working version of "exponentiation by cluster expansion with holes" as an interface (Thm F.1).
It cannot supply: the combinatorics that make the large-field sum converge in the O(N) regime where $\beta_k$ grows like $L^{k(d-2)}$ (needs K and R); the R operation; the lower bound Prop 3.1 for the O(N) action. For those, E9/E10 (gauge) or P5-P7 remain the sources. UNCERTAIN: Dimock's D3 §3 may contain a large-field summation argument that works without K; it should be read before concluding that K is dispensable for O(N).

## 10. Relevance to the formalization

- Interfaces that D2 suggests: (a) the Theorem 3.1 representation format (sum over nested histories $\Pi$, product $K\,\mathcal C\,\chi\,e^{-S+E+R+B}$ with $B$, $\tilde B$ split) is a good statement shape for the O(N) closed inductive description (P5 H1-H7, P7 Thm 5), without the integral-operator formalism; (b) Theorem F.1 as a frozen interface (polymer expansion with holes, local influence property); (c) Theorem 2.2 / Lemma 3.5 as Green's function interface.
