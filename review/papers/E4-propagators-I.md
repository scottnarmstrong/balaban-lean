# E4: Propagators and renormalization transformations for lattice gauge theories. I

Page convention: printed journal pages 17-40; PDF page = printed page - 16. All page numbers below are printed pages. Every page was read from the page image. "[1]-[8]" are this paper's own reference numbers (list on p. 40); "[2]" = E3.

## 0. Identification

T. Balaban, "Propagators and renormalization transformations for lattice gauge theories. I", Commun. Math. Phys. 95 (1984) 17-40 (received 21 Nov 1983, revised 2 Apr 1984). Bundle key E4. 24 PDF pages. DOI 10.1007/BF01215753.

Summary. The paper treats the simplest gauge-theory Gaussian: a real vector field $A$ on the bonds of a torus $T_\varepsilon\subset\varepsilon\mathbb Z^d$ with the quadratic action $\frac12\|\partial A\|^2=\frac12\sum_p|F(p)|^2$ (abelian field strength), invariant under $A\to A-\partial\lambda$. A Wilson-type renormalization transformation $T$ (block averaging of $A$ along contours, with axial gauge fixing inside blocks, followed by rescaling $S$) is applied $k$ times; the composition is again Gaussian with an explicit effective quadratic form $\frac12\langle B,\mathcal A_kB\rangle$ on the coarse lattice $T_1^{(k)}$ (Sect. A-B). After gauge fixing by a Faddeev-Popov term (Landau gauge, Sect. C), the paper builds explicitly the operators that appear in the Gaussian integral: the minimizer operator $H_k$ ($B\mapsto$ minimal-action fine field with $Q_kA=B$), a Gaussian-composed "propagator" $G_k=\mathcal A_a^{-1}$ with $\mathcal A_a=\Delta-\partial P\partial^*+aQ^*Q$, and the associated projection (Sects. D-E). Main results: Proposition 1.1 (p. 33): $G$ and its first and second derivatives are bounded on $L^2$ uniformly in $k$, torus size and $\eta$; Proposition 1.2 (p. 35): $G$ has pointwise exponential decay and Hoelder regularity in the scale-weighted unit-lattice sense (Sect. F). Proof of 1.2 is by a random walk expansion with a partition of unity ("very sketchy", p. 37), plus an alternative route through $G=G_0+G_0\partial P\partial^*G$ reducing everything to the scalar operator $G'=(\Delta+aQ'^*Q')^{-1}$ of [2] = E3.

Role. The abstract (p. 17) says it treats "the simplest propagators". The paper does NOT prove any estimate for the operator with Dirichlet conditions, a potential $\nu+\alpha$, or a nonconstant multiscale $Q^*(\mathbb B_k)a(\mathbb B_k)Q(\mathbb B_k)$; that is E5 (Part II). E4 contributes to E5 (a) the formalism (Gaussian composition of RG steps, effective form $\mathcal A_k$, minimizer operators $H_k$, $G$, projection formulas), (b) the technique (random walk expansion with the partition of unity $\sum_zh_z^2=1$ and commutator $K(h)$), (c) the reduction of everything to the scalar operator $G'$ of E3.

## 1. Setting and notation

Lattice and fields (pp. 17-18). $T_\varepsilon=\{x\in\varepsilon\mathbb Z^d:-L_\mu\le x_\mu<L_\mu\}$ a $d$-dimensional torus. Field configurations $A:\{\text{bonds }b\subset T_\varepsilon\}\to\mathbb R$, identified with vector-valued functions $A_\mu(x)=A(x,x+\varepsilon e_\mu)$ (1.1); $A_{\langle x,x'\rangle}=-A_{\langle x',x\rangle}$. Plaquette $p$, curl $(\partial A)(p)=\varepsilon^{-1}(A(x,y)+A(y,z)+A(z,w)+A(w,x))=(\partial_\mu A_\nu)-(\partial_\nu A_\mu)=F_{\mu\nu}(x)$ (1.2). Action (1.3): $S^\varepsilon(A)=\frac12\sum_p\varepsilon^d|F(p)|^2=\frac12\sum_p\varepsilon^d|(\partial A)(p)|^2=\frac14\sum_{x,\mu,\nu}\varepsilon^d|F_{\mu\nu}(x)|^2$. Gauge transformation $A^\lambda_b=A_b-(\partial\lambda)(b)$, $(\partial\lambda)(b)=\varepsilon^{-1}(\lambda(b_+)-\lambda(b_-))$ (1.4); $S$ is invariant. On the unit lattice, $A_\mu(\varepsilon x)=\varepsilon^{-(d-2)/2}A_\mu(x)$ gives $S(A)=\frac12\sum_{p\in T_1}|F(p)|^2$ (1.5).

Blocks and averaging (p. 18-19). $T_L^{(1)}=T_1\cap L\mathbb Z^d$; blocks $B(y)=\{x\in T_1:y_\mu\le x_\mu<y_\mu+L\}$, $y\in T_L^{(1)}$ (1.6). Contours $\Gamma_{y,x}$ from $y$ to $x$: lattice-path "staircase" of segments $[y,(y_1,\dots,y_{d-1},x_d)]\cup\dots\cup[(y_1,x_2,\dots,x_d),x]$ (1.7). Averaging operation (1.8): for a bond $c=\langle c_-,c_+\rangle$ of $T_L^{(1)}$,
$$B_c=\sum_{x\in B(c_-)}L^{-(d+1)}\big(A(\Gamma_{c_-,x})+A([x,x(c)])+A(\Gamma_{x(c),c_+})\big),$$
$A(\Gamma)=\sum_{b\in\Gamma}A_b$, $x(c)\in B(c_+)$ the translate of $x$ by the bond $c$. Under gauge transformations $B^\lambda_c=B_c-L^{-1}(\lambda(c_+)-\lambda(c_-))$ (1.9), so the gauge is fixed on $T_L^{(1)}$ ($\lambda(y)=0$), and the residual gauge freedom inside blocks is removed by axial gauge $\delta_{Ax}(A)=\prod_{y}\prod_{x\in B(y),x\ne y}\delta(A(\Gamma_{y,x}))$ (1.10). Equivalent description (1.11), (1.13): $(QA)_c=\sum_{x\in B(c_-)}L^{-(d+1)}A([x,x(c)])$ (a plain average over the transverse bonds in the block) and $B^\lambda=B-\partial(Q'\lambda)$ with $(Q'\lambda)(y)=\sum_{x\in B(y)}L^{-d}\lambda(x)$.

Renormalization transformation (1.12): $(Te^{-S})(B)=\int dA\,\delta(B-QA)\,\delta_{Ax}(A)e^{-S(A)}$. It is Gaussian: $=Z^{(0)}\exp(-\frac12\langle B,\mathcal A_1B\rangle)$ (1.14) with $\mathcal A_1$ gauge invariant (1.15). $S$ is the rescaling to the unit lattice.

Iterates (pp. 20-21). $\eta=L^{-k}$, $T_\eta$ the fine lattice, $T_1^{(k)}$ the coarse lattice ($T_1^{(k)}=T_1\cap L^k\mathbb Z^d$ in the $T_1$ picture), $Q_k$ with $L$ replaced by $L^k$ (1.18): $(Q_kA)_b=\sum_{x\in B^k(b_-)}\eta^{d+1}A([x,x(b)])$. $((ST)^ke^{-S})(B)=z^{(k)}\int dA\,\delta(B-Q_kA)\delta_{Ax}(Q_{k-1}A)\cdots\delta_{Ax}(A)e^{-S^\eta(A)}$ (1.17), $=Z_{k,Ax}\exp(-\frac12\langle B,\mathcal A_kB\rangle)$ (1.19); the composition law is $Q_2$ = "$Q$ with $L\to L^2$" (1.16). $\mathcal Q_k\lambda$: $A^\lambda=A-\partial^\eta\lambda$, $Q_kA^\lambda=Q_kA-\partial Q'_k\lambda$, $(Q'_k\lambda)(y)=\sum_{x\in B^k(y)}\eta^d\lambda(x)$ (1.20).

Gauge change (Sect. C, pp. 20-25). Rewrite $\langle\partial A,\partial A\rangle=\sum_\mu\langle A_\mu,\Delta A_\mu\rangle-\langle\partial^*A,\partial^*A\rangle$ (1.21) ($\Delta$ scalar $\eta$-lattice Laplacian, $\partial^*$ the divergence). Insert $1=\int d\lambda'\,\delta(Q'_k\lambda')\exp(-\frac1{2\alpha}\|\partial^*A^{\lambda'}\|^2)\big/\int\dots$ (1.22) to get gauge-fixing density $\mathscr G_\alpha(\partial^*A)$ (1.39), whose limit $\alpha\to0$ is Landau gauge: $\delta_R(\partial^*A)$ with $R=I-\Delta^{-1}Q'^*(Q'\Delta^{-2}Q'^*)^{-1}Q'\Delta^{-1}$ (1.38), the orthogonal projection onto $\Delta N(Q'_k)$, $N(Q'_k)=\{\lambda:Q'_k\lambda=0\}$ (1.41). Constants are treated as the zero mode; operators are extended by $0$ on constants (p. 22). Momentum representation (1.29)-(1.31): $\tilde f(p)=\sum\eta^de^{-ip\cdot x}f(x)$, $\Delta(p)=\sum_\mu|\partial_\mu(p)|^2$, $\partial_\mu(p)=(e^{i\eta p_\mu}-1)/\eta$, $u_k(p)=\prod_\mu\frac{e^{ip_\mu}-1}{e^{i\eta p_\mu}-1}=\prod_\mu\partial^1_\mu(p')/\partial_\mu(p)$ (the Fourier symbol of $Q'_k$, $p=p'+l$, $p'\in\tilde T_1^{(k)}$, $l\in 2\pi\mathbb Z^d$-translations), unit-lattice operator $\Delta_0(p')=\sum_\mu|e^{ip'_\mu}-1|^2$ with $\Delta_0(p')/\Delta(p'+l)\le O(1)/(1+|l|^2)$ (p. 24) and $|u_k(p'+l)|\le O(1)\prod_\mu(1+|l_\mu|)^{-1}$ (1.36).

The paper keeps three kinds of operators: the minimizer operator $H_k$ (1.60), $G=\mathcal A_a^{-1}$ (1.71)-(1.83), and the projection $P=\Delta^{-1}Q'^*(Q'\Delta^{-2}Q'^*)^{-1}Q'\Delta^{-1}$ (1.70) ($R=I-P$ on $\partial^*A$-configurations).

## 2. Main results

**Proposition 1.1 (p. 33).** The operator $G$ (defined by (1.71)-(1.83); here $G=\mathcal A_a^{-1}$) is a symmetric operator on $L^2(T_\eta)$ and
$$\|GJ\|,\ \|\nabla GJ\|,\ \|G\nabla^*J\|,\ \|\nabla G\nabla^*J\|,\ \|\nabla\nabla GJ\|,\ \|G\nabla^*\nabla^*J\|\le\gamma_0^{-1}\|J\|\quad(1.89)$$
with a positive constant $\gamma_0$ independent of $k$ and $T_\eta$, depending on $d$ only (if $a=1$). Hence $\mathcal A_a=G^{-1}\ge\gamma_0(\Delta+I)$ (1.90). (The symbol in the OCR/image reads "$\nabla V$"; I read "$\nabla\nabla G J$", "$G\nabla^*\nabla^*J$" etc. UNCERTAIN: which of the second-derivative terms exactly appear; the second line of (1.89) as printed is hard to read.) Proof: pp. 30-33, Fourier-multiplier computation of $G$ in (1.83) and bounds on the symbols $\Delta_0(p')\phi_\mu(p')\to a$ as $p'\to0$ (1.85)-(1.88).

**Proposition 1.2 (p. 35-36).** There is $\delta_0>0$ depending on $d$ only such that, for $x\in\tilde\Delta(y)$, $\operatorname{supp}J\subset\tilde\Delta(y')$:
$|(GJ)(x)|,|(\nabla GJ)(x)|,|(G\nabla^*J)(x)|,|(\Delta GJ)(x)|\le O(1)e^{-\delta_0|y-y'|}|J|$ (1.110); for $0\le\alpha<1$, $\zeta\in C_0^\infty(\tilde\Delta(y))$:
$\|\zeta\nabla GJ\|_\alpha,\ \|\zeta G\nabla^*J\|_\alpha\le O(1)e^{-\delta_0|y-y'|}(\|\zeta\|_\alpha+|\zeta|)|J|$ (1.111), with $O(1)\to\infty$ as $\alpha\to1$; for $0<\varepsilon<1$: $|(\nabla G\nabla^*J)(x)|\le O(1)e^{-\delta_0|y-y'|}(\|J\|_\varepsilon+|J|)$ (1.112), constant $\to\infty$ as $\varepsilon\to0$; (1.113) the Hoelder version $\|\zeta\nabla G\nabla^*J\|_\alpha\le O(1)e^{-\delta_0|y-y'|}(\|\zeta\|_\alpha+|\zeta|)(\|J\|_{\alpha+\varepsilon}+|J|)$ ($\alpha+\varepsilon<1$); finally $L^2$ decay $\|\zeta GJ\|,\|\zeta\nabla GJ\|,\dots\le O(1)e^{-\delta_0|y-y'|}|\zeta|\,\|J\|$ (1.114). All constants depend on $d$ (and $\alpha,\varepsilon$ where indicated), not on $k$, $\eta$, torus. Consequences (1.115)-(1.117): global bounds $|GJ|,|\nabla GJ|,\dots\le O(1)|J|$ etc. Remark (p. 36): the choice of the derivative combination (e.g. $\partial_\mu G\partial^*_\nu$, $\partial^*G\partial_\nu$) in (1.112)-(1.113) is arbitrary.

Other statements with equation numbers (derived identities rather than theorems):
- $\mathcal A_k$ is comparable to the unit-lattice Maxwell form: $\gamma_0\langle\partial_1B,\partial_1B\rangle\le\langle B,\mathcal A_kB\rangle\le\gamma_1\langle\partial_1B,\partial_1B\rangle$ (1.67), $d$-dependent constants, from the explicit symbol (1.66) (p. 29).
- $0<\gamma_0\le Q'_kG'^2_kQ'^*_k\le\gamma_1$ with $\gamma_1=a^{-2}$ (p. 26, uses (2.51), (2.52) of [2]).
- Minimizer formula $H_kB=GQ^*(QGQ^*)^{-1}B$ (1.103), with bounds $a^{-1}(\phi-1)\phi^{-1}\le QGQ^*\le a^{-1}I$ (1.100) and $H_kB$ has the bound $\frac1{|x'-x|^\alpha}|\partial_\nu(H_kB)_\mu(x')-\partial_\nu(H_kB)_\mu(x)|$ summable against $|p'+l|^\alpha|\partial_\nu(p'+l)|$ (p. 28-29, "see the proof of Lemma 2.4 in [2]").
- Projection formulas $A_0=PA=A-G\partial R\partial^*A-GQ^*(QGQ^*)^{-1}QA$ (1.106)-(1.107) (orthogonal projection onto $\{QA_0=0,R\partial^*A_0=0\}$ in the metric $\mathcal A_a$).

No inductive hypotheses (H.n) occur; the paper is non-inductive: everything is a calculation at fixed $k$ followed by $k$-uniform bounds.

## 3. Proof architecture

**Sect. A (pp. 17-20): action, symmetries, one RG transformation.** Defines $T$ via (1.8), (1.10)-(1.12), shows it is Gaussian (1.14)-(1.15) with gauge-invariant kernel $\mathcal A_1$. Routine: $T$ composed with a linear constraint gives a Gaussian.

**Sect. B (p. 20): composition.** $(ST)^2$ is again of the same form with $Q_2$ (1.16); induction gives (1.17)-(1.19). The structure is the same as in the scalar case: composition of linear averaging constraints is a single averaging constraint ($Q_{k-1}Q$-type consistency, p. 20 "easily seen"). The effective form $\mathcal A_k$ on $T^{(k)}_1$ is gauge invariant (invariant under $B\to B-\partial\lambda_1$) because of (1.20).

**Sect. C (pp. 20-26): gauge fixing.** Since $\langle\partial A,\partial A\rangle$ is degenerate on pure gauges, the integral (1.17) is undefined without an integration over gauge orbits; the paper inserts the identity (1.22) with the Faddeev-Popov-type integral, transforms (1.23), and evaluates the infimum over $\lambda$ with Lagrange multipliers (1.24)-(1.28) to find $\lambda_0=\Delta^{-1}\partial^*A-\Delta^{-2}Q'^*(Q'\Delta^{-2}Q'^*)^{-1}Q'\Delta^{-1}\partial^*A$ (1.26) and the value of the infimum. The momentum space computation (1.29)-(1.37) proves that the operator $R$ is a bounded projection: all the singular factors $\Delta(p)^{-1}$ at $p=0$ cancel by the unit-lattice symbols $\Delta_0$, because $u_k(p'+l)=0$ for $l\ne0$ when $p'=0$ (p. 23). The Landau limit $\alpha\to0$ (1.41) yields $\delta_R$ and the determinant $|\det(\Delta\restriction_{N(Q'_k)})|$. The alternative with an additional $\|a Q'_k\lambda\|^2$ term, $\Delta'_a=\Delta+aQ'^*Q'$, is sketched (1.42)-(1.45): there the scalar operator $G'_k=(\Delta+aQ'^*_kQ'_k)^{-1}$, "investigated in [2], its inverse is a bounded operator with good regularity properties described in Theorem of [2]", replaces $\Delta^{-1}$, and positivity of $Q'G'^2Q'^*$ follows from $0<Q'G'^2Q'^*\le a^{-2}$ and the Fourier representation (1.45) with bounds (2.51), (2.52) of [2].

**Sect. D (pp. 26-29): the minimizer operator $H_k$.** Defines $H_kB=A$, the minimizer of $\frac12\langle\partial A,\partial A\rangle$ with $Q_kA=B$, $R\partial^*A=0$ (1.47). Solved via Lagrange multipliers (1.48)-(1.59): $A=\Delta^{-1}Q^*(\phi^{-1}B'-\phi^{-1}\partial_1(\partial_1^*\phi^{-1}\partial_1)^{-1}\partial^*_1\phi^{-1}B')+\partial\Delta^{-2}Q'^*(\dots)^{-1}(\partial_1^*\phi^{-1}\partial_1)^{-1}\partial_1^*\phi^{-1}B'+Q^*B_0$ with $\phi=Q_k\Delta^{-1}Q^*_k$ (1.58) and $B=B'+B_0$ ($B_0$ the constant part). Momentum form (1.61)-(1.63) with $\phi_\mu(p')=\sum_l|u(p'+l)|^2|v_\mu(p'+l)|^2/\Delta(p'+l)$ (1.62), $0<\gamma_0\le\Delta_0\phi_\mu\le\gamma_1$. The consequences: $\mathcal A_k$ is defined by $\langle B,\mathcal A_kB\rangle=\langle\partial H_kB,\partial H_kB\rangle$ (1.64)-(1.65); in unit-lattice variables $\langle B,\mathcal A_kB\rangle=\langle\partial_1B,\sigma_k\partial_1B\rangle$ with the multiplier $\sigma_k(p')$ in (1.66); comparability with the plain unit-lattice action (1.67). Key mechanism: all symbols are expressed through quantities of the form $\sum_l|u|^2|v|^2/\Delta$, bounded above and below by $d$-dependent constants uniformly in $k$ (that is where the unit-lattice scaling $\Delta_0(p')\phi(p')\to a$ is used). This is the genuinely new gauge-theory content; it is a direct Fourier calculation, "well defined by continuity at $p'=0$" repeatedly invoked.

**Sect. E (pp. 29-35): the operator $G$.** With the gauge-fixed form $\langle A,\mathcal A_aA\rangle=\langle A,\partial^*\partial A\rangle+\langle A,\partial R\partial^*A\rangle+a\langle A,Q^*QA\rangle=\langle A,\Delta A\rangle-\langle A,\partial P\partial^*A\rangle+a\langle A,Q^*QA\rangle$ (1.69), positivity is proved (1.72)-(1.74), and $G=\mathcal A_a^{-1}$ is calculated in closed form (1.73)-(1.83): after splitting constants, apply $Q\Delta^{-1}$, solve for $Q A$ using $\phi=I+aQ\Delta^{-1}Q^*$ (1.76), then for $Q'\Delta^{-1}\partial^*A$ using positivity of $\partial_1^*\phi^{-1}\partial_1$ (since $\phi^{-1}\ge\alpha>0$, p. 31). The final formula is (1.81)/(1.83) in momentum space. The paper then shows $H_kB=GQ^*(QGQ^*)^{-1}B$ (1.103), the relations $R\partial^*GQ^*=0$ (1.95), $R\partial^*G\partial=\partial^*G\partial R=R$ (1.97), $QGQ^*=a^{-1}I-a^{-1}\phi^{-1}+a^{-1}\phi^{-1}\partial_1(\partial_1^*\phi^{-1}\partial_1)^{-1}\partial_1^*\phi^{-1}$ (1.99), and the orthogonal projection $P$-type formulas (1.104)-(1.107) in the metric $\mathcal A_a$. Proposition 1.1 is then a statement about the symbol (1.83): "we will prove that $G$ is a bounded operator by transforming (1.83) similar to (1.63)": the terms with $l'=l$ cancel, giving functions well defined and bounded at $p'=0$ (1.87)-(1.88) (pp. 32-33).

**Sect. F (pp. 35-40): regularity and decay.** The main statement is Prop. 1.2. Two proofs:
1. *Random walk expansion* (pp. 36-39). Cubes of size $M_0=L^{m_0}$ ($m_0$ depending on $d$ only) in $T^{(k+m_0)}_{M_0}$, double cubes $\square_z$ of size $2M_0$ centered at $z$; partition of unity $h_z(x)=\prod_\mu h((x_\mu-z_\mu)/M_0)$, $h\in C_0^\infty(]-\frac23,\frac23[)$, $h=1$ on $[-\frac13,\frac13]$, $\sum_nh^2(t-n)=1$, so $\sum_zh_z^2=1$ (1.118). Approximate inverse $C_0=\sum_zh_zGh_z$ (1.119). Commutator computations: $\mathcal A_ahA=h\mathcal A_aA-K(h)A$ (1.121) with $K(h)A=\sum_{b\in\text{best}(x)}(\partial h)(b)(\partial A)(b)-(\Delta h)A+S^*(\partial h)QA-Q^*S(\partial h)A+P_1(\partial h)A$ (here $S$ is a bond-localized operator, NOT the rescaling; the OCR text of the exact form of $K(h)$ is hard to read, UNCERTAIN in detail); $\mathcal A_aC_0=I-R$, $R=\sum_zK(h_z)Gh_z$ (1.122), hence the random walk expansion (1.123): $G=\sum_{\omega}h_{\omega_0}Gh_{\omega_0}K(h_{\omega_1})Gh_{\omega_1}\cdots K(h_{\omega_n})Gh_{\omega_n}$, with sum over sequences of neighbouring cubes. Convergence needs $\|K(h_z)\|\le O(M_0^{-1})$ small, i.e. $M_0$ large depending on $d$ only. Estimates: $K(h_{z_1})$ has norm $O(M_0^{-1})$ times $e^{-2\delta_0|z_1-z_2|}$ (1.128), using the exponential decay of $\partial P\partial^*$, namely $|(\partial P\partial^*)_{\mu\nu}(x,x')|\le O(1)e^{-\delta_0'|x-x'|}$ and its Hoelder version (1.126)-(1.127), which "follow from the representation $P=G'Q'^*(Q'G'^2Q'^*)^{-1}Q'G'$, from Lemma 2.4 of [2], the representation (1.45) and the analyticity method of proving an exponential decay (see the proof of Lemma 2.4 in [2])". Then (1.130)-(1.131): sum over walks $\le\sum_n O(1)^{2n}M_0^{-n}(\sum_xe^{-\delta_0M_0|x|})^n<\infty$, which gives (1.113); the others "exactly the same". The author states explicitly (p. 37) "We will be very sketchy with arguments because they are almost the same as in [2]".
2. *Alternative via $G'$* (pp. 39-40). Identity $G=G_0+G_0\partial P\partial^*G$ (1.132) with $G_0=(\Delta+aQ^*Q)^{-1}$, which has a simple explicit momentum representation (1.134), $G_0J=\Delta^{-1}J-a\Delta^{-1}Q^*\phi^{-1}Q\Delta^{-1}J$; reduction $G_0=G'+G'(aQ'^*Q'-aQ^*Q)G_0$ (1.133) to the scalar operator $G'$ of [2] (using weak $L^2$ bounds (1.89), (1.114) for $G_0$); and the multiscale (Gaussian-composed) representation (1.135)
$$G'_k=C^{(0),\eta}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}a_j^2(L^j\eta)^{-4}G'^\eta_jQ'^*_jC^{(j),L^j\eta}Q'_jG'^\eta_j,$$
(with $C^{(j)}$ covariances of scale $j$ and $a_j$ scale-dependent coefficients; the printed (1.135) uses $G_j'^{\eta}$ with superscript indicating the lattice spacing; UNCERTAIN about the exact meaning of superscripts), and the estimates (1.136)-(1.137) quoting "(2.35)-(2.37) of Lemma 2.4 in [2]" (and (2.34) of [2], "equality ... with $\square$ replaced by the whole torus") to get $|\partial_\mu G'_k\partial^*_\nu f(x)|\le O(1)e^{-\frac12\delta_0|y-y'|}(\|f\|_\varepsilon+|f|)$, i.e. (1.112) with $\delta_0=\frac12\delta_0'$. The paper says this "may serve as a basis of another proof".

Routine vs. genuine difficulty. Routine: Gaussian composition, Lagrange multiplier algebra, Fourier computations of symbols. Genuine difficulties: (i) the cancellations at $p'=0$ in the gauge-fixed momentum formulae (the whole of Sects. C-E); (ii) the exponential decay and regularity of $G$, which are essentially transferred from [2] = E3 (Hoelder-regular exponential decay of $(\Delta+aQ'^*Q')^{-1}$, plus Lemma 2.4 of [2]). The decay proof here is a sketch.

## 6. Constants and parameters

- $d$ (dimension) fixes everything.
- $L$ (block factor, an integer $>1$ presumably; no restriction stated), $k$ (number of RG steps), $\eta=L^{-k}$.
- $a>0$ (coefficient of $Q^*Q$ in $\mathcal A_a$; "we will take eventually $a=1$", p. 25; bounds independent of $a$ "if we put $a=1$", p. 33; general $a$ affects constants through $\gamma_1=a^{-2}$, p. 26, and positivity $\phi\ge1$, $\alpha>0$ on p. 31).
- $\alpha$ (gauge-fixing parameter, $\to0$: Landau) (1.22). NOT the same as the Hoelder exponent in (1.108)-(1.113) ($\alpha$ in $\|\cdot\|_\alpha$), nor the $\alpha$ of the later O(N) series.

- $\delta_0,\delta_0'$ decay rates, $d$ only, $\delta_0=\frac12\delta_0'$ via the second route.

## 10. Relevance to the formalization

Interfaces: none of E4's two propositions would become an interface for the O(N) formalization, because they refer to the gauge operator. The scalar versions that are needed are (i) uniform-in-$k$ exponential decay and Hoelder regularity of $(-\Delta^D_\Omega+Q^*aQ+\nu+\alpha)^{-1}$ (P2 (2.57) type), E5, and (ii) uniform-in-$k$ positivity and decay of the Gaussian fluctuation covariance $C^{(k)}$ (P4 (2.24)-(2.25), (2.37)), E3 Sect. 5.

Reusable arguments:
- *Gaussian block-spin composition* (Sects. A-B), explicit effective form: scalar analogue is elementary and would be fully formalizable early (finite-dimensional Gaussian algebra; P2 §4 Lemma 4.1 equivalent).
- *Closed formulas for minimizer, effective form and projection through Lagrange multipliers* ($H_k=GQ^*(QGQ^*)^{-1}$, $\mathcal A=(QGQ^*)^{-1}$, $P=\dots$ (1.104)-(1.107)): in the scalar case, these are standard block-Schur-complement formulas that can be proved directly in finite-dimensional linear algebra. Statements such as "$\mathcal A_k=(QGQ^*)^{-1}$ restricted ..." and the bound $0<QGQ^*\le a^{-1}$ (1.100) are the kind of identity worth formalizing generally.
- *Random walk expansion* (1.118)-(1.131): the template argument (partition of unity, commutator small in $M_0$, convergent sum over walks, exponential decay in cube distance) is the method P3 uses. A Lean proof could follow either this or a Combes-Thomas argument.

Possible alternative proofs (suggestion, not a claim). For the Green's function bounds of E5 / P2 (2.57), the following look available and more standard than the random walk expansion: (a) Combes-Thomas for $(-\Delta+Q^*aQ+\nu+\alpha)^{-1}$ on $L^2$: conjugation by $e^{\langle q,x\rangle}$ with $|q|$ small is a small bounded perturbation, because $Q^*aQ$ is block-diagonal and commutes with the multiplier up to a factor $e^{O(L^j\eta|q|)}$; this requires $|q|\lesssim(L^j\eta)^{-1}\gamma_0^{1/2}$ and gives the scale-weighted exponential decay in $L^2$ and, with Caccioppoli/discrete elliptic regularity, pointwise decay and Hoelder bounds; positivity from $-\Delta\ge0$ and $Q^*aQ$ bounded below on the kernel of $\Delta$-complement as in P3 (3.10); (b) the single-step composition formula (1.135)/E3 (2.34) together with (a) at one scale and summation over scales (geometric series from the $L^{-j}$ factors). The step E4 flags (1.133)-(1.134), $G_0=G'+G'(aQ'^*Q'-aQ^*Q)G_0$, i.e. perturbing between two averaging operators, is a Neumann series type argument; also possible in the scalar case. The one place where a Combes-Thomas argument may need extra work is the uniformity of the Hoelder constant ($O(\alpha^{-d-1})$ in P2 p. 611), where discrete elliptic regularity (e.g. a Moser/De Giorgi-type or lattice Harnack argument) would be needed.

Gauge-specific and irrelevant for O(N): contours $\Gamma_{y,x}$, axial gauge $\delta_{Ax}$, Faddeev-Popov and Landau gauge, $\partial P\partial^*$, $R$, $\phi_\mu$ and all of Sects. C-E except as a pattern.

Most valuable thing for the project: E4 does not contain the Green's function estimates of P2/P3; to settle the defective citations (CMP 96 (2.57), (2.33), Prop. 2.2, Sect. A) one needs E5 (and E3 for Sect. 5 and Lemma 2.4). E4's value is as the template for Sect. F's random walk expansion, as the source of the explicit formulas (1.100), (1.103), (1.135) and as confirmation that, in this author's framework, the scalar operator $(\Delta+aQ^*Q)^{-1}$ is the base case from which the gauge operator is built (not the other way around), so that the scalar bounds of E3 and E5 are the load-bearing external results for P2 and P3.
