Summary of the paper. In the gauge-theory R-operation of E9 (the first part), a large-field component $Z$ of the effective density, which is small (contained in a cube of size $100MR_k$) and in which no new large-field regions were created in the last $N$ steps, is handled by a ratio (1.1): the integral over the gauge-field variable $V'$ in the numerator, with the restriction $\chi_{k,1/2}$, is divided by the same integral without the large-field terms. This paper does the rest: (A) the Wilson-action terms of numerator and denominator are shown to cancel up to bounded and small terms around a critical configuration (pp. 356-368); (B) all other terms of the effective action are re-expanded around the new background field by localization expansions and resummed into terms localized in domains $Y$ with exponential decay (pp. 368-378); (C) the operator $\mathbf T'_k(X)$ (large-field integral operator) is defined (1.71), the R-operation is written out (1.72), and the integral operators are bounded by explicit large-field factors (1.73)-(1.79) (pp. 378-383); (D) the number $\kappa_j(Z)$ and the step-counting induction (1.80)-(1.89) show that these bounds control the number of steps needed, and the bound (1.89) (pp. 383-387); (E) the polymer (Mayer) expansion of the curly bracket in (1.72), its bounds and its exponentiation into new terms $\mathbf R'^{(k)}$ and boundary terms $\mathbf B'^{(k)}$ (pp. 387-391); (F) the final form (1.104) of $\mathbf R\rho_k$. It concludes the proof of Theorem 1 of the paper, the inductive description of the effective densities of four-dimensional lattice gauge theories for effective couplings in $]0,\gamma[$ with $\gamma$ small (UV stability: bound (0.1), p. 355-356).

The O(N) series P5-P7 is its scalar analogue: P5 = E8 Sect. 2, P6 = the proof of the inductive description + the counter $K$, P7 = E9 + E10. ## 1. Setting and notation

Everything is the gauge-theory setting of E6-E9 (compact group $G$-valued lattice gauge field $U$, four dimensions, $U=\exp(i\,\cdot)$). I record only what is needed to read the combinatorics and the localization/exponentiation, and mark what is not defined in this paper.

- Lattice and scales. Fine lattice $T_\eta$, $\eta=L^{-k}$; $M$-cubes (large cubes, $M=L^m$), partition $\pi_k$ of the lattice into $M$-cubes at scale $k$. $\mathcal Q_k$ = $k$-th averaging operation (linear), $M^k(U)$ = $k$-fold averaged (non-linear, group-valued) field. $g_k$ = effective coupling at step $k$ ($g_k^{-2}$ plays the role of $\beta_k$ of the O(N) papers), $g_k$ small; $(g''_k(\cdot))^{-2}$ = coupling function equal to $g_k^{-2}$ outside, other values inside. $\varepsilon_k$ = size of the small-field restriction (analyticity radius of the previous steps), $\alpha_{0,k},\alpha_{1,k}$ = enlarged analyticity radii ($\mathcal{\tilde U}_k^c(Z,\alpha_{0,k},\alpha_{1,k})$ = space of extended configurations, p. 358, 366).
- Large-field data (all from [III], [IV], not re-defined here). $\Omega_k$ = $k$-th small-field domain; $\Omega''^{\sim}_{k+1}$ etc. = successive enlargements of domains ($\sim$ = add a layer of $M$-cubes at the relevant scale); $Z_k$ = large-field region at step $k$; $P_j,Q_j,R_j$ = pieces of $Z_j$ created by the failing of the three kinds of small-field characteristic functions $\chi_j(P_j),\chi_j^c(Q_j),\chi^{(j-1)c}(R_j)$ (p. 380); $\chi'$ = the characteristic function of the restrictions on the new variable (1.82)[IV]; conditions (i), (ii) (see below). Determining set $\mathbf B_k$ = collection of sets $\{\Gamma_j\}$ describing at each scale where the field is given by its average (E8 (2.14)).
- Radii. $R_j=R_0(\log(\cdot))^2$-type buffer widths with $R_j$ comparable to the number of steps (p. 361: "we have assumed here that $N\le R_k$"); $N$ is the number of "preparatory" renormalization steps (E9), with conditions "$N\ge$ ..." written as $L^{-N}\le(\log g_k^{-2})^{-\nu}$, $\nu\ge 2p_0+dr_0$ (p. 363).
- Functions $p_0,p_1$: $p_0(g_j)$ is the large-field threshold function ($p_0(g)\sim A_0(\log g^{-1})^{p_0}$, compare P5 p. 498: $p_0(\beta_j)=A_0(\log\beta_ja)^{p_0}$, $\delta_j=(\beta_ja)^{-1/2}p_0(\beta_j)$). $p_0^2(g_j)$ appears in all large-field factors (e.g. (1.79)); $p_1$, $A_1$ analogously; $B_3,B_5$ are constants from [IV] and [III].
- Size functions. $d_k(Y)$ for $Y\in\mathcal D_k$ = scale-$k$ distance function of domains (the usual $M^{-1}\times$ length of a shortest tree; $d_{k,Z}(Y)$ in (1.67) defined as "$M^{-1}$ (the length of a shortest tree graph contained in $Y$ and intersecting all $M$-cubes of the components of $Y\setminus Z$)"). $d'_j(Z)$ = "linear size defined in terms of $MR_j$-cubes instead of $M$-cubes" (p. 380). Its precise definition is not in this paper (UNCERTAIN: from p. 384-385 it behaves like a tree-length: it is zero for a single $MR_j$-cube, $d'_n(S^{n-j}(Z))\le(63)^d(MR_n)^{-d}|Z^{(n-j)}|\le(63)^d3\cdot2^{d-1}d'_n(Z^{(n-j)})$ if the right side is nonzero, and $\le(64)^d$ if it is zero; and $|Z_j|\le O(1)M^dR_j^d d'_j(Z_j)$ on p. 380).
- Operation $S$ (p. 384). For a domain $Z$ that is a union of $MR_j$-cubes of the lattice $T_\xi$, $\xi=L^{-j}$: $Z'$ = cover of $Z$ by the smallest union of $LMR_{j+1}$-cubes, and $S(Z)=Z'^{\sim10}$ (ten layers of such cubes added; printed "$Z'^{-10}$"/"$\sim10$" in the OCR-like rendering of p. 384; I read the page image as "$\sim 10$"). $S$ is the geometric shadow of "one RG step with no new large field in a neighbourhood of $Z$": the large-field region of the next step is $S(Z)$ (added ten layers). $S^m$ is the $m$-fold iterate, $S^{n-j}(Z)$ considered at scale $L^{-n}$.
- Conditions (i), (ii) (E9 Sect. 1, p. 177). A component of the large-field region is in the class treated by $\mathbf R$ if (i) it is contained in a cube of size $100MR_k$, and (ii) in the preceding $N$ steps no new large-field regions were created inside it, and the previous regions contained in it satisfy (i) on the corresponding scales. E10 calls these components of the "first class" $\{X_1,\dots,X_n\}$ (the ones whose gauge-fixing $\delta$-functions are removed); the others, "second class" $\{Y_1,\dots,Y_m\}$, keep their integral operators (p. 378). NOTE the naming is opposite to P7: in P7 "components of the second kind" are those with $K=0$ (to be renormalized) and "first kind" are those with $K>0$ left unchanged. In E10, first class = satisfy (i),(ii) = analogue of P7's second kind; second class = analogue of P7's first kind.
- Local objects: critical configuration $U_0$ (minimizer of $U\mapsto A(U)$ under $M_{\mathbf B_k(Z)}(U)=M_{\mathbf B_k(Z)}(\mathcal Q^*_kV_k)$ on $Z\setminus\Lambda$, (1.3)), $\Lambda$ = rectangular parallelepiped of size $\le100M$, $G_0$ = gauge-fixing graph on $\Lambda$ (axial gauge), $\zeta_0,\zeta_1$ = smooth cutoffs, $H_{1,k}$, $\mathbf H''_{k,Z}$, $\mathbf H_{\mathbf B_1}$ = minimizer operators (E4, E5, E9), $\mathbf A_1(\zeta_0)$ = quadratic form of the action, $\mathcal D_k$ = family of admissible localization domains, $\mathbf R(X)$/$\mathbf B(X)$ = renormalization/boundary terms of the effective action ($\mathbf E,\mathbf R,\mathbf B$ terms), $\sigma$ = small term, $\mathbf T'_k$, $\mathbf T_k$ = large-field integral operators.
- Small numbers: $\beta,\beta_0$ in this paper are small *numbers* in exponents like $(1+3\beta)\kappa d$ and $(1+\beta_0)^{-1}$ (p. 389: "take $\beta_0=1/7$"), NOT inverse temperatures. $\alpha$ = small activity constant, $\gamma$ = small coupling bound, $\kappa$ = large decay rate, $\delta$ = decay parameter of the Green's functions, $\gamma_0$ = positivity constant of the quadratic form ($\gamma_0=1/2$ is allowed in the large-field factor, p. 380).

## 2. Main results

The paper contains one numbered theorem and one numbered result by reference; everything else is a chain of bounds.

**Theorem 1 (p. 355).** "If the sequence of the effective coupling constants is contained in an interval $]0,\gamma[$ with a sufficiently small positive $\gamma$, then the effective densities $\rho_k$ have the form, and satisfy all the conditions and bounds, described in Sect. 2 [III]." (Completed on p. 391: "This completes the proof of Theorem 1 and Corollary 3".) Consequence (0.1), p. 355-356: UV stability bounds $\chi_k\exp[-\frac1{g_k^2}A(U_k)-E_-|T_\eta|]\le\rho_k\le\exp E_+|T_\eta|$ with $E_\pm$ independent of $k,T_\eta,U_k$. The paper says Theorem 2 of [I] (p. 355; probably the printed numbering is of the older manuscript) removes the assumption on the effective couplings; this is not proved here.

**The step-counting statement, p. 384 ((1.80)), the combinatorial core (cited by P6 as "Prop. 3.2").** Not numbered, stated in prose. Exact content, with the definitions it uses:

> Let $Z$ be a connected domain of the large-field region at step $j$ (union of $MR_j$-cubes at scale $L^{-j}$), and let $\kappa_j(Z)$ be the numbers defined inductively in (1.82)-(1.85). Let $K=K_j(Z)$ be "the smallest positive integer having the property that the domain $S^K(Z)$, considered as a domain in the lattice of the scale $L^{-(j+K)}$, satisfies the conditions (i), (ii) with $N=R_j$." Then
> $$\kappa_j(Z)\ \ge\ \sum_{n=j+1}^{j+K}O(1)\,M^dR_n^{d+1}\,d'_n\big(S^{n-j}(Z)\big).\tag{1.80}$$
> "The factor $\exp(-\kappa_j(Z))$ controls $K$ renormalization steps, under the assumption that no large fields are created in these steps."

Here "$O(1)$" on the right is the same absolute constant that appears in the per-step growth factor of (1.79), (1.81)-(1.88) (it is fixed by the estimates of Sect. 1; the paper does not name it). The statement is meant for domains which arise in the construction (obtained from a union of large-field regions $Z_i^{(j)}$ with $Z\subset\bigcup(Z_j^{(i)})^{\sim2}$, (1.76)).

**Consequence (1.89) (p. 387).** For $X$ as in definition (1.71), i.e. satisfying the assumption of the statement with $K=0$, $\kappa_k(X)\ge0$, hence
$$\mathbf T'_k(X)1\le\exp\big(-2(1+\beta_0)^{-1}p_0(g_k)\big),\tag{1.89}$$
and for regions satisfying more, the improved bound with additional factor $\exp(-\kappa_k d_k(X))$ (stated in prose after (1.89)), which "implies the inequality (2.50) [III], hence Corollary 3".

**Large-field integral operator bound (1.79) (p. 383).**
$$\mathbf T'_k(X)1\le\sup\exp\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^kO(1)M^dR_j^{d+1}d'_j(Z_j)\Big\}\prod_{j=1}^k\prod_i\exp\!\Big(-\tfrac12\gamma_0A_1^2p_0^2(g_j)\big(d'_j(Z_j^{(i)})+1\big)-2p_0(g_j)\Big)\prod{}'\exp(-p_0(g_j)),$$
sup over admissible domains/conditions of the previous sections; $\prod'$ over components of $Z_j$ satisfying (i), (ii) "for which some large fields are created during the preparatory steps"; $Z_j^{(i)}$ = components of the cover of the large-field region $P_j\cup Q_j\cup R_j$ by $MR_j$-cubes. (Reading of the second-line coefficients: the page shows $\frac12\gamma_0A_1^2p_0^2$ here and $\frac14\gamma_0$ in (1.82); see section 7.)

**Positivity/analyticity of $\mathbf T'_k$, (1.73)-(1.75) (p. 380).** For an extended configuration $(\mathbf U,\mathbf J)$ with small complex term $\sigma$ in the exponent: $|\mathbf T'_k(X,(\mathbf U,\mathbf J))F|\le\mathbf T'_k(X,(U,0))\sup e^{|\sigma|}|F|$, $|\mathbf T'_k(X,(\mathbf U,\mathbf J))1|\ge\mathbf T'_k(X,(U,0))1e^{-2\sup|\sigma|}$, hence $|(\mathbf T'_k(X,(\mathbf U,\mathbf J))1)^{-1}\mathbf T'_k(X,(\mathbf U,\mathbf J))F|\le e^{3\sup|\sigma|}\sup|F|$. This is the key to normalizing by $\mathbf T'_k1$.

**Polymer/exponentiation results (pp. 387-391).** Activities $F(X')$ (1.91) obey $|F(X')|\le c_1\exp(-(1+\beta)\kappa d_{k,\cup Y_i}(X'))$ (1.97) with $c_1=\exp(-p_0(g_k))$ if $X'$ does not intersect $\bigcup Y_i$ and $c_1=\alpha^{1/3}$ otherwise; therefore $\{\cdots\}=\exp\sum\mathbf R'^{(k)}(X)$ (1.98) with $|\mathbf R'^{(k)}(X,(\mathbf U,\mathbf J))|\le O(1)c_1\exp(-(1+\frac12\beta)\kappa d_{k,\cup Y_i}(X))$ (1.99); the terms with $X$ disjoint from $Z_k\cup\bigcup Y_i$ satisfy $|\mathbf R^{(k)}(X)|\le\exp(-p_0(g_k))\exp(-\kappa d_k(X))$ (1.100). Final representation of the effective density: (1.101), (1.102), (1.104) below.

No hypotheses (H.1)-(H.n) are listed here; the inductive hypotheses are those of Sect. 2 of [III] (CMP 119), not reproduced.

## 3. Proof architecture

All proofs are one long chain of "expand, localize, estimate" steps in the style of [I], [III], [IV]; many steps are explicitly marked as "the same as", "we omit details". Letters A-G below follow the order of the paper.

**A. Cancellation of the Wilson term (pp. 356-368).** Starting point (1.1): the result of the R'-operation on one term of the density is a quotient. Numerator: $\chi_k\chi_{k,\Lambda}\delta_{G_0}(V'_k)\chi\exp[-g_k^{-2}A(\zeta_0,U_{k,Z}(V'_kV_\Lambda))]\cdot\int dV'\Gamma_{B_0}\delta_{T_0}(V')\chi'\chi_{k,1/2}\exp A''_k$; denominator: $\int dV'\Gamma_\Lambda\delta_{G_0}(V')\chi\exp[-g_k^{-2}A(\zeta_0,U_{k,Z}(V'V_\Lambda))]$. The problem: the Wilson terms carry $g_k^{-2}$, so any non-cancelling piece spoils the bounds.

 A1 (pp. 356-358, denominator). Write $V'=\exp ig_kB'$. Expansion (1.2) of the exponent around the critical configuration $U_0$ (minimizer of $A$ on $Z\setminus\Lambda$ with fixed block averages, (1.3)): constant $g_k^{-2}A(\zeta_0,U_0)$ + $-\frac12\langle H_{1,k}B',A_1(\zeta_0)H_{1,k}B'\rangle$ + linear term + cubic remainder. The linear term $\langle DH_{1,k}B',\zeta\eta^{-2}\mathrm{Im}\,\partial U_0\rangle$ vanishes to leading order by the Euler-Lagrange condition (1.4) ($J_0=0$ on $\Lambda$, because $\delta A$ supported in $\Lambda$ satisfies the averaging constraint); the rest, (1.5), is bounded by exponential decay of the minimizer and localization of $1-\zeta_0$ and of $B'$ by $3B_3M_0A_0p_0(g_k)\exp(-\delta\,\mathrm{dist}(\Omega_k,\Lambda))3\varepsilon_k|\Lambda|<3B_3M_0A_0^2p_0^2(g_k)\exp(-R_k)(100M)^4$ (1.6), "arbitrarily small" (it is enough to have it $\le1$). The cubic remainder is $O(g_k^{1-\beta_0})|\Lambda|$.
 Quadratic form: lower bound $\langle H_{1,k}B',A_1(\zeta_0)H_{1,k}B'\rangle\ge\gamma_0\|\partial B'\|^2-O(1)(M^6R_k\varepsilon_k+\exp(-R_k))\|B'\|^2$ (1.7), obtained by replacing $\zeta_0$ by 1 (the form changes by $O(\exp(-R_k))$) and using the $k$-th minimizer bound (1.67)[10]. Poincaré-type inequality on $\Lambda$ in the axial gauge (1.8): $\sum_{b\in\Lambda}|B'(b)|^2\le d(100M)^{d+1}\sum_p|\partial B'(p)|^2$ (constant $(100M)^3$ in the gauge $B'(\langle x-e_1,x\rangle)=0$); conclusion (1.9): form $\ge\frac{\gamma_0}{2d(100M)^5}\|B'\|^2$.
 Bound below of the integral: $\ge\exp(-O(M^4))$; but one needs it for the analytically extended configurations ($V_k$ replaced by averages $M^k(\mathbf U)$, $\mathbf U\in\mathcal U^c_k(Z,\alpha_{0,k},\alpha_{1,k})$) with $\varepsilon_k$ replaced by $O(\alpha_{0,k}+\alpha_{1,k})$.
 Proposition 1 [IV] (existence and analyticity of the critical point $V'_\Lambda$ of $V'\mapsto A(U_{k,Z}(V'V_\Lambda))$) is re-proved (pp. 359) by the contraction/fixed point theorem: (1.12)-(1.13) with $P_0$ = projection onto the gauge-fixed subspace, inverse of $P_0H^*A_1H_{1,k}P_0$ bounded by $\gamma_0^{-1}2d(100M)^5$, solution bounded by $2\gamma_0^{-1}2d(100M)^5B_3^24\varepsilon_k$; "Proposition 4 [15]" and the contractive fixed point theorem. Result: (1.10) $(\int\cdots)^{-1}=\exp[+g_k^{-2}A(\zeta_0,U_0)+I(U_0)-E_k(\Lambda)]$ with $E_k(\Lambda)=(-\frac12d(g)\log g_k^{-2}+\log\sigma_0)|\Lambda^{(k)}\setminus G_0|$ and $|I(U_0)|<O(1)\log M|\Lambda|<O(1)M^5$ (1.11). The factor $g_k^{-2}A(\zeta_0,U_0)$ is the large term that must cancel against the numerator.

 A2 (pp. 359-368, numerator). Split $A(1/(g''_k(\cdot))^2,U''_k)=g_k^{-2}A(U''_k)+A(1/(g''_k)^2-g_k^{-2},U''_k)$ (1.14), $A(U''_k)=A(\zeta_0,U''_k)+A(1-\zeta_0,U''_k)$ (1.15). Localize $U''_k$ in $Z$ by (1.16) ($U''_k=U''_{k,Z}(M'(U''_k))$ with an explicit $\mathbf H''_{k,Z}$ whose argument is supported in a layer of width $2M_1$ at $\partial Z$). Taylor-expand in $\mathbf H''_{k,Z}$ (1.17); the terms are small by exponential decay (inequality (190)[15]). For the main term, write $\zeta=\zeta_0(1-\zeta_1)$ and $A(\zeta_0,U''_{k,Z})=A(\zeta_0(1-\zeta_1),U''_{k,Z})+A(\zeta_1,U''_{k,Z})$ (1.18), with $V'=\exp ig_kB$, expand around the new background $U^0_{k,Z}$ (1.19)-(1.20): Wilson term $A(\zeta,U^0_{k,Z})$ + linear term $g_k\langle DH''_{1,k,Z}B,\zeta\eta^{-2}\mathrm{Im}\,\partial U^0_{k,Z}\rangle$ + quadratic form $\frac12g_k^2\langle H''B,A_1H''B\rangle$ + cubic. The quadratic form is a sum of nonnegative terms $\sum_p\eta^d\zeta(x(p))|(DH''_{1,k,Z}B)(p)|^2$ (1.21) plus small terms; it is kept (it is the Gaussian factor in $dB\,\sigma(g_kB)\ldots\exp[-\frac12\langle DH''B,\zeta DH''B\rangle]$). The cubic and higher terms are bounded by $O(1)|\mathbf B_0|\delta_k^3\le g_k^2O(1)(100MR_k)^dN^2\delta_k^3$... small after division by $g_k^2$.
 The linear term would be large but vanishes on the critical configuration: $U^0_{k,Z}$ differs from $U_0$ only slightly; by the representation (1.22) $U^0_{k,Z}=U_{\mathbf B_1}([M'(U^0_{k,Z})(M'(U_0^{(AL)}))^{-1}]M'(U_0^{(AL)}))$ with determining set $\mathbf B_1=\mathbf B''_k(Z)\cup\mathbf B_h(\Omega''^{\sim2}_{h+1})$, and $\mathbf H_{\mathbf B_1}$ exponentially small off the boundary layer (1.23), the Wilson action around $U^0_{\mathbf B_1}$ splits again like (1.17) (1.24); the new background is $U^0_{\mathbf B_1}=U_{\mathbf B_1}(M'(U^{(AL)}_0))$ (1.25). Result: (1.104) $\mathbf R\rho_k=\sum_{Z_k}\sum_{\{Y_1..Y_m\}}\chi_k(\ldots)(\sum\mathbf T''_k(Z_k))\prod_i\chi_k^c(Y_i^{-6})\chi_{k,A_i}\chi_i\delta_{G_i}(V_kV_{A_i}^{-1})(\mathbf T'_k(Y_i)1)\cdot\exp[A'_k+\sum\mathbf R'^{(k)}(X)+\sum\mathbf B'^{(k)}(X)]$. Now the $Y_i$ are still large-field domains but "there are no integral operations connected with them, only characteristic functions, $\delta$-functions and the functions $\mathbf T'_k(Y_i)1$ multiplying the action density, hence $\mathbf T'_k(Y_i)$ is the multiplication operation"; these functions depend only on the new field variables on $Y_i$, so they do not take part in later renormalization steps; contributions from previous large-field regions are isolated in the boundary terms and in $\mathbf T'_k(Y_i)1$. "The representation (1.104) can be used alternatively in the inductive description of the effective actions."

### 3(F). The step-counting induction, pp. 383-387 (full proof structure)

This is the proof that is "cited" by P6 Prop. 3.2. I give it in full from the page images.

**Setting and the induction hypothesis.** The right-hand side of (1.79) is a product of factors, one per renormalization step $j\le k$ and per large-field component. Factorize it into components. For a component $Z$ of $Z_j$ let $j(Z)$ be the index of the first large-field region contained in $Z$; the factor attached to $Z$ is written $\exp(-\kappa_j(Z)-2p_0(g_{j(Z)}))$ (p. 384). The number $\kappa_j(Z)$ is defined inductively:

- $j=1$ (1.82). Let $Z$ be a component of $Z_1$; it is built from first-step large-field regions $Z_1^{(i)}$ with $Z\subset\bigcup_i(Z_1^{(i)})^{\sim2}$. Then $d'_1((Z_1^{(i)})^{\sim3})\le7^d(3\cdot2^{d-1}d'_1(Z_1^{(i)})+2^d)\le2(14)^d(d'_1(Z_1^{(i)})+\frac12)$ and $d'_1(Z)+1\le2(14)^d\sum_i(d'_1(Z_1^{(i)})+1)$, so
 $$\prod_i\exp\big(-\tfrac12\gamma_0A_1^2p_0^2(g_1)(d'_1(Z^{(i)}_1)+1)\big)\le\exp\big(-\tfrac1{4}\gamma_0(14)^{-d}A_1^2p_0^2(g_1)(d'_1(Z)+1)\big),$$
 and
 $$\kappa_1(Z)\ge\tfrac14\gamma_0(14)^{-d}A_1^2p_0^2(g_1)\big(d'_1(Z)+1\big)-O(1)M^dR_1^{d+1}d'_1(Z).\tag{1.82}$$
 (printed constant $\frac14\gamma_0$ on the page image of p. 385; the second-line constant in (1.79) is $\frac12\gamma_0$; consistent up to the covering factor 2 shown above.)
- Inductive step, three cases (pp. 385-386):
 1. (1.83) $Z=S(Z_0)$ with $Z_0$ a component of $Z_j$ and no new large field introduced in the last step: $\kappa_{j+1}(Z)=\kappa_j(Z_0)-O(1)M^dR_{j+1}^{d+1}d'_{j+1}(Z)$. (The loss is the per-step growth factor from (1.79).)
 2. A new large-field region appears, and $Z$ is small, i.e. contained in a cube of size $100MR_{j+1}$: define $\kappa_{j+1}(Z)=p_0(g_j)-O(1)M^dR_{j+1}^{d+1}d'_{j+1}(Z)$. It satisfies (1.80) because for small domains one simply takes $K=R_{j+1}$ (condition (i) holds at once, and $R_{j+1}$ further steps give (ii)).
 3. General case (1.84)-(1.85): $Z$ is obtained from components $Z_j^{(n)}$ of $Z_j$ and new large-field regions $Z_{j+1}^{(i)}$ joined together by the operations of the step: $Z\subset\bigcup_n(Z_j^{(n)})^{\sim10}\cup\bigcup_i(Z_{j+1}^{(i)})^{\sim2}$ (1.84), and
 $$\kappa_{j+1}(Z)=\sum_n\big(\kappa_j(Z_j^{(n)})+2p_0(g_{j(Z_j^{(n)})})\big)+\sum_i\big(\gamma_0A_1^2p_0^2(g_{j+1})(d'_{j+1}(Z_{j+1}^{(i)})+1)+2p_0(g_{j+1})\big)-O(1)M^dR_{j+1}^{d+1}d'_{j+1}(Z)-2p_0(g_{j(Z)}).\tag{1.85}$$
 (UNCERTAIN: the coefficient in front of $\gamma_0A_1^2p_0^2(g_{j+1})$ in the second sum is printed as a fraction I could not read with certainty on p. 386; compare $\frac12\gamma_0$ in (1.79).) The two sums are exactly the factors (1.79) for the old components and for the new regions; the last two terms are the new growth loss and the correction of the prefactor $\exp(-2p_0)$ (each component has exactly one $\exp(-2p_0(g_{j(Z)}))$, the old ones' are put into $\kappa$).

**Proof of (1.80) by induction on $j$, with an inner induction on the number of domains (pp. 384-387).**

1. *Geometry of $S$ (p. 384).* Fix a cube $\square$ of side $MR_j$ in $Z$. $S^{n-j}(\square)$ is a cube consisting of $L_{n-j}^d$ $MR_n$-cubes, with $L_{n-j}\le42\frac{1-L^{-(n-j)}}{1-L^{-1}}<63$ (uses $L\ge2$; the geometric sum comes from the ten added layers at each of the $n-j$ steps, rescaled by $L^{-1}$ per step, and from the $L$-fold coarsening of the lattice of $MR$-cubes), and its center $MR_n$-cube contains $\square$; hence $S^{n-j}(Z)\subset\bigcup_{\square\subset Z^{(n-j)}}\square^{\sim31}$ where $Z^{(n-j)}$ is the cover of $Z$ by $L^{n-j}$-rescaled ($MR_n$-)cubes; so $d'_n(S^{n-j}(Z))\le(63)^d(MR_n)^{-d}|Z^{(n-j)}|\le(63)^d3\cdot2^{d-1}d'_n(Z^{(n-j)})$ when the linear size on the right is nonzero, and $d'_n(S^{n-j}(Z))\le(64)^d$ when it is zero. The scaling property (6.31)[I] gives $d'_n(Z^{(n-j)})\le L^{-\frac12(n-j)}d'_j(Z)\le2^{-(n-j)}d'_j(Z)$ for $n-j>1$ ("The square root appears here because for some steps we do not gain the scaling factor $L^{-1}$ (then $R_{m+1}=LR_m$)").
2. *Number of steps $K$ and the sum (1.81).* Let $n_0$ be the last index $n$ with $d'_n(Z^{(n-j)})>0$. Then $S^{n_0+1-j}(Z)$ is contained in a cube of size $64MR_{n_0+1}$, i.e. satisfies (i); at most $R_j$ further steps give a domain satisfying also (ii). Thus $K\le n_0-j+R_j$, and
 $$\sum_{n=j+1}^{j+K}O(1)M^dR_n^{d+1}d'_n(S^{n-j}(Z))\le\sum_{n=j+1}^{n_0}O(1)M^dR_n^{d+1}3(126)^d2^{-(n-j)}d'_j(Z)+\sum_{n=n_0+1}^{n_0+R_j}O(1)M^ddR_n^{d+1}(64)^d\le O(1)3(126)^dM^dL^{d+1}R_j^{d+1}d'_j(Z)+O(1)(64)^dM^dL^{d+1}R_j^{d+2}\le O(1)(64)^dM^dL^{d+1}R_j^{d+2}(d'_j(Z)+1).\tag{1.81}$$
 (Printed with $3(126)^d$ and $(64)^d$; the use of $R_n\le L R_j$ for $n\le j+R_j$.)
3. *Base case $j=1$.* By (1.82) and (1.81): the statement holds for $j=1$ if $\frac14\gamma_0(14)^{-d}A_1^2p_0^2(g_1)\ge O(1)(64)^dM^dL^{d+1}R_1^{d+2}$, "satisfied for $p_0$ large and $g_1$ sufficiently small".
4. *Inductive step $j\to j+1$.* Take a component $Z$ of $Z_{j+1}$ and distinguish.
 (a) $Z=S(Z_0)$, $Z_0$ a component of $Z_j$, no new large field (1.83): $\kappa_{j+1}(Z)=\kappa_j(Z_0)-O(1)M^dR_{j+1}^{d+1}d'_{j+1}(Z)$; (1.80) at $j$ for $Z_0$ and the equality $Z=S(Z_0)$ (so $K_{j+1}(Z)=K_j(Z_0)-1$ and $S^{n-j-1}(Z)=S^{n-j}(Z_0)$) give (1.80) at $j+1$ for $Z$; the extra term $-O(1)M^dR_{j+1}^{d+1}d'_{j+1}(Z)$ is exactly the $n=j+1$ term of the sum (1.80) at $j$ that is dropped. Similarly if $Z=S(Z_0)$ with a new large field in a neighborhood but $Z_0$ satisfying (i),(ii): then $\kappa_j(Z_0)\ge0$ and "we do not have a better bound for it but we have the new factor $\exp(-p_0(g_j))$": define $\kappa_{j+1}(Z)=p_0(g_j)-O(1)M^dR_{j+1}^{d+1}d'_{j+1}(Z)$; (1.80) holds because $Z$ is a small domain in a cube of size $100MR_{j+1}$, hence $K=R_{j+1}$ (p. 386, case 2 above).
 (b) $Z$ obtained from several components $Z_j^{(n)}$ and new regions $Z_{j+1}^{(i)}$ (1.84)-(1.85). Induction on the number of domains in the family $\{Z_j^{(n)},Z_{j+1}^{(i)}\}$. If this number is 1, one of the two previous cases holds. Otherwise define the graph $G$ whose vertices are the domains of the family, with an edge when the corresponding domains in (1.84) intersect or touch; $G$ is connected because $Z$ is. Pick a maximal tree graph in $G$; it has at least one endpoint (a vertex joined to the rest of the tree by exactly one edge). Let $X$ be the corresponding domain and remove the vertex and its edge; the remaining graph is still a connected tree and the union of the remaining domains of (1.84) is a connected domain $Y$, with fewer domains, hence satisfying (1.80) by the induction hypothesis for $\kappa_{j+1}(Y)$ (as defined by (1.85)); (1.80) holds also for $X$ and $\kappa_{j+1}(X)$ because $X$ is a single domain. By (1.84), $Z\subset X\cup Y$ (with enlarged domains). From (1.85):
 $$\kappa_{j+1}(Z)=\kappa_{j+1}(X)+\kappa_{j+1}(Y)+2p_0(g_{j(X)})+2p_0(g_{j(Y)})-2p_0(g_{j(Z)})+O(1)M^dR_{j+1}^{d+1}d'_{j+1}(X)+O(1)M^dR_{j+1}^{d+1}d'_{j+1}(Y)-O(1)M^dR_{j+1}^{d+1}d'_{j+1}(Z)\tag{1.86}$$
 (the printed signs are as on the page: the three $O(1)M^dR^{d+1}$-terms are the three growth losses). Since $j(Z)$ equals $j(X)$ or $j(Y)$, $2p_0(g_{j(X)})+2p_0(g_{j(Y)})-2p_0(g_{j(Z)})\ge2(1+\beta_0)^{-1}p_0(g_{j+1})$ (this uses that $p_0(g_j)\ge(1+\beta_0)^{-1}p_0(g_{j+1})$, i.e. $p_0(g_i)$ varies slowly along the sequence, a consequence of $g_{j+1}^{-2}\approx L^{d-2}g_j^{-2}$). Since $Z$ is connected and $Z\subset X\cup Y$: $d'_{j+1}(X)+d'_{j+1}(Y)+2d\ge d'_{j+1}(Z)$. Hence
 $$\kappa_{j+1}(Z)\ge\kappa_{j+1}(X)+\kappa_{j+1}(Y)+2(1+\beta_0)^{-1}p_0(g_{j+1})-O(1)2dM^dR_{j+1}^{d+1}.\tag{1.87}$$
 Then the sum in (1.80) is estimated with $j+1$ in place of $j$. The domains $X,Y$ intersect or touch, so $S^{n-j-1}(X)$, $S^{n-j-1}(Y)$ for $n>j+1$ share at least a cube of size $20MR_n$, hence $d'_n(S^{n-j-1}(Z))\le d'_n(S^{n-j-1}(X))+d'_n(S^{n-j-1}(Y))$. Let $K_1,K_2$ be the step numbers of $X,Y$; by symmetry $K_1\le K_2$. $S^{K_1}(X)$ satisfies (i),(ii), in particular lies in a cube of size $100MR_{j+1+K_1}$, and intersects $S^{K_1}(Y)$; apply $S$ to $Y$ a further $n_1<10$ times so that $S^{K_1+n_1}(Y)\supset S^{K_1+n_1}(X)$. Then $S^{n-j-1}(Z)=S^{n-j-1}(Y)$ for $n\ge j+1+K_1+n_1$, and $K\le K_2+n_1+R_{j+1}$. So
 $$\sum_{n=j+2}^{j+1+K}O(1)M^dR_{j+1}^{d+1}d'_n(S^{n-j-1}(Z))\le\sum_{n=j+2}^{j+1+K_1+n_1}(\ldots X)+\sum_{n=j+2}^{j+1+K_2+n_1+R_{j+1}}(\ldots Y)\le\kappa_{j+1}(X)+\kappa_{j+1}(Y)+O(1)2(100M)^dR_{j+1}^{d+2}\le\kappa_{j+1}(Z)-2(1+\beta_0)^{-1}p_0(g_{j+1})+O(1)3(100M)^dR_{j+1}^{d+2}\le\kappa_{j+1}(Z),\tag{1.88}$$
 "for $p_0$ large and $\gamma$ small". (The sum is written with $R_{j+1}^{d+1}$ in place of $R_n^{d+1}$; the two are comparable, $R_n\le LR_{j+1}$ for the relevant $n$, absorbed in $O(1)$ — UNCERTAIN whether the exact printed form is $R_n$ or $R_{j+1}$.) This closes the induction.

**Conclusions (p. 387).** (1) For the first-class $X$ of definition (1.71) the hypothesis holds with $K=0$, so $\kappa_k(X)\ge0$, hence (1.89). (2) Inequality (1.80) "holds quite generally for such regions", hence also an improved bound with an additional term $-\kappa_1d_k(X)$ in the exponential, which gives (2.50)[III] and "Corollary 3" (the paper does not state Corollary 3; it is a statement of [III]).

## 7. Weak spots and open points

1. **Mismatch between E10's statement and P6 Prop. 3.2.** E10 states (1.80) for a geometric $K_j(Z)$; P6 defines $K(Z,\mathbb A_k)$ analytically by $\kappa_{k,n}-\ell p_2(\beta_{k+n})>0$ ((3.19)-(3.20) there) and states (i)-(iii). E10 contains none of (i)-(iii) verbatim, nor the critical size $12M[R]$ (E10 uses the class "contained in a cube of size $100MR_k$"), nor the constant $\log\beta_{k_1}a$. The identification of E10's $K$ with P6's $K$ is not proved in either paper.
2. **"Smallest positive integer" vs $K=0$.** p. 384 defines $K$ as the smallest positive integer with $S^K(Z)$ satisfying (i),(ii); p. 387 says first-class $X$ "satisfies the assumption of the statement with $K=0$". Conventions inconsistent; $K=0$ must be allowed.
3. **$d'_j$ is not defined in E10**, only used. Its definition is in [III]/[IV] (UNCERTAIN) and the arithmetic of section 3(F) depends on it (a count of $MR_j$-cubes along a tree: zero for one cube).
4. **Constants.** The coefficient in front of $\gamma_0A_1^2p_0^2(g_{j+1})$ in (1.85) is hard to read ($\frac12$? see section 2); (1.82) has $\frac14\gamma_0(14)^{-d}$. The extra decay in $K$ ($R_n^{d+1}$ vs $R_{j+1}^{d+1}$ inside (1.88)) is blurred. I mark these UNCERTAIN; the logic of the induction is clear.
5. **Inequality (1.86)-(1.87).** The sign bookkeeping of the three $O(1)M^dR^{d+1}d'$ terms is written compactly; the inference "$d'(X)+d'(Y)+2d\ge d'(Z)$" gives $-O(1)M^dR^{d+1}d'(Z)\ge-O(1)M^dR^{d+1}(d'(X)+d'(Y)+2d)$, so the combination in (1.87) is correct, with the $2dM^dR^{d+1}$ constant error. The claim $2p_0(g_{j(X)})+2p_0(g_{j(Y)})-2p_0(g_{j(Z)})\ge2(1+\beta_0)^{-1}p_0(g_{j+1})$ uses a relation between $p_0(g_j)$ and $p_0(g_{j+1})$ not proved (needs that $\min(j(X),j(Y))\le j(Z)$ is equal to the first index, and slow variation of $p_0$).
6. **Cancellation of the Wilson term.** The estimates (1.5)-(1.6), (1.27)-(1.31), (1.47) are given with explicit powers of $M$, $R_k$ but with "similar", "we omit details", "bounds for the other terms are similar" (e.g. p. 368, (1.47); p. 362-363; "the same argument as in the proof of Lemma 2.4 in [11]"). This is the part where the *form* of the cancellation is shown and the O(1) constants are not tracked; the consequence is only "small after dividing by $g_k^2$".
7. **Analytic extension (pp. 366-367, 374-375).** The extension of the Wilson action to $G^c$-valued fields via the critical point $V'_\Lambda$ and the identity (1.38) is the technical device that replaces the lost positivity. It is argued quickly ("In the proof of Proposition 1 [IV] we have noticed ... but now we need a more quantitative statement").
8. **Cluster theorem.** The convergence of the exponentiated cluster expansion is taken from the literature without naming a theorem or a hypothesis list; the condition actually verified is (1.97), exponential decay of activities along tree lengths with a small prefactor. Compare P7 (3.27)-(3.28), which cites [9, 11, 13-15] of P7.
9. **Citation numbering.** The paper's bare references `[10]`-`[16]`, `[26]` are not in the printed bibliography (only [I]-[IV]); also "Theorem 2 of [I]" (p. 355), where the theorem (removal of the assumption on couplings) seems to be of [III] or of a not-yet-published paper. This matters for tracking what E10 actually depends on.
10. **Corollary 3 / Theorem 2 / (2.50)[III].** Mentioned but not stated here.
11. **Pagination.** The OCR-like variant of the page for "S(Z)=Z'^{~10}" (p. 384) is read from the image as "$\sim10$"; the coefficient ten is also consistent with E9 p. 177 ("each renormalization step adds at least ten layers of $MR_k$-cubes").

## 8. Preliminary formalization notes

- The step-counting induction (section 3(F)) is the only part with a complete proof and is formalizable on its own: it is a statement about numbers $\kappa_j(Z)$, domains $Z\subset\mathbb Z^d$ of cubes, a shadow operation $S$, and a sum bound. One would formalize it as a lemma about an abstract family of finite sets with a monotone shadow operation and a tree-length function $d'$, with explicit constants $C_1=O(1)M^dR^{d+1}$ etc. Dependencies: geometry of cubes in $\mathbb Z^d$ (Mathlib has `Finset` and box neighborhoods; tree-length would need to be defined by a minimal spanning tree over cube centers), geometric series. Difficulty: moderate; most of the labor is in fixing the definitions ($d'$, $S$, $K$) so that (1.80)-(1.88) are literally true; the printed arithmetic uses several rough absorbing steps.
- The polymer expansion/exponentiation step (1.90)-(1.101) is the standard abstract "polymer expansion with activities bounded by $c_1\exp(-(1+\beta)\kappa d(X))$" and could be proved by any Kotecký-Preiss or Brydges tree-graph theorem; here the bookkeeping (components modulo the second-class large-field region, restriction to terms intersecting a fixed region) is nonstandard but simple. Recommend formalizing it as an abstract theorem once, to be used by P6 (4.46)-(4.51), P7 (3.27)-(3.29), (4.14), P9 (4.4)-(4.6).
- Everything in sections A-D (Wilson cancellation, localization of the effective-action terms around new backgrounds, analytic extensions) is gauge-specific in form but conceptually similar to P7 Sect. 2-3; those would be treated via P7, not via E10.

## 9. What the O(N) series uses from this paper

Content here: the numbers $\kappa_j(Z)$ and the step-counting (1.80)-(1.89), plus the definition (1.71) of the operator whose positivity (1.73)-(1.75) allows normalization. Applies as stated? Item 2. P6 p. 18-19 after (3.10): "the same functions [$\kappa_k$] have been used in the papers [6b,c,d] ... We have analyzed these functions extensively in [6d]". In E10 the corresponding objects are $\kappa_j(Z)$, (1.82)-(1.85), defined as the exponent of the surviving large-field factor of the component $Z$ (decay of $\exp(-\kappa_j(Z)-2p_0(g_{j(Z)}))$). P6's recursion (3.10) is $\kappa_{k+1}(Z)=\kappa_k(Z\cap Z_k,\mathbb A_k\cap(Z\cap Z_k))+\kappa^{(k)}(\mathbb A_{k+1}\cap(Z_k^c\cap Z))$, additive over components, exactly the structure (1.85) (sum over old components plus new regions minus per-step loss). Does apply: yes (as an analogue, not as a citation of a stated result).

Item 3. P6 p. 21, Proposition 3.2 (i)-(iii) and "(3.19)-(3.21)", "Details can be found in [6d], on pp. 383-387". **Precise statement in P6** (checked on the text layer of p. 21):

> (i) If a component $Z$ of $Z_k$ has a diameter greater than the critical size in the $\eta$-scale, then there is an index $K<K(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)$ such that $S^K(Z)$ has a diameter smaller than the critical size in the $L^{-K}\eta$-scale, and $K+\log\beta_{k+K}a<K(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)$.
> (ii) If $K(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)=0$, then there exists an index $k_1<k$ such that for all $j$, $k_1\le j\le k$, the domains $Z_j\cap Z$ have diameters smaller than the critical sizes in the $L^j\eta$-scales, the corresponding multi-indices $\mathbb A_j\cap Z=\mathbb A_k\cap Z_j\cap Z$ control at least $k-j$ steps, there are no large field regions in $Z_{k_1}^c\cap Z$, and $n_1=k-k_1>\log\beta_{k_1}a$ (3.20).
> (iii) If $K(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)>0$, $\mathbb A_{k+1}$ is obtained as in (5.1), and $Z'$ is a component of $Z_{k+1}$ containing $Z$, then $K(Z',\mathbb A_{k+1}\cap Z')\ge K(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)-1$ (3.21).

with $K(Z_k,\mathbb A_k)$ defined (P6 pp. 20-21) as the unique index $K$ such that $\kappa_{k,n}(Z_k,\mathbb A_k)-\ell p_2(\beta_{k+n})>0$ for $n\le K$ and $\le0$ for $n>K$, where $\kappa_{k,n}$ is defined by (3.19): $C(\mathbb A_k)\exp(-\kappa_k(Z_k,\mathbb A_k)+H_k|Z_k|\eta^{-d})\cdot\exp(\sum_{p=1}^n\mathrm{const.}\log\beta_{k+p}|(S^{p-1}(Z_k))^c\cap S^p(Z_k)|L^{-p}\eta)=\exp(-\kappa_{k,n}(Z_k,\mathbb A_k))$, critical size $12M[R_{k+n-1}]$, $S$ as in (3.13)-(3.16).

## 10. Relevance to the formalization

With large fields in scope (Roland: "the crux of the matter"):

1. **What to formalize from E10.** The step-counting lemma, in a model-free form. Suggested Lean interface: a finite-volume cube system $\mathbb Z^d$ with $MR_j$-cube partitions, shadow $S$ (one step with no new large field; $S(Z)=Z'^{\sim10}$ at the right scale), a tree-length function $d'_j$, constants $C_0=O(1)M^dR^{d+1}$-type, and a decreasing sequence of exponents. Statement: for every domain $Z$ built by the inductive rule (1.82)-(1.85) (generated by: new region; shadow without new region; join of two generated domains), (1.80) holds with the geometric $K$. This can be proved independently of the physics (section 3(F) is complete). Then add a second lemma translating the geometric $K$ to P6's analytic $K(Z,\mathbb A_k)$ and proving P6 Prop. 3.2 (i)-(iii) literally; this is the gap in the citation (section 9 item 3) and should be written as a new lemma, with P6's definitions (3.10)-(3.20) as the data. Note P6's own $\kappa$ recursion (3.10) is additive in components, so the induction in (1.84)-(1.88) transfers: joining components adds $\kappa$'s and does not lose.
2. **Interfaces.** (1.73)-(1.75) (positivity of the integral operator under small complex perturbations) is the reusable analytic ingredient; in P7 it appears with the new O(N)-specific difficulty (analyticity through $\psi_0$). (1.97) -> (1.98) is an abstract polymer exponentiation step (see section 8); one lemma "convergent polymer expansion with activities $\le c_1\exp(-(1+\beta)\kappa d(X))$ and hard-core only inside the second-class region" can serve P6 (4.46)-(4.51), P7 (3.27)-(3.29), P9.
3. **Gauge-specific and irrelevant for O(N):** Faddeev-Popov and axial gauge fixing, $G$-valued fields and their analytic extension $G^c$ (1.38)-(1.45), determining sets with $\mathbf H_{\mathbf B}$ gauge-fixed minimizers, and the delicate bookkeeping of "preparatory steps" with $N$ and $\chi'$ in (1.79)-(1.83) (the case analysis of the characteristic function $1-\chi'$ uses the gauge conditions, pp. 381-383). The corresponding O(N) estimates are in P6 Prop. 3.1 (action lower bound) and P6 (3.7)-(3.9).
4. **Green's function / propagator results.** E10 uses such results only as black boxes (the minimizer operators $H_k$ and their exponential decay, from [13], [15] = gauge papers). Nothing to prove here; the O(N) statements are in P2/P3 and E3/E5. For the lower bound of the quadratic form (1.7)-(1.9) a standard Poincaré inequality on a box suffices (E10's (1.8): $\sum_b|B'(b)|^2\le d(100M)^{d+1}\sum_p|(\partial B')(p)|^2$ by summing along axes from the gauge-fixed line); for the Dirichlet problem in O(N) (P7 Lemma 2.1) the same argument gives the positivity constant needed for the box of diameter $<15$.
5. **What can be dispensed with.** E10's (1.1)-(1.48) as such (the Wilson-term cancellation) need not be formalized: P7 reproves the O(N) analogue. The proof of the counting lemma and the polymer bounds are the parts of E10 to formalize.

Difficulty ranking within E10: (a) geometric counting lemma (1.80): moderate; (b) polymer exponentiation: standard; (c) Wilson cancellation and localization: skip (replaced by P7).
