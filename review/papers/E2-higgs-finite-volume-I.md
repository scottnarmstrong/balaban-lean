# E2: (Higgs)_{2,3} quantum fields in a finite volume. I. A lower bound

## 0. Identification

T. Balaban, "(Higgs)$_{2,3}$ quantum fields in a finite volume. I. A lower bound", Commun. Math. Phys. 85 (1982) 603–626 (the running head on p. 603 prints "603–636"; the last page is 626, the file has 24 pages, PDF page = journal page − 602). Bundle key E2. Received 16 March 1982, communicated by A. Jaffe.

Summary. The paper treats a lattice approximation of the Euclidean Proca-type model of a scalar field $\phi:T_\varepsilon\to\mathbb R^N$ coupled to an abelian vector field $A$ (the "(Higgs)$_{2,3}$" model, $d=2,3$), and proves the vacuum-energy lower bound $Z^\varepsilon\ge\exp(-E_-|T_\varepsilon|)$ uniformly in the lattice spacing $\varepsilon$ and the torus (p. 606, Theorem; proved in §3, p. 625 (3.68)–(3.69)). The method is Balaban's first multiscale scheme: a Gaussian block-averaging renormalization transformation $T_{a,L,A}$ ((2.4)–(2.7), p. 608) iterated $k$ times, with restrictions on the sizes of the fields imposed by characteristic functions ((3.1)–(3.5)), and after each step a translation of the fields, a perturbative (cumulant) expansion of the interaction, and an induction hypothesis on the form of the action ((3.26)–(3.36)). Only the lower bound is done; the upper bound is Part II and the full perturbative bookkeeping ("graphs", renormalization of the expansions) is deferred to a separate paper. Almost all hard estimates are stated without proof (§2 propositions: propagator decay; §3 Props. 3.1, 3.2). It is the earliest published form of the RG/background-field machinery that appears in the O(N) series.

## 1. Setting and notation

Lattice and objects (pp. 603–607).
- Torus $T_\varepsilon=\{x\in\varepsilon\mathbb Z^d:\ -L_\mu\le x_\mu<L_\mu\}$ (1.2), with $\varepsilon^{-1}L_\mu=L^KML'_\mu$ for positive integers $K,M,L'_\mu$ ($K=O(\log\varepsilon^{-1})$; $L$ the block factor, $M$ the large-block factor "to be determined later"). Distance $|x-y|=\max_\mu\min\{|x_\mu-y_\mu|,2L_\mu-|x_\mu-y_\mu|\}$ (1.3).
- Fields: scalar $\phi:T_\varepsilon\to\mathbb R^N$; vector field $A:T^*_\varepsilon\to\mathbb R$ on oriented bonds $b=\langle b_-,b_+\rangle$, $A_{-b}=-A_b$. Difference derivative $(\partial^\varepsilon f)(b)=\varepsilon^{-1}(f(b_+)-f(b_-))$ (1.4), curl $(\partial^\varepsilon A)(P)=\varepsilon^{-1}\sum_{b\subset\partial P}A_b$ (1.6) on plaquettes $P$.
- Coupling: antisymmetric $N\times N$ matrix $q$, $U(A)=\exp(q\varepsilon eA)$ unitary, covariant derivative $(D^\varepsilon_A\phi)(b)=\varepsilon^{-1}(U(A_b)\phi(b_+)-\phi(b_-))$ (1.7).
- Action (1.8), then with gauge-fixing (Feynman gauge) (1.10)–(1.11):
$$S^\varepsilon(A,\phi)=\tfrac12\langle\phi,(-\Delta^\varepsilon_A)\phi\rangle+\sum_x\varepsilon^d\big(\tfrac12m_0^2|\phi(x)|^2+\lambda|\phi(x)|^4\big)+\tfrac12\langle A,(-\Delta^\varepsilon+\mu_0^2)A\rangle+E ,$$
$-\Delta^\varepsilon_A=D^{\varepsilon*}_AD^\varepsilon_A$, $m_0^2=m^2+\delta m^2$ with $\delta m^2$ a mass counterterm given by a perturbation series in $e,\lambda$ (a number $\ge6$ orders, $\bar n\ge6$, is needed for renormalization), $E=E_0+E_1$ a vacuum-energy counterterm ((1.12)–(1.13)); $\mu_0>0$, $\lambda>0$ ($\lambda>0$ is stressed as essential, p. 606). For simplicity $N=2$, $q^2=-1$ (p. 605).
- Partition function $Z^\varepsilon=\int dA\,d\phi\,e^{-S^\varepsilon(A,\phi)}$ (1.10).
- Blocks and scales: for $\Lambda\subset\eta\mathbb Z^d$ the "prime" $\Lambda'=\Lambda\cap L\eta\mathbb Z^d$ (1.16), block $B(y)=\{x\in\eta\mathbb Z^d:y_\mu\le x_\mu<y_\mu+L\eta\}$ (1.17), dual operation $B(\Lambda)$ (1.18), $T^{(k+1)}_{L^{k+1}\varepsilon}=(T^{(k)}_{L^k\varepsilon})'$, $T_\varepsilon=B^k(T^{(k)}_{L^k\varepsilon})$ (1.19–1.20); large blocks are the same with $L$ replaced by $ML$. $|\Lambda|=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\eta^d$ (1.21). Canonical rescaling (1.22): $\phi(x)=(\eta/\delta)^{-(d-2)/2}\phi'(\delta x/\eta)$, so that the action keeps the form (1.11) with $\varepsilon\to s\varepsilon$, $m_0^2\to m_0^2s^{-2}$, $\mu_0^2\to\mu_0^2 s^{-2}$, $\lambda_s=\lambda s^{-(4-d)}$, $e_s=es^{-(4-d)/2}$ (p. 607). One may take $\lambda_s=1$, $s=\lambda^{1/(4-d)}$.
- Renormalization transformation (2.4)–(2.7), p. 608: for $\Omega\subset T_\varepsilon$ with $B(\Omega')=\Omega$,
$$\varrho'(A,\psi)=T^{L\varepsilon}_{a,L,A}[\Omega,\varrho]=\int d\phi\; t^{L\varepsilon}_{a,L,A}(\Omega;\psi,\phi)\varrho(A,\phi),\quad t=\prod_{y\in\Omega'}\Big(\tfrac{a(L\varepsilon)^{d-2}}{2\pi}\Big)^{N/2}\exp\!\big[-\tfrac12a(L\varepsilon)^{d-2}|\psi(y)-(Q(A)\phi)(y)|^2\big],$$
with the covariant block average $(Q(A)\phi)(y)=L^{-d}\sum_{x\in B(y)}U(A(\Gamma_{y,x}))\phi(x)$, where $\Gamma_{y,x}$ is a specified lattice path from $y$ to $x$ built coordinate by coordinate (2.1) and $A(\Gamma)=\sum_{b\in\Gamma}A_b$ (2.3). The $k$-th order transformation uses $a_k$ and $Q_k(A)$ with paths $\Gamma^{(k)}_{y,x}$ through the scales (2.2), (2.10)–(2.11). Normalisation (2.8)–(2.9). Composition law: $T_{a,L,A}T_{a_k,L^k,A}=T_{a_{k+1},L^{k+1},A}$ with $a_{k+1}=aa_k/(aL^{-2}+a_k)$ (2.13)–(2.14), giving $a_k=a\frac{1-L^{-2}}{1-L^{-2k}}$, $a_k\searrow a_\infty=a(1-L^{-2})$ (2.15). (This is the same free flow as in P4 (2.9) / BIG_PICTURE §2.1; with $A=0$ it is the scalar block-spin transformation of the O(N) series, with $Q$ the plain block average.)
- Propagators: $\Delta^{(0)}(\Omega,A)=-\Delta^{\varepsilon,N}_{A,\Omega}+m^2$ (Neumann b.c.); $G^\varepsilon_k(\Omega,A)=(-\Delta^{\varepsilon,N}_{A,\Omega}+m^2+a_k(L^k\varepsilon)^{-2}P_k(A))^{-1}$, $P_k=Q_k^*Q_k$ (2.20); $\Delta^{(k),L^k\varepsilon}(\Omega,A)$ defined by $Z^{(k),\varepsilon}(\Omega,A)\exp(-\frac12\langle\psi,\Delta^{(k+1),L^{k+1}\varepsilon}\psi\rangle)=T[\Omega,\exp(-\frac12\langle\phi,\Delta^{(k),L^k\varepsilon}\phi\rangle)]$ (2.17)–(2.18), with the explicit form (2.21) $\langle\psi,\Delta^{(k),L^k\varepsilon}\psi\rangle=a_k(L^k\varepsilon)^{-2}\langle\psi,\psi\rangle-a_k^2(L^k\varepsilon)^{-4}\langle\psi,Q_kG^\varepsilon_kQ_k^*\psi\rangle$; rescaled propagator $G_k(\Omega,A)=(-\Delta^{\eta,N}_{A,\Omega}+m^2(L^k\varepsilon)^2+a_kP_k(A))^{-1}$ (2.22); covariances $C^{(k),L^k\varepsilon}(\Omega,A)=(a(L^{k+1}\varepsilon)^{-2}P(A)+\Delta^{(k),L^k\varepsilon}(\Omega,A))^{-1}$ (2.30), rescaled (2.31), and the version restricted to a union $\Lambda$ of big blocks, $C^{(k)}_\Lambda$ (2.32). Recursions: (2.39)–(2.43), notably (2.41) $Q_{k+1}(A)G^\varepsilon_{k+1}=\frac{aa_k}{a_{k+1}}(L^k\varepsilon)^{-2}Q(A)C^{(k),L^k\varepsilon}Q_k(A)G^\varepsilon_k$ and (2.42) $G^\varepsilon_{k+1}=a_k^2(L^k\varepsilon)^{-4}G^\varepsilon_kQ_k^*C^{(k),L^k\varepsilon}Q_kG_k^\varepsilon+G^\varepsilon_k$, and its $k$-fold iterate (2.43) $G^\varepsilon_k=\sum_{j<k}a_j^2(L^j\varepsilon)^{-4}G^\varepsilon_jQ_j^*C^{(j),L^j\varepsilon}Q_jG^\varepsilon_j+C^{(0),\varepsilon}$, which exhibits the propagator as a sum of independent Gaussian layers.
- Regular fields: a configuration $A$ is "regular on $\Omega$" if $|(\partial^\eta_\mu A)(x)|\le c\,e(L^k\varepsilon)^{\beta-1}$, $e(L^k\varepsilon)=e(L^k\varepsilon)^{(4-d)/2}$, $\beta>0$ (2.23); the constants $\delta_0,c_0,R_0$ below are uniform over such $A$.
- Restrictions (3.1)–(3.5), (3.26)–(3.28): characteristic functions of $|A(x)|\le\varepsilon^{-(d-2)/2}p(\varepsilon)$, $|(\partial A)(x)|\le\varepsilon^{-(d+2)/2}p(\varepsilon)$, the same for $\phi$ (with the covariant Laplacian $\Delta^\varepsilon_A\phi$), and for the background fields $B^{(1),\varepsilon}=a(L\varepsilon)^{-2}G^\varepsilon_1Q^*B$, $\psi^{(1),\varepsilon}=a(L\varepsilon)^{-2}G^\varepsilon_1(B^{(1),\varepsilon})Q^*(B^{(1),\varepsilon})\psi$ (3.3); $p(\varepsilon)=b_0(1+\log\varepsilon^{-1})^p$; on the unit lattice $p_1(\varepsilon)=b_1(1+\log\varepsilon^{-1})^{p_1}$. These are the logarithmic small-field cut-offs; there is no large-field analysis in this paper (the lower bound simply discards the region outside the restrictions).

## 2. Main results

**Theorem (p. 606, (1.14)).** For $d=2,3$ there exist constants $E_-,E_+$ independent of $\varepsilon$ and $T_\varepsilon$ such that
$$\exp(-E_-|T_\varepsilon|)\le Z^\varepsilon\le\exp(E_+|T_\varepsilon|).$$
Only the lower bound is proved here (title "A lower bound"); the upper bound is in Part II (cited in E3 as "[2] ... II. An upper bound"). The standing assumptions are those of §1: $\mu_0^2>0$, $\lambda>0$, $m^2>0$, mass and energy counterterms chosen by the perturbation series to order $\bar n\ge6$ (p. 605). The dependence of $E_\pm$ on parameters ($e,\lambda,m,\mu_0,q$) is not stated; the proof gives a constant depending on $L^K\varepsilon\sim\varepsilon_0$ (p. 625).

**Remark (p. 606, (1.15)).** The same method gives bounds for the generating functional with sources, $\exp(-E_-(\|J\|,\|f\|)|T_\varepsilon|)\le Z^\varepsilon(J,f)\le\exp(E_+(\|J\|,\|f\|)|T_\varepsilon|)$, with $E_\pm$ continuous and independent of $\varepsilon,T_\varepsilon$. Asserted, not proved.

**Proposition 2.1 (p. 610) (propagator bounds; stated, proof deferred).** Let $\Omega=B^k(\Omega^{(k)})$, $\Omega^{(k)}\subset T^{(k)}_1$ a sum of big blocks with $M$ large, and $A$ regular on $\Omega$ (2.23). For $e(L^k\varepsilon)$ small and $\beta<1$ there exist $\delta_0,c_0,R_0>0$ (independent of $d$-independent data: depending on $d,a,L,M$; $c_0$ also on $\beta$) such that for all $f:\Omega\to\mathbb R^N$, with $\Gamma_{x,x'}$ a shortest contour,
$$\frac1{|x-x'|^\beta}\big|U(A(\Gamma_{x,x'}))(D^\eta_{A,\mu}G_k(\Omega,A)f)(x')-(D^\eta_{A,\mu}G_k(\Omega,A)f)(x)\big|\le c_0e^{-\delta_0\operatorname{dist}(\{x,x'\},\operatorname{supp}f)}\|f\|_\infty \quad(2.24)$$
for $x,x'\in\Omega$ with $\operatorname{dist}(\{x,x'\},\Omega^c)\ge R_0$ (hence H\"older-type regularity of the covariant gradient of the Green's function), and
$$|D^\eta_{A,\mu}G_k(\Omega,A)f(x)|,\ |G_k(\Omega,A)f(x)|\le c_0e^{-\delta_0\operatorname{dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}f)}\|f\|_\infty\quad(2.25).$$
For $\Omega\subset\Omega_0$ the difference $\delta G_k=G_k(\Omega,A)-G_k(\Omega_0,A)$ (2.26) satisfies (2.24)–(2.25) with an extra factor $\exp(-\delta_0\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{supp}f,\Omega^c)-\delta_0\operatorname{dist}(\{x,x'\},\Omega^c))$. For rectangular parallelepipeds the restriction $\operatorname{dist}\ge R_0$ can be dropped.

**Proposition 2.2 (p. 611).** For $A$ regular as above, the rescaled effective quadratic form kernel satisfies $|\Delta^{(k)}(\Omega,A;x,x')|\le c_0e^{-\delta_0|x-x'|}$ on $\Omega^{(k)}$ (2.27) and, for $\Omega\subset\Omega_0$, $|\delta\Delta^{(k)}(\Omega,\Omega_0,A;x,x')|\le c_0e^{-\delta_0(|x-x'|+\operatorname{dist}(x,\Omega^{(k)c})+\operatorname{dist}(x',\Omega^{(k)c}))}$ (2.29).

**Proposition 2.3 (p. 611–612).** Under the same assumptions there exist $\delta_0,c_0,\gamma_0,\gamma_1>0$ (depending on $d$, $a$, not on $A,k,\Omega$) such that
$$\gamma_0I\le aL^{-2}P(A)+\Delta^{(k)}(\Omega,A)\le\gamma_1I\ (2.33),\qquad |C^{(k)}_\Lambda(\Omega,A;x,x')|\le c_0e^{-\delta_0|x-x'|}\ (2.34),$$
(for $\Lambda$ a union of big blocks of $T^{(k)}_1$) and the analogous bounds (2.36), (2.38) for the differences $\delta C_\Lambda$ w.r.t. $\Lambda$ and w.r.t. $\Omega$ ((2.35)–(2.37)). Remark (p. 611): "It is not clear from the formulas (2.30), (2.31) that the covariances are well defined"; (2.33) is exactly what makes them well defined.

**Proposition 3.1 (p. 620) (R-terms).** The sum of the "R-terms" (terms containing $R_{\bar n+1}(\cdot)$ from the Taylor remainder of $U(A)=e^{\eta q e(L^k\varepsilon)A}$, (3.14)) in the expansion of the action $S^{(k)}$ is $O(1)(L^k\varepsilon)^{\kappa}|T^{(k)}_1|$ for some $\kappa>d$; likewise the sum of terms in which the powers of the factors $L^k\varepsilon$ at the vertices add up to more than $d$; $O(1)$ independent of $k$, depending on coupling constants and $\bar n$. Stated as "a corollary of an analysis of the perturbation expansions" to be given in a separate paper.

**Proposition 3.2 (p. 622).** The polynomial $V^{(k)}(B^{(k+1)},\psi,A'^{(k)},\phi')$ has the representation (3.57)
$$V^{(k)}=\sum_{n,m\le\bar n}\ \sum_{x_i,y_j\in T^{(k)}_1}\ \sum_{j_i,\mu_i}v^{(k)}_{j_1..j_n;\mu_1..\mu_m}(B^{(k+1)},\psi;x_1..x_n,y_1..y_m)\,\phi'_{j_1}(x_1)\cdots\phi'_{j_n}(x_n)A'_{\mu_1}(y_1)\cdots A'_{\mu_m}(y_m)$$
with $|v^{(k)}|\le O(1)(L^k\varepsilon)^{\kappa_0}\exp(-\delta_0d(x_1,..,y_m))$, $d$ the length of the shortest graph connecting the points, constants independent of $k$; $\kappa_0$ can be taken $\frac12-\beta$ with $\beta>0$ arbitrarily small (p. 623). The text says the representation is "obvious" and the content is the bound (3.58), "a proof will be given together with the proofs of the other properties of the perturbation expansions".

**Inductive hypothesis (induction on $k$, (3.26)–(3.32), (3.36), p. 617).** After $k$ steps, with fields restricted by $\chi_k(A)\chi_k(\phi)$,
$$Z^\varepsilon\ge\int dA\,d\phi\,\chi_k(A)\chi_k(\phi)\,e^{-S^{(k),L^k\varepsilon}(A,\phi)}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\exp\big(O(1)(L^j\varepsilon)^{\kappa}|T_1^{(j)}|\big),$$
$$S^{(k),L^k\varepsilon}(A,\phi)=-\log Z_k-\log Z_k(A^{(k),\varepsilon})+\tfrac12\langle A,\Delta^{(k),L^k\varepsilon}A\rangle+\tfrac12\langle\phi,\Delta^{(k),L^k\varepsilon}(A^{(k),\varepsilon})\phi\rangle-\mathscr P^{(k),L^k\varepsilon}(A^{(k),\varepsilon},\phi)+E_0 ,$$
where $\mathscr P^{(k)}$ is a perturbatively defined polynomial with the structure (3.35)–(3.36): a Taylor sum up to order $\bar n$ in $(e',\lambda')$ of $E_k(e',\lambda',eA^{(k),\varepsilon},\phi)=-\log[\prod_{j<k}\!\int dA'_j\,e^{-\frac12\langle A'_j,(C^{(j)})^{-1}A'_j\rangle}\,T[\exp(-\tfrac12\langle\phi',(-\Delta_{eA^{(k)}+e'\sum A'^{(j)}})\phi'\rangle)-\sum\varepsilon^d(\mathscr P(e',\lambda',\phi)-E)]]$. The induction step is carried out in (3.37)–(3.65).

**Proof of the Theorem (lower bound), p. 624–625.** Choose $K$ with $L^K\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0<L^{K+1}\varepsilon$, apply (3.26)–(3.32) with $k=K$; because $A^{(K),\varepsilon}$ is small, expand around $A^{(K)}=0$ (Prop. 3.1), bound $|V^{(K)}|\le O(1)(L^K\varepsilon)^{\kappa}|T_1^{(K)}|$ via Prop. 3.2, bound the remaining quadratic forms (bounded because $L^K\varepsilon\sim\varepsilon_0$) to get (3.66)–(3.68) $Z^\varepsilon\ge Z_KZ_K(0)\exp(-E_0+O(1)|T_\varepsilon|)$, and then (3.69), $Z_KZ_K(0)e^{-E_0}=[\det(I+a_K(L^K\varepsilon)^{d-2}P_KG^\varepsilon)]^{-1/2}[\det(I+a_K(L^K\varepsilon)^{d-2}P_KG^\varepsilon(0))]^{-1/2}e^{O(1)|T_\varepsilon|}\ge\exp(O(1)|T_\varepsilon|)$ (using $\log\det(I+X)\le\operatorname{Tr}X$ and the bound $\operatorname{Tr}(P_KG)=O(|T_\varepsilon|)$).

## 3. Proof architecture

§1 (pp. 603–607): statement, notation, rescalings. §2 (pp. 608–612): the RG transformation for scalar fields in an external field $A$, its composition law, and the propagator/covariance bounds (Props. 2.1–2.3, stated without proof). §3 (pp. 612–625): the lower bound.

Mechanism of §3.
1. Step 1 (3.1)–(3.22). Insert restrictions $\chi_0(A)\chi_0(\phi)$ (small-field cut-off at scale $\varepsilon$) and $\chi_1(B)\chi_1(\psi)$ on the block-averaged "background" fields. Rescale to the unit lattice (3.7). Translate $A=A'+aL^{-2}C^{(0)}Q^*B$ (3.10) and $\phi=\phi'+aL^{-2}C^{(0)}(B^{(1)})Q^*(B^{(1)})\psi$ (3.18), which separates the quadratic form into one in $(B,\psi)$ (with the new quadratic form $\Delta^{(1),L}$, whose kernel is $\frac12\langle B,\Delta^{(1),L}B\rangle$) plus one in the fluctuations $(A',\phi')$ with covariance $C^{(0)}$, $C^{(0)}(B^{(1)})$. The restrictions are arranged so that the new $\chi$'s imply the old ones: (3.12)–(3.13), (3.20)–(3.21), with $p-1\ge p_1$, $b_0p\log L\ge4db_1(1+\log L)^{p_1}$ (this makes $\chi_1(B)\chi(A')\chi_0(A'+B^{(1)})$ collapse, i.e. the large cut-off is implied by the small one on the sum). The action is then Taylor expanded in the field $A$ using $U(A)=1+\sum_{n\le\bar n}\frac{(\eta qe A)^n}{n!}+\frac{(\ldots)^{\bar n+1}}{(\bar n+1)!}R_{\bar n+1}(\cdot)$, $|R_{\bar n+1}(qA)|\le1$ for real $A$ (3.14)–(3.16). The interaction terms have coefficients $\propto$ powers of $\varepsilon^{(4-d)/2}$ (3.17).
2. Cumulant expansion (3.23)–(3.25). One needs a lower bound on $\int d\mu_C\,\chi\,e^V$. They use $\langle\chi e^{V}\rangle=\exp[\langle V\rangle+\frac1{2!}\langle V^2\rangle^T+\dots+\frac1{\bar n!}\langle V^{\bar n}\rangle^T+O(\varepsilon^\kappa|T_1|)]$, $\kappa>d$ (3.24), citing a "cluster expansion" lemma of Benfatto, Cassandro, Gallavotti, Nicol\`o, Olivieri, Presutti, Scacciatelli [2] (stated as "the lemma formulated on p. 152 of [2]", i.e. CMP 59 (1978) 143–166) and the method of Gawedzki–Kupiainen [14]; terms with $n>\bar n$ are discarded into the error. $\langle V^n\rangle^T=O((\varepsilon^{\kappa})|T_1|)$ for $n>6$ because of connected graphs with exponentially decaying propagators. The result is a polynomial $\mathscr P'^{(1),L}(B^{(1)},\psi)$ and the lower bound (3.25).
3. Induction (3.26)–(3.65). The same operations after the $(k+1)$-st transformation: rescale from the $L^k\varepsilon$ lattice to the unit lattice (3.38)–(3.40), translate (3.41)–(3.42), expand the propagator $G_k(\Omega,A+B)=\sum_{n\le\bar n}G_k(\Omega,B)[V_kG_k(\Omega,B)]^n+\dots$ with $V_k=F_{1,k}$-terms (3.44)–(3.45), expand the determinant factor $Z_k(A^{(k)})$ using $\log(1+\lambda)\le\lambda-\frac{\lambda^2}2+\dots+\frac{(-1)^{n-1}\lambda^n}{n}$ ($n$ odd) to get a lower bound (3.46)–(3.47) (i.e. a sum of one-loop graphs), then the second translation (3.48) and the cumulant expansion (3.56)–(3.60) via Props. 3.1–3.2. The restrictions must be consistent at each step, and this gives conditions $p-1\ge p_1$, $b_0p\log L\ge c_1b_1(1+\log L)^{p_1}$, $\frac12b_0(p-1)\log L\ge c_2(L^k\varepsilon)^\alpha$ (3.54); the last needs $L^k\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$ small, which is why one stops at $K$ with $L^K\varepsilon\sim\varepsilon_0$. The new action is again of the form (3.30) with the effective perturbative expansion defined through $E_k$ (3.62)–(3.65), using the composition law (2.14) and convention (3.34) for contours $\Gamma^{(k)}_{y,x}$ built from the sum $A=A^{(k),\varepsilon}+\sum_jA'^{(j)}$ of independent Gaussian layers (3.33).
4. Conclusion (3.66)–(3.69), as described in §2 above.

Which steps are routine: algebra of translations and the Gaussian integration; the compatibility of cut-offs (3.54). Genuine content (all deferred): Props. 2.1–2.3 (decay/regularity of covariant Green's function, proved later in E3); the cumulant expansion in a fixed finite volume with restrictions (cited from [2], [14] with "some obvious modifications", pp. 616, 623); Props. 3.1, 3.2 (control of the perturbation expansion), promised in a separate paper.

## 7. Weak spots and open points

- The results are all deferred: Props. 2.1–2.3 (later E3), 3.1–3.2 ("a separate paper"). So the paper as printed does not prove its Theorem. The cumulant expansion in finite volume with the restrictions $\chi$ is cited from [2], [14] with "obvious modifications"; the connectedness argument and exponential decay for "graphs" are only indicated.
- Statements such as (3.24) include the characteristic function $\chi$ inside the expectation; the validity of the cumulant expansion with the restriction (needed for $e^V$ unbounded below since $V$ has no sign) is the delicate point, not proved.
- (3.62) is acknowledged as "not well defined" because the exponent is a high-order polynomial, not necessarily positive; fixed only by remarks (p. 624).
- Typos and inconsistencies: running head "603–636" vs actual 603–626; "(3.60)" is used for two different displays (p. 623, the definition of $E(e',\lambda',B,\psi)$, and the next one, which should be (3.61)); the Prop. 2.1 statement uses "$\delta_0,c_0,R_0$ independent of $d$, $a$, $k$, $\Omega$ and depending on $d,a,M$ only" — i.e. the wording is self-contradictory in the original; "(1.24)" is cited on p. 613 but there is no (1.24) (probably (1.23)); (3.48) refers to "(2.66), (3.70)", which do not exist in this paper (apparently referring to a longer manuscript version). $e(L^k\varepsilon)$ denotes both the coupling and its running version with the exponent $\frac{4-d}2$.
- Only the vacuum-energy bound, not correlation or large-field control; no large-field region is analysed (lower bound discards it).
- The model is the abelian Higgs model (Proca, $N=2$) with the quartic coupling $\lambda>0$; the "(Higgs)" content is the coupling to $A$.

## 8. Preliminary formalization notes

Not a candidate for direct formalization: its main statements are deferred to other papers and are model-specific (abelian gauge field, ultraviolet-stable continuum limit, counterterms). Reusable pieces: (i) the composition law for Gaussian block transformations (2.12)–(2.15) with $A=0$, $Q$ plain block average (this is already in the O(N) series and is elementary: Gaussian convolution); (ii) the telescoping identity (2.42)–(2.43), which writes the propagator after $k$ blocks as a sum of independent layers; this is useful for a formal treatment of the free flow; (iii) elementary $\log\det$ and Taylor-remainder manipulations in §3. Hardest: Props. 2.1–2.3 (exponentially decaying lattice Green's function bounds) and the cumulant expansion lemma.

## 9. What the O(N) series uses from this paper

**Verdict: the O(N) series does not use this paper at all, apart from (probably) P1's citation of "[2a]" for the definition of $T$.** P4's bibliography entry [3] is a defective citation: none of the four uses of "[3]" in the P4 text refers to E2. The four uses are as follows.

I read P4 PDF pages 13 and 19 (journal pp. 687, 693), all of E2, and §5 and the reference list of E3.

(a) "Sect. 4 of [3]" (analyticity spaces $\Xi^c_j(\mathbb B_j(X))$, P4 p. 677). E2 has only three sections (§1 pp. 603–607, §2 pp. 608–612, §3 pp. 612–625); there is no Sect. 4. E2 contains nothing about analyticity spaces of effective actions. The spaces $\Xi^c_j(\mathbb B_j(X);\sigma,\varepsilon)$ are defined in P3 Sect. 4 ((4.25)–(4.28), pp. 72–76; P3 has exactly four sections). This is P3 (CMP 182, 33). I did not reopen P4 p. 677; this identification rests on the P3 review's description of its own §4 and on the task description.

(b) "Sect. 3 of [3]" (a constant $B_4$, P4 p. 696). E2 §3 contains no constant $B_4$. P3 §3 defines $B_4=9\cdot2^{d+1}B_0^2c_1^2(2L+1)^d$ in (3.20) (bound $|D_{k,x}f|\le B_4|f|$). This is P3. (Same caveat on P4 p. 696 as in (a): not reopened.)

(c) "the theorem in Sect. 5 of [3] on unit lattice operators" (P4 p. 687). Page 687 (viewed): after (2.24) and (2.25), $\frac12aL^{-2}\|Q\psi'\|^2+\frac12\langle\psi',\Delta^{(k)}\psi'\rangle\ge\frac12\gamma_0L^{-2}\|\psi'\|^2$, P4 says "Thus the operator of this form satisfies all the assumptions of the theorem in Sect. 5 of [3] on unit lattice operators, hence its inverse has a uniform exponential decay. This inverse is a covariance of the corresponding Gaussian measure." E2 has no Sect. 5 and no theorem on unit-lattice operators (its Props. 2.2–2.3 give, under an assumption of a regular external gauge field, the lower bound (2.33) and the decay (2.34) for specific covariances, but are not a general theorem about abstract operators). The theorem is **E3 (CMP 89, 571), Sect. 5, "A General Theorem on Unit Lattice Operators"** (pp. 593–597). Statement there (E3 p. 594, (5.6)–(5.10)): Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb Z^d$ and let $A$ be a symmetric operator on $L^2(\Omega;\mathbb R^N)$ with $A\ge\gamma_0I$ and $|A(x,x')|\le c_0e^{-\delta_0|x-x'|}$ for $x,x'\in\Omega$ (5.6). Then there are $c_1,\delta_1>0$ such that for all $\Lambda\subset\Omega$ and $C_\Lambda=(A_\Lambda)^{-1}$ ($A_\Lambda=\chi_\Lambda A\chi_\Lambda$) one has $|C_\Lambda(x,x')|\le c_1e^{-\delta_1|x-x'|}$ (5.7) and $|\delta C_\Lambda(x,x')|\le c_1e^{-\delta_1(|x-x'|+\operatorname{dist}(x,\Lambda^c)+\operatorname{dist}(x',\Lambda^c))}$, $\delta C_\Lambda=C_\Lambda-C_\Omega$ (5.8); and if $B$ is a perturbation with $A+B$ satisfying (5.6) and $|B(x,x')|\le c_0e^{-\delta_0(|x-x'|+\operatorname{dist}(x,\Omega^c)+\operatorname{dist}(x',\Omega^c))}$ (5.9), then $|(A_\Lambda^{-1}-(A+B)_\Lambda^{-1})(x,x')|\le c_1e^{-\delta_1(|x-x'|+\operatorname{dist}(x,\Omega^c)+\operatorname{dist}(x',\Omega^c))}$ (5.10). This is exactly what P4 p. 687 needs (positivity (2.25) plus exponential decay of matrix elements implies exponential decay of the inverse). The same E3 section derives E3's Prop. I.2.3 (E2's Prop. 2.3 analogue) from it. Proof method: partition of unity $h_j$ on cubes of size $M$, approximate inverse $C=\sum_jh_jC_jh_j$ built from Neumann-boundary local inverses, $A_\Lambda C=I-R$, $\|R\|$ small for $M$ large, Neumann series $C_\Lambda=C\sum R^n$ (5.12)–(5.16), expressed as a random walk over index sequences $\omega=(\omega_0,\ldots,\omega_{2n})$.

(d) "(5.17) in [3]" (P4 p. 693). Page 693 (viewed): after (2.49), "the operator of the quadratic form (2.49) satisfies the assumptions of the theorem on unit lattice operators in [3], so its inverse has an exponentially decaying kernel and a localization expansion (see (5.17) in [3])." E2 has no (5.17) (its last equation of §3 is (3.69); there is no section 5). **E3 (5.17)** is the constraint on the multi-indices in the random-walk representation of the inverse, $C_\Lambda=\sum_\omega h_{\omega_0}C_{\omega_0}h_{\omega_0}R_{\omega_0\omega_1}\cdots C_{\omega_{2n}}h_{\omega_{2n}}$, $\max_\mu|\omega^\mu_{2i}-\omega^\mu_{2i+1}|<1$ for $i=0,..,n-1$ (E3 p. 595, OCR text; the page image should be checked for the exact index placement). This is a "localization expansion" in the sense P4 needs: a representation as a sum over walks of local operators. So (d) matches E3 (5.17) exactly; it does not match anything in E2.

Summary: (a), (b) = P3 (the entry "[3]" in the text of P4 stands for the P3 preprint "Localization expansions"); (c), (d) = E3 Sect. 5 and (5.17). The printed bibliography entry "CMP 85, 603 (1982)" is E2, which matches none of the four. Note that in P4 p. 687 the sentence just before the (c) citation cites "[2c]" for a lower bound uniform in $k$ on $\frac12\gamma_0\langle\psi',(-\Delta+\nu_k)\psi'\rangle$ ("investigated thoroughly in many papers, e.g. in [2c]"); [2c] is P1's [2c] = CMP 89, 571 = E3 (Sect. 4, Prop. 3.1'), so the same page uses both labels for E3. The two labels "[2c]" and "[3]" thus both mean E3 on P4 p. 687. This is a conjecture about the cause; the verdict on the content is not conjectural. Consequence for the formalization: treat P4's "[3]" as P3 in (a), (b) and as E3 Sect. 5 in (c), (d); E2 is not needed for P4.

## 10. Relevance to the formalization

- Interfaces: none. E2 does not supply statements the O(N) series uses.
- Reusable: the Gaussian composition law and the free flow of $a_k$ (elementary; already in the series); the layer decomposition (2.42)–(2.43) of the propagator ($G_k=\sum_{j<k}\ldots+C^{(0)}$), which may be useful as a lemma in a formal treatment of the free flow in P2/P4.
- Irrelevant (gauge/Higgs-specific): the vector field $A$, covariant Laplacian, holonomy $U(A(\Gamma))$, the mass and energy counterterms, the ultraviolet limit $\varepsilon\to0$ at fixed torus volume, Props. 3.1–3.2 on renormalized expansions, the lower bound itself (the O(N) series has a built-in lattice cut-off at scale 1).
- Green's function bounds (Props. 2.1–2.3): the scalar, $A=0$ versions are what the O(N) series needs (decay of $G_k$ and of the covariance $C^{(k)}$, uniform lower bound $\gamma_0$); but E2 states them without proof, and the actual proof is in E3. For a Lean proof one could use more standard routes (suggestion, not a claim): positivity $aL^{-2}P+\Delta^{(k)}\ge\gamma_0I$ by explicit quadratic-form lower bounds as in E3 §4 (Neumann Laplacian on blocks plus projection onto constants), and exponential decay of the inverse by Combes–Thomas or by the Neumann-series/partition-of-unity argument of E3 §5 (the latter is a self-contained algebraic argument and is a reasonable formalization target; in the O(N) setting no gauge field is present, so the "regular $A$" hypotheses disappear).
- Process note: the citation question is settled; no action on E2 is needed for the roadmap. E3 should be reviewed (it is) for items (c) and (d); in the roadmap E3 Sect. 5 should be recorded as the source of the "unit lattice operator theorem" and (5.17).
