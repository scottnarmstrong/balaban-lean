## 0. Identification

T. Balaban, "Renormalization and Localization Expansions. II. Expectation Values of the 'Fluctuation' Measures", Commun. Math. Phys. 198 (1998) 1-45. Bundle key **P6**. 45 pages (44 pages of text plus 1 page of references). Received 19 Dec 1997, accepted 12 Feb 1998.

Bibliography mapping (p. 44-45): [1] = P1; [2] = P2; [3] = P3 ("Localization expansions. I"); [4] = P4; [5] = P5. Note the printed references misprint [4] and [5]: [4] is given as "CMP 182, 675-721 (1997)", which is right for P4, while [5] is given with the same journal data "182, 675-721 (1997)" although its title ("III. Content cross-references ("(3.18) [5]", "Propositions 3.1-3.4 [5]", "(H.1)-(H.7) [5]") are consistent with P5. [6a-d] are Balaban's lattice gauge papers; [7]-[13] are outside the bundle (see 4b).

Summary. P5 gave the inductive description of the density space R_k (hypotheses (H.1)-(H.7)) and wrote the (k+1)-st transformation S^(k)T^(k) applied to rho_k as a formula (3.18) of P5, with "new contributions" to the effective action (Propositions 3.1-3.4 of P5) that are not yet renormalized and not yet localized into the small field region Z^c_{k+1}. P6 does two things.

(i) Section 2: it chooses the final small-field domain Z^c_{k+1}, cuts every term of P5's (3.18) into a part localized in Z^c_{k+1} and boundary terms, and carries out the renormalization (re-expansion of the main action around the new background configurations with the new renormalized coefficients zeta, b, c, d, e of P4, Sect. 4-5) for the new contributions. It then lists the resulting localization-expansion statements as Propositions 2.1-2.4.

(ii) Section 3: it gives the inductive definition of the new multi-index A_{k+1} and the new large-field integral operator T_{k+1} (3.3), proves the new bound with the function kappa_{k+1} and combinatorial factor C(A_{k+1}) (3.10)-(3.11), the function K(Z,A) counting "controlled steps" (Proposition 3.2, cited from [6d]), and concludes the proofs of Theorem 1 and Theorem 4 of P5 (with a weakening of (H.6), see Sect. 2 below).

(iii) Section 4: it constructs the localization expansions needed for P5's Propositions 3.1-3.4 (and the Propositions 2.1-2.3 of Sect. 2). These are expansions of expectation values with respect to Gaussian "fluctuation" measures whose interaction potential (the cubic remainder of the action, in the variable rescaled by beta_k^{-1/2}) is first localized (using [3]) and then the expectation is expanded by an elementary cluster (polymer) expansion with decoupling parameters t_Y.

What it takes from earlier papers: everything of P5 (formulas, domain definitions, propositions to be proved), P4 (renormalization procedure, bounds (4.10), (4.11), (5.31), (H.1), (H.6)), P3 (localization expansions of functions of background configurations, Propositions 2.1-2.3, 4.1, 4.3, Lemma 4.2), P2 (variational problems, Proposition 3.1 for delta-phi, delta-alpha), P1 (localization of the effective action, Section 3 of P1; Lemma 3.8, (3.104)-(3.131)), and [6d] (functions kappa, K, Proposition 3.2). What it supplies to later papers: the completed inductive step for k < k_0 except the sharp inequality in (H.6) (restored by the large field renormalization operation of P7) and the full Theorem 4 for k >= k_0.

The paper is written in a terse, "sketchy" style: many steps are stated to be "the same as in [3]/[4]" with changed notation. The emphasis given in the task (random-walk expansion, Gaussian integration by parts) does not appear in this paper explicitly; see Sect. 3 and 7.

## 1. Setting and notation

All of this is imported from P1-P5; P6 does not restate it. I record what is used and how P6 refers to it. Anything I could not check in this paper is flagged.

- Model: N-vector spin model on a torus T, N >= 2, d >= 3 (the dimension d appears in the exponents, e.g. L^{d-2}). Spin field theta; "scales" j = 0,1,...,k+1 with lattices T^(j) (the lattice T_1^(k+1) is mentioned as carrying the new spin variable psi_{k+1}, p. 3). Block size L, and a second integer M used for "M-cubes", "LM-cubes" (a larger cube scale used for localization/cluster geometry). Lengths are measured in the "eta-scale" or the L^{-1}eta-scale: the lattice spacing sequence is eta, L^{-1}eta, ... (e.g. "all volumes are measured in the L^{-1} eta-scale", p. 18; |.|_eta, |.|_{L^{-1}eta} for volumes). The partitions pi_k, pi_k' of the torus into M-cubes and LM-cubes are used for the domains; d_k(X) is a tree-length distance ("diam"/"d_k") with the measure 1/M times the length of an admissible tree graph (cf. (4.11), "(2.27) [1]").
- Renormalization step: the (k+1)-st transformation S^(k)T^(k) acts on rho_k. Averaging operator Q (block average, Q^* its adjoint), covariance of the fluctuation field C^(k) (and C^(k)1/2, its square root, which in P6 is only used as an operator with localization expansion from [3]), fluctuation variable psi (written psi'_k for the "old" fluctuation on B(Lambda_{k+1}) cap Omega''^c_{k+1}), new block-spin variable psi_{k+1} with the first equation of (4.1)[4]: theta = z_{k+1} psi_{k+1}, theta = psi + delta psi, delta psi = chi_{Lambda_{k+1}} zeta_{k+1} psi_{k+1} (p. 9).
- Main action: beta_k A_k, with the leading non-linear term -beta_k A_k(Z; psi, phi) (A_k^* its variational form, with generating set B_{k+1}, background configurations phi_{k+1,u}, alpha_{k+1,u} solving the variational problem of P2/P4, u refers to the version with the external field nu h). Coefficients beta_k, a_k, lambda_k, nu_k with the canonical scaling beta_j = beta_{k+1}(L^{j-k-1} eta)^{d-2}, lambda_j, nu_j (p. 17). Renormalized coefficients beta_{k+1}, a_{k+1}, lambda_{k+1}, nu_{k+1} determined by renormalization constants zeta_{k+1}, b_{k+1}, c_{k+1}, d_{k+1}, e_{k+1} of P4 Eqs. (5.5), (5.14); bounds (5.31)[4]. Hypothesis (H.1)[4] is on the coefficients.
- Effective densities: rho_k = (main action) + E_k (effective action, sum of local terms E^(j)(y,X;psi_j), j <= k, localized in X in D_j) + F_k (generating functional with source g) + R_n^(k) (n = 1..m, higher order remainders) + boundary terms B'^(k), B''^(k) (hypothesis (H.7)[5] with localization domains X in D_k(mod Omega^c_k)). Large field integral operators T_k(Z, A_k cap Z) with kernel bounds (1.26) of P5; combinatorial factor C(A_k cap Z), function kappa_k(Z, A_k), constants H_k, p_0, p_1, p_2 (functions of beta), inequality (1.30)-(1.32) of P5 (and (3.12) here). The exponent "kappa", "kappa_0" are the exponential decay rate of localization expansions: terms satisfy |.| <= E_0 exp(-(kappa - 1) d_k(X)) etc. (p. 8).
- Domains/multi-indices: A_k is a sequence of domains (large field regions P_k, Q_k, R_k, S_k, Omega_k, Omega''_k, Z^c_k, ...). New ones (3.1): A_{k+1} = A_k union {P_{k+1}, Q_{k+1}, R_{k+1}, S_{k+1}, Omega_{k+1}, Omega''_{k+1}, Z^c_{k+1}}. The superscript "c" denotes complement (in the torus), "~" an enlargement of a domain by a block layer (X~ = union of all cubes contained in or touching X in the relevant scale), "~n", "approx" (Z^approx, Z^{approx 2}) are iterated enlargements; the exact scale rules are in P5 / [3] and not restated. Final small-field domain Z^c_{k+1} (2.3)-(2.4); W_{k+1} = (Z^c_{k+1})^{approx 2}, U_{k+1} = Z^approx_{k+1} (2.7). Integrals for the new variable psi_{k+1} are restricted by characteristic functions chi_{k+1}(box'), defined by (1.19)[5] with k replaced by k+1 (p. 3-4).
- Localization expansions: a function depending on (theta, h, psi, g) is written as sum over "localization domains" Y in D_{k+1} (or D_{k+1}(mod Omega^c_{k+1}), meaning connectedness is modified by treating Omega^c_{k+1} as one connected piece, p. 44) of terms depending only on variables restricted to Y, analytic on spaces Xi^c(Y, B_{k+1}; {epsilon_j}) x {psi: |psi| < p_1(beta_k)} x {g: ||g||_{l^1} < 1} (4.25), with exponential decay in d_{k+1}(Y). Analyticity domains Psi^c, Xi^c are the ones of [3] and P1 (4.2), (4.33)[3].
- Gaussian "fluctuation" measure: dmu_{s,t}(psi): Gaussian measure with covariance related to unit covariance in the rescaled variable (the form chi^(k) exp(-1/2 ||psi||^2) dpsi) times exp of the "interaction potential" U_{t,u}^(k) (4.1), after rescaling psi -> C^(k)1/2 psi; the parameters s,t,u in [0,1] interpolate: u multiplies B''^(k) boundary term; s, t interpolate the Taylor expansions in the fluctuation variable (formulas (3.14)-(3.16)[5], not reproduced here; see Section 3).
- Parameter sigma: the factor beta_k^{-1/2} sigma replaces beta_k^{-1/2} in (4.1), so the expansion is analytic in sigma on a disc and equals 0 at sigma = 0; sigma = 1 corresponds to the true measure (3.16)[5] (p. 23).

## 2. Main results

The paper has two kinds of statements: the "Theorems" 1 and 4 (restated from P5, completed here), and Propositions 2.1-2.4, 3.1-3.2, Lemmas 4.1-4.2, and the proofs of Propositions 3.1-3.4 of P5.

**Theorem 1 (p. 1-2), restated.** "Under the assumptions of Theorem 1 [4], i.e. under the appropriate assumptions on the constants determining the space R_k(beta,a,lambda,nu;B,nu~), in particular for $\tilde\nu\le\beta_k^{-1}\le\frac78 L^{-2}$, which implies $k\le k_0$, the transformation $S^{(k)}T^{(k)}$ maps this space into a space defined in the same way as $R_{k+1}(\beta,a,\lambda,\nu;BL^{d-2},\tilde\nu L^2)$, but with the following changes. For hypothesis (H.6) the sharp inequality '>' in (1.32)[5] is replaced by the weaker inequality '>='. For the new contributions in the exponentials, i.e. the contributions with superscript j = k+1, the hypotheses (H.2)-(H.4), (H.7) are satisfied with an improved constant $\frac35\kappa$ instead of $\kappa$. The transformation $S^{(k)}T^{(k)}$ determines uniquely the transformation $\mathcal S^{(k)}T^{(k)}$ [calligraphic S, on extended spaces of effective actions (1.6)[4]]. It satisfies all the conclusions of Theorem 2 [4], i.e. establishes the mapping (1.7)[4] between the extended spaces, which satisfies Eqs. (1.8), (1.9)[4] with $c_{k+1}=0$, $d_{k+1}=b_{k+1}$, and the inequalities (1.10)[4]."

Reading: the "k <= k_0" in the statement is a consequence of the numerical assumption nu~ <= beta_k^{-1} <= (7/8) L^{-2} as stated; the paper itself says the theorem is applied "in the case k < k_0 and all the background configurations in the inductive description are determined by the variational problems without the external field term, that is we put nu_k = 0" (p. 2). The cases k >= k_0 are discussed after Theorem 4. (The printed statement "(1.7)[4] ... c_{k+1}=0, d_{k+1}=b_{k+1}" -- I transcribe exactly as printed; I did not check against P4 whether it should read d = b.)

## 3. Proof architecture

**Section 1 (Introduction, p. 1-3).** States Theorems 1, 4, explains the plan, and says the cluster expansion of the form of [8,10] is used only for bounds; localization is done "in an elementary way". It stresses that the method needs "complete localization": because of the non-linear leading term -beta_k A_k, a term localized in X depends on other expressions (covariances, background configurations) localized in subdomains of X; this is claimed to be the strongest restriction among renormalization group methods (compare [8,10-12]). Constructions of Section 4 also cover expansions needed for the next paper (large field renormalization).

**Section 2 (renormalization of the effective actions, pp. 3-15).** Mechanism: the crucial property is that localizations of the "primed" effective action $\mathcal E'^{(k+1)}_0(Z^c_k,\Omega_{k+1},\Omega''_{k+1};\tilde\theta,\psi)$ to subdomains of Omega''_{k+1} equal localizations of the same action constructed on the whole torus (the torus is one admissible Omega''). So renormalization can be done on the whole torus (by P4, Sect. 4, 5, with thermodynamic limit of the pre-localized functions, Proposition 5.1 [4] and (5.31)[4]) and then restricted. Steps:
1. Small field domain (2.1)-(2.7): partition of unity (2.1) in large field regions S_{k+1}, with chi'_{k+1} chi_{k+1} = chi_{k+1} (2.2); Z^c_{k+1} defined by (2.3) as union of cubes of pi'_{k+1} at distance > 2MR_k + M from (Omega''^c_{k+1})^{~4} union S^~_{k+1}; (2.6): ((Z^c_{k+1})^{approx 2})^~ is disjoint from (Omega''^c_{k+1})^{~3} union S_{k+1}, so only small field functions chi_{k+1} act on W~_{k+1}.
2. Decomposition of new contributions (2.8)-(2.10): E'_0^(k+1) split into the piece localized in Z^c_{k+1} (2.9) (which is the restriction to Z^c_{k+1} of the whole-torus function) and boundary terms; F'_0 via the representation of [4] (2.52) in terms of M'^(k+1) split into three groups (2.10); R'_n similarly. R'_m is kept primed because the large field renormalization will contribute (the factor beta_{k+1}/beta_k to the power n: const. beta_k^{-n-1} beta_{k+1}^n = const.(gamma_{k+1}L^{d-2})^n beta_k^{-1} <= 1/2 for beta large, p. 5).
3. Main action and boundary corrections (2.11)-(2.17): A^*_{k+1,u}(Z^c_k, B_{k+1}; theta, phi') is split as A^*(Z^c_k cap Z_{k+1}, ...) + A^*(Z^c_{k+1}; ...). 4. Old effective action E_k: split (2.19) into terms with points in Z^c_k cap Z_{k+1} (boundary, E') and the remainder E_k(Z^c_{k+1}; psi~...), plus an integral over t of the derivative of E^(j) along psi + t delta psi' for each scale j (2.18)-(2.19), using that delta psi^(j)_{k+1,u}(delta psi')=0 on W_{k+1}. Renormalization proper (2.21)-(2.28): re-expansion of the old main action around the *renormalized* background phi_{k+1}, alpha_{k+1} (the ones with the new coefficients), (2.22): $\beta_{k+1,u}A^*_{k+1,u}(Z^c_{k+1};\theta,\phi_{k+1,u},\alpha_{k+1,u})=\beta_{k+1}A^*_{k+1}(Z^c_{k+1};\psi_{k+1},\phi_{k+1})+\beta_{k+1}C^{(k+1)}(Z^c_{k+1};\delta\psi,\delta\phi_{k+1},\delta\alpha_{k+1})+\dots$ with explicit quadratic forms (2.23) and the variational equations (2.24) for delta phi, delta alpha (a modified inhomogeneous term f_0 with the nu_{k+1} piece).
