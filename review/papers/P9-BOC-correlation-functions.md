# P9: Balaban–O'Carroll, correlation functions (one- and two-point) of the low-temperature N-vector model

Page convention: journal pages 493–520 as printed; PDF page = journal page − 492 (PDF p.1 = journal p.493). Pages below are journal pages. The paper is short and sketchy: it is a "bookkeeping" extension of P5 with several steps cited, not proved. Reference numbers [1]–[7] in P9 are P1–P7 respectively (checked against the bibliography, p.519–520). P8 is NOT cited in P9's bibliography.

## 0. Identification

T. Balaban, M. O'Carroll, "Low Temperature Properties for Correlation Functions in Classical N-Vector Spin Models", Commun. Math. Phys. 199 (1999) 493–520 (received 13 Oct 1996, accepted 12 Jan 1998). Bundle key P9. 28 PDF pages (of which p.519–520 are references and a half-empty last page; the actual content is about 25 pages, with 7 pages of Sect. 1 and statements).

Summary. The paper takes the renormalization group (RG) description of the generating function $\log Z(g)$ with a source $g$ from P1–P7 (mainly the inductive description of P5 and the fluctuation-integral analysis of P4) and adds to the effective action, at every RG step $k$, two pieces of "correlation-function" data: the first-order-in-$g$ vector function $M_k$ (one-point function) and the second-order-in-$g$ matrix kernel $G_k=C_k+R_k$ (two-point function). It re-proves, with the same type of localization expansions as in P5, that these descriptions (hypotheses (H.1), (H.2), pp. 496–497) are preserved by one more RG step (Sect. 3). It then goes to the last step, where the integral over the remaining zero-momentum scale is done in the presence of $v_k\approx$ O(1) and the large-field polymer expansion of P6/P7 is used (Sect. 4), and extracts: $|m-1|<c\beta^{-1+\rho_1}$ for the magnetization; for $v>0$ exponential decay with rate $\propto\sqrt v$ of the longitudinal and transverse truncated two-point functions; for $v\downarrow0$ the transverse function is $\beta^{-1}\gamma_\infty^{-1}m^2\Delta^{-1}+R$ with $|R|<c(1+|x-y|)^{-(d-2+\rho_0)}$ and the longitudinal function is $O(|x-y|^{-(d-2+\rho_0)})$ (Theorem 1.1, pp. 494–495). Role in the series: it is the endpoint; it takes everything from P1–P7 and supplies nothing to later papers. P8 (the "simple method") is not used here in the form of P8 (see Sect. 4a/7); the generating-function approach of P9 is in the same spirit (use $\mathcal F_k$ at all scales), but P8 is not cited.

## 1. Setting and notation

### 1.1 Model (p.493, (1.1), and abstract)

Lattice $\mathbb Z^d$, $d\ge3$, spin $\phi(x)\in\mathbb R^N$, $N\ge2$; fixed unit vector $h\in\mathbb R^N$, $|h|=1$ (taken as $e_1$ in Sect. 4). Gibbs factor (abstract and (1.1)), with a source term:
$$\rho(\phi)=\exp\Big[-\beta\big(\tfrac12\|\partial\phi\|^2+\tfrac\lambda8\|\,|\phi|^2-1\|^2+\tfrac v2\|\phi-h\|^2\big)+\langle g,\phi\rangle-E\Big],\qquad Z(g)=\int\rho\,d\phi,$$
with $E$ chosen so that $Z(0)=1$; $\beta<\infty$, $\lambda\le\infty$ large (soft spins $\lambda<\infty$ allowed; $\lambda=\infty$ is the hard-spin model $|\phi|=1$), $v\in(0,1]$, $g$ complex with $\|g\|_{\ell^1}<1$. Generating function of connected correlation functions: $\log Z(g)$. The $v$-term is the external field (magnetic field $\propto v$ in direction $h$). The statement of "Theorem 1" in the introduction is for $v=0$ obtained as $v\downarrow0$ limit.

Boundary conditions / volumes: following P5, finite-volume densities on tori $T$ with periodic boundary conditions; $\langle\cdot\rangle=\lim_T\langle\cdot\rangle_T$ (p.494). The thermodynamic limit is taken for fixed $v>0$ first (that is what the "limit" in the theorem means; for $v=0$ one then takes $v\downarrow0$). The paper never discusses the limit procedure; it is implicit in the use of whole-lattice functions $\mathcal F_k$ "defined on the whole lattice $T$" (p.495).

Objects: $M(x)=\langle\phi(x)\rangle$; $G(x,y)=\langle\phi(x);\phi(y)\rangle^T=\langle\phi(x)\otimes\phi(y)\rangle-\langle\phi(x)\rangle\otimes\langle\phi(y)\rangle$ (matrix valued). Theorem 1.1 writes $M=m(\beta,v)h$ and $G=G_{tr}(x-y)(I-h\otimes h)+G_{lg}(x-y)(h\otimes h)$.

Formal perturbation theory (p.494, (1.2)), at $\lambda=\infty$, $h=e_1$, $\Delta=-\partial^*\partial$ (so $-\Delta\ge0$ here; the paper writes $(\Delta+v)^{-1}$ which is positive only if $\Delta$ is the positive Laplacian; sign convention is inconsistent — see Sect. 7):
$\langle\phi_1(x)\rangle=1-\frac{N-1}{2\beta}(\Delta+v)^{-1}(0,0)$, $\langle\phi_i(x)\phi_i(y)\rangle=\frac{N-1}{2\beta}(\Delta+v)^{-1}(x,y)$ for $i=2..N$ (the factor $N-1$ looks as printed; as a transverse component one would expect $\frac1\beta(\Delta+v)^{-1}$ per component — UNCERTAIN, the factor appears inconsistent with the final result (4.17)–(4.18) and Theorem 1.1b, which have $1/\beta$ without $N-1$), $\langle\phi_1(x);\phi_1(y)\rangle^T=\frac{N-1}{4\beta^2}[(\Delta+v)^{-1}(x,y)]^2$. Remarks: massless canonical transverse behaviour, non-canonical longitudinal ($|x|^{-2(d-2)}$), parallel susceptibility infinite for $d\le4$, finite $d\ge5$ (p.494). This formal calculation is not proved in P9; it is motivation only.

- Fluctuation integral measure: $Z^{(k)-1}\exp[-\beta_kV^{(k)}+\delta\mathcal E_k]d\mu_0(\psi)$, $d\mu_0(\psi)=\prod_x e^{-|\psi(x)|^2/2}d\psi(x)/(2\pi)^{N/2}$, fluctuation field $\psi'=C^{(k)1/2}\psi$, $|\psi|<p_1(\beta_k)=A_1(\log\beta_k)^{p_1}$ (Prop. 3.1, p.502), $p_1(\beta)\le O(1)\beta^{\rho_2}$ (p.505).
- $\rho$-parameters: $\rho_0,\rho_1,\rho_2,\gamma$ and a constant $\kappa$ (as in (H.3) of P5), $\delta_0=\kappa/M$ (p.509).

## 2. Main results

### Theorem 1.1 (p.494–495)

Setting: finite volume torus $T$, periodic boundary conditions, $\langle\cdot\rangle=\lim_T\langle\cdot\rangle_T$, $M(x)=\langle\phi(x)\rangle$, $G(x,y)=\langle\phi(x);\phi(y)\rangle^T$.

Statement (transcribed; the printed text has an obvious typographical omission in the display of part a)):

There exist $\beta_0<\infty$, $\lambda_0\le\infty$ sufficiently large such that for $\beta_0\le\beta<\infty$, $\lambda_0\le\lambda\le\infty$, $M$ and $G$ can be written as
$$M(x)=m(\beta,v)\,h,\qquad G(x,y)=G_{tr}(x-y)\,(I-h\otimes h)+G_{lg}(x-y)\,(h\otimes h).$$
There exist positive constants $c,c',\rho_0$ and small $\rho_1$ such that

a) for $v>0$:
$$|m(\beta,v)-1|<c\,\beta^{-1+\rho_1},\qquad |G_{tr}(x-y)|,\ |G_{lg}(x-y)|\le\frac{c}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\exp\big[-c'\sqrt v\,|x-y|\big].$$
(The printed display reads "$|G_{tr}(x-y)-|$" with the absolute value of a second term missing: UNCERTAIN what was intended; the natural reading is $|G_{tr}(x-y)|$.)

b) for the limit $v\downarrow0$:
$$G_{tr}(x-y)=\frac1{\beta\gamma_\infty}m^2(\beta)\,\Delta^{-1}(x-y)+R(x-y),\qquad|\gamma_\infty-1|<c\beta^{-1},\quad |R(x-y)|<\frac c{(1+|x-y|)^{d-2+\rho_0}}.$$
Also $|G_{lg}(x-y)|\le c/(1+|x-y|)^{d-2+\rho_0}$. (The printed exponent is "$(1+|x-y|)^{d-2+\rho_0}$" without a minus sign; it must be in the denominator. Same sign omission in the abstract.)

Notes on scope:
- Hypotheses: $d\ge3$, $N\ge2$ (title/abstract). No separate statement for $d=3$ or $N=3$; the argument is uniform. $\gamma_0=\min\{\frac{d-2}2,\frac12\}$ is where $d$ enters; $d=3$ gives $\gamma_0=\frac12$.
- Constants depend on $d,N,L$ (implicit).
- The abstract states also $\langle\phi_1(x)\rangle=1+O(\beta^{-1/2})$ ("$1+0(1/\beta^{1/2})$"), $\langle\phi_i(x)\phi_i(y)\rangle=c_0\Delta^{-1}(x,y)+R(x,y)$, $i=2,\dots,N$, and $\langle\phi_1(x)\phi_1(y)\rangle^T=R'(x,y)$ with $|R|,|R'|<O(1)(1+|x-y|)^{-(d-2+\rho)}$ "for some $\rho>0$"; $c_0$ "a precisely determined constant". Note the abstract has $\beta^{-1/2}$ for the magnetization, while the theorem has $\beta^{-1+\rho_1}$: the abstract is weaker/inconsistent (the theorem's version follows from (2.8)–(2.9) and (4.10), noting that (2.9) has $\beta^{-1/2+\rho_1}$ per term but summing with the weights $\beta_{j-1}^{-1/2}$ gives $\beta^{-1+\rho_1}$ — as stated p.497 "by (2.8), (2.9) we have $|m_k-1|<O(1)\beta^{-1+\rho_1}$").

## 3. Proof architecture

### Sect. 2 (pp.495–501): inductive description

Defines (H.1), (H.2) (above), Lemma 1 (summation giving $|x-x'|^{-(d-2+\varepsilon')}$ decay and (2.20)), and Lemma 2 (improved bounds on $M^{(j)}$ from (2.7)). Mechanism: the main contributions to the two-point function after $k$ steps come from $C_k=\sum_j\beta_{j-1}^{-1}m_kG^{(j)}m_k$; $R_k$ has strictly subdominant decay because each $R^{ij}$ term at scale $i$ has range $L^{i}$ and carries a factor $L^{-\gamma i}$ or better; sum is controlled by Lemma 1. Lemma 2 is needed because in the next step one wants a small factor like $L^{j}\eta$ to a positive power in front of $\delta M^{(j)}$: $M^{(j)}$ vanishes when its argument is the constant background $\psi=h$ (renormalization condition (2.7)), hence it is small by $\sigma\varepsilon/\varepsilon_j=C_0(L^j\eta)^{d/2-\gamma}$ on the subdomain $\Psi_j^c(C_0L^j\eta,\varepsilon_k)$ (p.501).

### Sect. 3 (pp.501–514): the inductive hypotheses are preserved by one RG step

Step $k\to k+1$ produces three contributions: fluctuation integral; renormalization (change of background due to $\delta\phi_{k+1},\delta\psi^{(j)}_{k+1}$); large-field renormalization $\mathcal R^{(k+1)}$ of (H.4) [P5]. From P4 Theorem 2: the only effect of the RGT on the old generating functional is $\psi_k\to\psi^{(k)}(\theta)$, adding $\mathcal E^{(k+1)}_0,\mathcal F^{(k+1)}_0$.

First order in $g$ (pp.501, 504–506). Define $\delta\phi_k,\delta M^{(j)}$ (3.2). $\langle\delta M_k\rangle=m_k\langle\delta\phi_k\rangle+\sum_j\beta_{j-1}^{-1/2}\langle\delta M^{(j)}\rangle$ (3.1). To extract $\beta_k^{-1/2}$: write $\delta\phi_k=\beta_k^{-1/2}\Gamma_k(x;\beta_k^{-1/2}\psi')\psi'$ with $\Gamma_k$ given by a double Cauchy integral (3.14); expand in $\beta_k^{-1/2}$ to second order using $\langle\psi'\rangle_0=0$ (the linear term vanishes), (3.15); apply Prop. 3.1 to $F=\phi(x)$ ($j=0$, $X=\{x\}$, complex parameter $z=t\beta_k^{-1/2}\sigma+\tau$) to get localization expansion (3.16) with bound (3.17) $\Phi_k$ of order $\beta^{-1/2+3\rho_2}\eta^{(d-2)/2-2(d-2)\rho_2}$, where the inductive hypothesis $\beta_k=O(\beta L^{k(d-2)})$, $p_1(\beta)\le O(1)\beta^{\rho_2}$ is used. Terms $\langle\delta M^{(j)}\rangle$: (3.18)–(3.21), using Lemma 2 (improved bounds) on the domain $\Psi^c_j(C_0L^j\eta,\varepsilon_k)$; resummation (3.22) and bound (3.23) $|M_1^{(k+1)}(x,Y;\theta,h)|<O(1)\beta^{-1+\rho_1+\rho_2}(L^{-1}\eta)^{\rho_0}e^{-2\kappa d_{k+1}(Y)}$ requiring $1-\rho_0-(d-2)\rho_2\ge0$, $\gamma-(d-2)\rho_2>0$. ## 6. Constants and parameters

- Model: $\beta\ge\beta_0$, $\lambda\ge\lambda_0$ ($\lambda=\infty$ allowed), $v\in(0,1]$, fixed unit vector $h$; $\|g\|_{\ell^1}<1$ complex. $\beta_0,\lambda_0$ depend on $d,N$ and on the other constants. The order of choice is implicit: first $L$ (large, as in P1–P5), $\kappa$, $\delta_0=\kappa/M$, then $\gamma$, $\rho_2$, $\rho_0,\rho_1$ satisfying the constraints below, finally $\beta_0$ large (each "for $\beta$ sufficiently large" in Sect. 3 absorbs $O(1)\beta^{-\text{power}}$ factors; the power is $\min$ of $\rho$-dependent exponents). The paper does not state the order explicitly (UNCERTAIN: relies on P5's choice of $L,\kappa,K_1,M,\dots$).
- Exponent constraints (p.511), all met by small $\rho_2>0$:
 $\rho_1\ge3\rho_2$; $0<\rho_0\le\min\{\frac{d-2}2,1\}-3(d-2)\rho_2$; $0<\gamma<\min\{\frac{d-2}2,1\}$ (p.495 earlier: $0<\gamma<\gamma_0=\min\{\frac{d-2}2,\frac12\}$ — the two ranges differ: the $\frac12$ on p.495 is for hypotheses, the 1 on p.511 is stated as obtainable; the paper says on p.495 "actually a bit more careful analysis would give the best possible constant $\gamma_0=\min\{\frac{d-2}2,1\}$"); $\gamma-2(d-2)\rho_2>0$ (also $\gamma-(d-2)\rho_2>0$ in (3.23)); $1-\rho_0-(d-2)\rho_2\ge0$ (3.23); $\rho_0\le\gamma$ (p.512); $\rho_1\ge3\rho_2$; $O(1)\beta^{-1/2+\rho_2}<\frac12$; $O(1)\beta^{-1/2+2\rho_2}$ small (p.510).

## 7. Weak spots and open points

1. The paper is a sketch for most of Sect. 3: after the first-order $\phi_k$, $\delta M^{(j)}$ terms and $R_1^{k+1,k+1}$ the other terms are described as "similar", "almost exactly the same", "mostly a repetition ... we discuss a couple of cases as an illustration" (pp.510, 512). In particular, the renormalization/large-field contributions (3.43)–(3.45) are not verified term-by-term; their bounds $(3.46)$–$(p.514)$ are sketched only.
2. Prop. 3.1, the truncated product expansion (3.13): explicitly "has not been proved in [5]"; the argument given is two sentences (p.503–504). This is the essential new technical ingredient for the two-point function, so it is a real gap in the written proof.
3. The relation between the generating functional $\mathcal F_k$ restricted to $Z_k^c$ and its whole-lattice extension and the new correlation-function data in the representation (4.2) is cited, not shown; the final steps (4.3)–(4.6) cite polymer expansions in P6/P7 (footnote p.516) without checking that the $g$-dependent terms (new $\mathcal R^{(k+1)}(g)$, $\mathcal P(g)$) satisfy the hypotheses (e.g. the uniformity in $g$ for $\|g\|_{\ell^1}<1$ and analyticity, which is needed for taking $g$-derivatives).
4. Typos and inconsistencies: (i) Theorem 1.1a display "$|G_{tr}(x-y)-|$" has a missing term; (ii) missing minus signs in $(1+|x-y|)^{d-2+\rho_0}$ in the abstract and Theorem 1.1b (must be in the denominator); (iii) abstract states $\langle\phi_1\rangle=1+O(\beta^{-1/2})$ vs theorem $|m-1|<c\beta^{-1+\rho_1}$; (iv) (1.2) factor $\frac{N-1}{2\beta}$ for the transverse components versus final result $\frac1{\beta\gamma_\infty}$ in Theorem 1.1b (for the Goldstone modes one expects $\frac1\beta(-\Delta+v)^{-1}$ per component, with $\frac{N-1}{2\beta}$ appearing in the one-point function $1-\frac{N-1}{2\beta}\Delta^{-1}(0,0)$; the entry for $\langle\phi_i\phi_i\rangle$ with $\frac{N-1}{2\beta}$ looks like a copy error — UNCERTAIN); and sign convention $\Delta=-\partial^*\partial$ gives $(\Delta+v)^{-1}$ negative-definite while the formulas need positive; (v) (2.5) left-hand side "$M_k$" should be $M^{(j)}$; (vi) $\kappa_i$ in (2.18) undefined; (vii) $\eta$ never defined; (viii) $\delta_0$, $\rho$, $G_j$ vs $G_k$ symbol clashes; (ix) (4.17) formula $a_j=\frac{1-L^{-2}}{1-L^{-2j}}a$ degenerate at $j=0$; (x) index shift between (2.11) ($j=1..k$) and (4.14) ($j=0..k-1$); (xi) footnote-style reference "[6, 7]" for spontaneous magnetization in the introduction does not match the bibliography; (xii) P5 reference year printed 1997 vs 1998; (xiii) in Lemma 1 hypothesis "$\gamma+\delta\ge d-2+\varepsilon'$" vs (2.20) application where the effective exponent must be justified.
5. The relation (4.18) $\beta_j^{-1}=(\beta\gamma_\infty)^{-1}L^{-j(d-2)}+O(\beta^{-1})L^{-j(d-2+2\gamma)}$ and the claim $|\gamma_\infty-1|<c\beta^{-1}$ are asserted without reference; they require the RG flow of $\beta_j$ from P4/P5 (not stated in P9 as a hypothesis).
6. The bound $G_{lg}=O(\beta^{-1}\lambda^{-2}(1+|x|)^{-(d+2)})$ (p.519) concerns only the $C_{lg}$ part; the contribution of $R_k$ gives only $(1+|x|)^{-(d-2+\rho_0)}$, so the theorem is consistent but the leading-order claim of (1.2) that $G_{lg}\sim[(\Delta)^{-1}]^2\cdot\beta^{-2}$ is not recovered (its decay $|x|^{-2(d-2)}$ would require $\rho_0\ge d-2$, but $\rho_0\le\frac{d-2}2$). 7. The thermodynamic limit: existence of $\lim_T$ is taken for granted; periodic b.c. only; no discussion of other boundary conditions or of the infinite-volume Gibbs state for $v=0$ (obtained as $v\downarrow0$ limit of the $v>0$ infinite volume limit; the $v\downarrow 0$ limit of the transverse function is shown using the dominated convergence argument (4.15) which assumes the form for $G^{(j)}(v)$, $G^{(j)}(0)$ — fine — but not for $\mathcal R,\mathcal P$ pieces: these are shown bounded uniformly by $O(1)\beta^{-2}L^{-(k+1)(d-2)/2}|x-x'|_1^{-(d-2)/2}\ldots$, which goes to zero for $k\to\infty$ at fixed $x-x'$; that these yield the limit of $R(x-y)$ with the bound $(1+|x-y|)^{-(d-2+\rho_0)}$ requires combining this bound with the factor decay; the stated exponent $\frac{d-2}2$ in the $|x-x'|_1^{-(d-2)/2}$ factor is weaker than $d-2+\rho_0$, so the remark on p.517 ("sub-dominant decay") should not give $R=O(|x|^{-(d-2+\rho_0)})$ unless the first-order exponential decay at scale $L^{k}$ is used: for $|x-x'|\gg L^{k+1}$ the exponential makes it small, for $|x-x'|\lesssim L^{k+1}$ the prefactor $L^{-(k+1)(d-2)/2}$ times $\ldots$ is $\ge|x-x'|^{-(d-2)/2}$ — UNCERTAIN whether Theorem 1.1b's bound on $R$ is fully justified by the paper's text for the $\mathcal R,\mathcal P$ contributions; the $\beta^{-2}$ and $L^{-(k+1)(d-2)/2}\ldots$ factors as printed do not obviously give $(1+|x|)^{-(d-2+\rho_0)}$). ## 8. Preliminary formalization notes

- Theorem 1.1 is the natural headline statement but cannot be formalized without the entire P1–P7 stack: the model-theoretic part (statement of the Gibbs measure on the torus, correlation functions, thermodynamic limit) is formalizable early; the statement should be formulated carefully: the displayed theorem has typos; formalization must fix the intended statement (denominator exponent $d-2+\rho_0$; $|G_{tr}|$ with $\sqrt v$ decay), and the limit procedure ($T\to\infty$ at fixed $v>0$, then $v\downarrow 0$).
- Self-contained pieces: Lemma 1 (elementary summation, Mathlib: geometric sums, comparison); the algebra of Sect. 4 (4.10)–(4.13), (4.19) (resolvent identities, $O(N)$ equivariance, block structure) once $H_j$, $C^{(j)}$ of P2 are available; dominated convergence step (4.15) (Mathlib has dominated convergence for series / tsum).
- Needed infrastructure: complex analytic functions of many variables (Cauchy estimates in several variables; Mathlib has one-variable Cauchy/circle integrals and `DifferentiableOn` for several complex variables only partially — flagged guess), polymer/cluster expansion machinery (not in Mathlib), Gaussian measures on finite-dimensional spaces (partially in Mathlib), discrete Green functions with exponential decay (P2; not in Mathlib), the localization expansion bookkeeping (P3, P5), and $O(N)$ symmetry reduction.
- Hardest parts: everything that depends on P5–P7 (multiscale large-field analysis); Proposition 3.1 for the truncated product (new and only sketched here); the control of $\beta_j$ asymptotics and $\gamma_\infty$; the justification of the thermodynamic and $v\downarrow0$ limits. For roadmap purposes P9 adds relatively little new infrastructure beyond a "second-order and first-order in $g$" layer on top of the P5 induction, but it carries all the headline statements.
