**Summary.** E8 is the sequel of [I] = E6 (small-field generation of effective actions for YM$_4$) and [II] = E7 (cluster expansions). It treats the complete model with no restriction on the field: all large-field regions are kept. It (i) carries out the first renormalization transformation in detail with a large-field/small-field decomposition of unity (§1, pp. 245-254); (ii) states an inductive description of the effective densities $\varrho_k$ after $k$ steps as a sum over sequences of domains $\{\Omega_j\},\{A_j\},\{S_j\}$ of (characteristic function) $\times$ (integral operator $\mathbf T_k$) $\times$ $\exp$ (effective action $A_k=-A+E_k+R_k+B_k-E_k$) (§2, pp. 254-264); (iii) proves Theorem 1 (the form is preserved by $\mathbf{RT}$) and Theorem 2 (uniform bounds on $E^{(j)}-\beta_jA$, $R^{(j)}$), Corollary 3 (ultraviolet stability) by treating the $(k+1)$-st step (§3, pp. 264-279) and giving a proof of the renormalization of $E^{(j)}$ via Ward-Takahashi identities (pp. 279-284). The large-field operation $\mathbf R$ is **not defined in this paper**: it is assumed to exist with stated properties and is deferred to later papers (E9/E10 presumably; E8 says "the next paper", p. 260). The bounds on the large-field operators $\mathbf T_k$ in terms of the smallness of the large-field characteristic functions (the gauge counterpart of P5's (1.26)-(1.32)) are not formulated as hypotheses in E8.

## 1. Setting and notation

**Model.** $d=4$ lattice Yang-Mills (non-Abelian, compact group $G$ with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$), Wilson action $A(U)=\sum_p[1-\mathrm{Re\,tr}\,U(\partial p)]$, $\varrho_0=\exp[-(1/g_0^2)A-E]$, $E$ a normalization constant (p. 243-244). Unit lattice reached after finitely many steps (p. 244; the procedure "finishes when we reach the unit lattice").

**Lattices and scales.** $\mathbb T_\eta$ = fine lattice of mesh $\eta$; $\mathbb T^{(k)}$ = lattice after $k$ steps (block factor $L$); $\mathbb T_\xi$, $\xi=L^{-j}$, lattice of mesh $L^{-j}$ ("the $L^{-j}$-lattice"); $\mathbb T_1^{(k)}$ = unit lattice of the rescaled picture (notation of [I]). Gauge fields $U$ on $\mathbb T_\eta$, $V_k$ on $\mathbb T^{(k)}$. The renormalization transformation is (0.1) (p. 243)
$$(T\varrho)(V)=\int dU\,\delta(\bar U V^{-1})\varrho(U),$$
$\bar U=M'(U)$ the averaged field (averaging operation of [I]); the full step is $\mathbf{RT}$, $\varrho_k=\mathbf{RT}\varrho_{k-1}=(\mathbf{RT})^k\varrho_0$ (0.2, p. 244).

**Large-field parameters.** $g_j$ coupling at scale $j$; $p_0(g)=A_0(\log g^{-2})^{p_0}$ with integer $p_0\ge5r$, $A_0$ large (p. 246 after (1.1)); $\varepsilon_j=g_jA_0(\log g_j^{-2})^{p_0}=g_jp_0(g_j)$ (2.4, p. 255); $R_j$ = smallest number of the form $L^{r'}$ with $R_j\ge(\log g_j^{-2})^{r}$, $r\ge2$ (2.5; first step p. 245 with $R_0$). Large cubes: $M=L^m$, $M_2=L^{m_2}$ with $M_1<M_2<M$; $M_1$ is the size of large cubes for which the variational-problem theorems of [I] hold, $M_2=L^2M_1$ (p. 245); partitions into $MR_0$-cubes, $LM_2R_0$-cubes, etc.; $\sim$ adds one layer of cubes of the relevant partition. Axial gauge fixing in blocks, Faddeev-Popov (1.5)-(1.6), chosen to preserve Euclidean invariance and localizability (p. 247).

**Decompositions of unity (first step).** (1.1) on plaquettes: $1=\sum_{P_0}\chi_0(P_0)\chi_0^c(P_0)$ with small-field condition $\sup_{p\subset\square^{\sim}}|U(\partial p)-1|<\varepsilon_0$, where $\varepsilon_0=g_0p_0(g_0)$ (first choice, as in [I]) or $\varepsilon_0$ obtained by replacing $g_0^{-2}$ in the logarithm by $\gamma^{-2}+\beta\log\varepsilon^{-1}$ (second choice, p. 246). Further decompositions on the new field $V$: (1.4) (plaquette bound $\varepsilon_1L^{-2}$), (1.8) (restriction of the *fluctuation* field with size $2\delta_0$, $\delta_0=g_0A_1p_0(g_0)=(A_1/A_0)\varepsilon_0$, $A_0\gg A_1$), (1.18) (restriction $|A(b)|<g_0^{-1}\delta_0$ on the true fluctuation field $A$, to restore gauge invariance), (3.2), (3.3), (3.16) at step $k+1$.

**Domains.** Sequences (2.1)-(2.3) and the sets $\mathbf B$ (2.2) are described in §3. The background (minimal) configuration $U_{\mathbf B}(V)=U_k(V)$ (2.12), $U_{k,\square}$ (2.16), the spaces $\tilde{\mathcal U}^c_j(X,\tilde\alpha_0,\tilde\alpha_1)$ (2.34)-(2.39), $\alpha_{0,j}=g_jC_0(\log g_j^{-2})^{q_0}$, $\alpha_{1,j}=g_jC_1(\log g_j^{-2})^{q_1}$ (2.28).

## 2. Main results

All statements are in terms of the "inductive assumptions" of §2 (pp. 254-262), which I list in 2.1.

### 2.1 The inductive assumptions (pp. 254-262)

There are no numbered hypotheses (no "H1-H7"). The assumptions are the following list, in the order of the text. (Each is a statement about $\varrho_k$ after $k$ steps of $\mathbf{RT}$.)

**(A1) General form (2.18), p. 257.** With $\chi_k(\Omega_k)$ from (2.17),
$$\varrho_k(V_k)=\sum_{\{\Omega_j\},\{A_j\}}\chi_k(\Omega_k)\,\mathbf T_k(\{\Omega_j\},\{A_j\})\exp A_k\Big(\frac1{g_k^2},U_k\Big),$$
summation over *admissible* sequences of domains; the sum over $\{S_j\}$ is included in $\mathbf T_k$ and $A_k$ ("we suppress this dependence"); $\chi_k(\Omega_k)=\prod_{\square\subset\Omega_k}\chi\{\sup_{p\subset\square^\sim}|U_{k,\square}(V_k,\partial p)-1|<\varepsilon_k\eta^2\}$ (2.17), $\square$ in the partition of $\mathbb T_\eta$ into $LM_2R_k$-cubes.

**(A2) Sequences of domains (2.1)-(2.3), pp. 254-255.** $\Omega_1\supset A_1\supset\Omega_2\supset A_2\supset\dots\supset\Omega_k\supset A_k$ with $\Omega_j\in\mathcal D_j$, $A_j\in\mathcal D_j$ (unions of $MR_j$-cubes in $\mathbb T_{L^{-j}}$); $S_j\subset\Omega_j\cap A_j^c$ empty or a union of $LM_2R_j$-cubes; if $\Omega_j\cap A_j^c\neq\emptyset$ then $S_j\neq\emptyset$ and it contains $\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_j^{\sim-1}=\Omega_j\cap(\Omega_j^c)^\sim$. A function $\chi(\Omega_j\cap A_j^c,S_j)$ (small-field characteristic function of the fluctuation field associated with $R_j=S_j\setminus(\Omega_j\cap(\Omega_j^c)^\sim)$) and $\zeta(\Omega_j^c)$ (large-field characteristic functions, summed). $A_j=\Omega_j$ may occur "as a result of an $\mathbf R$-operation" (p. 255). Large-field regions $Z_j=A_j^c$ (2.3): any component of $Z_j$ contained in a cube of size $100MR_j$ is a rectangular parallelepiped. The distance between the boundaries of consecutive domains is at least $2MR_j$, "which will be determined inductively by the operations in the $k+1$-st step" (p. 256). **There is no list of the conditions that make a sequence admissible**; see §7.

**(A3) Coupling constants (2.4)-(2.9), pp. 255-256.** $\varepsilon_j=g_jA_0(\log g_j^{-2})^{p_0}$; $R_j$ smallest power of $L$ with $R_j\ge(\log g_j^{-2})^r$; for $n>m$
$$g_n\le(1+g_n^2\beta'(n-m))^{1/2}g_m\le(1+\beta_0)(n-m)^{1/2}g_m,\quad g_m\le(1+\beta_0)g_n,$$
with $\beta_0>0$ arbitrarily small for $g$ small; consequences (2.7)-(2.9): $\varepsilon_n\le(1+\beta_0)(n-m)^{1/2}\varepsilon_m$, $\varepsilon_m\le(1+\beta_0)^2(n-m)^{\beta_0}\varepsilon_n$, $R_n\le LR_m$, $R_m\le(L+1)(n-m)^{\beta_0}R_n$. These follow from the RG equations (0.20) of [I] and properties of the $\beta$-function ([I]).

**(A4) Factorization of $\mathbf T_k$ (2.19)-(2.22), pp. 257-258.** If the last large-field region $Z_k=X_1\cup\dots\cup X_n$ (components),
$$\mathbf T_k(Z_k)=\prod_{i=1}^n\mathbf T_k(X_i)\quad(2.19),$$
and the factors commute. For one component, an ordered product of one-step operations
$$\mathbf T_k(X)=\prod_{j=k-1}^{0}\mathbf T^{(j)}(Z_{j+1}\cap X)\quad(2.20)$$
(operation $\mathbf T^{(j)}$ integrates the gauge variables $V_j$ on $\Omega_{j+1}^c\cap X$ and the fluctuation field $A_j$ on $Z_{j+1}\cap\Omega_{j+1}\cap X$ if nonempty). If not changed by an $\mathbf R$-operation,
$$\mathbf T^{(j)}(Z_{j+1}\cap X)=\int dV_j|_{\Omega_{j+1}^c\cap X}\,\delta(\bar V_jV_{j+1}^{-1})\,\zeta(\Omega_{j+1}^c)\int dA_j|_{Z_{j+1}\cap\Omega_{j+1}\cap X}\chi(Z_{j+1}\cap\Omega_{j+1}\cap X)\exp\Big[-\tfrac12\langle A_j,C^*\Delta^{(j)}CA_j\rangle+\tfrac12\langle A_j,C^*\Delta^{(j)}CC^{(j)}(A_{j+1})C^*\Delta^{(j)}CA_j\rangle\Big]\quad(2.21),$$
the quadratic form coupling only fields in the same component of $Z_{j+1}\cap\Omega_{j+1}$ (other components' terms go into $\mathbf B$-terms). After $\mathbf R$-operations the form (2.21) persists, but characteristic functions change and "the domain $Z_{j+1}\cap\Omega_{j+1}\cap X$ may be empty" (p. 258). Alternative factorization if the last $k-m$ operations have the form (2.21): $\mathbf T_k(X)=\prod_{j=k-1}^{m}\mathbf T^{(j)}(Z_{j+1}\cap\Omega_{j+1}\cap X)\mathbf T_m(Z_m\cap X)$ (2.22).

**(A5) Effective action (2.23)-(2.33), pp. 258-260.**
$$A_k\Big(\frac1{g_k^2},U_k\Big)=-A\Big(\frac1{g_k^2},U_k\Big)+\mathbf E_k(U_k)+\mathbf R_k(U_k)+\mathbf B_k(U_k,A)-E_k\quad(2.23).$$
Coupling constants defined by (2.24): $\dfrac1{g_{j-1}^2(x)}=\dfrac1{g_j^2(x)}+\beta_j(g_{j-1})\phi_j(x)$, $\phi_j\in C_0^\infty(A_j)$, $\phi_j=1$ on $A_j^{\sim-1}$, so that $g_j^2(x)=g_j^2$ on the final domain.
  - $\mathbf E_k(U_k)=\sum_{j=1}^k[\mathbf E^{(j)}(A_j,g_{j-1},U_k)-\mathbf E^{(j)}(A_j,g_{j-1},1)-\beta_j(g_{j-1})A(\phi_j,U_k)]$ (2.25), with $\mathbf E^{(j)}(A_j,U_k)=\sum_{z\in A_j^0}\mathbf E^{(j)}(A_j,U_k,z)$, $\mathbf E^{(j)}(A_j,U_k,z)=\sum_{X\ni z}\mathbf E^{(j)}(X,U_k,z)$ over $X\in\mathcal D_j$, $X\subset A_j$ (2.26)-(2.27); $A_j^0$ = $A_j^{(j)}$ minus one layer of $MR_j$-cubes. Properties of a term: (i) depends on $U_k|_X$; (ii) has an analytic extension $\mathbf E^{(j)}(X,(\mathbf U,\mathbf J),z)$ to the space $\mathcal U_j^c(X,\alpha_{0,j},\alpha_{1,j})$ with $\alpha_{0,j}=g_jC_0(\log g_j^{-2})^{q_0}$, $\alpha_{1,j}=g_jC_1(\log g_j^{-2})^{q_1}$, $q_0,q_1>1$ integers, $C_0,C_1$ large; (iii) gauge invariant; (iv) the bound (I.1.18) (of [I]). Euclidean covariance (2.29): the *unrestricted* sums $\mathbf E^{(j)}(U_j,z)$ over all $X\in\mathcal D_j$ containing $z$ satisfy $\mathbf E^{(j)}(rU_j,rz)=\mathbf E^{(j)}(U_j,z)$ for Euclidean transformations $r$ of $\mathbb T^{(j)}$.
  - $\mathbf R_k(U_k)=\sum_{j=1}^k[\mathbf R^{(j)}(A_j,U_k)-\mathbf R^{(j)}(A_j,1)]$ (2.30), localized as in (I.1.7) over $X\in\mathcal D_j$, $X\subset A_j^{\sim-1}$; properties (i)-(iii) as for $\mathbf E$, and (iv) replaced by the stronger
$$|\mathbf R^{(j)}(X,(\mathbf U,\mathbf J))|\le g_j^{\kappa_0}\exp(-\kappa d_j(X))\quad(2.31),$$
$\kappa_0$ arbitrarily large (like $\kappa$). No coupling constant counterterm for $\mathbf R$; only vacuum-energy renormalization. Euclidean covariance (2.32) only for $r$ leaving the partition of $\mathbb T_\xi$ into $MR_j$-cubes invariant: $\mathbf R^{(j)}(rX,rU_j)=\mathbf R^{(j)}(X,U_j)$; summed over $X$: (2.33).
  - $\mathbf B_k=\sum_{j=1}^k\mathbf B^{(j)}(U_k,A,\{S_i\})$ (2.40), $\mathbf B^{(j)}=\sum_X\mathbf B^{(j)}(X,U_k,A,\{S_i\cap X\})$ (2.41), over $X\in\mathcal D_j$ with $X\cap\Omega_j\ne\emptyset$ and $X\cap Z_j^\sim\neq\emptyset$; terms depend on $U_k,A$ restricted to $X$; have analytic extension to $\tilde{\mathcal U}^c_j(X,\tilde\alpha_0,\tilde\alpha_1)\times\{\dots\}$ (spaces (2.34)-(2.39), $\beta=1/4$ in the decay factors $(1-\beta(1-2^{-(j-n)}))$); gauge invariant; and
$$|\mathbf B^{(j)}(X,(\mathbf U,\mathbf J),A,\{S_i\cap X\})|<B_0\exp(-\kappa d_j(X))\quad(2.42).$$
Components of $X\cap\Omega_0$, $\Omega_0=\Omega_1^{\sim}$... that do not meet $\Omega_j$ carry no dependence (p. 262). Alternative form (p. 262): resum over $X$ containing a component of $Z_j$, losing part of the exponential factor ("(2.42) with $d_j(X)$ replaced by $d_j(X\setminus Z_j)$").
  - $E_k$ constant, depends on $\{\Omega_j\},\{A_j\}$.
  - Improved bounds: terms with index $j<k$ are as above; the newly created $\mathbf E^{(k)},\mathbf R^{(k)},\mathbf B^{(k)}$ are defined on slightly larger spaces with $\beta$ times a number bigger, and with $\kappa$ replaced by $(1+4\beta)\kappa$ ("such improved bounds are needed for the $\mathbf R$-operation", p. 262).

**(A6) The operation $\mathbf R$.** Never defined. Pp. 244-245: "The operation $\mathbf R$ serves this purpose. We will not describe it here, we will only assume that it has some properties incorporated in the inductive description of the effective actions." P. 245: "even analyzing the $R$-operations in the future we will consider only small field parts of the corresponding large field regions".

### 2.2 Theorems

**Introductory Theorem (p. 245, informal).** "If $\varrho_k$ satisfies the assumptions described in detail in Sect. 2, then $T\varrho_k$ satisfies also the corresponding assumptions." (No $\mathbf R$ in this statement.)

**Theorem 1 (p. 262).** There exist constants, introduced in the above description, such that if the sequence of coupling constants $\{g_k\}$, determined by the recursive renormalization group (Callan-Symanzik) equations (0.18), (0.20) of [I], satisfies the inequalities (I.0.33), then the sequence of densities $\{\varrho_k\}$, generated by successive applications of the operations $\mathbf{RT}$ to $\varrho_0=\exp[-(1/g_0^2)A-E]$, satisfies all the inductive assumptions. "The constants satisfy numerous restrictions introduced in the proof." Order of constants: not stated; some of them are those of [I] ($\kappa$, $M$, $\varepsilon_j$, $\alpha_{0,j}$, $\alpha_{1,j}$), "we assume that all of them are satisfied here, and we will introduce some new restrictions also" (p. 262). Remark (p. 262): a version for a fixed index $k$ (the map from the space of densities of index $k$ into that of index $k+1$) "does not seem to be useful, or interesting now". The theorem is a statement about the specific sequence, not about a space of densities.

**Theorem 2 (p. 263).** Under the assumptions of Theorem 1 there exists a constant $E_1$ independent of $j,k,\Omega,\{\Omega_j\},\{A_j\},T$ (but depending on the other constants in Theorem 1) such that, for $\Omega=B^j(A_j^0)$-type domains ($\Omega\in\mathcal D_k$ contained in $B^j(A_j^0)$; $\phi\in C_0^\infty(\Omega^\sim)$, $\phi=1$ on $\Omega$; $\Omega^\sim$ means the union of the domains $(\Omega\cap(\Omega_n\setminus\Omega_{n+1}))^\sim$, $n=j,\dots,k$, $\sim$ in the corresponding scale), for $\beta<1$ and sufficiently regular configurations $U_k=U_k(V)$ (e.g. $V$ restricted by the characteristic functions in (2.18)):
$$\Big|\sum_{z\in A_j^0\cap\Omega}[\mathbf E^{(j)}(A_j,U_k,z)-\mathbf E^{(j)}(A_j,1,z)]-\beta_j(g_{j-1})A(\phi,U_k)\Big|\le E_1\sum_{n=j}^k(L^{j-n})^\beta|\Gamma_n\cap\Omega|\quad(2.43),$$
$E_1$ depends on $\beta$, $\to\infty$ as $\beta\to1$, $|\Gamma_n\cap\Omega|$ = number of points of $\Gamma_n\cap\Omega\subset\mathbb T_1^{(n)}$; and, for an absolute constant $R_1$ (which "can be taken equal to 1 for $g_j$ sufficiently small"),
$$\Big|\sum_{X\in\mathcal D_j,\,X\subset A_j^{\sim-1},\,X\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset}[\mathbf R^{(j)}(X,U_k)-\mathbf R^{(j)}(X,1)]\Big|\le R_1g_j^{\kappa_0}\sum_{n=j}^k|\Gamma_n\cap\Omega|\quad(2.44).$$
Consequences (pp. 263-264): $|\mathbf E_k(U_k)|\le E_1\sum_{n=1}^k|\Gamma_n|\sum_{j=1}^n(L^{j-n})^\beta<E_1(1-L^{-\beta})^{-1}\sum_n|\Gamma_n|$ (2.45); $|\mathbf R_k(U_k)|\le R_1\sum_{n=1}^k|\Gamma_n|g_n^{\kappa_0-6}$ for $\kappa_0\ge7$ (2.46); $|\mathbf B^{(j,n)}|\le B_12^{-(j-n)}|\Gamma_n|$ for $j>n$ (2.47) and $|(2.40)|\le2B_1\sum_n|\Gamma_n|$ (2.48); the same bound for $E_k$ up to a different constant.

**Corollary 3, Ultraviolet stability (p. 264).** Under the assumptions of Theorem 1 there exist constants $E_-,E_+$ independent of $\eta$ and $\mathbb T$ (but depending on $g_k$) such that
$$\chi_k(\mathbb T_\eta)\exp\Big[-\frac1{g_k^2}A(U_k(V_k))-E_-|\Gamma_n|\Big]\le\varrho_k(V_k)\le e^{E_+|\mathbb T_\eta|}\quad(2.50)$$
(as printed; the left side has $|\Gamma_n|$ presumably meaning $\sum$ over scales, UNCERTAIN). The derivation: "the first term on the right-hand side [of (2.49)] yields the small factors for large field characteristic functions. It also controls the logarithmic terms. Thus we estimate the integral $\int dV_k\varrho_k$ by a sum of terms similar to the one considered in Sect. 3 [6], e.g. for the integral... see (3.42). The procedure is constructed in such a way that the combinatorics now is the same, relative to the $\eta$-scale of the lattice $\mathbb T_\eta$, as in [6]" (p. 264). This is the only statement on the size of the large-field part of $\varrho_k$; no bound for $\mathbf T_k$ as an operator is stated.

### 2.3 What the paper says about largeness of the large-field factor (p. 244, motivation of $\mathbf R$)

A plaquette that violates the first-step small-field condition ($|U(\partial p)-1|\ge g_0p_0(g_0)$) costs $\exp[-\frac{1}{g_0^2}(1-\mathrm{Re\,tr}U(\partial p))]\le\exp(-\frac12p_0^2(g_0^2))\le g_0^{A_0(\log g_0^{-2})^{p_0-1}}$. For $d<4$, $g_0=g\varepsilon^{1/2(4-d)}$ and this is an arbitrarily large power of $\varepsilon$. For $d=4$ the bare coupling behaves as $(a+b\log\varepsilon^{-1})^{-1/2}$ and the bound gives no positive power of $\varepsilon$; it is "still small for $\varepsilon$ small, and it controls a large number of steps, but this number is a small fraction of the total number of steps. Thus for some large field regions there is a difficulty in continuing the procedure of [16], the small factor arising from large fields in this region does not control further steps. In such situations we have to change the procedure in order to improve the small factor". The operation $\mathbf R$ does this. This is exactly the mechanism behind P5's counter $K$ and the O(N) $R^{(k)}$ (P7): E8 states it but does not quantify it.

## 3. Proof architecture

**§0 Introduction (pp. 243-245).** Aims, motivation of $\mathbf R$ (2.3 above), statement of the informal theorem, remark on method: only changes relative to [I] and [16] are described; the analysis of small-field expressions "is the most important part"; characteristic functions in large-field regions "play an important, although quite technical, role".

**§1 The first renormalization transformation (pp. 245-254).** Mechanism, in order:
1. (1.1) Decompose $1$ into plaquette small-field/large-field indicators; $P_0$ = union of $LM_2R_0$-cubes containing the large-field plaquettes (p. 246).
2. (1.2)-(1.4) After the averaging, restrict the new field $V$ on cubes with indicators at $\varepsilon_1L^{-2}$ (on $\square'$ with the local background $U_{1,\square'}$ built from the $\mathbf B_1$-determining set); sum over $P_1\subset(P_0^{\prime\sim})^c$.
3. (1.5)-(1.7) Insert the delta-function gauge-fixing (axial gauge in blocks) with Faddeev-Popov; the Euclidean invariance is preserved by averaging over the Euclidean group of the lattice leaving block centre invariant (p. 247).
4. (1.8)-(1.10) Approximate fluctuation field $UU_{1,\square}^{-1}$; decomposition of unity on it into $Q_1$ (small, $<2\delta_0$) and complement; define $\Omega_1=(Q_1'^{\sim2}\cup(B(P_1^1)^c)^{\sim3})^c$, at distance $\ge2LMR_1$ from large-field regions; on $\Omega_1^\sim$ all large-field characteristic functions equal 1.
5. (1.11)-(1.16) Resummation, translation $U=U'U_1$, with $U_1=U_1(\Omega_1,V)$ the (unique, nondegenerate, minimal; Theorem 1 [15]) critical point of $A(U)+G(\Omega_1^{(1)},U)$ with $U=V_0$ on $\Omega_1^c$, $\bar U=V$ on $\Omega_1^{(1)}$. Fluctuation field $A'=\frac1i\log U'$, expansion as in [16], [I]; scaling $A=g_0A'$; linearization removes the $\delta$-functions via the operator $C$ (so $\bar QCA=0$); the quadratic form is $\langle A,C^*\Delta_1CA\rangle$ (1.15) with $\langle A,\Delta_1A\rangle$ in (1.16); expansion around the critical point so the linear term vanishes (p. 250).
6. (1.17)-(1.20) To restore gauge invariance of the characteristic function $\chi_0'(\Omega_1)$ replace it by a function of $A$ only: (1.18) with $\sup|A(b)|<g_0^{-1}\delta_0$; then $R_1$, $A_1=\Omega_1^{\sim-2}\cap(R_1'^{\sim})^c$, $S_1=(\Omega_1\setminus\Omega_1^{\sim-1})\cup R_1$ (1.20); small components of $A_1^c$ (inside cubes of size $<100LMR_1$) are replaced by the smallest rectangular parallelepiped (p. 251).
7. (1.21)-(1.25) $U_1$ on $S_1$ depends on $V$ on the whole $A_1$ (undesirable), so replace $U_1(\Omega_1,V)$ by $U_1(\Omega_1\cap A_1^c,V)$ using the exponentially decaying "transition" field $\mathbf H_{1,Ax}$ (1.21) (decay "formulated in (190) Sect. G of [15] for the Landau gauge; on the unit lattice it holds for any gauge, since propagators are exponentially decaying"); change of variables (1.22) (with a cutoff $g(A')$) on bonds in $R_1^{\sim2}\cap S_1$ and (1.22) with $g=1$ elsewhere in $S_1^*$; Jacobians exponentiated (1.24); the new terms $V^{(0)}(S_1,A,\mathbf H_{1,Ax})$ localized in $S_1$ (boundary terms).
8. (1.26) Conditional Gaussian integral over $A_1$ restricted to $A_1$ gives $\mathbf E^{(1)}$ (localized cluster expansion as in [I]) plus $\mathbf B^{(1)}$ (terms localized within $MR_1$ of $A_1^c$, $O(1)|A_1\cap A_1^c|$-bounded, "controlled by large field estimates and does not need to be renormalized").
9. (1.27)-(1.30) Coupling-constant renormalization on $A_1$ via $\beta_1(g_0)A(\phi_1,U_1)$ with $\phi_1\in C_0^\infty(A_1)$, $\phi_1=1$ on $A_1^{\sim-1}$; resulting form $\varrho_1(V)=\sum_{\{\Omega_1,A_1\}}\chi_1(\Omega_1)\mathbf T_1(\{\Omega_1,A_1\})\exp A_1(1/g_1^2,U_1)$ (1.28), with $\mathbf T_1$ (1.29) and $A_1$ (1.30) explicit. This is the base case that P5 only describes narratively.

**§2 Inductive assumption and results (pp. 254-264).** As in 2.1, 2.2. Section 2 also states what is needed from [15] for backgrounds: the determining set $\mathbf B(\Omega)$, $\mathbf B\cup\mathbf B_j(\Omega)$ (2.13)-(2.15), and the local configurations $U_{k,\square}$ (2.16).

## 7. Weak spots and open points

1. **Admissibility is not defined.** "Admissible sequences of domains" occurs (p. 257, 270, 261...) but the only conditions listed are: nesting (2.1); $S_j$ conditions (p. 255); $Z_j$ parallelepiped condition (2.3); mutual distance of boundaries $\ge2MR_j$ "which will be determined inductively by the operations in the $k+1$-st step" (p. 256); the sets are unions of cubes of the proper partition. The constructions in §3 add: $\Omega_{k+1}$ at distance $\ge2LMR_{k+1}$ from large-field regions (1.10), (3.5); $A_{k+1}$, $S_{k+1}$ given by (3.20) and the parallelepiped modification. There is no explicit enumeration and no count of sequences, no weight $C(\mathbb A)$ with $\sum C^{-1}\le1$; summation over admissible sequences is treated as a finite combinatorial sum whose entropy is absorbed implicitly.
2. **No quantitative bound on $\mathbf T_k$.** The large-field operators are not required to satisfy any bound as operators ($\int d\psi|\mathbf T_kF|\le e^{-\kappa_k}\sup\chi|F|$ in P5 (1.26)). The only substitute is the global bound (2.50) and the remark that $\int dV_k\varrho_k$ is estimated by the "small factors for large field characteristic functions". No function $\kappa_k(Z,\mathbb A_k\cap Z)$, no counter $K$ and no condition like H6 exist in E8. These must come from E9/E10 (not read). So the claim in P6 p. 18 that "the same functions have been used in [6b,c,d]" is, for [6b] = E8, unsupported by the text.
3. **The $\mathbf R$ operation is deferred.** pp. 244-245, 260, 270, 279. The properties assumed are only those listed in (A2), (A4), (A5): characteristic functions changed, domains $Z_{j+1}\cap\Omega_{j+1}\cap X$ may become empty, $A_j=\Omega_j$ may occur, $\mathbf R$-terms in $\mathbf R_k$ with bound (2.31) and reduced covariance (2.32). So E8 proves that the one-step map $T$ preserves the form, but not that the form is preserved after $\mathbf R$; Theorem 1 as stated (with $\mathbf{RT}$) is proved only in the sense that "$\mathbf R$ preserves it", which is assumed. The text p. 245 says that R-operations will act only on "small field parts of the corresponding large field regions".
4. **Theorem 1 is not a theorem about a space.** It is a statement about the specific sequence; the remark p. 262 explicitly declines the version "space $k$ into space $k+1$". P5 Theorems 1-3 are that version. The constants' order of choice is not stated.
5. **Typo-like or unclear points.** (2.50) has "$E_-|\Gamma_n|$" on the left without sum, UNCERTAIN. Theorem 2: the inequality is stated for "$\beta<1$" but the proof gives $1-\beta$, and the text says "(with $1-\beta$, $\beta>0$, instead of $\beta<1$)" (p. 283). ## 8. Preliminary formalization notes

1. P5 [5b]: **no in-text citation**; only the bibliography entry (P5 p. 521). The text cites [5d] on p. 496 (H6 discussion).
2. P6 [6b]: p. 18, after (3.10): "the same functions [$\kappa_k$] have been used in the papers [6b,c,d] on lattice gauge field theory, the only difference is in different 'running' constants used to express the smallness of the exponential factors (3.7), (3.8), etc.". 
3. P7 [8]: general listing, no result used (per the P7 review).
4. BIG_PICTURE §5 item 7 repeats "origin of the functions $\kappa$ and of the $R$ operation" for [5b,c]/[6b,c].

**What E8 actually contains relative to those citations.** The functions $\kappa_k$ (P5 (1.26), P6 (3.10)) are **not in E8**. In E8 the letter $\kappa$ denotes only the localization decay rate; the exponent of the large-field operator is nowhere defined; the inductive step for the "small factor" is not formulated; the counter $K$ is not present; the operation $\mathbf R$ is not defined. Therefore the citation of [6b] for the functions $\kappa_k$ is *defective for E8 as a precise reference* (the content is presumably in E9 and E10 = [6c], [6d]; P6 says it analyzed them "extensively in [6d]"). The nearest content in E8 is the qualitative one: the large-field small factor is $\exp(-\frac12p_0^2)$-type per violated plaquette (p. 244), the buffer width $R_j\ge(\log g_j^{-2})^r$, the sequence structure, and the explanation why for $d=4$ $\mathbf R$ is needed. No adaptation is required for these citations since nothing specific is used.

**Question: does E8 define admissibility and $\mathbb T_k$ in one place, so that the O(N) definitions can be reconstructed?**
- $\mathbf T_k$: **yes, essentially.** (2.19)-(2.22) with (2.21) give a closed explicit definition of the operator as a composition of one-step operations, with the base case (1.29) and the inductive step (3.23)-(3.24); the formulas in §3 ((3.1)-(3.25)) show how $\mathbf T^{(k+1)}$ arises from the fluctuation integral on $\Omega_{k+1}\cap A_{k+1}^c$ and from the delta-function averaging on $\Omega_{k+1}^c$. This is a complete-enough template to reconstruct P5's $\mathbb T_k$: the O(N) analogue replaces $\delta(\bar V_jV_{j+1}^{-1})$ by $\delta(\mathcal Q\psi_j-\psi_{j+1})$-type averaging (P1/P4), $A_j$ by the fluctuation $\psi'_j$, and (2.21) by the P4/P5 Gaussian with covariance $C^{(k)}$ and the action $V_{\rm eff}^{(k)}$. The gauge fixing and $\mathbf H$ transition-field changes of variable (1.21)-(1.25) become the replacement of the global background by the local one $\phi_k(\mathbb B_k(W_k);\tilde\psi_k)$ in P5 (1.4), with $\tilde\psi_k=Q^*\psi_k$, and the exponential decay of the transition is that of P2/P3.
- Admissibility: **no.** E8 does not collect the admissibility conditions either. The conditions present: nesting (2.1), $S_j$ (p. 255), the parallelepiped condition (2.3), "distance between boundaries at least $2MR_j$" (p. 256), $\Omega_{k+1}$ via (3.5), $A_{k+1},S_{k+1}$ via (3.20). Analogous conditions appear in P5 (1.20), $X^\approx$, (2.19), (2.31)-(2.32). So admissibility can be *reconstructed* only as "all sequences that the construction produces", i.e. by induction: $(\{\Omega_j\},\{A_j\},\{S_j\})$ for step $k+1$ is admissible iff it arises from an admissible step-$k$ sequence and sets $P_{k+1},Q_{k+1},R_{k+1},S_{k+1}$ satisfying the decompositions (3.2), (3.3), (3.16), (3.20). That is also the only workable definition in the O(N) case (P5 notes "all elements of $\mathbb A_k$ are determined by their restrictions to $Z_k$"). The other admissibility condition of P5 (positivity of $K$ for earlier steps) has no counterpart in E8.

## 10. Relevance to the formalization

- **Interfaces from E8 that transfer verbatim in structure:** (a) the form (2.18) of $\varrho_k$ as a sum over admissible domain sequences of $\chi_k\mathbf T_k e^{A_k}$; (b) factorization of $\mathbf T_k$ in space and in levels (2.19)-(2.22); (c) the inductive definition $\mathbf T_{k+1}=\mathbf T^{(k+1)}\mathbf T_k$ with explicit $\mathbf T^{(k+1)}$ (3.23)-(3.25); (d) the decomposition of the exponent into $\mathbf E_k$ (fully renormalized, localized in $A_j$, Euclidean covariant), $\mathbf R_k$ (reduced symmetry, small $g_j^{\kappa_0}$, no coupling renormalization, merged into $\mathbf E$ after $\log$-many steps) and $\mathbf B_k$ (boundary, bounded, no renormalization); (e) the localization of the transition field by an exponentially decaying function $\mathbf H$ and the change of variables that makes the characteristic functions depend only on the *large-field* variables; (f) the control of the density at the end (Corollary 3 type statement (2.50)).
- **What a Lean development would take from E8 as a design guide:** the geometry of domains and buffers; the principle that large-field factors come from characteristic functions with $\varepsilon_j=g_jp_0(g_j)$ and buffer $R_j\ge(\log g_j^{-2})^r$; the observation (p. 244) that for the marginal case the large-field smallness controls only a fraction of the steps (explains $K$, H6, $R$-operation in the O(N) case, which has the same logarithmic running of the coupling per scale, $\beta_k\sim L^{k(d-2)}$ being in fact a *power*; note for the O(N) model with $d\ge3$ the situation is different: the O(N) analogue of "no positive power of $\varepsilon$" is the $\log\beta_k$ factors in P5 (1.30); the two are not the same statement, see below).
- **Gauge-specific, irrelevant to O(N):** Faddeev-Popov and axial gauge-fixing, gauge covariance of all expressions, Ward-Takahashi derivation of the $\beta$-function (3.49)-(3.64) (in the spin case the analogous renormalization conditions are fixed by P1 §3 and the symmetry by $O(N)$-invariance and Euclidean covariance), the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff and group-valued fluctuation fields, the variational problem for Wilson configurations ([15]; replaced by P2), the transition field $\mathbf H$ built from $\log$ of group elements (replaced by the difference of local and global backgrounds).
- **Not in E8 and therefore unavailable from it:** the $\kappa_k$ functions and $K$ (needed for P5 H5-H6 and P6 §3), the $\mathbf R$ operation (P7's O(N) version is the only full description in the bundle), the quantitative bound on $\mathbf T_k$. For a faithful Lean development with large fields in scope, these must come from P5/P6/P7 directly (and E9/E10 for the combinatorics: P6 Prop. 3.2). E8 should not be treated as the source of those items.
- **Differences in setting that matter for the O(N) translation.** In E8 (d=4, YM) the scale-$j$ coupling goes to zero slowly ($g_j\sim j^{-1/2}$) and the small factor $g^{A_0(\log g^{-2})^{p_0-1}}$ is subpolynomial in the total number of scales (p. 244). In the O(N) setting with $d\ge3$, $\beta_k\sim L^{k(d-2)}$ grows geometrically and the large-field factor is $\exp(-c(\log\beta_k)^{2p_0})$, which controls $O(\log\beta_k)$-many further steps (P7 pp. 494-495). Hence the qualitative picture in E8 p. 244 carries over, but the quantitative inequalities of P5 (1.30) (and the $K$ counter) are the O(N) replacements, not copies. [Statement about comparison is my reading of P5/P6/P7 reviews; not checked against E9/E10.]
- **Green's function / propagator results:** E8 contains none; it cites exponential decay of $\mathbf H$ and of the minimizing function to [15]. A standard replacement (Combes-Thomas or Neumann series with block decomposition) is plausible for the O(N) background and propagator bounds; flagged as a suggestion only, not verified here.
- **Recommendation.** E8 is useful as the *only place* where an explicit inductive definition of the large-field operator $\mathbf T_k$ and a complete first step with large fields are written out, in a structure that parallels P5. A Lean development can take (2.19)-(2.22), (2.21), (3.23)-(3.25) as the template for $\mathbb T_k$ and the admissibility-by-construction convention, but should not look to E8 for $\kappa$, $K$, $\mathbf R$, or for an estimate on $\mathbb T_k$. E9 and E10 must be read for those (P5 [5c], [5d]; P6 [6c], [6d]).
